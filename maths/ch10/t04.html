Topic 4 of 5

Theorem 10.2: tangents from an external point are equal

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • State and prove Theorem 10.2 (the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal), by RHS congruence and by Pythagoras.
  • Use the angle facts of two tangents from a point (OP bisects ∠APB and ∠AOB; ∠APB + ∠AOB = 180°; △PAB is isosceles, so ∠APB = 2∠OAB).
  • Solve tangent-pair problems with numbers (tangent length, angles, area of the kite OAPB), including sample-paper case studies.

Stand at a point P outside a round pond and stretch two ropes from P so that each just grazes the edge, one on the left and one on the right. Measure both ropes from P to where they touch: they are exactly the same length. Move to another spot and try again — still equal. This is the second theorem of the chapter, and like the first, the syllabus asks you to prove it. Together, the two theorems give a figure — the "kite" — that appears in almost every Circles question in the sample papers.

  1. The theorem and its proof

Theorem 10.2. The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

Given: a circle with centre O; a point P outside it; tangents PQ and PR touching the circle at Q and R.

To prove: PQ=PRPQ = PRPQ=PR.

Construction: join OP, OQ and OR.

Statement Reason
1. ∠OQP=∠ORP=90°\angle OQP = \angle ORP = 90°∠OQP=∠ORP=90° Tangent ⟂ radius at the point of contact (Theorem 10.1)
2. OQ=OROQ = OROQ=OR Radii of the same circle
3. OP=OPOP = OPOP=OP Common (the hypotenuse of both right triangles)
4. △OQP≅△ORP\triangle OQP \cong \triangle ORP△OQP≅△ORP RHS (steps 1, 2, 3)
5. PQ=PRPQ = PRPQ=PR CPCT. Hence proved.

The same result by Pythagoras. In the right triangles OQP and ORP:

PQ2=OP2−OQ2PQ^2 = OP^2 - OQ^2PQ2=OP2−OQ2Swipe to see the whole equation →
PR2=OP2−OR2PR^2 = OP^2 - OR^2PR2=OP2−OR2Swipe to see the whole equation →

and OQ=OROQ = OROQ=OR, so PQ2=PR2PQ^2 = PR^2PQ2=PR2, which gives PQ=PRPQ = PRPQ=PR.

  1. More from the same congruence

The congruence △OQP≅△ORP\triangle OQP \cong \triangle ORP△OQP≅△ORP gives two more pairs of equal angles (CPCT):

  • ∠OPQ=∠OPR\angle OPQ = \angle OPR∠OPQ=∠OPR — OP bisects the angle between the tangents. The centre lies on the bisector of ∠QPR\angle QPR∠QPR.
  • ∠POQ=∠POR\angle POQ = \angle POR∠POQ=∠POR — OP also bisects the angle between the radii, ∠QOR\angle QOR∠QOR.

  1. The kite OQPR

Put the four points together — O, Q, P, R. Two sides are equal radii, two sides are equal tangents, and the angles at Q and R are right angles. Call it the kite. Its facts:

  • Angles: the quadrilateral's angles add up to 360°, and two of them are 90°, so
∠QPR+∠QOR=180°\angle QPR + \angle QOR = 180°∠QPR+∠QOR=180°Swipe to see the whole equation →

The angle between the tangents and the angle between the radii are supplementary (NCERT Exercise 10.2 Q10 asks you to prove this).

  • Halves: ∠OPQ=12∠QPR\angle OPQ = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle QPR∠OPQ=21​∠QPR and ∠POQ=12∠QOR\angle POQ = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle QOR∠POQ=21​∠QOR, and in right triangle OQP these two halves add up to 90°.
  • Area: the kite is two congruent right triangles, each 12×OQ×PQ\dfrac{1}{2} \times OQ \times PQ21​×OQ×PQ, so
area of OQPR=OQ×PQ\text{area of OQPR} = OQ \times PQarea of OQPR=OQ×PQSwipe to see the whole equation →

that is, radius × tangent length.

  1. The chord joining the points of contact

PQ=PRPQ = PRPQ=PR, so △PQR is isosceles and ∠PQR=∠PRQ=180°−∠QPR2=90°−12∠QPR\angle PQR = \angle PRQ = \dfrac{180° - \angle QPR}{2} = 90° - \dfrac{1}{2}\angle QPR∠PQR=∠PRQ=2180°−∠QPR​=90°−21​∠QPR. Since ∠OQP=90°\angle OQP = 90°∠OQP=90°,

∠OQR=90°−∠PQR=12∠QPR\angle OQR = 90° - \angle PQR = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle QPR∠OQR=90°−∠PQR=21​∠QPRSwipe to see the whole equation →

That is, the angle between the tangents is twice the angle between the chord QR and a radius: ∠QPR=2∠OQR\angle QPR = 2\angle OQR∠QPR=2∠OQR (NCERT Example 2; asked in the sample papers as "∠APB=2∠OAB\angle APB = 2\angle OAB∠APB=2∠OAB").

Also, P and O are each the same distance from Q and from R, so OP is the perpendicular bisector of the chord QR.

NCERT Example 3 asks "Why?" at the line ∠TPR+∠RPO=90°=∠TPR+∠PTR\angle TPR + \angle RPO = 90° = \angle TPR + \angle PTR∠TPR+∠RPO=90°=∠TPR+∠PTR (there the tangents from T touch at P and Q, and OT meets PQ at R). The first equality holds because ∠TPO=90°\angle TPO = 90°∠TPO=90° (tangent ⟂ radius at the point of contact). The second holds because OT ⟂ PQ, which is the fact just above, so △TRP is right-angled at R and its other two angles add up to 90°.

Worked examples

Worked example 1

Proving Theorem 10.2

Question. Prove that the lengths of the tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

Given: a circle with centre O; an external point T; tangents TA and TB touching the circle at A and B. To prove: TA=TBTA = TBTA=TB. Construction: join OA, OB and OT.

Concept used: Theorem 10.1 gives two right angles; RHS congruence; CPCT. Why it applies: the two triangles share the hypotenuse OT and have equal radii.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. ∠OAT=∠OBT=90°\angle OAT = \angle OBT = 90°∠OAT=∠OBT=90° Tangent ⟂ radius at the point of contact
    2. OA=OBOA = OBOA=OB Radii of the same circle
    3. OT=OTOT = OTOT=OT Common
    4. △OAT≅△OBT\triangle OAT \cong \triangle OBT△OAT≅△OBT RHS
    5. TA=TBTA = TBTA=TB CPCT. Hence proved.

  2. Check (with numbers): circle of radius 20 and OT=52OT = 52OT=52: TA=522−202=2304=48TA = \sqrt{52^2 - 20^2} = \sqrt{2304} = 48TA=522−202​=2304​=48 and TB=522−202=48TB = \sqrt{52^2 - 20^2} = 48TB=522−202​=48 ✔ — the Pythagoras proof in numbers.

  3. Answer TA=TBTA = TBTA=TB — proved.

    Common trap: Writing "SAS" in step 4. The right angles are not between OA and OT, so SAS does not fit; the rule is RHS (right angle, common hypotenuse, equal radii).

Worked example 2

The angle at the centre

Question. PA and PB are tangents from P to a circle with centre O, and ∠APB=64°\angle APB = 64°∠APB=64°. Find (a) ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB (b) ∠OPA\angle OPA∠OPA (c) ∠AOP\angle AOP∠AOP.

Given: ∠APB=64°\angle APB = 64°∠APB=64°. Asked: three angles.

Concept used: the kite: ∠APB+∠AOB=180°\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180°∠APB+∠AOB=180°; OP bisects both angles. Why it applies: OAPB has right angles at A and B.

  1. 1

    (a) ∠OAP=∠OBP=90°\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90°∠OAP=∠OBP=90° (tangent ⟂ radius). In quadrilateral OAPB:

    ∠AOB=360°−90°−90°−64°\angle AOB = 360° - 90° - 90° - 64°∠AOB=360°−90°−90°−64°Swipe to see the whole equation →
    =116°= 116°=116°Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    (b) OP bisects ∠APB\angle APB∠APB: ∠OPA=64°2=32°\angle OPA = \dfrac{64°}{2} = 32°∠OPA=264°​=32°.

  3. 3

    (c) In right triangle OAP: ∠AOP=90°−32°=58°\angle AOP = 90° - 32° = 58°∠AOP=90°−32°=58° (which is also 116°2\dfrac{116°}{2}2116°​).

  4. Check: 64°+116°=180°64° + 116° = 180°64°+116°=180° ✔ and 32°+58°=90°32° + 58° = 90°32°+58°=90° ✔.

  5. Answer (a) 116° (b) 32° (c) 58°

    Common trap: Taking ∠AOB=64°\angle AOB = 64°∠AOB=64° ("the same as the angle between the tangents") or 128°128°128° ("double it"). The two angles are supplementary, not equal and not double.

Worked example 3

The angle between the chord and a radius

Question. Tangents PA and PB are drawn from P to a circle with centre O, and ∠APB=76°\angle APB = 76°∠APB=76°. Find ∠PAB\angle PAB∠PAB and ∠OAB\angle OAB∠OAB.

Given: ∠APB=76°\angle APB = 76°∠APB=76°. Asked: ∠PAB\angle PAB∠PAB and ∠OAB\angle OAB∠OAB.

Concept used: PA=PBPA = PBPA=PB, so △PAB is isosceles; ∠OAP=90°\angle OAP = 90°∠OAP=90°. Why it applies: the chord AB and the radius OA both start at A.

  1. 1

    PA=PBPA = PBPA=PB (tangents from an external point), so ∠PAB=∠PBA\angle PAB = \angle PBA∠PAB=∠PBA.

  2. 2

    ∠PAB=180°−76°2=104°2=52°\angle PAB = \dfrac{180° - 76°}{2} = \dfrac{104°}{2} = 52°∠PAB=2180°−76°​=2104°​=52°.

  3. 3

    ∠OAP=90°\angle OAP = 90°∠OAP=90° (tangent ⟂ radius), so ∠OAB=90°−52°=38°\angle OAB = 90° - 52° = 38°∠OAB=90°−52°=38°.

  4. Check: ∠APB=2∠OAB\angle APB = 2\angle OAB∠APB=2∠OAB: 2×38°=76°2 \times 38° = 76°2×38°=76° ✔.

  5. Answer ∠PAB=52°\angle PAB = 52°∠PAB=52°; ∠OAB=38°\angle OAB = 38°∠OAB=38°

    Common trap: Taking ∠OAB=12(180°−76°)=52°\angle OAB = \dfrac{1}{2}(180° - 76°) = 52°∠OAB=21​(180°−76°)=52° — that is ∠PAB\angle PAB∠PAB, the angle between the chord and the tangent, not the radius.

Worked example 4

Tangent length and the area of the kite

Question. From a point P, 15 cm from the centre O of a circle of radius 9 cm, tangents PA and PB are drawn. Find (a) PA and PB (b) the area of the quadrilateral OAPB.

Given: OP=15OP = 15OP=15 cm; radius 9 cm. Asked: PA, PB; area of OAPB.

Concept used: Theorem 10.1 and Pythagoras; Theorem 10.2; area of a right triangle. Why it applies: OAPB is two congruent right triangles.

  1. 1

    (a) ∠OAP=90°\angle OAP = 90°∠OAP=90°, so PA=152−92=225−81=144=12PA = \sqrt{15^2 - 9^2} = \sqrt{225 - 81} = \sqrt{144} = 12PA=152−92​=225−81​=144​=12 cm.

  2. 2

    PB=PA=12PB = PA = 12PB=PA=12 cm (tangents from an external point are equal).

  3. 3

    (b) Area of △OAP =12×OA×PA=12×9×12=54= \dfrac{1}{2} \times OA \times PA = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 9 \times 12 = 54=21​×OA×PA=21​×9×12=54 cm².

  4. 4

    △OBP is congruent to △OAP, so area of OAPB =2×54=108= 2 \times 54 = 108=2×54=108 cm².

  5. Check: area = radius × tangent =9×12=108= 9 \times 12 = 108=9×12=108 cm² ✔.

  6. Answer (a) 12 cm each (b) 108 cm²

    Common trap: Using 12×OP×PA\dfrac{1}{2} \times OP \times PA21​×OP×PA for a triangle. OP is the hypotenuse, not a height; the two sides at the right angle are OA and PA.

Worked example 5

Tangents inclined at 60°

Question. Two tangents PA and PB to a circle of radius 5 cm with centre O are inclined to each other at 60°. Find the length of each tangent and the distance OP.

Given: ∠APB=60°\angle APB = 60°∠APB=60°; radius 5 cm. Asked: PA and OP.

Concept used: OP bisects ∠APB\angle APB∠APB; right triangle OAP; tan and sin of 30° (Chapter 8). Why it applies: we know one side (the radius) and an angle of the right triangle.

  1. 1

    OP bisects ∠APB\angle APB∠APB, so ∠OPA=30°\angle OPA = 30°∠OPA=30°; and ∠OAP=90°\angle OAP = 90°∠OAP=90° (tangent ⟂ radius).

  2. 2

    In right triangle OAP, OA is opposite the 30° angle and PA is next to it:

    tan⁡30°=OAPA\tan 30° = \dfrac{OA}{PA}tan30°=PAOA​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    13=5PA\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{5}{PA}3​1​=PA5​, so PA=53PA = 5\sqrt{3}PA=53​ cm; also PB=53PB = 5\sqrt{3}PB=53​ cm.

  4. 4

    sin⁡30°=OAOP\sin 30° = \dfrac{OA}{OP}sin30°=OPOA​, so 12=5OP\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{OP}21​=OP5​ and OP=10OP = 10OP=10 cm.

  5. Check: Pythagoras: 52+(53)2=25+75=100=1025^2 + (5\sqrt{3})^2 = 25 + 75 = 100 = 10^252+(53​)2=25+75=100=102 ✔.

  6. Answer PA=PB=53PA = PB = 5\sqrt{3}PA=PB=53​ cm (about 8.66 cm); OP=10OP = 10OP=10 cm

Worked example 6

OP is the perpendicular bisector of the chord of contact

Question. PA and PB are tangents from P to a circle with centre O. OP meets AB at M. Prove that AM=MBAM = MBAM=MB and OP⊥ABOP \perp ABOP⊥AB.

Given: tangents PA, PB; OP meets AB at M. To prove: AM=MBAM = MBAM=MB and ∠AMP=90°\angle AMP = 90°∠AMP=90°.

Concept used: Theorem 10.2 (PA=PBPA = PBPA=PB) and the congruence it comes from (∠APM=∠BPM\angle APM = \angle BPM∠APM=∠BPM); SAS. Why it applies: triangles PMA and PMB have two pairs of equal sides with the included angle equal.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. PA=PBPA = PBPA=PB Tangents from an external point are equal (Theorem 10.2)
    2. ∠APM=∠BPM\angle APM = \angle BPM∠APM=∠BPM OP bisects the angle between the tangents (△OAP≅△OBP\triangle OAP \cong \triangle OBP△OAP≅△OBP, CPCT)
    3. PM=PMPM = PMPM=PM Common
    4. △PMA≅△PMB\triangle PMA \cong \triangle PMB△PMA≅△PMB SAS (the angle at P is between PA and PM, and between PB and PM)
    5. AM=MBAM = MBAM=MB CPCT
    6. ∠AMP=∠BMP\angle AMP = \angle BMP∠AMP=∠BMP, and they add up to 180° CPCT; angles on the straight line AB
    7. ∠AMP=90°\angle AMP = 90°∠AMP=90°, so OP⊥ABOP \perp ABOP⊥AB Each of two equal angles on a line is 90°. Hence proved.

  2. Check (with numbers): radius 9, OP=15OP = 15OP=15: A and B are at (5.4,±7.2)(5.4, \pm 7.2)(5.4,±7.2) with O at the origin and P at (15, 0); M = (5.4, 0) is the mid-point of AB and AB is vertical while OP is horizontal ✔.

  3. Answer AM=MBAM = MBAM=MB and OP⊥ABOP \perp ABOP⊥AB — proved.

    Common trap: Using RHS in step 4. Triangles PMA and PMB are not yet known to be right-angled — that is what we are proving. Here the rule is SAS.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that confusing the angle between the tangents with the angle at the centre, proof gaps (a missing construction or the wrong congruence rule) and pairing equal tangents wrongly are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • ∠AOB=∠APB\angle AOB = \angle APB∠AOB=∠APB (or =2∠APB= 2\angle APB=2∠APB)

    OAPB has two right angles, so the other two angles add up to 180°.

    ∠AOB=180°−∠APB\angle AOB = 180° - \angle APB∠AOB=180°−∠APB.

  • Halving the wrong angle: ∠OPA=12∠AOB\angle OPA = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle AOB∠OPA=21​∠AOB

    OP halves each angle at its own end.

    ∠OPA=12∠APB\angle OPA = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle APB∠OPA=21​∠APB; ∠AOP=12∠AOB\angle AOP = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle AOB∠AOP=21​∠AOB.

  • ∠OAB\angle OAB∠OAB taken as 180°−∠APB2\dfrac{180° - \angle APB}{2}2180°−∠APB​

    That is ∠PAB\angle PAB∠PAB, the angle with the tangent.

    ∠OAB=90°−∠PAB=12∠APB\angle OAB = 90° - \angle PAB = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle APB∠OAB=90°−∠PAB=21​∠APB.

  • Proof of Theorem 10.2 with no "join OP, OQ, OR", or with "SAS"

    Without the radii there are no triangles; the right angles are not between the equal sides.

    Write the construction; use RHS with the reason "tangent ⟂ radius" for the right angles.

  • Setting equal two tangents from different points

    Theorem 10.2 is about the two tangents from one point.

    Before writing "=", check that both tangents start at the same outside point.

Key points

  • Theorem 10.2: tangents from an external point are equal. Proof: join OP, OQ, OR; RHS (right angles, common OP, equal radii); CPCT.
  • OP bisects the angle between the tangents and the angle between the radii.
  • Kite OAPB: ∠APB+∠AOB=180°\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180°∠APB+∠AOB=180°; area =radius×tangent= \text{radius} \times \text{tangent}=radius×tangent.
  • △PAB is isosceles: ∠PAB=90°−12∠APB\angle PAB = 90° - \dfrac{1}{2}\angle APB∠PAB=90°−21​∠APB and ∠OAB=12∠APB\angle OAB = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle APB∠OAB=21​∠APB.
  • OP is the perpendicular bisector of the chord AB joining the points of contact.
