Topic 5 of 5

Circles inside polygons and two circles

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Prove and use AB + CD = AD + BC for a quadrilateral circumscribing a circle, and its consequences (a circumscribing parallelogram is a rhombus; opposite sides subtend supplementary angles at the centre; ∠AOB = 90° between two parallel tangents).
  • Find the sides of a triangle circumscribing a circle from its tangent segments (including area = ½ × r × perimeter, and the inradius of a right triangle).
  • Solve problems with a third tangent (the perimeter of the triangle it cuts off), common tangents of two circles and concentric circles.

Look at the logo of a sports day: a circle sitting snugly inside a triangle, touching all three sides. Or a coin in a square frame. Each side of the frame is a tangent to the circle, and each corner of the frame is an external point with two tangents coming out of it. So Theorem 10.2 applies at every corner at once — and that one idea gives you the side lengths, the perimeter and even the area. This is where the sample papers take most of their 2- and 3-mark Circles questions.

  1. The tangent-segment habit

When a circle touches all the sides of a polygon, the polygon circumscribes the circle (and the circle is inscribed in the polygon). Each side touches the circle at one point and is split there into two tangent segments.

At each corner, the two tangent segments that start there are equal (Theorem 10.2 — they are the two tangents from that corner). So, before writing any equation:

  1. Mark the points of contact.
  2. Give the two equal pieces at each corner one letter: aaa at A, bbb at B, and so on.
  3. Write every side as a sum of two letters: AB=a+bAB = a + bAB=a+b, BC=b+cBC = b + cBC=b+c, …

  1. A quadrilateral circumscribing a circle

With the letters a,b,c,da, b, c, da,b,c,d at A, B, C, D:

AB+CD=(a+b)+(c+d)AB + CD = (a + b) + (c + d)AB+CD=(a+b)+(c+d)Swipe to see the whole equation →
AD+BC=(a+d)+(b+c)AD + BC = (a + d) + (b + c)AD+BC=(a+d)+(b+c)Swipe to see the whole equation →

Both sums are a+b+c+da + b + c + da+b+c+d, so

AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BCSwipe to see the whole equation →

The two pairs of opposite sides have equal sums. (NCERT Exercise 10.2 Q8 asks you to write this as a proof — see the NCERT solutions.) Three results follow:

  • A parallelogram that circumscribes a circle is a rhombus (Exercise 10.2 Q11): opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal, so 2AB=2AD2AB = 2AD2AB=2AD and AB=ADAB = ADAB=AD.
  • Opposite sides subtend supplementary angles at the centre (Exercise 10.2 Q13): ∠AOB+∠COD=180°\angle AOB + \angle COD = 180°∠AOB+∠COD=180°.
  • Two parallel tangents and a third tangent AB between them: ∠AOB=90°\angle AOB = 90°∠AOB=90° (Exercise 10.2 Q9).

  1. A triangle circumscribing a circle

Let the circle touch BC at D, CA at E and AB at F, with tangent pieces xxx at A, yyy at B, zzz at C:

AB=x+yAB = x + yAB=x+ySwipe to see the whole equation →
BC=y+zBC = y + zBC=y+zSwipe to see the whole equation →
CA=z+xCA = z + xCA=z+xSwipe to see the whole equation →

Adding, AB+BC+CA=2(x+y+z)AB + BC + CA = 2(x + y + z)AB+BC+CA=2(x+y+z). With s=AB+BC+CA2s = \dfrac{AB + BC + CA}{2}s=2AB+BC+CA​ (the semi-perimeter):

x=s−BCx = s - BCx=s−BCSwipe to see the whole equation →
y=s−CAy = s - CAy=s−CASwipe to see the whole equation →
z=s−ABz = s - ABz=s−ABSwipe to see the whole equation →

Area with the radius r. Join the centre O to A, B and C. The triangle splits into three triangles, each with a side as base and height r (the radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the side):

area=12r×AB+12r×BC+12r×CA\text{area} = \dfrac{1}{2} r \times AB + \dfrac{1}{2} r \times BC + \dfrac{1}{2} r \times CAarea=21​r×AB+21​r×BC+21​r×CASwipe to see the whole equation →
=12×r×perimeter= \dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times \text{perimeter}=21​×r×perimeterSwipe to see the whole equation →

Quick reminder — Heron's formula (Class 9): a triangle with sides aaa, bbb, ccc and semi-perimeter sss has area s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)\sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)​. NCERT's answer to Exercise 10.2 Q12 uses it together with the formula above.

A right triangle. If ∠C=90°\angle C = 90°∠C=90°, the radii to the two legs and the legs themselves make a square of side r at C, so z=rz = rz=r, and r=leg1+leg2−hypotenuse2r = \dfrac{\text{leg}_1 + \text{leg}_2 - \text{hypotenuse}}{2}r=2leg1​+leg2​−hypotenuse​.

  1. A third tangent

From an outside point A draw the tangents AP and AQ. Draw a third tangent touching the circle at R, on the arc between P and Q nearest to A, meeting AP at B and AQ at C. Then BR=BPBR = BPBR=BP and CR=CQCR = CQCR=CQ (tangents from B and from C), so

perimeter=AB+(BR+RC)+CA\text{perimeter} = AB + (BR + RC) + CAperimeter=AB+(BR+RC)+CASwipe to see the whole equation →
=(AB+BP)+(AC+CQ)= (AB + BP) + (AC + CQ)=(AB+BP)+(AC+CQ)Swipe to see the whole equation →
=AP+AQ=2AP= AP + AQ = 2AP=AP+AQ=2APSwipe to see the whole equation →

The perimeter of the small triangle is twice the tangent from A — wherever R is.

  1. Two circles

  • Concentric circles: a chord of the larger circle that touches the smaller one is bisected at the point of contact (Topic 3).
  • Circles that touch: if two circles touch at T and the line through T touching both is their common tangent, then from any point P on that line, the tangent to each circle equals PT. So the tangents from P to the two circles are equal.

Worked example 1

A missing side of a circumscribed quadrilateral

Question. A quadrilateral ABCD circumscribes a circle. AB=9AB = 9AB=9 cm, BC=11BC = 11BC=11 cm and CD=13CD = 13CD=13 cm. Find AD.

Given: ABCD circumscribes a circle; three sides. Asked: AD.

Concept used: AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC (equal tangent segments from each corner). Why it applies: every side of ABCD touches the circle.

  1. 1

    AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC.

  2. 2

    9+13=AD+119 + 13 = AD + 119+13=AD+11.

  3. 3

    AD=22−11=11AD = 22 - 11 = 11AD=22−11=11 cm.

  4. Check: a drawing exists with tangent pieces 4, 5, 6 and 7 cm from A, B, C and D: AB=4+5=9AB = 4 + 5 = 9AB=4+5=9, BC=5+6=11BC = 5 + 6 = 11BC=5+6=11, CD=6+7=13CD = 6 + 7 = 13CD=6+7=13, DA=7+4=11DA = 7 + 4 = 11DA=7+4=11 ✔.

  5. Answer AD=11AD = 11AD=11 cm

    Common trap: Adding adjacent sides: "AB+BC=CD+DAAB + BC = CD + DAAB+BC=CD+DA" gives DA=7DA = 7DA=7 cm. The rule pairs opposite sides: AB with CD, AD with BC.

Worked example 2

A rectangle that circumscribes a circle is a square

Question. Prove that a rectangle that circumscribes a circle is a square.

Given: a rectangle ABCD whose sides touch a circle. To prove: ABCD is a square.

Concept used: AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC; opposite sides of a rectangle are equal. Why it applies: a rectangle is a parallelogram, and its sides are tangents.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC A quadrilateral circumscribing a circle (tangents from each corner are equal)
    2. CD=ABCD = ABCD=AB and BC=ADBC = ADBC=AD Opposite sides of a rectangle are equal
    3. 2AB=2AD2AB = 2AD2AB=2AD, so AB=ADAB = ADAB=AD Substitute step 2 in step 1
    4. ABCD is a square A rectangle with two adjacent sides equal has all four sides equal. Hence proved.

  2. Check (with numbers): a circle of radius 3 fits in a 6 by 6 square — each side is 3 from the centre ✔; in a 6 by 8 rectangle, a circle touching both long sides has radius 3 and cannot reach the short sides, which are 4 from the centre ✔.

  3. Answer ABCD is a square — proved.

    Common trap: Stopping at "AB = AD, so it is a rhombus". A rectangle with equal adjacent sides is a square — say so.

Worked example 3

The tangent pieces of a triangle

Question. A circle is inscribed in △ABC, touching BC at D, CA at E and AB at F. AB=11AB = 11AB=11 cm, BC=13BC = 13BC=13 cm and CA=12CA = 12CA=12 cm. Find AF, BD and CE.

Given: the three sides. Asked: the tangent pieces from A, B and C.

Concept used: equal tangent pieces xxx, yyy, zzz from A, B, C; x=s−BCx = s - BCx=s−BC and so on. Why it applies: each corner is an external point with two tangents.

  1. 1

    Let AF=AE=xAF = AE = xAF=AE=x, BF=BD=yBF = BD = yBF=BD=y, CD=CE=zCD = CE = zCD=CE=z (tangents from an external point are equal).

  2. 2

    Semi-perimeter: s=11+13+122=18s = \dfrac{11 + 13 + 12}{2} = 18s=211+13+12​=18 cm, and x+y+z=s=18x + y + z = s = 18x+y+z=s=18.

  3. 3

    x=s−BC=18−13=5x = s - BC = 18 - 13 = 5x=s−BC=18−13=5 cm; y=s−CA=18−12=6y = s - CA = 18 - 12 = 6y=s−CA=18−12=6 cm; z=s−AB=18−11=7z = s - AB = 18 - 11 = 7z=s−AB=18−11=7 cm.

  4. Check: x+y=5+6=11=ABx + y = 5 + 6 = 11 = ABx+y=5+6=11=AB ✔, y+z=6+7=13=BCy + z = 6 + 7 = 13 = BCy+z=6+7=13=BC ✔, z+x=7+5=12=CAz + x = 7 + 5 = 12 = CAz+x=7+5=12=CA ✔.

  5. Answer AF=5AF = 5AF=5 cm, BD=6BD = 6BD=6 cm, CE=7CE = 7CE=7 cm

    Common trap: Taking AF=s−ABAF = s - ABAF=s−AB. The piece at a corner is sss minus the side opposite that corner — the side the corner does not touch.

Worked example 4

Sides from the radius and the area

Question. A triangle ABC circumscribes a circle of radius 2 cm. The point of contact D divides BC into BD=3BD = 3BD=3 cm and DC=10DC = 10DC=10 cm. The area of △ABC is 30 cm². Find AB and AC.

Given: r=2r = 2r=2 cm; BD=3BD = 3BD=3 cm; DC=10DC = 10DC=10 cm; area 30 cm². Asked: AB and AC.

Concept used: equal tangent pieces; area =12×r×= \dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times=21​×r× perimeter. Why it applies: the radius to each side is the height of one of three small triangles.

  1. 1

    Tangents from B: BF=BD=3BF = BD = 3BF=BD=3 cm. Tangents from C: CE=CD=10CE = CD = 10CE=CD=10 cm. Tangents from A: AF=AE=xAF = AE = xAF=AE=x cm.

  2. 2

    Sides: AB=x+3AB = x + 3AB=x+3, AC=x+10AC = x + 10AC=x+10, BC=13BC = 13BC=13; perimeter =2x+26= 2x + 26=2x+26.

  3. 3

    Area =12×2×(2x+26)=2x+26= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times (2x + 26) = 2x + 26=21​×2×(2x+26)=2x+26.

  4. 4

    2x+26=302x + 26 = 302x+26=30, so x=2x = 2x=2.

  5. 5

    AB=2+3=5AB = 2 + 3 = 5AB=2+3=5 cm and AC=2+10=12AC = 2 + 10 = 12AC=2+10=12 cm.

  6. Check: sides 5, 12, 13: 52+122=169=1325^2 + 12^2 = 169 = 13^252+122=169=132, a right triangle with area 12×5×12=30\dfrac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 12 = 3021​×5×12=30 cm² ✔; and its inradius is 5+12−132=2\dfrac{5 + 12 - 13}{2} = 225+12−13​=2 cm ✔.

  7. Answer AB=5AB = 5AB=5 cm, AC=12AC = 12AC=12 cm

    Common trap: Using area=r×perimeter\text{area} = r \times \text{perimeter}area=r×perimeter (forgetting the half): 2(2x+26)=302(2x + 26) = 302(2x+26)=30 gives x=−5.5x = -5.5x=−5.5 — a negative length is a warning sign.

Worked example 5

The perimeter cut off by a third tangent

Question. A point A is 25 cm from the centre O of a circle of radius 7 cm. AP and AQ are the tangents from A. A tangent at a point R of the minor arc PQ meets AP at B and AQ at C. Find the perimeter of △ABC.

Given: OA=25OA = 25OA=25 cm; radius 7 cm. Asked: AB+BC+CAAB + BC + CAAB+BC+CA.

Concept used: Theorem 10.1 and Pythagoras for AP; Theorem 10.2 at A, B and C. Why it applies: B and C are external points too.

  1. 1

    ∠OPA=90°\angle OPA = 90°∠OPA=90°, so AP=252−72=625−49=576=24AP = \sqrt{25^2 - 7^2} = \sqrt{625 - 49} = \sqrt{576} = 24AP=252−72​=625−49​=576​=24 cm; AQ=AP=24AQ = AP = 24AQ=AP=24 cm.

  2. 2

    BR=BPBR = BPBR=BP (tangents from B) and CR=CQCR = CQCR=CQ (tangents from C).

  3. 3

    Perimeter =AB+BR+RC+CA=(AB+BP)+(CQ+CA)=AP+AQ= AB + BR + RC + CA = (AB + BP) + (CQ + CA) = AP + AQ=AB+BR+RC+CA=(AB+BP)+(CQ+CA)=AP+AQ.

  4. 4

    Perimeter =24+24=48= 24 + 24 = 48=24+24=48 cm.

  5. Check: take R as the point of the circle nearest A (on OA, 18 cm from A). Then BC is perpendicular to OA, and the numbers work out to AB=AC=754AB = AC = \dfrac{75}{4}AB=AC=475​ cm and BC=212BC = \dfrac{21}{2}BC=221​ cm: 754+754+212=962=48\dfrac{75}{4} + \dfrac{75}{4} + \dfrac{21}{2} = \dfrac{96}{2} = 48475​+475​+221​=296​=48 ✔.

  6. Answer 48 cm

    Common trap: Adding AP+AQ+PQAP + AQ + PQAP+AQ+PQ or looking for the position of R. The perimeter does not depend on where R is — the pieces BR and CR are swapped for BP and CQ.

Worked example 6

Two circles that touch

Question. Two circles touch each other externally at T, and the line ℓ\ellℓ through T touches both circles. P is a point on ℓ\ellℓ with PT=6PT = 6PT=6 cm. PA is a tangent from P to the first circle and PB a tangent from P to the second (A and B not on ℓ\ellℓ). Prove that PA=PBPA = PBPA=PB, and find PB.

Given: ℓ\ellℓ touches both circles at T; PT=6PT = 6PT=6 cm; tangents PA and PB. To prove: PA=PBPA = PBPA=PB. Asked: PB.

Concept used: Theorem 10.2 for each circle separately. Why it applies: from P, PA and PT are the two tangents to the first circle; PB and PT are the two tangents to the second.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. PA=PTPA = PTPA=PT Tangents from P to the first circle
    2. PB=PTPB = PTPB=PT Tangents from P to the second circle
    3. PA=PBPA = PBPA=PB Both equal PT. Hence proved.
    4. PB=6PB = 6PB=6 cm PB=PTPB = PTPB=PT

  2. Check (with numbers): circles of radii 4 and 3 cm touching at T(0,0)T(0, 0)T(0,0), centres (−4,0)(-4, 0)(−4,0) and (3,0)(3, 0)(3,0), ℓ\ellℓ the y-axis, P(0,6)P(0, 6)P(0,6): tangent to the first circle 42+62−42=6\sqrt{4^2 + 6^2 - 4^2} = 642+62−42​=6, to the second 32+62−32=6\sqrt{3^2 + 6^2 - 3^2} = 632+62−32​=6 ✔.

  3. Answer PA=PBPA = PBPA=PB; PB=6PB = 6PB=6 cm

    Common trap: Trying to use the radii (which are not given) and Pythagoras. Here the common tangent PT links the two circles — Theorem 10.2 twice is all you need.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that pairing equal tangents wrongly is among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter, and that the right angle at a point of contact is often forgotten when the radius is used as a height.

  • AB+BC=CD+DAAB + BC = CD + DAAB+BC=CD+DA for a circumscribed quadrilateral

    The equal sums are for opposite sides.

    AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC. Label a, b, c, d first and check.

  • Tangent piece at A taken as s−ABs - ABs−AB

    AB has A as one end; the piece at A is sss minus the side opposite A.

    AF=s−BCAF = s - BCAF=s−BC, BD=s−CABD = s - CABD=s−CA, CE=s−ABCE = s - ABCE=s−AB.

  • Setting a tangent piece equal to a whole side, e.g. AF=ABAF = ABAF=AB

    A side is the sum of two pieces.

    Each side = piece at one end + piece at the other end.

  • Area =r×perimeter= r \times \text{perimeter}=r×perimeter

    Each small triangle is 12×base×r\dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times r21​×base×r.

    Area =12×r×perimeter= \dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times \text{perimeter}=21​×r×perimeter.

  • Using the radius as a height without saying why

    The radius is a height only because it is ⟂ to the side.

    Write "(radius ⟂ tangent at the point of contact)".

Key points

  • A polygon circumscribing a circle: label the two equal tangent pieces at each corner with one letter before anything else.
  • Quadrilateral: AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC. A circumscribing parallelogram is a rhombus; a circumscribing rectangle is a square.
  • Triangle: piece at a corner =s−= s -=s− the opposite side; area =12×r×= \dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times=21​×r× perimeter; right triangle: r=leg1+leg2−hypotenuse2r = \dfrac{\text{leg}_1 + \text{leg}_2 - \text{hypotenuse}}{2}r=2leg1​+leg2​−hypotenuse​.
  • A third tangent between AP and AQ cuts off a triangle of perimeter 2AP2AP2AP.
  • Two circles touching at T with common tangent through T: from any point P on it, the tangents to both circles equal PT.
