Wrap-up

The three tangent facts, the kite, the tangent pieces and mixed problems

About 30 minutes

Every question in this chapter uses the same few moves: join the radius to the point of contact (right angle), join the outside point to the centre (two congruent right triangles), and label the equal tangent pieces at every outside point. This page puts the three cards you need on one sheet and then gives four mixed problems in the styles of the sample papers — a case study with a 45° angle, a circle in a right triangle, a proof that uses both theorems, and a logo case with Heron's formula.

The three tangent facts

Fact Reason to write Typical use
Radius ⟂ tangent at the point of contact "tangent ⟂ radius at the point of contact" right triangle OTP; angle sums in OAPB; a radius as a height
Tangents from one outside point are equal "tangents from an external point are equal" PA = PB; isosceles △PAB; tangent pieces of polygons
OP bisects ∠APB and ∠AOB "△OAP ≅ △OBP (RHS), CPCT" halving an angle; trigonometry in △OAP

Mixed example 1

Case study: the bandstand

Question. A circular bandstand has centre O and radius 10 m. A light pole P stands outside it, and two straight paths PA and PB run from P and just touch the edge of the bandstand at A and B. P is 10210\sqrt{2}102​ m from O.
(i) What is ∠OAP\angle OAP∠OAP? (1 mark)
(ii) Find the length of the path PA. (1 mark)
(iii) Find ∠APB\angle APB∠APB and ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB, and name the shape OAPB. (2 marks)

Given: radius 10 m; OP=102OP = 10\sqrt{2}OP=102​ m. Asked: ∠OAP\angle OAP∠OAP; PA; ∠APB\angle APB∠APB, ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB and the shape.

Concept used: Theorem 10.1, Pythagoras, sin of 45°, Theorem 10.2. Why it applies: PA and PB are tangents from P.

  1. 1

    (i) ∠OAP=90°\angle OAP = 90°∠OAP=90° (tangent ⟂ radius at the point of contact).

  2. 2

    (ii) PA=OP2−OA2=200−100=100=10PA = \sqrt{OP^2 - OA^2} = \sqrt{200 - 100} = \sqrt{100} = 10PA=OP2−OA2​=200−100​=100​=10 m.

  3. 3

    (iii) In right △OAP: sin⁡∠OPA=OAOP=10102=12\sin \angle OPA = \dfrac{OA}{OP} = \dfrac{10}{10\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}sin∠OPA=OPOA​=102​10​=2​1​, so ∠OPA=45°\angle OPA = 45°∠OPA=45°.

  4. 4

    OP bisects ∠APB\angle APB∠APB, so ∠APB=2×45°=90°\angle APB = 2 \times 45° = 90°∠APB=2×45°=90°; then ∠AOB=180°−90°=90°\angle AOB = 180° - 90° = 90°∠AOB=180°−90°=90°.

  5. 5

    OAPB has four right angles and OA=OB=PA=PB=10OA = OB = PA = PB = 10OA=OB=PA=PB=10 m, so it is a square.

  6. Check: the diagonal of a square of side 10 m is 10210\sqrt{2}102​ m — exactly OP ✔.

  7. Answer (i) 90° (ii) 10 m (iii) ∠APB=∠AOB=90°\angle APB = \angle AOB = 90°∠APB=∠AOB=90°; OAPB is a square

    Common trap: In (iii), taking ∠OPA\angle OPA∠OPA as the whole angle between the paths. OP cuts ∠APB\angle APB∠APB in half.

Mixed example 2

A circle in a right triangle

Question. A circle with centre O touches the sides of △ABC, right-angled at B, with AB=8AB = 8AB=8 cm and BC=15BC = 15BC=15 cm. The circle touches AB at F, BC at D and CA at E. Find the radius and the lengths AF and CE.

Given: ∠B=90°\angle B = 90°∠B=90°; AB=8AB = 8AB=8 cm; BC=15BC = 15BC=15 cm. Asked: r, AF, CE.

Concept used: Pythagoras; at the right angle, OFBD is a square of side r; equal tangent pieces. Why it applies: the radii OF and OD are perpendicular to AB and BC.

  1. 1

    CA=82+152=289=17CA = \sqrt{8^2 + 15^2} = \sqrt{289} = 17CA=82+152​=289​=17 cm.

  2. 2

    ∠OFB=∠ODB=90°\angle OFB = \angle ODB = 90°∠OFB=∠ODB=90° (tangent ⟂ radius) and ∠B=90°\angle B = 90°∠B=90°, with OF=OD=rOF = OD = rOF=OD=r: OFBD is a square, so BF=BD=rBF = BD = rBF=BD=r.

  3. 3

    AF=AE=8−rAF = AE = 8 - rAF=AE=8−r and CD=CE=15−rCD = CE = 15 - rCD=CE=15−r (tangents from A and from C).

  4. 4

    CA=AE+ECCA = AE + ECCA=AE+EC: 17=(8−r)+(15−r)17 = (8 - r) + (15 - r)17=(8−r)+(15−r), so 2r=62r = 62r=6 and r=3r = 3r=3 cm.

  5. 5

    AF=8−3=5AF = 8 - 3 = 5AF=8−3=5 cm; CE=15−3=12CE = 15 - 3 = 12CE=15−3=12 cm.

  6. Check: area =12×8×15=60= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 15 = 60=21​×8×15=60 cm², and 12×r×perimeter=12×3×40=60\dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times \text{perimeter} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 40 = 6021​×r×perimeter=21​×3×40=60 cm² ✔.

  7. Answer r=3r = 3r=3 cm; AF=5AF = 5AF=5 cm; CE=12CE = 12CE=12 cm

    Common trap: Taking r as half of a leg. The radius comes from the tangent pieces (or from the area), not from halving a side.

Mixed example 3

A proof that uses both theorems

Question. PA and PB are tangents from P to a circle with centre O. A third tangent touches the circle at C, on the minor arc AB, and meets PA at D and PB at E. Prove that ∠DOE=12∠AOB\angle DOE = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle AOB∠DOE=21​∠AOB. Find ∠DOE\angle DOE∠DOE when ∠APB=40°\angle APB = 40°∠APB=40°.

Given: tangents PA, PB; tangent DE touching at C. To prove: ∠DOE=12∠AOB\angle DOE = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle AOB∠DOE=21​∠AOB.

Concept used: from D, the tangents DA and DC; from E, the tangents EC and EB; in each case OD (or OE) bisects the angle between the radii (RHS, CPCT). Why it applies: D and E are external points.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. △ODA≅△ODC\triangle ODA \cong \triangle ODC△ODA≅△ODC, so ∠AOD=∠DOC\angle AOD = \angle DOC∠AOD=∠DOC ∠OAD=∠OCD=90°\angle OAD = \angle OCD = 90°∠OAD=∠OCD=90° (tangent ⟂ radius); OA=OCOA = OCOA=OC (radii); OD common; RHS; CPCT
    2. △OEC≅△OEB\triangle OEC \cong \triangle OEB△OEC≅△OEB, so ∠COE=∠EOB\angle COE = \angle EOB∠COE=∠EOB The same reasons at E
    3. ∠AOB=∠AOD+∠DOC+∠COE+∠EOB=2∠DOC+2∠COE\angle AOB = \angle AOD + \angle DOC + \angle COE + \angle EOB = 2\angle DOC + 2\angle COE∠AOB=∠AOD+∠DOC+∠COE+∠EOB=2∠DOC+2∠COE Steps 1 and 2 (C lies on the arc AB)
    4. ∠DOE=∠DOC+∠COE=12∠AOB\angle DOE = \angle DOC + \angle COE = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle AOB∠DOE=∠DOC+∠COE=21​∠AOB Divide step 3 by 2. Hence proved.

    Now ∠APB=40°\angle APB = 40°∠APB=40°: ∠AOB=180°−40°=140°\angle AOB = 180° - 40° = 140°∠AOB=180°−40°=140° (kite OAPB), so ∠DOE=140°2=70°\angle DOE = \dfrac{140°}{2} = 70°∠DOE=2140°​=70°.

  2. Check: with C at the middle of the arc, the figure is symmetric about OP and ∠DOE=70°\angle DOE = 70°∠DOE=70°; and with C anywhere else on the arc, steps 1–4 hold just the same, so the answer does not depend on C ✔ (measure it on your own drawing with a protractor).

  3. Answer proved; ∠DOE=70°\angle DOE = 70°∠DOE=70°

    Common trap: Writing ∠DOE=∠APB\angle DOE = \angle APB∠DOE=∠APB or 180°−∠APB180° - \angle APB180°−∠APB. The angle at O between OD and OE is half of ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB, so it is 90°−12∠APB90° - \dfrac{1}{2}\angle APB90°−21​∠APB.

Mixed example 4

Case study: the club logo

Question. A club logo is a triangle ABC with AB=13AB = 13AB=13 cm, BC=20BC = 20BC=20 cm and CA=11CA = 11CA=11 cm, with a circle inside it touching all three sides — BC at D, CA at E, AB at F.
(i) Find the semi-perimeter s. (1 mark)
(ii) Find BD and CD. (1 mark)
(iii) Find the area of the triangle by Heron's formula, and hence the radius of the circle. (2 marks)

Given: sides 13, 20, 11 cm. Asked: s; BD and CD; the area and r.

Concept used: tangent pieces s−s -s− opposite side; Heron's formula; area =12×r×= \dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times=21​×r× perimeter. Why it applies: the sides are tangents to the circle.

  1. 1

    (i) s=13+20+112=22s = \dfrac{13 + 20 + 11}{2} = 22s=213+20+11​=22 cm.

  2. 2

    (ii) BD=s−CA=22−11=11BD = s - CA = 22 - 11 = 11BD=s−CA=22−11=11 cm; CD=s−AB=22−13=9CD = s - AB = 22 - 13 = 9CD=s−AB=22−13=9 cm.

  3. 3

    (iii) Area =22×(22−13)×(22−20)×(22−11)=22×9×2×11=4356=66= \sqrt{22 \times (22 - 13) \times (22 - 20) \times (22 - 11)} = \sqrt{22 \times 9 \times 2 \times 11} = \sqrt{4356} = 66=22×(22−13)×(22−20)×(22−11)​=22×9×2×11​=4356​=66 cm².

  4. 4

    12×r×44=66\dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times 44 = 6621​×r×44=66, so 22r=6622r = 6622r=66 and r=3r = 3r=3 cm.

  5. Check: BD+CD=11+9=20=BCBD + CD = 11 + 9 = 20 = BCBD+CD=11+9=20=BC ✔; and the piece at A is 22−20=222 - 20 = 222−20=2, so AB=2+11=13AB = 2 + 11 = 13AB=2+11=13 ✔.

  6. Answer (i) 22 cm (ii) BD=11BD = 11BD=11 cm, CD=9CD = 9CD=9 cm (iii) 66 cm²; r=3r = 3r=3 cm

    Common trap: In (ii), BD=s−BC=2BD = s - BC = 2BD=s−BC=2 cm. The piece at B is sss minus the side opposite B, which is CA.
