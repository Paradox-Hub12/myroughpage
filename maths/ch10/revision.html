The whole chapter on one page

Words — a line and a circle: non-intersecting (0 common points), secant (2), tangent (exactly 1, the point of contact). A tangent is the limiting position of a secant whose two common points come together. The length of the tangent from an outside point P is PT, where T is the point of contact.

Counting — tangents through a point inside the circle: 0; on it: exactly 1; outside: exactly 2. A circle has infinitely many tangents (one at each point), but at most two are parallel to a given line — they touch at the ends of a diameter.

Theorem 10.1 (prove it) — the tangent at a point is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact. Proof: every other point Q of the tangent is outside the circle, so OQ>OPOQ > OPOQ>OP; OP is the shortest segment from O to the tangent, so it is perpendicular. Consequences: one tangent at each point; the perpendicular to a tangent at its point of contact passes through the centre; tangents at the ends of a diameter are parallel; in △OTP the right angle is at T and OP is the hypotenuse.

Theorem 10.2 (prove it) — the tangents from an external point are equal. Proof: join OP, OQ, OR; right angles at Q and R (Theorem 10.1), OQ=OROQ = OROQ=OR (radii), OP common → RHS → PQ=PRPQ = PRPQ=PR (CPCT). Also OP bisects ∠QPR\angle QPR∠QPR and ∠QOR\angle QOR∠QOR.

The kite OAPB — ∠APB+∠AOB=180°\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180°∠APB+∠AOB=180°; ∠OPA=12∠APB\angle OPA = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle APB∠OPA=21​∠APB; ∠OAB=12∠APB\angle OAB = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle APB∠OAB=21​∠APB; area =r×PA= r \times PA=r×PA; OP is the perpendicular bisector of AB.

Polygons round a circle — label the two equal tangent pieces at each corner with one letter. Quadrilateral: AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC (a circumscribing parallelogram is a rhombus; opposite sides subtend supplementary angles at the centre). Triangle: piece at a corner =s−= s -=s− opposite side; area =12×r×= \dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times=21​×r× perimeter. A third tangent between AP and AQ cuts off a triangle of perimeter 2AP2AP2AP.

Concentric circles — the chord of the larger circle that touches the smaller one is bisected at the point of contact: half2=R2−r2\text{half}^2 = R^2 - r^2half2=R2−r2, then double.
