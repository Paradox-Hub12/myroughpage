This section currently holds the Circles items from the CBSE Mathematics Sample Question Papers 2026-27 and 2025-26 — Mathematics Standard (041) and Mathematics Basic (241) — answered with their official marking schemes (newest first). Past year board questions, and the "Exam focus" analysis, will be added once the papers are sourced.

Both 2026-27 sample papers state that there is no change in the question paper design and assessment pattern for 2026-27, so the 2025-26 items remain good practice. Standard and Basic share the same syllabus, so every student should try all twenty-four items. This chapter is part of Unit IV, Geometry (15 marks, shared with Triangles). It carries 7 marks in the 2026-27 Standard paper (a 3-mark proof and a 4-mark case study), 7 marks in the 2026-27 Basic paper (two MCQs, a 2-mark and a 3-mark question), 7 marks in the 2025-26 Standard paper (two MCQs, a 2-mark and a 3-mark question) and 6 marks in the 2025-26 Basic paper (three MCQs and a 3-mark question) — plus a 2-mark question on a tangent to a circle given by coordinates (Q22), which also appears on the Coordinate Geometry page. Seven items are alternatives for visually impaired candidates; each is given in full. The proof that a parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus appears in three of the four papers (as a main question in the 2026-27 Standard paper, and as the version for visually impaired candidates in both Basic papers).

Each item has two parts:

  • Understand it — the full step-by-step explanation: Given / Asked → Concept used (and why) → steps → Check → Common trap.
  • Write it in the exam — the answer as you should write it, following the official marking scheme, with marks shown. Where the scheme gives only a total for a group of lines, the split shown is inferred and marked so.

CBSE SQP 2026-27, Mathematics Standard, Section C, Q293 marks

Question: Prove that the parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.

(Note: this is NCERT Exercise 10.2 Q11 — see the NCERT solutions for the full Statement | Reason table.)

Given: a parallelogram ABCD whose sides touch a circle — with the scheme's labels, AB at S, BC at P, CD at Q and DA at R. To prove: ABCD is a rhombus.

Concept used: tangents from an external point are equal, at each corner; opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal (Topic 5). Why it applies: each corner is an external point with two tangents.

  1. 1

    AS=ARAS = ARAS=AR, BS=BPBS = BPBS=BP, CQ=CPCQ = CPCQ=CP, DQ=DRDQ = DRDQ=DR (tangents from A, B, C and D).

  2. 2

    Adding: AS+BS+CQ+DQ=AR+BP+CP+DRAS + BS + CQ + DQ = AR + BP + CP + DRAS+BS+CQ+DQ=AR+BP+CP+DR, i.e. AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC.

  3. 3

    In a parallelogram CD=ABCD = ABCD=AB and BC=ADBC = ADBC=AD, so 2AB=2AD2AB = 2AD2AB=2AD and AB=ADAB = ADAB=AD.

  4. 4

    A parallelogram with two adjacent sides equal is a rhombus.

  5. Check: a rhombus does have a circle touching all four sides (its diagonals bisect its angles and meet at a point equally far from all sides) ✔.

    Common trap: Ending at "AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC". The last mark needs "2AB=2AD2AB = 2AD2AB=2AD, so adjacent sides are equal — a rhombus".
Figure: parallelogram ABCD touching the circle at S (on AB), P (on BC), Q (on CD), R (on DA).
AS=ARAS = ARAS=AR, BS=BPBS = BPBS=BP, CQ=CPCQ = CPCQ=CP, DQ=DRDQ = DRDQ=DR (lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal)
1 mark
Adding: AS+BS+CQ+DQ=AR+BP+CP+DR⇒AB+CD=AD+BCAS + BS + CQ + DQ = AR + BP + CP + DR \Rightarrow AB + CD = AD + BCAS+BS+CQ+DQ=AR+BP+CP+DR⇒AB+CD=AD+BC
1 mark
⇒2AB=2AD⇒AB=AD\Rightarrow 2AB = 2AD \Rightarrow AB = AD⇒2AB=2AD⇒AB=AD; adjacent sides of the parallelogram ABCD are equal, so ABCD is a rhombus
1 mark

(Note: this is NCERT Exercise 10.2 Q11 and 2026-27 Standard Q29 above. This scheme labels the points of contact P on AB, Q on BC, R on CD and S on DA.)
