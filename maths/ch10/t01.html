Toolkit: circle words, triangle facts and congruence

About 30 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Use the circle words (centre, radius, chord, diameter, arc) and the facts that all radii of a circle are equal and that the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
  • Find unknown sides and angles with Pythagoras' theorem and the angle sums of a triangle (180°) and a quadrilateral (360°), including isosceles triangles.
  • Prove two triangles congruent (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS) in a Statement | Reason table and use CPCT.
Why this lesson exists

This chapter has only two new facts about tangents. Every question then finishes with something you met in Class 9: two equal radii, a right triangle and Pythagoras, an angle sum, or a pair of congruent triangles. When an answer goes wrong, the slip is very often in one of these. If you already have these skills, prove it below and go straight to Topic 2.

Work through this toolkit — it takes about 30 minutes. Then try the questions again.

Put a coin flat on the table and push a ruler against its edge. The ruler touches the coin at one point only. Now imagine a line from the centre of the coin to that point: it meets the ruler at a perfect right angle. That one picture — a radius meeting a straight edge at 90° — is the whole of Chapter 10. The right angle turns every tangent figure into a right triangle, and everything you already know about triangles takes over. Half an hour here makes sure that "everything you already know" is ready.

  1. Circle words

A circle is the set of all points in a plane at the same distance from a fixed point, the centre (usually O). That distance is the radius.

  • A chord joins two points of the circle. A chord through the centre is a diameter: diameter =2×= 2 \times=2× radius.
  • An arc is a piece of the circle between two of its points.
  • Circles with the same centre and different radii are concentric.

  1. Two Class 9 facts you will use again and again

Fact A — all radii are equal. If A and B are on a circle with centre O, then OA=OBOA = OBOA=OB. So triangle OAB is isosceles, and the angles opposite the equal sides are equal: ∠OAB=∠OBA\angle OAB = \angle OBA∠OAB=∠OBA.

Fact B — the perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord. If OM⊥ABOM \perp ABOM⊥AB with M on the chord AB, then AM=MBAM = MBAM=MB. With the radius as the hypotenuse:

half-chord2+distance2=radius2\text{half-chord}^2 + \text{distance}^2 = \text{radius}^2half-chord2+distance2=radius2Swipe to see the whole equation →

Here "distance" means the distance of the chord from O.

  1. Pythagoras' theorem

In a right triangle, the side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse — the longest side.

hypotenuse2=one side2+other side2\text{hypotenuse}^2 = \text{one side}^2 + \text{other side}^2hypotenuse2=one side2+other side2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  • To find the hypotenuse: add the squares, then take the square root.
  • To find a shorter side: subtract the other side's square from the hypotenuse's square.
  • Before you square anything, ask: which side is opposite the right angle? That one, and only that one, stands alone on the left.

Useful whole-number triples: 3, 4, 5 · 5, 12, 13 · 8, 15, 17 · 7, 24, 25 · 20, 21, 29 · 9, 40, 41 (and their multiples).

  1. Angle sums

  • Angles of a triangle add up to 180°.
  • Angles of a quadrilateral add up to 360° (a diagonal cuts it into two triangles).
  • Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
  • In an isosceles triangle with apex angle xxx, each base angle is 180°−x2\dfrac{180° - x}{2}2180°−x​.

A quadrilateral with two right angles opposite each other has its other two angles adding up to 360°−90°−90°=180°360° - 90° - 90° = 180°360°−90°−90°=180°. You will meet this shape in almost every tangent question.

  1. Congruent triangles and proof tables

Two triangles are congruent (≅\cong≅) when one fits exactly on the other. The Class 9 rules:

Rule What must be equal
SSS all three pairs of sides
SAS two pairs of sides and the angle between them
ASA two pairs of angles and the side between them
RHS both triangles right-angled; the hypotenuses equal; one other pair of sides equal

Once two triangles are congruent, every pair of matching parts is equal — written CPCT (corresponding parts of congruent triangles).

How a geometry proof is written. Start with Given, To prove and (if you join or draw anything) Construction. Then write the proof in a two-column table: each Statement on the left has its Reason on the right — "radii of the same circle", "common", "given", "RHS", "CPCT". A line without a reason usually earns no mark.

Worked example 1

Two radii make an isosceles triangle

Question. P and Q are points on a circle with centre O, and ∠POQ=124°\angle POQ = 124°∠POQ=124°. Find ∠OPQ\angle OPQ∠OPQ.

Given: ∠POQ=124°\angle POQ = 124°∠POQ=124°; OP and OQ are radii. Asked: ∠OPQ\angle OPQ∠OPQ.

Concept used: equal radii → isosceles triangle → equal base angles; angle sum 180°. Why it applies: both OP and OQ join the centre to the circle.

  1. 1

    OP=OQOP = OQOP=OQ (radii of the same circle), so in △OPQ, ∠OPQ=∠OQP\angle OPQ = \angle OQP∠OPQ=∠OQP.

  2. 2

    Angle sum: ∠OPQ+∠OQP=180°−124°=56°\angle OPQ + \angle OQP = 180° - 124° = 56°∠OPQ+∠OQP=180°−124°=56°.

  3. 3

    The two angles are equal, so ∠OPQ=56°2=28°\angle OPQ = \dfrac{56°}{2} = 28°∠OPQ=256°​=28°.

  4. Check: 28°+28°+124°=180°28° + 28° + 124° = 180°28°+28°+124°=180° ✔.

  5. Answer ∠OPQ=28°\angle OPQ = 28°∠OPQ=28°

    Common trap: Halving 124° instead of halving what is left: 124°2=62°\dfrac{124°}{2} = 62°2124°​=62° gives 62°+62°+124°=248°62° + 62° + 124° = 248°62°+62°+124°=248°, which is too much for a triangle.

Worked example 2

The distance of a chord from the centre

Question. A chord AB of a circle with centre O and radius 30 cm is 48 cm long. Find the distance of the chord from O.

Given: radius 30 cm; chord 48 cm. Asked: the distance OM, where OM⊥ABOM \perp ABOM⊥AB.

Concept used: the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord; Pythagoras with the radius as hypotenuse. Why it applies: OM, MB and the radius OB make a right triangle.

  1. 1

    Draw OM⊥ABOM \perp ABOM⊥AB. It bisects the chord: MB=482=24MB = \dfrac{48}{2} = 24MB=248​=24 cm.

  2. 2

    In right triangle OMB (right angle at M), the hypotenuse is the radius OB =30= 30=30 cm.

    OM2=OB2−MB2=302−242OM^2 = OB^2 - MB^2 = 30^2 - 24^2OM2=OB2−MB2=302−242Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    OM2=900−576=324OM^2 = 900 - 576 = 324OM2=900−576=324, so OM=18OM = 18OM=18 cm.

  4. Check: 182+242=324+576=900=30218^2 + 24^2 = 324 + 576 = 900 = 30^2182+242=324+576=900=302 ✔ (the 3, 4, 5 triple, six times over).

  5. Answer The chord is 18 cm from the centre.

    Common trap: Using the whole chord: 482−302\sqrt{48^2 - 30^2}482−302​ is not a side of any right triangle in the figure. Only half the chord is a side of triangle OMB.

Worked example 3

A quadrilateral with two right angles

Question. In a quadrilateral OAPB, ∠OAP=∠OBP=90°\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90°∠OAP=∠OBP=90° and ∠APB=58°\angle APB = 58°∠APB=58°. Find ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB.

Given: two right angles at A and B; ∠APB=58°\angle APB = 58°∠APB=58°. Asked: ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB.

Concept used: the angles of a quadrilateral add up to 360°. Why it applies: three of the four angles are known.

  1. 1

    ∠AOB=360°−∠OAP−∠OBP−∠APB\angle AOB = 360° - \angle OAP - \angle OBP - \angle APB∠AOB=360°−∠OAP−∠OBP−∠APB.

  2. 2

    ∠AOB=360°−90°−90°−58°=122°\angle AOB = 360° - 90° - 90° - 58° = 122°∠AOB=360°−90°−90°−58°=122°.

  3. Check: with two right angles, the other two angles add up to 180°: 122°+58°=180°122° + 58° = 180°122°+58°=180° ✔.

Worked example 4

A congruence proof in a table

Question. In the figure, ∠PQR=∠PSR=90°\angle PQR = \angle PSR = 90°∠PQR=∠PSR=90° and PQ=PSPQ = PSPQ=PS. The side PR is common to △PQR and △PSR. Prove that QR=SRQR = SRQR=SR.

Given: ∠PQR=∠PSR=90°\angle PQR = \angle PSR = 90°∠PQR=∠PSR=90°; PQ=PSPQ = PSPQ=PS. To prove: QR=SRQR = SRQR=SR.

Concept used: RHS congruence, then CPCT. Why it applies: both triangles are right-angled, PR is the hypotenuse of each, and one other pair of sides is equal.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. ∠PQR=∠PSR=90°\angle PQR = \angle PSR = 90°∠PQR=∠PSR=90° Given
    2. PR=PRPR = PRPR=PR Common (the hypotenuse of both triangles)
    3. PQ=PSPQ = PSPQ=PS Given
    4. △PQR≅△PSR\triangle PQR \cong \triangle PSR△PQR≅△PSR RHS (steps 1, 2, 3)
    5. QR=SRQR = SRQR=SR CPCT. Hence proved.

  2. Check (with numbers): take PR=17PR = 17PR=17 and PQ=PS=8PQ = PS = 8PQ=PS=8. Then QR=172−82=15QR = \sqrt{17^2 - 8^2} = 15QR=172−82​=15 and SR=172−82=15SR = \sqrt{17^2 - 8^2} = 15SR=172−82​=15 — equal ✔.

  3. Answer QR=SRQR = SRQR=SR, by RHS and CPCT.

    Common trap: Writing "SAS" here. The given right angle is not between PQ and PR, so SAS does not apply. With a right angle, the hypotenuse and one side, the rule is RHS.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that Pythagoras with the wrong side as hypotenuse and proof lines without the right reason (for example SAS where the rule is RHS) are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • In a right triangle with hypotenuse 41 and a side 9: "third side =412+92= \sqrt{41^2 + 9^2}=412+92​"

    Only the hypotenuse stands alone; the shorter sides are found by subtracting.

    Mark the right angle; the side opposite it is the hypotenuse.

  • Half-chord found, but given as the chord

    Pythagoras in triangle OMB gives half the chord.

    Double the half-chord at the end.

  • Two right triangles with equal hypotenuses and one equal side: "congruent by SAS"

    The right angle is not between the two sides.

    Write RHS and name the right angle, the hypotenuse and the side.

  • A proof line such as "OA = OB" with no reason

    Each line of a proof needs its reason to earn its mark.

    "OA = OB (radii of the same circle)".

  • Angle sum of a quadrilateral taken as 180°

    A quadrilateral is two triangles.

    360° for a quadrilateral, 180° for a triangle.

Key points

  • Radii of one circle are equal, so a triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles.
  • The perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord: half-chord2+distance2=radius2\text{half-chord}^2 + \text{distance}^2 = \text{radius}^2half-chord2+distance2=radius2.
  • In Pythagoras, the side opposite the right angle (the longest) is the hypotenuse — add to find it, subtract to find a shorter side.
  • Triangle 180°; quadrilateral 360°; two opposite right angles leave 180° for the other two angles.
  • Right angle + hypotenuse + one side → RHS; then CPCT. Every proof line needs a reason.
