Exercise 10.1, Q1How many tangents?1 mark
  1. How many tangents can a circle have?

Given: a circle. Asked: the number of tangents it can have.

Concept used: at each point of a circle there is exactly one tangent (Topics 2 and 3). Why it applies: a circle has infinitely many points.

  1. 1

    Take any point of the circle: exactly one tangent touches the circle there.

  2. 2

    Different points give different tangents (each tangent has only one point of contact).

  3. 3

    A circle has infinitely many points, so it has infinitely many tangents.

  4. Check: a bicycle wheel rolling along a road touches the road at a new point at every moment — each position of the road relative to the wheel is a different tangent ✔.

  5. Answer Infinitely many.

    Common trap: Answering "two". Two is the number of tangents from a point outside the circle (or the most that can be parallel to one line) — not the number of tangents the circle has.
Exercise 10.1, Q2Fill in the blanks2 marks
  1. Fill in the blanks :

(i) A tangent to a circle intersects it in ______ point (s).
(ii) A line intersecting a circle in two points is called a ______.
(iii) A circle can have ______ parallel tangents at the most.
(iv) The common point of a tangent to a circle and the circle is called ______.

Given: four statements. Asked: the missing words.

Concept used: the definitions and counting facts of Topic 2. Why it applies: each blank is a definition.

  1. 1

    (i) A tangent meets the circle in exactly one point.

  2. 2

    (ii) A line meeting a circle in two points is a secant.

  3. 3

    (iii) Lines parallel to one direction can touch the circle only once on each side, so at most two parallel tangents.

  4. 4

    (iv) The common point is the point of contact.

  5. Check: (iii) the two parallel tangents touch at the two ends of a diameter (Topic 3, Worked example 6) ✔.

  6. Answer (i) one (ii) secant (iii) two (iv) point of contact

    Common trap: In (iii), writing "infinitely many". A circle has infinitely many tangents, but only two of them can be parallel to each other.

Exercise 10.1, Q4A tangent and a secant parallel to a line2 marks
  1. Draw a circle and two lines parallel to a given line such that one is a tangent and the other, a secant to the circle.

Given: a line ℓ\ellℓ. Asked: a drawing of a circle, a tangent parallel to ℓ\ellℓ and a secant parallel to ℓ\ellℓ.

Concept used: a line parallel to ℓ\ellℓ is a tangent when its distance from the centre equals the radius, and a secant when its distance is less (Topic 2). Why it applies: the tangent parallel to ℓ\ellℓ touches at an end of the diameter perpendicular to ℓ\ellℓ.

  1. 1

    Draw the line ℓ\ellℓ and a circle with centre O, say of radius 3 cm, not touching ℓ\ellℓ.

  2. 2

    Through O draw a line perpendicular to ℓ\ellℓ; it meets the circle at A and B (a diameter).

  3. 3

    At A draw the line perpendicular to AB (use a set square). It is parallel to ℓ\ellℓ (both are perpendicular to AB) and meets the circle only at A — this is the tangent.

  4. 4

    Mark a point M on OA, between O and A (for example 1.5 cm from O). Through M draw the line perpendicular to AB. It is parallel to ℓ\ellℓ and is only 1.5 cm from O, so it cuts the circle at two points C and D — this is the secant.

  5. 5

    Label the tangent, the secant and the point of contact A.

  6. Check: the tangent is 3 cm from O (equal to the radius) and the secant is 1.5 cm from O (less than the radius) ✔; both make right angles with AB, so both are parallel to ℓ\ellℓ ✔.

  7. Answer The drawing: circle (centre O), diameter AB ⟂ ℓ\ellℓ, the tangent at A ⟂ AB, and a line through M on OA ⟂ AB cutting the circle at C and D.

    Common trap: Drawing the "tangent" by eye. It must be drawn at the end of the diameter perpendicular to the given line, at right angles to that diameter — otherwise it may cut the circle twice or miss it.

Exercise 10.2, Q4Tangents at the ends of a diameter3 marks
  1. Prove that the tangents drawn at the ends of a diameter of a circle are parallel.

Given: a circle with centre O; a diameter AB; tangents ℓ\ellℓ at A and mmm at B. To prove: ℓ∥m\ell \parallel mℓ∥m.

Concept used: Theorem 10.1 at A and at B; two lines making equal alternate angles with a transversal are parallel (Topic 3). Why it applies: AB is a radius-line at both points of contact.

  1. (Figure: a circle with centre O; a vertical diameter AB, A at the top; the tangent ℓ\ellℓ through A and the tangent mmm through B, both horizontal; P on ℓ\ellℓ to the right of A and Q on mmm to the left of B.)

    Statement Reason
    1. OA⊥ℓOA \perp \ellOA⊥ℓ, so ∠PAB=90°\angle PAB = 90°∠PAB=90° Tangent ⟂ radius at the point of contact (Theorem 10.1); A, O, B lie on the diameter
    2. OB⊥mOB \perp mOB⊥m, so ∠QBA=90°\angle QBA = 90°∠QBA=90° Tangent ⟂ radius at the point of contact
    3. ∠PAB=∠QBA\angle PAB = \angle QBA∠PAB=∠QBA Both are 90°
    4. ℓ∥m\ell \parallel mℓ∥m ∠PAB\angle PAB∠PAB and ∠QBA\angle QBA∠QBA are alternate angles made by the transversal AB, and they are equal. Hence proved.

  2. Check (with numbers): circle x2+y2=9x^2 + y^2 = 9x2+y2=9, diameter from A(0,3)A(0, 3)A(0,3) to B(0,−3)B(0, -3)B(0,−3): the tangents are y=3y = 3y=3 and y=−3y = -3y=−3, which are parallel ✔.

  3. Answer The two tangents are parallel — proved.

    Common trap: Saying "both are tangents, so they are parallel". Most pairs of tangents meet; only tangents at the ends of a diameter are parallel, and the reason is the two right angles.
Exercise 10.2, Q5The perpendicular at the point of contact2 marks
  1. Prove that the perpendicular at the point of contact to the tangent to a circle passes through the centre.

Given: a circle with centre O; a tangent XY touching it at P; the line PZ drawn perpendicular to XY at P. To prove: PZ passes through O.

Concept used: Theorem 10.1; at a point of a line there is only one perpendicular to it (Topic 3). Why it applies: OP is also perpendicular to XY at P.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. OP⊥XYOP \perp XYOP⊥XY Tangent ⟂ radius at the point of contact (Theorem 10.1)
    2. PZ⊥XYPZ \perp XYPZ⊥XY Given
    3. PZ and PO are the same line At the point P of XY there is only one line perpendicular to XY (steps 1 and 2)
    4. PZ passes through O O lies on PO. Hence proved.

  2. Check (with numbers): circle x2+y2=16x^2 + y^2 = 16x2+y2=16 with the tangent y=−4y = -4y=−4 at P(0,−4)P(0, -4)P(0,−4): the perpendicular to y=−4y = -4y=−4 at P is the line x=0x = 0x=0, which passes through O(0,0)O(0, 0)O(0,0) ✔.

  3. Answer The perpendicular at the point of contact passes through the centre — proved.

    Common trap: Assuming what is to be proved ("the perpendicular passes through O, so it is the radius"). Start from Theorem 10.1 and the uniqueness of the perpendicular at P.

  1. Two concentric circles are of radii 5 cm and 3 cm. Find the length of the chord of the larger circle which touches the smaller circle.

  1. A quadrilateral ABCD is drawn to circumscribe a circle (see Fig. 10.12). Prove that AB + CD = AD + BC

  1. Prove that the parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.

Given: a parallelogram ABCD whose sides touch a circle at P (on AB), Q (on BC), R (on CD) and S (on DA). To prove: ABCD is a rhombus.

Concept used: Theorem 10.2 at the four corners, giving AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC; opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal (Topic 5). Why it applies: every corner is an external point.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. AP=ASAP = ASAP=AS, BP=BQBP = BQBP=BQ, CR=CQCR = CQCR=CQ, DR=DSDR = DSDR=DS Tangents from an external point are equal
    2. (AP+BP)+(CR+DR)=(AS+DS)+(BQ+CQ)(AP + BP) + (CR + DR) = (AS + DS) + (BQ + CQ)(AP+BP)+(CR+DR)=(AS+DS)+(BQ+CQ) Adding the four equalities and regrouping
    3. AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC Each bracket is a side
    4. CD=ABCD = ABCD=AB and BC=ADBC = ADBC=AD Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal
    5. 2AB=2AD2AB = 2AD2AB=2AD, so AB=ADAB = ADAB=AD Step 4 in step 3
    6. ABCD is a rhombus A parallelogram with two adjacent sides equal has all its sides equal. Hence proved.

  2. Check (with numbers): a rhombus of side 10 with an angle of 60° has height 10sin⁡60°=5310 \sin 60° = 5\sqrt{3}10sin60°=53​; the circle of radius 532\dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}253​​ at its centre touches all four sides ✔ — whereas a 6 by 10 parallelogram with its sides unequal cannot have a circle touching all four.

  3. Answer ABCD is a rhombus — proved.

    Common trap: Stopping at "AB+CD=AD+BCAB + CD = AD + BCAB+CD=AD+BC". The mark for the conclusion needs the parallelogram step: opposite sides equal, so AB=ADAB = ADAB=AD — adjacent sides equal.

Exercise 10.2, Q13Opposite sides subtend supplementary angles3 marks
  1. Prove that opposite sides of a quadrilateral circumscribing a circle subtend supplementary angles at the centre of the circle.

Given: a quadrilateral ABCD circumscribing a circle with centre O, touching AB at P, BC at Q, CD at R and DA at S. To prove: ∠AOB+∠COD=180°\angle AOB + \angle COD = 180°∠AOB+∠COD=180° (and ∠AOD+∠BOC=180°\angle AOD + \angle BOC = 180°∠AOD+∠BOC=180°). Construction: join OA, OB, OC, OD, OP, OQ, OR, OS.

Concept used: at each corner, the line to the centre bisects the angle between the radii (RHS, CPCT — Topic 4); the angles round O add up to 360°. Why it applies: each corner is an external point.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. △OAP≅△OAS\triangle OAP \cong \triangle OAS△OAP≅△OAS, so ∠AOP=∠AOS\angle AOP = \angle AOS∠AOP=∠AOS; call each ∠1\angle 1∠1 ∠OPA=∠OSA=90°\angle OPA = \angle OSA = 90°∠OPA=∠OSA=90° (tangent ⟂ radius), OP=OSOP = OSOP=OS (radii), OA common: RHS; CPCT
    2. In the same way ∠BOP=∠BOQ=∠2\angle BOP = \angle BOQ = \angle 2∠BOP=∠BOQ=∠2, ∠COQ=∠COR=∠3\angle COQ = \angle COR = \angle 3∠COQ=∠COR=∠3, ∠DOR=∠DOS=∠4\angle DOR = \angle DOS = \angle 4∠DOR=∠DOS=∠4 RHS and CPCT at B, C and D
    3. 2(∠1+∠2+∠3+∠4)=360°2(\angle 1 + \angle 2 + \angle 3 + \angle 4) = 360°2(∠1+∠2+∠3+∠4)=360° The eight angles round O make a complete turn
    4. (∠1+∠2)+(∠3+∠4)=180°(\angle 1 + \angle 2) + (\angle 3 + \angle 4) = 180°(∠1+∠2)+(∠3+∠4)=180° Divide by 2
    5. ∠AOB+∠COD=180°\angle AOB + \angle COD = 180°∠AOB+∠COD=180° ∠AOB=∠1+∠2\angle AOB = \angle 1 + \angle 2∠AOB=∠1+∠2 and ∠COD=∠3+∠4\angle COD = \angle 3 + \angle 4∠COD=∠3+∠4. Hence proved.
    6. Also ∠AOD+∠BOC=180°\angle AOD + \angle BOC = 180°∠AOD+∠BOC=180° ∠AOD=∠1+∠4\angle AOD = \angle 1 + \angle 4∠AOD=∠1+∠4, ∠BOC=∠2+∠3\angle BOC = \angle 2 + \angle 3∠BOC=∠2+∠3; add, and use step 3

  2. Check (with numbers): a square of side 10 round a circle of radius 5 — each side subtends 90° at the centre, and 90°+90°=180°90° + 90° = 180°90°+90°=180° for both pairs ✔.   3. Answer ∠AOB+∠COD=180°\angle AOB + \angle COD = 180°∠AOB+∠COD=180° and ∠AOD+∠BOC=180°\angle AOD + \angle BOC = 180°∠AOD+∠BOC=180° — proved.
