Which ratio, which method?

About 35 minutes

In the exam, questions from this chapter come in a handful of familiar shapes. This page gives you a card for recognising each shape in a few seconds, the value table on one sheet, and five mixed problems that make you choose.

The "Which ratio, which method?" card

Mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

One ratio, two routes

Question. If 2tan⁡A=12\tan A = 12tanA=1, find the value of sin⁡2A+2cos⁡2A\sin^2 A + 2\cos^2 Asin2A+2cos2A.

Given: tan⁡A=12\tan A = \dfrac{1}{2}tanA=21​. Asked: the value of the expression.

Concept used: either the triangle method (Topic 3) or the identities (Topic 5). Why it applies: one ratio fixes every ratio.

  1. 1

    Route 1 — triangle

    Opposite =k= k=k, adjacent =2k= 2k=2k. Hypotenuse by Pythagoras (add the squares).

    hyp2=k2+4k2=5k2\text{hyp}^2 = k^2 + 4k^2 = 5k^2hyp2=k2+4k2=5k2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Take the square root.

    hyp=5 k\text{hyp} = \sqrt{5}\,khyp=5​kSwipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Read off sine and cosine.

    sin⁡A=15,cos⁡A=25\sin A = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}, \qquad \cos A = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}sinA=5​1​,cosA=5​2​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Square each (top and bottom).

    sin⁡2A=15,cos⁡2A=45\sin^2 A = \dfrac{1}{5}, \qquad \cos^2 A = \dfrac{4}{5}sin2A=51​,cos2A=54​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Substitute.

    15+2×45=15+85\dfrac{1}{5} + 2 \times \dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{8}{5}51​+2×54​=51​+58​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Add.

    =95= \dfrac{9}{5}=59​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Route 2 — identities

    Split 2cos⁡2A2\cos^2 A2cos2A as cos⁡2A+cos⁡2A\cos^2 A + \cos^2 Acos2A+cos2A and use sin⁡2A+cos⁡2A=1\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1sin2A+cos2A=1.

    sin⁡2A+2cos⁡2A=1+cos⁡2A\sin^2 A + 2\cos^2 A = 1 + \cos^2 Asin2A+2cos2A=1+cos2ASwipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Use 1+tan⁡2A=sec⁡2A1 + \tan^2 A = \sec^2 A1+tan2A=sec2A with tan⁡A=12\tan A = \dfrac{1}{2}tanA=21​.

    sec⁡2A=1+14=54\sec^2 A = 1 + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{5}{4}sec2A=1+41​=45​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    cos⁡2A\cos^2 Acos2A is the reciprocal of sec⁡2A\sec^2 Asec2A.

    cos⁡2A=45\cos^2 A = \dfrac{4}{5}cos2A=54​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  10. 10

    Substitute into step 7.

    1+45=951 + \dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{9}{5}1+54​=59​Swipe to see the whole equation →

  11. Check: both routes agree ✔.

  12. Answer 95\dfrac{9}{5}59​

    Common trap: Writing 2cos⁡2A=(2cos⁡A)2=1652\cos^2 A = (2\cos A)^2 = \dfrac{16}{5}2cos2A=(2cosA)2=516​. The 2 multiplies cos⁡2A\cos^2 Acos2A; it is not squared.

Mixed example 2

Find the angle, then evaluate

Question. If 3tan⁡θ=3\sqrt{3}\tan\theta = 33​tanθ=3 and θ\thetaθ is acute, find θ\thetaθ and the value of sin⁡θ+cos⁡θ\sin\theta + \cos\thetasinθ+cosθ.

Given: 3tan⁡θ=3\sqrt{3}\tan\theta = 33​tanθ=3. Asked: θ\thetaθ and sin⁡θ+cos⁡θ\sin\theta + \cos\thetasinθ+cosθ.

Concept used: get the ratio alone and read the angle from the table (Topic 4). Why it applies: 33\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}}3​3​ simplifies to a table value.

  1. 1

    Divide both sides by 3\sqrt{3}3​.

    tan⁡θ=33\tan\theta = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}}tanθ=3​3​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Simplify: 33=333=3\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{3} = \sqrt{3}3​3​=333​​=3​.

    tan⁡θ=3\tan\theta = \sqrt{3}tanθ=3​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    From the table, tan⁡60°=3\tan 60° = \sqrt{3}tan60°=3​.

    θ=60°\theta = 60°θ=60°Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Substitute sin⁡60°=32\sin 60° = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}sin60°=23​​ and cos⁡60°=12\cos 60° = \dfrac{1}{2}cos60°=21​.

    sin⁡θ+cos⁡θ=32+12=3+12\sin\theta + \cos\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2}sinθ+cosθ=23​​+21​=23​+1​Swipe to see the whole equation →

  5. Check: 3tan⁡60°=3×3=3\sqrt{3}\tan 60° = \sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 33​tan60°=3​×3​=3 ✔.

  6. Answer θ=60°\theta = 60°θ=60°; sin⁡θ+cos⁡θ=3+12\sin\theta + \cos\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2}sinθ+cosθ=23​+1​

    Common trap: Answering sin⁡θ+cos⁡θ=1\sin\theta + \cos\theta = 1sinθ+cosθ=1 "by the identity". The identity is about squares.

Mixed example 3

Trigonometry meets coordinate geometry

Question. Find the distance between the points P(cos⁡0°,sin⁡0°)P(\cos 0°, \sin 0°)P(cos0°,sin0°) and Q(cos⁡60°,sin⁡60°)Q(\cos 60°, \sin 60°)Q(cos60°,sin60°).

Given: two points whose coordinates are table values. Asked: PQ.

Concept used: table values, then the distance formula (Chapter 7). Why it applies: once the values are substituted, the points have ordinary coordinates.

  1. 1

    Substitute the values: P(1,0)P(1, 0)P(1,0) and Q(12,32)Q\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)Q(21​,23​​).

  2. 2

    Distance formula.

    PQ=(12−1)2+(32−0)2PQ = \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{1}{2} - 1\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - 0\right)^2}PQ=(21​−1)2+(23​​−0)2​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Simplify the brackets.

    =(−12)2+(32)2= \sqrt{\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2}=(−21​)2+(23​​)2​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Square.

    =14+34= \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{3}{4}}=41​+43​​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Add and take the root.

    =1=1= \sqrt{1} = 1=1​=1Swipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: both points are at distance 1 from the origin (12+02=11^2 + 0^2 = 112+02=1 and 14+34=1\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{3}{4} = 141​+43​=1), and with the origin they form an equilateral triangle (angle 60° at the origin), so PQ = 1 ✔.

  7. Answer PQ=1 unitPQ = 1 \text{ unit}PQ=1 unit

Mixed example 4

Squaring a given condition

Question. If tan⁡A+cot⁡A=2\tan A + \cot A = 2tanA+cotA=2, find the value of tan⁡2A+cot⁡2A\tan^2 A + \cot^2 Atan2A+cot2A.

Given: tan⁡A+cot⁡A=2\tan A + \cot A = 2tanA+cotA=2. Asked: tan⁡2A+cot⁡2A\tan^2 A + \cot^2 Atan2A+cot2A.

Concept used: (a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2(a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2; tan⁡A×cot⁡A=1\tan A \times \cot A = 1tanA×cotA=1. Why it applies: squaring the sum produces the squares we want, plus a product we know.

  1. 1

    Square both sides of the given equation (this is an equation, not a proof, so both sides may be changed).

    (tan⁡A+cot⁡A)2=22(\tan A + \cot A)^2 = 2^2(tanA+cotA)2=22Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Expand the left side.

    tan⁡2A+2tan⁡Acot⁡A+cot⁡2A=4\tan^2 A + 2\tan A\cot A + \cot^2 A = 4tan2A+2tanAcotA+cot2A=4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    tan⁡Acot⁡A=1\tan A \cot A = 1tanAcotA=1 (reciprocals).

    tan⁡2A+2+cot⁡2A=4\tan^2 A + 2 + \cot^2 A = 4tan2A+2+cot2A=4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract 2 from both sides.

    tan⁡2A+cot⁡2A=2\tan^2 A + \cot^2 A = 2tan2A+cot2A=2Swipe to see the whole equation →

  5. Check: tan⁡A+cot⁡A=2\tan A + \cot A = 2tanA+cotA=2 happens at A=45°A = 45°A=45° (1+1=21 + 1 = 21+1=2), and then tan⁡2A+cot⁡2A=1+1=2\tan^2 A + \cot^2 A = 1 + 1 = 2tan2A+cot2A=1+1=2 ✔.

  6. Answer 222

    Common trap: Writing (tan⁡A+cot⁡A)2=tan⁡2A+cot⁡2A(\tan A + \cot A)^2 = \tan^2 A + \cot^2 A(tanA+cotA)2=tan2A+cot2A and answering 4. The middle term 2tan⁡Acot⁡A2\tan A\cot A2tanAcotA is missing.

Mixed example 5

A 2-move proof

Question. Prove that (sin⁡A+cos⁡A)2+(sin⁡A−cos⁡A)2=2(\sin A + \cos A)^2 + (\sin A - \cos A)^2 = 2(sinA+cosA)2+(sinA−cosA)2=2.

Given: the identity. To prove: LHS = RHS.

Concept used: expand with (a±b)2(a \pm b)^2(a±b)2, then Move 4 (sin⁡2A+cos⁡2A=1\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1sin2A+cos2A=1). Why it applies: the middle terms cancel and two copies of the first identity remain.

  1. 1

    Start from the LHS.

    LHS=(sin⁡A+cos⁡A)2+(sin⁡A−cos⁡A)2\text{LHS} = (\sin A + \cos A)^2 + (\sin A - \cos A)^2LHS=(sinA+cosA)2+(sinA−cosA)2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Expand both squares.

    =(sin⁡2A+2sin⁡Acos⁡A+cos⁡2A)+(sin⁡2A−2sin⁡Acos⁡A+cos⁡2A)= (\sin^2 A + 2\sin A\cos A + \cos^2 A) + (\sin^2 A - 2\sin A\cos A + \cos^2 A)=(sin2A+2sinAcosA+cos2A)+(sin2A−2sinAcosA+cos2A)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    The terms +2sin⁡Acos⁡A+2\sin A\cos A+2sinAcosA and −2sin⁡Acos⁡A-2\sin A\cos A−2sinAcosA cancel; collect the rest.

    =2sin⁡2A+2cos⁡2A= 2\sin^2 A + 2\cos^2 A=2sin2A+2cos2ASwipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Take out the common factor 2.

    =2(sin⁡2A+cos⁡2A)= 2(\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A)=2(sin2A+cos2A)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Move 4: sin⁡2A+cos⁡2A=1\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1sin2A+cos2A=1.

    =2×1=2=RHS= 2 \times 1 = 2 = \text{RHS}=2×1=2=RHSSwipe to see the whole equation →

    Hence proved.

    Common trap: Writing (sin⁡A+cos⁡A)2=1(\sin A + \cos A)^2 = 1(sinA+cosA)2=1 and (sin⁡A−cos⁡A)2=1(\sin A - \cos A)^2 = 1(sinA−cosA)2=1 straight away — that would give 2 by luck, with no marks for the method.
