Topic 4 of 6

Ratios of 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°

About 70 minutes · 5 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Derive the values for 30°, 45° and 60° from geometric triangles and state those for 0° and 90° (including "not defined").
  • Recall the full table of values.
  • Evaluate expressions using the table.
  • Find an unknown angle from a value, including equations such as 2 sin 5x = √3 and sin(A − B), cos(A + B) problems.
  • Describe how sin and cos change as the angle increases from 0° to 90°.

Open your geometry box. The two set squares inside are not random shapes: one has angles 45°, 45°, 90° and the other has 30°, 60°, 90°. They are exactly the triangles that give the most-used trigonometric values. By the end of this topic you will know the sine, cosine and tangent of these angles by heart — and, more importantly, you will be able to rebuild them in thirty seconds if you ever forget.

Why these angles are special

For most angles, sin⁡A\sin AsinA is an unending decimal that needs a calculator. But for 30°, 45° and 60° we can draw a triangle whose sides we know exactly, using two shapes from Class 9 geometry:

  • Half a square. Cut a square along its diagonal. Each half is a right triangle with two equal sides, so its two acute angles are both 45°.
  • Half an equilateral triangle. Every angle of an equilateral triangle is 60°. Drop a perpendicular from the top to the base: it cuts the base in half and splits the top angle into two angles of 30°. Each half is a right triangle with angles 30°, 60°, 90°.
Quick reminder

In an equilateral triangle all sides are equal and all angles are 60°. The perpendicular from a vertex to the opposite side bisects that side and that angle.

Worked examples 1 and 2 below work out the sides exactly. The results are the heart of this topic:

sin⁡30°=12,sin⁡45°=12,sin⁡60°=32\sin 30° = \dfrac{1}{2}, \quad \sin 45° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \quad \sin 60° = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}sin30°=21​,sin45°=2​1​,sin60°=23​​Swipe to see the whole equation →

What happens at 0° and 90°

Use the angle explorer (hypotenuse fixed at 1). As you drag A down towards 0°, the opposite side shrinks to nothing and the adjacent side stretches until it lies along the hypotenuse. So we define

sin⁡0°=0,cos⁡0°=1.\sin 0° = 0, \qquad \cos 0° = 1.sin0°=0,cos0°=1.Swipe to see the whole equation →

As you drag A up towards 90°, the opposite side grows until it matches the hypotenuse and the adjacent side shrinks to nothing:

sin⁡90°=1,cos⁡90°=0.\sin 90° = 1, \qquad \cos 90° = 0.sin90°=1,cos90°=0.Swipe to see the whole equation →

Now the tricky part. tan⁡90°=sin⁡90°cos⁡90°=10\tan 90° = \dfrac{\sin 90°}{\cos 90°} = \dfrac{1}{0}tan90°=cos90°sin90°​=01​, and division by zero has no answer. So tan⁡90°\tan 90°tan90° is not defined — it is not 0, not 1, and not "infinity". In the same way sec⁡90°=1cos⁡90°\sec 90° = \dfrac{1}{\cos 90°}sec90°=cos90°1​, cosec⁡0°=1sin⁡0°\operatorname{cosec} 0° = \dfrac{1}{\sin 0°}cosec0°=sin0°1​ and cot⁡0°=cos⁡0°sin⁡0°\cot 0° = \dfrac{\cos 0°}{\sin 0°}cot0°=sin0°cos0°​ are all not defined.

How to rebuild it:

  1. sin: the pattern 02,12,22,32,42\dfrac{\sqrt{0}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{1}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{4}}{2}20​​,21​​,22​​,23​​,24​​, simplified. (22\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}22​​ and 12\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}2​1​ are the same number.)
  2. cos: the sin row backwards.
  3. tan: sin ÷ cos, column by column. At 90° this is 1÷01 \div 01÷0: not defined.
  4. cosec, sec, cot: flip sin, cos and tan. Flipping 0 gives "not defined"; flipping 1 gives 1.

The pattern is a memory aid; the triangles are the reason. If you are ever unsure in the exam, sketch the half-square or the half-equilateral triangle in the margin and read the value off.

How the ratios change from 0° to 90°

Read along the rows: as A increases from 0° to 90°, sin⁡A\sin AsinA increases from 0 to 1, cos⁡A\cos AcosA decreases from 1 to 0, and tan⁡A\tan AtanA increases from 0 and keeps growing without limit (it is not defined at 90°). This matches the explorer: the opposite side grows, the adjacent side shrinks. So, for instance, cos⁡60°\cos 60°cos60° is smaller than cos⁡30°\cos 30°cos30°.

Finding an angle from a value

The table also works backwards. If tan⁡θ=3\tan \theta = \sqrt{3}tanθ=3​ and θ\thetaθ is acute, the tan row shows θ=60°\theta = 60°θ=60°. For an equation such as 2sin⁡5x=32 \sin 5x = \sqrt{3}2sin5x=3​:

  1. Get the ratio alone: sin⁡5x=32\sin 5x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}sin5x=23​​.
  2. Read the angle from the table: the angle whose sine is 32\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}23​​ is 60°, so 5x=60°5x = 60°5x=60°.
  3. Solve for the letter: x=12°x = 12°x=12°.

Treat 5x5x5x — or anything in a bracket after the ratio, such as A+BA + BA+B — as one angle. Be careful: sin⁡5x\sin 5xsin5x is not 5sin⁡x5 \sin x5sinx, and sin⁡(A+B)\sin(A + B)sin(A+B) is not sin⁡A+sin⁡B\sin A + \sin BsinA+sinB. For example, sin⁡(30°+60°)=sin⁡90°=1\sin(30° + 60°) = \sin 90° = 1sin(30°+60°)=sin90°=1, while sin⁡30°+sin⁡60°=1+32\sin 30° + \sin 60° = \dfrac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{2}sin30°+sin60°=21+3​​, which is bigger than 1.

Increasing or decreasing?

Question. As θ\thetaθ increases from 0° to 90°, which statement is true? (a) sin⁡θ\sin\thetasinθ and cos⁡θ\cos\thetacosθ both increase. (b) sin⁡θ\sin\thetasinθ increases and cos⁡θ\cos\thetacosθ decreases. (c) tan⁡θ\tan\thetatanθ decreases. (d) cos⁡θ\cos\thetacosθ increases and tan⁡θ\tan\thetatanθ decreases.

Given: four statements. Asked: the true one.

Concept used: read along the rows of the table (or use the explorer). Why it applies: the rows list values in increasing order of angle.

  1. 1

    sin row: 0,12,12,32,10, \dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, 10,21​,2​1​,23​​,1 — increasing.

  2. 2

    cos row: 1,32,12,12,01, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \dfrac{1}{2}, 01,23​​,2​1​,21​,0 — decreasing.

  3. 3

    tan row: 0,13,1,30, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, 1, \sqrt{3}0,3​1​,1,3​ — increasing (not defined at 90°).

  4. 4

    So (a), (c) and (d) are false and (b) is true.

  5. Check: in the explorer the opposite side (sin) grows and the adjacent side (cos) shrinks as the angle opens ✔.

  6. Answer (b)

    Common trap: Thinking every ratio "grows with the angle". Cosine falls.

Common mistakes!

  • Table values mixed up is one of the four most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter — especially swapping the values of 30° and 60°.

  • sin⁡30°=32\sin 30° = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}sin30°=23​​, sin⁡60°=12\sin 60° = \dfrac{1}{2}sin60°=21​

    Swapped: the smallest angle has the smallest sine.

    Rebuild the pattern 02…42\dfrac{\sqrt{0}}{2} \dots \dfrac{\sqrt{4}}{2}20​​…24​​ in the margin.

  • tan⁡90°=0\tan 90° = 0tan90°=0 or 111

    tan⁡90°=10\tan 90° = \dfrac{1}{0}tan90°=01​, which has no value.

    Write "not defined" in words.

  • cot⁡0°=0\cot 0° = 0cot0°=0

    cot⁡0°=cos⁡0°sin⁡0°=10\cot 0° = \dfrac{\cos 0°}{\sin 0°} = \dfrac{1}{0}cot0°=sin0°cos0°​=01​.

    Not defined. (It is cot⁡90°\cot 90°cot90° that equals 0.)

  • sin⁡2A=2sin⁡A\sin 2A = 2 \sin Asin2A=2sinA

    sin⁡2A\sin 2Asin2A is the sine of the angle 2A2A2A. E.g. sin⁡60°=32\sin 60° = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}sin60°=23​​, but 2sin⁡30°=12\sin 30° = 12sin30°=1.

    Work out the angle first, then take its sine.

  • sin⁡(A+B)=sin⁡A+sin⁡B\sin(A + B) = \sin A + \sin Bsin(A+B)=sinA+sinB

    A bracket is one angle; sine does not split over a sum.

    Add the angles first: sin⁡(30°+60°)=sin⁡90°\sin(30° + 60°) = \sin 90°sin(30°+60°)=sin90°.

  • "cos increases as the angle increases"

    The adjacent side shrinks as the angle opens.

    sin and tan increase; cos decreases.

Key points

  • Half a square gives 45°; half an equilateral triangle gives 30° and 60°.
  • sin: 0,12,12,32,10, \dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, 10,21​,2​1​,23​​,1 for 0°,30°,45°,60°,90°0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°0°,30°,45°,60°,90°. cos is the same list backwards. tan = sin ÷ cos.
  • tan⁡90°\tan 90°tan90°, sec⁡90°\sec 90°sec90°, cosec⁡0°\operatorname{cosec} 0°cosec0° and cot⁡0°\cot 0°cot0° are not defined.
  • From 0° to 90°: sin and tan increase, cos decreases.
  • To find an angle: get the ratio alone → read the angle from the table → solve for the letter. A bracket after a ratio is one angle.

Complementary angles (optional — not for the board exam)

Beyond the syllabus — not assessed. Trigonometric ratios of complementary angles were removed from the NCERT textbook and are not in the 2026-27 CBSE syllabus. There are no practice or test questions on them. You will still see them in older guidebooks and in the NCERT Exemplar, so this short section explains the idea for anyone curious.

Two angles are complementary if they add up to 90°. In △ABC right-angled at B, ∠A+∠C=90°\angle A + \angle C = 90°∠A+∠C=90°, so ∠C=90°−A\angle C = 90° - A∠C=90°−A.

Look at the triangle from both acute angles (Topic 1): the side opposite to A is adjacent to C, and the side adjacent to A is opposite to C. So

sin⁡C=ABAC=cos⁡A,cos⁡C=BCAC=sin⁡A,tan⁡C=ABBC=cot⁡A.\sin C = \dfrac{AB}{AC} = \cos A, \qquad \cos C = \dfrac{BC}{AC} = \sin A, \qquad \tan C = \dfrac{AB}{BC} = \cot A.sinC=ACAB​=cosA,cosC=ACBC​=sinA,tanC=BCAB​=cotA.Swipe to see the whole equation →

Writing C=90°−AC = 90° - AC=90°−A gives six results:

sin⁡(90°−A)=cos⁡A,cos⁡(90°−A)=sin⁡A,tan⁡(90°−A)=cot⁡A,\sin(90° - A) = \cos A, \quad \cos(90° - A) = \sin A, \quad \tan(90° - A) = \cot A,sin(90°−A)=cosA,cos(90°−A)=sinA,tan(90°−A)=cotA,Swipe to see the whole equation →
cot⁡(90°−A)=tan⁡A,sec⁡(90°−A)=cosec⁡A,cosec⁡(90°−A)=sec⁡A.\cot(90° - A) = \tan A, \quad \sec(90° - A) = \operatorname{cosec} A, \quad \operatorname{cosec}(90° - A) = \sec A.cot(90°−A)=tanA,sec(90°−A)=cosecA,cosec(90°−A)=secA.Swipe to see the whole equation →

You have already seen this in the table: sin⁡30°=cos⁡60°\sin 30° = \cos 60°sin30°=cos60° and tan⁡30°=cot⁡60°\tan 30° = \cot 60°tan30°=cot60°, because 30°+60°=90°30° + 60° = 90°30°+60°=90°.
