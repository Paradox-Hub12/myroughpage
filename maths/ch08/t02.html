Toolkit: Pythagoras, surds and fractions

About 30 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find the third side with Pythagoras' theorem and recognise common triples (3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25).
  • Simplify and rationalise surds.
  • Square and add fractions accurately.
Why this lesson exists

Trigonometry itself is not hard — but its answers are fractions full of square roots. Most marks lost in this chapter are lost to Class 8 and 9 skills: a wrong third side, a surd left in the denominator, or (23)2\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2(32​)2 written as 43\dfrac{4}{3}34​. This short lesson rebuilds those three skills. If you already have them, prove it below and go straight to Topic 3.

A cricket pitch is 22 yards long. If you stand at one set of stumps and want to know how far you are from a fielder standing square of the other stumps, you need Pythagoras. Every trigonometry question starts the same way: a right triangle, two sides you know, one side you must find — and then fractions to tidy up.

  1. Pythagoras' theorem

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides:

hypotenuse2=side12+side22\text{hypotenuse}^2 = \text{side}_1^2 + \text{side}_2^2hypotenuse2=side12​+side22​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  • To find the hypotenuse: square the two sides, add, take the square root.
  • To find a shorter side: square the hypotenuse, subtract the square of the known side, take the square root.

Pythagorean triples are whole-number sets that fit the theorem. Learn these four — they turn up again and again:

Triple Check
3, 4, 5 9+16=259 + 16 = 259+16=25
5, 12, 13 25+144=16925 + 144 = 16925+144=169
8, 15, 17 64+225=28964 + 225 = 28964+225=289
7, 24, 25 49+576=62549 + 576 = 62549+576=625

Any multiple of a triple is also a triple: 6, 8, 10 and 15, 20, 25 come from 3, 4, 5. Spotting a triple saves time, but if you are not sure, just calculate.

  1. Surds: simplifying and rationalising

A surd is a square root that is not a whole number, such as 2\sqrt{2}2​ or 5\sqrt{5}5​.

  • Simplify by taking out the largest perfect square: 72=36×2=62\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = 6\sqrt{2}72​=36×2​=62​.
  • a×a=a\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{a} = aa​×a​=a, so (3)2=3\left(\sqrt{3}\right)^2 = 3(3​)2=3.
  • Rationalising means removing the surd from the denominator. Multiply the top and the bottom by the same surd — this is multiplying by 1, so the value does not change:
13=1×33×3=33\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{1 \times \sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}3​1​=3​×3​1×3​​=33​​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  • If the denominator is a sum such as 2+32 + \sqrt{3}2+3​, multiply top and bottom by its conjugate 2−32 - \sqrt{3}2−3​, and use (a+b)(a−b)=a2−b2(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2(a+b)(a−b)=a2−b2: (2+3)(2−3)=4−3=1(2 + \sqrt{3})(2 - \sqrt{3}) = 4 - 3 = 1(2+3​)(2−3​)=4−3=1.

Both 12\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}2​1​ and 22\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}22​​ are correct and equal. Boards accept either, unless a question asks you to rationalise.

  1. Squaring, adding and dividing fractions

  • Square the top and the bottom: (23)2=49\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \dfrac{4}{9}(32​)2=94​, not 43\dfrac{4}{3}34​. With a surd: (32)2=34\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{3}{4}(23​​)2=43​.
  • Add only with a common denominator: 49+59=99=1\dfrac{4}{9} + \dfrac{5}{9} = \dfrac{9}{9} = 194​+95​=99​=1. Never add the denominators.
  • A whole number is a fraction too: 1=991 = \dfrac{9}{9}1=99​, so 1+49=1391 + \dfrac{4}{9} = \dfrac{13}{9}1+94​=913​.
  • Dividing: multiply by the reciprocal. A "fraction over a fraction" with the same denominator simplifies fast — the denominators cancel:
  32    12  =32×21=3\dfrac{\;\dfrac{3}{2}\;}{\;\dfrac{1}{2}\;} = \dfrac{3}{2} \times \dfrac{2}{1} = 321​23​​=23​×12​=3Swipe to see the whole equation →

Worked example 1

Finding the hypotenuse (spot a triple)

Question. The two shorter sides of a right triangle are 12 cm and 16 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

Given: the two shorter sides. Asked: the hypotenuse.

Concept used: Pythagoras' theorem; the 3-4-5 triple. Why it applies: we know two sides of a right triangle.

  1. 1

    Notice that 12=3×412 = 3 \times 412=3×4 and 16=4×416 = 4 \times 416=4×4: the sides are 3 and 4, each multiplied by 4. So the hypotenuse should be 5×4=205 \times 4 = 205×4=20 cm. Confirm it.

  2. 2

    Write the theorem.

    hyp2=122+162\text{hyp}^2 = 12^2 + 16^2hyp2=122+162Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Square each side.

    hyp2=144+256\text{hyp}^2 = 144 + 256hyp2=144+256Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Add.

    hyp2=400\text{hyp}^2 = 400hyp2=400Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Take the square root (a length is positive).

    hyp=20 cm\text{hyp} = 20 \text{ cm}hyp=20 cmSwipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: matches the triple prediction ✔.

  7. Answer 20 cm20 \text{ cm}20 cm

    Common trap: Adding the sides before squaring: (12+16)2=784(12 + 16)^2 = 784(12+16)2=784, giving 784=28\sqrt{784} = 28784​=28 cm — exactly the two sides added together, which is impossible: the hypotenuse is always shorter than the other two sides put together. Square first, then add.

Worked example 2

Finding a shorter side

Question. The hypotenuse of a right triangle is 61 cm and one side is 11 cm. Find the third side.

Given: hypotenuse 61 cm, one side 11 cm. Asked: the other side.

Concept used: Pythagoras' theorem, rearranged to find a shorter side. Why it applies: the unknown is not the hypotenuse, so we subtract.

  1. 1

    Write the theorem with the unknown side xxx.

    612=112+x261^2 = 11^2 + x^2612=112+x2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Subtract 11211^2112 from both sides.

    x2=612−112x^2 = 61^2 - 11^2x2=612−112Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Square each number.

    x2=3721−121x^2 = 3721 - 121x2=3721−121Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract.

    x2=3600x^2 = 3600x2=3600Swipe to see the whole equation →

Worked example 3

A surd answer

Question. The hypotenuse of a right triangle is 6 cm and one side is 2 cm. Find the third side in simplest surd form.

Given: hypotenuse 6 cm, one side 2 cm. Asked: the third side.

Concept used: Pythagoras, then simplifying a surd. Why it applies: the answer will not be a whole number.

  1. 1

    Subtract the square of the known side from the square of the hypotenuse.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Subtract.

    x2=32x^2 = 32x2=32Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Take the square root.

    x=32x = \sqrt{32}x=32​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Take out the largest perfect square: 32=16×232 = 16 \times 232=16×2.

    x=16×2=42 cmx = \sqrt{16} \times \sqrt{2} = 4\sqrt{2} \text{ cm}x=16​×2​=42​ cmSwipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: (42)2+22=16×2+4=36=62\left(4\sqrt{2}\right)^2 + 2^2 = 16 \times 2 + 4 = 36 = 6^2(42​)2+22=16×2+4=36=62 ✔.

Common mistakes!

  • Adding the squares when finding a shorter side

    Only the hypotenuse is found by adding.

    Hypotenuse → add squares; shorter side → subtract from hyp².

  • (a+b)2(a + b)^2(a+b)2 used instead of a2+b2a^2 + b^2a2+b2

    Pythagoras squares each side separately.

    Square, then add.

  • (23)2=43\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \dfrac{4}{3}(32​)2=34​

    Squaring a fraction squares the bottom too.

    49\dfrac{4}{9}94​.

  • 49+59=918\dfrac{4}{9} + \dfrac{5}{9} = \dfrac{9}{18}94​+95​=189​

    Denominators are never added.

    Same denominator → add the tops only.

  • (3)2=9\left(\sqrt{3}\right)^2 = 9(3​)2=9

    3×3=3\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 33​×3​=3.

    The square and the square root undo each other.

  • Thinking 12\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}2​1​ is "wrong" and 22\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}22​​ is "right"

    They are equal.

    Use either, unless the question says "rationalise".

Key points

  • Pythagoras: hyp² = side² + side². Add to find the hypotenuse, subtract to find a shorter side.
  • Triples to know: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25 and their multiples.
  • Simplify surds by taking out the largest perfect square; rationalise with the surd, or with the conjugate for a±ba \pm \sqrt{b}a±b​.
  • Square the top and the bottom of a fraction; add only with a common denominator.
  • Fraction over fraction: multiply by the reciprocal of the bottom.
