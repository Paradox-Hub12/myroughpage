Topic 3 of 6

Finding all ratios from one

About 55 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Given one ratio, draw the triangle, find the missing side and write the other five ratios.
  • Evaluate expressions such as (1 − tan²A)/(1 + tan²A) from one given ratio.
  • Judge true/false statements about ratios with a reason.

If someone tells you only that a road climbs 28 m for every 45 m it runs horizontally, can you work out how long the road surface is, and every other ratio of its angle? Yes — one ratio fixes the angle, and a fixed angle fixes the shape of the triangle. One number is enough to unlock all six.

One ratio is enough

In Topic 1 you saw that a ratio depends only on the angle. So if you know one ratio, say tan⁡B=2845\tan B = \dfrac{28}{45}tanB=4528​, you know the shape of the triangle: the opposite and adjacent sides are in the ratio 28 : 45. The actual lengths could be 28 cm and 45 cm, or 56 m and 90 m — it does not matter, because every ratio comes out the same.

To keep this general, we write the sides as multiples of a positive number kkk: opposite =28k= 28k=28k, adjacent =45k= 45k=45k. When you form a ratio, the kkk cancels.

The method — five steps

  1. Write the given ratio as a fraction of sides. For example, tan⁡B=oppositeadjacent=2845\tan B = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \dfrac{28}{45}tanB=adjacentopposite​=4528​.
  2. Draw a right triangle, mark the angle, and write the two known sides as multiples of kkk — on the correct sides, labelled from that angle.
  3. Find the third side by Pythagoras (Topic 2). Remember: add to get the hypotenuse, subtract to get a shorter side.
  4. Read off the other ratios using SOH-CAH-TOA and the reciprocals. The kkk cancels in every one.
  5. Check: sin⁡\sinsin and cos⁡\coscos must be less than 1; sin⁡cos⁡\dfrac{\sin}{\cos}cossin​ must equal tan⁡\tantan; reciprocal pairs must multiply to 1.

A ratio written without a fraction works the same way. "3tan⁡A=23 \tan A = 23tanA=2" means tan⁡A=23\tan A = \dfrac{2}{3}tanA=32​; "cot⁡θ=2\cot \theta = 2cotθ=2" means cot⁡θ=21\cot \theta = \dfrac{2}{1}cotθ=12​.

Evaluating an expression

Once you have all the ratios, an expression such as 2sin⁡A−cos⁡A2sin⁡A+cos⁡A\dfrac{2\sin A - \cos A}{2\sin A + \cos A}2sinA+cosA2sinA−cosA​ is just substitution, followed by fraction work. Two tips keep the arithmetic short:

  • If every ratio has the same denominator (like 13\sqrt{13}13​), it cancels between the top and bottom of the big fraction — you do not need to rationalise first.
  • When the expression contains tan⁡2\tan^2tan2, square the fraction (top and bottom) before adding 1.

Can this ratio exist?

Before any working, ask whether the given ratio is possible for an acute angle:

Ratio Possible values for acute A Why
sin⁡A\sin AsinA, cos⁡A\cos AcosA between 0 and 1 the hypotenuse is on the bottom, and it is the longest side
cosec⁡A\operatorname{cosec} AcosecA, sec⁡A\sec AsecA greater than 1 the hypotenuse is on top
tan⁡A\tan AtanA, cot⁡A\cot AcotA any positive number they compare the two shorter sides

So "cos⁡A=75\cos A = \dfrac{7}{5}cosA=57​" or "sec⁡A=45\sec A = \dfrac{4}{5}secA=54​" can be rejected straight away. True/false questions in the exam often test exactly this.

Two routes to the same answer

There are two ways to get from one ratio to the others, and both earn full marks:

  • The triangle route (this topic): draw, label with kkk, use Pythagoras, read off. It is visual, and it works even if you have forgotten the identities.
  • The identity route (Topic 5): for example, cos⁡A=1−sin⁡2A\cos A = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 A}cosA=1−sin2A​ and tan⁡A=sin⁡Acos⁡A\tan A = \dfrac{\sin A}{\cos A}tanA=cosAsinA​. It is quicker once you know the identities, but it is easy to drop a square.

Use whichever you trust more, and use the other one as your check. If both routes give the same value, you can be confident.

What to write in the exam

A 2- or 3-mark "find the other ratios" question is marked in steps: the figure with the sides as multiples of kkk, the third side by Pythagoras, then the ratios. So always draw the triangle and show the Pythagoras line — even if you can see the answer at once from a triple. Leave surds in a simple form (35\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{5}}5​3​ or 355\dfrac{3\sqrt{5}}{5}535​​ — both are accepted unless the question asks you to rationalise).

True or false, with reasons

Common mistakes!

  • Putting the given numbers on the wrong sides (e.g. the top of tan⁡B=2845\tan B = \dfrac{28}{45}tanB=4528​ on the adjacent side)

    The fraction tells you which side is which from the given angle.

    Write "opp/adj" (or hyp/adj …) under the fraction before drawing.

  • Finding the third side, then labelling it from the other angle

    Opposite and adjacent swap between the two acute angles.

    Keep the marked angle in view while reading off each ratio.

  • Adding squares when the unknown is a shorter side

    Only the hypotenuse is found by adding.

    Hyp unknown → add; shorter side unknown → subtract.

  • Forgetting the hypotenuse is on top in sec and cosec

    sec⁡=hypadj\sec = \dfrac{\text{hyp}}{\text{adj}}sec=adjhyp​, cosec⁡=hypopp\operatorname{cosec} = \dfrac{\text{hyp}}{\text{opp}}cosec=opphyp​.

    Turn them into cos and sin first if that is easier.

  • Accepting sin⁡A\sin AsinA or cos⁡A\cos AcosA greater than 1

    The hypotenuse is the longest side.

    A value above 1 for sin or cos means a mistake — go back and check.

Key points

  • One ratio fixes the angle, so it fixes all six ratios.
  • Method: ratio as sides → draw, mark the angle, label with kkk → Pythagoras → read off → check.
  • To evaluate an expression, find the ratios it needs and substitute; common denominators often cancel.
  • sin⁡A\sin AsinA, cos⁡A<1\cos A < 1cosA<1; cosec⁡A\operatorname{cosec} AcosecA, sec⁡A>1\sec A > 1secA>1; tan⁡A\tan AtanA, cot⁡A\cot AcotA can be any positive number.
