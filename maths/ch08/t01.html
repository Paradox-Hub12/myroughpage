Topic 1 of 6

Meet the trigonometric ratios

About 55 minutes · 4 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Name the opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse sides with respect to a given acute angle.
  • Define the six trigonometric ratios and the reciprocal pairs.
  • Explain why a ratio depends only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle.
  • State that sin A and cos A are never more than 1 for an acute angle A.

A good wheelchair ramp rises about 1 cm for every 12 cm it runs along the ground. A short ramp at a shop door and a long ramp at a railway station can both follow this rule — they look different in size, but they are equally steep, because they make the same angle with the ground. Steepness is a ratio of two lengths, and that ratio tells you the angle. Trigonometry is the study of these ratios. It is how surveyors find the height of a hill, and how engineers set the slope of a road, without climbing anything.

Stand at the angle: naming the sides

Every right-angled triangle has one right angle and two acute angles. Its three sides get names, and two of those names depend on which acute angle you are looking from.

  • The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle. It is always the longest side, and its name never changes.
  • Choose an acute angle, say ∠A\angle A∠A, and imagine standing at that corner. The side opposite to ∠A\angle A∠A is the one across from you — it does not touch corner A.
  • The side adjacent to ∠A\angle A∠A is the side that touches corner A and is not the hypotenuse.

Now walk to the other acute angle, ∠C\angle C∠C. The hypotenuse stays the same, but the other two sides swap names: the side that was opposite to A is adjacent to C, and the side that was adjacent to A is opposite to C.

So before you write any ratio, do two things: mark the angle you are working with, and label the three sides from that angle. Most mistakes in this chapter start with skipping this step.

The six trigonometric ratios

For an acute angle A in a right-angled triangle, the trigonometric ratios compare pairs of sides:

Read them as "sine A", "cosine A" and "tangent A". A popular memory aid is SOH-CAH-TOA: Sine = Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse, Cosine = Adjacent ÷ Hypotenuse, Tangent = Opposite ÷ Adjacent.

The other three ratios are the reciprocals (the same fractions turned upside down):

These are "cosecant", "secant" and "cotangent". To remember the pairs, notice that every reciprocal pair contains exactly one "co": cosec goes with sin, sec goes with cos, cot goes with tan.

One more link is useful. Divide sin A by cos A: the hypotenuse cancels and you are left with opposite ÷ adjacent. So

A ratio is a fingerprint of the angle

Lean two ladders, one short and one long, against a wall at exactly the same angle. The two right triangles they make (ladder, wall, ground) have the same angles, so they are similar triangles (Chapter 6).

Quick reminder

In similar triangles, corresponding sides are in the same ratio. If one triangle is an enlargement of the other by 3 times, every side is 3 times as long.

When every side is multiplied by the same number, every ratio of two sides stays exactly the same. So sin⁡A\sin AsinA, cos⁡A\cos AcosA and tan⁡A\tan AtanA depend only on the angle A, never on how big the triangle is. That is why it makes sense to talk about "the sine of 30°" as one fixed number. Mathematicians say the ratios are well defined.

How big can a ratio be?

The hypotenuse is the longest side. In sin⁡A\sin AsinA and cos⁡A\cos AcosA the hypotenuse is on the bottom, so for an acute angle A:

0<sin⁡A<1and0<cos⁡A<10 < \sin A < 1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 0 < \cos A < 10<sinA<1and0<cosA<1Swipe to see the whole equation →

A value such as sin⁡A=54\sin A = \dfrac{5}{4}sinA=45​ is impossible — it would need the opposite side to be longer than the hypotenuse. Flip the fractions and you get the opposite story: cosec⁡A\operatorname{cosec} AcosecA and sec⁡A\sec AsecA are always greater than 1. But tan⁡A\tan AtanA and cot⁡A\cot AcotA compare the two shorter sides, so they can be any positive number: a steep ramp can have tan⁡A=3\tan A = 3tanA=3.

Reading the notation

"sin⁡A\sin AsinA" is one symbol: the sine of the angle A. It is not "sin multiplied by A", so you can never cancel the A in sin⁡AA\dfrac{\sin A}{A}AsinA​, and "sin" on its own has no value — just as "x\sqrt{\phantom{x}}x​" on its own has no value. When we square a ratio we write sin⁡2A\sin^2 Asin2A, which means (sin⁡A)2(\sin A)^2(sinA)2. You will use this from Topic 3 onwards.

Worked example 1

Labelling sides from both acute angles

Question. In △PQR, ∠Q=90°\angle Q = 90°∠Q=90°. Name the hypotenuse, and the opposite and adjacent sides of ∠P\angle P∠P and of ∠R\angle R∠R. Then write sin⁡P\sin PsinP, cos⁡P\cos PcosP, tan⁡P\tan PtanP, sin⁡R\sin RsinR, cos⁡R\cos RcosR and tan⁡R\tan RtanR in terms of the sides.

Given: a right triangle with the right angle at Q. Asked: the side names from each acute angle, and six ratios.

Concept used: the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle; opposite and adjacent depend on the angle you stand at. Why it applies: we need the ratios for two different angles of the same triangle.

  1. 1

    The right angle is at Q, so the side opposite to Q is the hypotenuse.

    hypotenuse=PR\text{hypotenuse} = PRhypotenuse=PRSwipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Stand at P. The side across from P (not touching P) is QR.

    opposite to ∠P=QR\text{opposite to } \angle P = QRopposite to ∠P=QRSwipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    The side touching P that is not the hypotenuse is PQ.

    adjacent to ∠P=PQ\text{adjacent to } \angle P = PQadjacent to ∠P=PQSwipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Stand at R. The side across from R is PQ.

    opposite to ∠R=PQ\text{opposite to } \angle R = PQopposite to ∠R=PQSwipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    The side touching R that is not the hypotenuse is QR.

    adjacent to ∠R=QR\text{adjacent to } \angle R = QRadjacent to ∠R=QRSwipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Use SOH-CAH-TOA for ∠P\angle P∠P.

    sin⁡P=QRPR,cos⁡P=PQPR,tan⁡P=QRPQ\sin P = \dfrac{QR}{PR}, \qquad \cos P = \dfrac{PQ}{PR}, \qquad \tan P = \dfrac{QR}{PQ}sinP=PRQR​,cosP=PRPQ​,tanP=PQQR​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Use SOH-CAH-TOA for ∠R\angle R∠R.

    sin⁡R=PQPR,cos⁡R=QRPR,tan⁡R=PQQR\sin R = \dfrac{PQ}{PR}, \qquad \cos R = \dfrac{QR}{PR}, \qquad \tan R = \dfrac{PQ}{QR}sinR=PRPQ​,cosR=PRQR​,tanR=QRPQ​Swipe to see the whole equation →

  8. Check: PR appears on the bottom of every sine and cosine ✔. The opposite and adjacent sides of P are the adjacent and opposite sides of R ✔.

  9. Answer Hypotenuse PR. For ∠P\angle P∠P: opposite QR, adjacent PQ. For ∠R\angle R∠R: opposite PQ, adjacent QR. The six ratios are as in steps 6 and 7.

    Common trap: Writing sin⁡R=QRPR\sin R = \dfrac{QR}{PR}sinR=PRQR​ because QR "was the opposite side" a moment ago. It was opposite to P, not to R. Re-label every time you change the angle.

Worked example 2

The same triangle, two different tangents

Question. In △XYZ, ∠Y=90°\angle Y = 90°∠Y=90°, XY = 9 cm, YZ = 40 cm and XZ = 41 cm. Find tan⁡X\tan XtanX and tan⁡Z\tan ZtanZ.

Given: the three sides; the right angle is at Y. Asked: tan⁡X\tan XtanX and tan⁡Z\tan ZtanZ.

Concept used: tan⁡=oppositeadjacent\tan = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}tan=adjacentopposite​, with the sides labelled from the angle asked. Why it applies: the two tangents use the same two sides, but the other way up.

  1. 1

    Check that the triangle is right-angled at Y (Pythagoras).

    XY2+YZ2=81+1600=1681=412=XZ2XY^2 + YZ^2 = 81 + 1600 = 1681 = 41^2 = XZ^2XY2+YZ2=81+1600=1681=412=XZ2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Stand at X: the opposite side is YZ and the adjacent side is XY.

    tan⁡X=YZXY\tan X = \dfrac{YZ}{XY}tanX=XYYZ​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Substitute the lengths.

    tan⁡X=409\tan X = \dfrac{40}{9}tanX=940​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Stand at Z: the opposite side is XY and the adjacent side is YZ.

    tan⁡Z=XYYZ\tan Z = \dfrac{XY}{YZ}tanZ=YZXY​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Substitute the lengths.

    tan⁡Z=940\tan Z = \dfrac{9}{40}tanZ=409​Swipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: tan⁡X×tan⁡Z=409×940=1\tan X \times \tan Z = \dfrac{40}{9} \times \dfrac{9}{40} = 1tanX×tanZ=940​×409​=1 — the two tangents are reciprocals, as expected when opposite and adjacent swap ✔. tan⁡X>1\tan X > 1tanX>1 is fine: tan can be more than 1.

  7. Answer tan⁡X=409,tan⁡Z=940\tan X = \dfrac{40}{9}, \quad \tan Z = \dfrac{9}{40}tanX=940​,tanZ=409​

    Common trap: Giving tan⁡X=940\tan X = \dfrac{9}{40}tanX=409​ because "tan X should be less than 1". Tan has no upper limit; label the sides from X and trust the labels.

All six ratios

Question. In △ABC, ∠B=90°\angle B = 90°∠B=90°, AB = 35 cm, BC = 12 cm and AC = 37 cm. Write all six trigonometric ratios of ∠A\angle A∠A.

Worked example 4

Same angle, bigger triangle

Question. A small ramp rises 0.9 m over a horizontal distance of 4 m, and its sloping surface is 4.1 m long. A larger ramp rises 1.35 m over 6 m, with a sloping surface of 6.15 m. Show that the two ramps make the same angle with the ground.

Given: the three sides of each ramp's right triangle. Asked: to show the angles are equal.

Concept used: a trigonometric ratio depends only on the angle. Why it applies: if the ratios are equal, the angles are equal.

  1. 1

    Call the angle with the ground A (small ramp) and B (large ramp). For each, the rise is opposite, the horizontal distance is adjacent, the slope is the hypotenuse.

  2. 2

    Sine of the small ramp's angle.

    sin⁡A=0.94.1=941\sin A = \dfrac{0.9}{4.1} = \dfrac{9}{41}sinA=4.10.9​=419​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Sine of the large ramp's angle.

    sin⁡B=1.356.15=135615=941\sin B = \dfrac{1.35}{6.15} = \dfrac{135}{615} = \dfrac{9}{41}sinB=6.151.35​=615135​=419​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Tangent of each, as a second check.

    tan⁡A=0.94=940,tan⁡B=1.356=940\tan A = \dfrac{0.9}{4} = \dfrac{9}{40}, \qquad \tan B = \dfrac{1.35}{6} = \dfrac{9}{40}tanA=40.9​=409​,tanB=61.35​=409​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    The ratios are equal, so the angles are equal.

    ∠A=∠B\angle A = \angle B∠A=∠BSwipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: each side of the large ramp is 1.5 times the matching side of the small one (0.9×1.5=1.350.9 \times 1.5 = 1.350.9×1.5=1.35, 4×1.5=64 \times 1.5 = 64×1.5=6, 4.1×1.5=6.154.1 \times 1.5 = 6.154.1×1.5=6.15), so the triangles are similar ✔.

  7. Answer Both ramps have sin⁡=941\sin = \dfrac{9}{41}sin=419​ and tan⁡=940\tan = \dfrac{9}{40}tan=409​, so they make the same angle with the ground.

    Common trap: Thinking the longer ramp is "steeper" because its rise is bigger. Steepness is the ratio, not the length.

True or false? Justify your answer. "If every side of a right triangle is doubled, sin⁡A\sin AsinA is doubled, so sin⁡A\sin AsinA could become more than 1."

Answer: False.

Working:

  1. Doubling every side doubles both the opposite side and the hypotenuse.
  2. sin⁡A=2×opposite2×hypotenuse=oppositehypotenuse\sin A = \dfrac{2 \times \text{opposite}}{2 \times \text{hypotenuse}} = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}sinA=2×hypotenuse2×opposite​=hypotenuseopposite​ — the 2s cancel, so sin⁡A\sin AsinA does not change.

Common mistakes!

  • Opposite and adjacent swapped is one of the four most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter. It nearly always happens when a question asks about one angle and you label the sides from the other.

  • Using the opposite side of ∠C\angle C∠C when the question asks about ∠A\angle A∠A

    Opposite and adjacent swap when you move to the other acute angle.

    Mark the asked angle with an arc, then label opposite/adjacent from that corner.

  • Calling the hypotenuse "adjacent" because it touches the angle

    The hypotenuse always keeps its own name.

    Adjacent = touches the angle and is not the hypotenuse.

  • sec⁡A=1sin⁡A\sec A = \dfrac{1}{\sin A}secA=sinA1​

    Sec pairs with cos.

    Each reciprocal pair has exactly one "co": cosec–sin, sec–cos, cot–tan.

  • Treating sin⁡A\sin AsinA as "sin × A" and cancelling A

    sin⁡A\sin AsinA is one symbol, a number that depends on A.

    Never split "sin" from its angle.

  • sin⁡A=54\sin A = \dfrac{5}{4}sinA=45​ accepted as an answer

    The opposite side cannot be longer than the hypotenuse.

    If you get sin⁡A\sin AsinA or cos⁡A\cos AcosA above 1, look for a labelling error.

Key points

  • Hypotenuse: opposite the right angle, the longest side. Opposite and adjacent depend on the angle you stand at — they swap between the two acute angles.
  • SOH-CAH-TOA: sin⁡A=opphyp\sin A = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}sinA=hypopp​, cos⁡A=adjhyp\cos A = \dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}cosA=hypadj​, tan⁡A=oppadj\tan A = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}tanA=adjopp​.
  • Reciprocal pairs: cosec⁡A=1sin⁡A\operatorname{cosec} A = \dfrac{1}{\sin A}cosecA=sinA1​, sec⁡A=1cos⁡A\sec A = \dfrac{1}{\cos A}secA=cosA1​, cot⁡A=1tan⁡A\cot A = \dfrac{1}{\tan A}cotA=tanA1​; also tan⁡A=sin⁡Acos⁡A\tan A = \dfrac{\sin A}{\cos A}tanA=cosAsinA​.
  • A ratio depends only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle (similar triangles).
  • For an acute angle, 0<sin⁡A<10 < \sin A < 10<sinA<1 and 0<cos⁡A<10 < \cos A < 10<cosA<1; tan⁡A\tan AtanA can be any positive number.
