The whole chapter on one page

Naming the sides — in a right triangle, the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. Opposite and adjacent depend on the acute angle you stand at, and they swap between the two acute angles. Mark the angle first, then label.

The six ratios — sin⁡A=opphyp\sin A = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}sinA=hypopp​, cos⁡A=adjhyp\cos A = \dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}cosA=hypadj​, tan⁡A=oppadj\tan A = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}tanA=adjopp​ (SOH-CAH-TOA); cosec⁡A\operatorname{cosec} AcosecA, sec⁡A\sec AsecA, cot⁡A\cot AcotA are their reciprocals (each pair has exactly one "co"). tan⁡A=sin⁡Acos⁡A\tan A = \dfrac{\sin A}{\cos A}tanA=cosAsinA​. A ratio depends only on the angle (similar triangles).

Sizes — for acute A: 0<sin⁡A,cos⁡A<10 < \sin A, \cos A < 10<sinA,cosA<1; sec⁡A,cosec⁡A>1\sec A, \operatorname{cosec} A > 1secA,cosecA>1; tan⁡A,cot⁡A\tan A, \cot AtanA,cotA any positive value.

All ratios from one — write the ratio as sides with kkk → draw and label from the angle → Pythagoras → read off. Or use an identity, e.g. cos⁡A=1−sin⁡2A\cos A = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 A}cosA=1−sin2A​.

Special angles — sin is 02,12,22,32,42\dfrac{\sqrt{0}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{1}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{4}}{2}20​​,21​​,22​​,23​​,24​​ for 0°,30°,45°,60°,90°0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°0°,30°,45°,60°,90°; cos is the same list backwards; tan = sin ÷ cos. tan⁡90°\tan 90°tan90°, sec⁡90°\sec 90°sec90°, cosec⁡0°\operatorname{cosec} 0°cosec0°, cot⁡0°\cot 0°cot0° are not defined. From 0° to 90°, sin and tan increase, cos decreases.

Finding an angle — ratio alone → table → solve for the letter. A bracket after a ratio is one angle: sin⁡(A+B)≠sin⁡A+sin⁡B\sin(A + B) \neq \sin A + \sin Bsin(A+B)=sinA+sinB, sin⁡2A≠2sin⁡A\sin 2A \neq 2\sin Asin2A=2sinA.

Identities — sin⁡2A+cos⁡2A=1\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1sin2A+cos2A=1; 1+tan⁡2A=sec⁡2A1 + \tan^2 A = \sec^2 A1+tan2A=sec2A; 1+cot⁡2A=cosec⁡2A1 + \cot^2 A = \operatorname{cosec}^2 A1+cot2A=cosec2A. They are about squares: sin⁡A+cos⁡A≠1\sin A + \cos A \neq 1sinA+cosA=1.

Proofs — one side only → one change per line → the five moves → "= RHS. Hence proved."

Not for the exam: complementary angles, sin⁡(90°−A)=cos⁡A\sin(90° - A) = \cos Asin(90°−A)=cosA (optional section at the end of Topic 4).
