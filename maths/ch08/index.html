Chapter 8 · Unit V

Introduction to Trigonometry

6 topicsAbout 8–9 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

How do surveyors find the height of a hill without climbing it, or engineers decide how steep a ramp or a railway track may be? They measure one angle and one length, and let ratios of the sides of a right triangle do the rest. Those ratios — sine, cosine, tangent and their partners — are the subject of this chapter.

You will learn what the six trigonometric ratios are and why each depends only on the angle, how to find all six from just one, the exact values for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, and the three identities that connect the ratios — including how to prove identities in a clear, step-by-step layout. In the board exam this chapter forms the Trigonometry unit together with Chapter 9 (12 marks in all); in both 2025-26 sample papers it supplies 1-mark MCQs (plus an assertion–reason item in the Standard paper), a 2-mark evaluation or angle question, and a 3-mark identity proof.

What you'll learn

  • Name the opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse from any acute angle; define all six ratios and the reciprocal pairs; explain why a ratio depends only on the angle; know that sin and cos never exceed 1.
  • Find a missing side, recognise common triples, rationalise surds and square fractions correctly. Take the 5-question skip-check first — score 5 out of 5 and go straight to Topic 3.
  • Draw the triangle from one given ratio, find the missing side and write the other five; evaluate expressions; judge true/false statements about ratios.
  • Derive and recall the value table; evaluate expressions; find an unknown angle (e.g. 2sin⁡5x=32\sin 5x = \sqrt{3}2sin5x=3​); describe how sin and cos change. (Includes an optional, not-for-exam section on complementary angles.)
  • Prove sin⁡2A+cos⁡2A=1\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1sin2A+cos2A=1, derive the other two identities, use them in rearranged forms, and write any ratio in terms of another.
  • Use five standard moves and a fixed layout to prove 1-, 2- and 3-move identities.
  • Recognise each question type in seconds with the "Which ratio, which method?" card, and solve mixed problems.
