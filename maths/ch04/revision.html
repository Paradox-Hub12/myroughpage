Quadratic equation — an equation that can be written as ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 with a≠0a \neq 0a=0 (the standard form). Read aaa, bbb, ccc with their signs, and only after every term is on the left. Always simplify before deciding whether an equation is quadratic: x2x^2x2 terms can cancel (then it is linear) and x3x^3x3 terms can cancel (then it may be quadratic).

Roots — values of xxx that make the equation true; the same as the zeroes of ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c. A quadratic has at most two roots. Check a root by substituting it.

Factorisation — standard form → split the middle term (two numbers with product acacac and sum bbb) → factorise → each factor =0= 0=0 → solve. The zero-product rule needs 0 on the right. Never divide by xxx: take it out as a common factor. A squared factor gives two equal roots.

Quadratic formula — x=−b±b2−4ac2ax = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}x=2a−b±b2−4ac​​, when b2−4ac≥0b^2 - 4ac \geq 0b2−4ac≥0. Work out DDD first; −b-b−b is the opposite of bbb; 2a2a2a divides the whole top; simplify surds.

Discriminant — D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac. D>0D > 0D>0: two distinct real roots. D=0D = 0D=0: two equal real roots x=−b2ax = -\dfrac{b}{2a}x=−2ab​. D<0D < 0D<0: no real roots. "Real roots" means D≥0D \geq 0D≥0.

kkk-questions — condition on DDD → solve for kkk (both signs!) → reject any kkk that makes a=0a = 0a=0.

Word problems — Let xxx be … → equation → solve → reject what doesn't fit, with a reason → answer in words with units. D<0D < 0D<0 means the situation is impossible.

Not for the exam: completing the square and the derivation of the formula (optional section at the end of Topic 4).
