Topic 5 of 6

The discriminant and nature of roots

About 55 minutes · 4 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Calculate the discriminant D = b² − 4ac of a quadratic equation.
  • State the nature of the roots from D (two distinct real roots, two equal real roots, no real roots).
  • Find an unknown k for equal (or real) roots, including checking that a ≠ 0.
  • Link D to the graph crossing, touching or missing the x-axis.

Before you open a lunch box you can often tell from its weight whether there is something inside. The discriminant is the weight test of a quadratic equation: one small calculation tells you, before solving, whether the equation has two roots, one repeated root, or none at all.

What the discriminant tells you

In the quadratic formula, everything depends on the number under the square root:

D=b2−4ac(the discriminant)D = b^2 - 4ac \qquad\text{(the discriminant)}D=b2−4ac(the discriminant)Swipe to see the whole equation →
Value of DDD D\sqrt{D}D​ Nature of the roots Graph of y=ax2+bx+cy = ax^2 + bx + cy=ax2+bx+c
D>0D > 0D>0 a real number, ≠0\neq 0=0 two distinct real roots: −b+D2a\dfrac{-b + \sqrt{D}}{2a}2a−b+D​​ and −b−D2a\dfrac{-b - \sqrt{D}}{2a}2a−b−D​​ crosses the x-axis at two points
D=0D = 0D=0 0 two equal real roots: x=−b2ax = -\dfrac{b}{2a}x=−2ab​, twice touches the x-axis at one point
D<0D < 0D<0 not a real number no real roots does not meet the x-axis
  • If D>0D > 0D>0 and is a perfect square (with whole-number aaa, bbb, ccc), the roots are rational and the equation can be factorised. If it is not a perfect square, the roots are irrational (surds).
  • "Real roots" (with no other word) means D≥0D \geq 0D≥0: distinct or equal. "Distinct real roots" means D>0D > 0D>0 only.
  • "No real roots" does not mean your working is wrong. In a word problem it can mean the situation is impossible (Topic 6).

Questions with an unknown kkk

Many board questions give an equation with an unknown, such as 3x2−kx+12=03x^2 - kx + 12 = 03x2−kx+12=0, and ask for the value of kkk that makes the roots equal (or real, or distinct). The method:

  1. Write the equation in standard form and read aaa, bbb, ccc — some of them contain kkk.
  2. Write the condition: equal roots → D=0D = 0D=0; real roots → D≥0D \geq 0D≥0; distinct real roots → D>0D > 0D>0; no real roots → D<0D < 0D<0.
  3. Substitute and solve for kkk. For k2=144k^2 = 144k2=144, remember both k=12k = 12k=12 and k=−12k = -12k=−12.
  4. Check a≠0a \neq 0a=0 for every value of kkk. If a value makes a=0a = 0a=0, the equation is not quadratic any more, so reject that value.
  5. If asked, find the roots: for equal roots, x=−b2ax = -\dfrac{b}{2a}x=−2ab​.

Worked example 1

D > 0

Question. Find the nature of the roots of 3x2−5x+1=03x^2 - 5x + 1 = 03x2−5x+1=0.

Given: a=3a = 3a=3, b=−5b = -5b=−5, c=1c = 1c=1. Asked: the nature of the roots.

Concept used: the sign of D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac. Why it applies: the nature of the roots is decided by DDD alone, without solving.

  1. 1

    Write the discriminant.

    D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4acSwipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Substitute, with the negative bbb in brackets.

    D=(−5)2−4(3)(1)D = (-5)^2 - 4(3)(1)D=(−5)2−4(3)(1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Work out each part.

    D=25−12D = 25 - 12D=25−12Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract.

    D=13D = 13D=13Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    13>013 > 013>0, and 13 is not a perfect square.

  6. Check: the roots are 5±136\dfrac{5 \pm \sqrt{13}}{6}65±13​​ — two different irrational numbers ✔.

Worked example 2

D = 0

Question. Find the nature of the roots of 4x2−20x+25=04x^2 - 20x + 25 = 04x2−20x+25=0. If they are real, find them.

Worked example 4

Find k for equal roots

Question. Find the values of kkk for which 3x2−kx+12=03x^2 - kx + 12 = 03x2−kx+12=0 has two equal roots. Find the roots in each case.

Given: a=3a = 3a=3, b=−kb = -kb=−k, c=12c = 12c=12. Asked: kkk, and the roots.

Concept used: equal roots ⇔ D=0D = 0D=0. Why it applies: the condition gives an equation in kkk.

  1. 1

    Write the condition for equal roots.

    b2−4ac=0b^2 - 4ac = 0b2−4ac=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Substitute b=−kb = -kb=−k.

    (−k)2−4(3)(12)=0(-k)^2 - 4(3)(12) = 0(−k)2−4(3)(12)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    (−k)2=k2(-k)^2 = k^2(−k)2=k2.

    k2−144=0k^2 - 144 = 0k2−144=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Add 144 to both sides.

    k2=144k^2 = 144k2=144Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Take the square root — both signs.

    k=12ork=−12k = 12 \quad\text{or}\quad k = -12k=12ork=−12Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Check a≠0a \neq 0a=0: a=3a = 3a=3 for every kkk ✔. Both values are valid.

  7. 7

    Roots for k=12k = 12k=12 (b=−12b = -12b=−12): x=−b2a=126=2x = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = \dfrac{12}{6} = 2x=−2ab​=612​=2.

  8. 8

    Roots for k=−12k = -12k=−12 (b=12b = 12b=12): x=−126=−2x = -\dfrac{12}{6} = -2x=−612​=−2.

    Common trap: Giving only k=12k = 12k=12. Every equation k2=k^2 =k2= (positive number) has two answers.

Worked example 5

Find k, and check a ≠ 0

Question. Find the value of kkk for which (k−3)x2+2(k−3)x+2=0(k - 3)x^2 + 2(k - 3)x + 2 = 0(k−3)x2+2(k−3)x+2=0 has two equal roots.

Given: a=k−3a = k - 3a=k−3, b=2(k−3)b = 2(k - 3)b=2(k−3), c=2c = 2c=2. Asked: kkk.

Concept used: equal roots ⇔ D=0D = 0D=0, and the equation must stay quadratic (a≠0a \neq 0a=0). Why it applies: here aaa itself contains kkk.

  1. 1

    Write the condition.

    b2−4ac=0b^2 - 4ac = 0b2−4ac=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Substitute.

    [2(k−3)]2−4(k−3)(2)=0[2(k - 3)]^2 - 4(k - 3)(2) = 0[2(k−3)]2−4(k−3)(2)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Square the bracket: [2(k−3)]2=4(k−3)2[2(k - 3)]^2 = 4(k - 3)^2[2(k−3)]2=4(k−3)2.

    4(k−3)2−8(k−3)=04(k - 3)^2 - 8(k - 3) = 04(k−3)2−8(k−3)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Take out the common factor 4(k−3)4(k - 3)4(k−3).

    4(k−3)[(k−3)−2]=04(k - 3)[(k - 3) - 2] = 04(k−3)[(k−3)−2]=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Simplify the square bracket.

    4(k−3)(k−5)=04(k - 3)(k - 5) = 04(k−3)(k−5)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Zero-product rule.

    k=3ork=5k = 3 \quad\text{or}\quad k = 5k=3ork=5Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Check a≠0a \neq 0a=0. For k=3k = 3k=3: a=3−3=0a = 3 - 3 = 0a=3−3=0, and the equation becomes 0x2+0x+2=00x^2 + 0x + 2 = 00x2+0x+2=0, i.e. 2=02 = 02=0 — not a quadratic (not even true). Reject k=3k = 3k=3. For k=5k = 5k=5: a=2≠0a = 2 \neq 0a=2=0 ✔.

  8. Check: k=5k = 5k=5 gives 2x2+4x+2=2(x+1)2=02x^2 + 4x + 2 = 2(x + 1)^2 = 02x2+4x+2=2(x+1)2=0, two equal roots x=−1,−1x = -1, -1x=−1,−1 ✔.

Worked example 6

For which k are the roots real?

Question. For what values of kkk does x2−6x+k=0x^2 - 6x + k = 0x2−6x+k=0 have real roots?

Given: a=1a = 1a=1, b=−6b = -6b=−6, c=kc = kc=k. Asked: the values of kkk.

Common mistakes!

  • Discriminant and kkk questions are one of the four most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • (−5)2=−25(-5)^2 = -25(−5)2=−25 in DDD

    A square is never negative.

    Put bbb in brackets: (−5)2=+25(-5)^2 = +25(−5)2=+25.

  • D=b2+4acD = b^2 + 4acD=b2+4ac, or forgetting the 4

    The formula is b2−4acb^2 - 4acb2−4ac, every time.

    Write D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac on its own line before substituting.

  • Finding DDD before writing standard form

    bbb and ccc may have the wrong signs.

    Standard form first, then aaa, bbb, ccc, then DDD.

  • k2=144⇒k=12k^2 = 144 \Rightarrow k = 12k2=144⇒k=12 only

    (−12)2=144(-12)^2 = 144(−12)2=144 too.

    Write k=±12k = \pm 12k=±12 and check both.

  • Keeping a kkk that makes a=0a = 0a=0

    The equation is then not quadratic.

    After solving for kkk, test a≠0a \neq 0a=0 for each value.

  • "Real roots" → D>0D > 0D>0

    Equal roots are real too.

    Real: D≥0D \geq 0D≥0. Distinct real: D>0D > 0D>0. Equal: D=0D = 0D=0. None: D<0D < 0D<0.

  • "Equal roots" → D>0D > 0D>0

    Equal roots happen when D=0\sqrt{D} = 0D​=0.

    Equal roots ⇔ D=0D = 0D=0.

Key points

  • Discriminant: D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac.
  • D>0D > 0D>0: two distinct real roots (graph crosses the x-axis twice). D=0D = 0D=0: two equal real roots, x=−b2ax = -\dfrac{b}{2a}x=−2ab​ (graph touches). D<0D < 0D<0: no real roots (graph misses).
  • Real roots ⇔ D≥0D \geq 0D≥0; distinct real roots ⇔ D>0D > 0D>0.
  • kkk-questions: standard form → condition on DDD → solve for kkk (both signs!) → reject any kkk that makes a=0a = 0a=0.
  • "No real roots" is a valid answer — it can mean a situation is impossible.
