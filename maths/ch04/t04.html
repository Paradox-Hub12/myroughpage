Topic 4 of 6

Solving with the quadratic formula

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Identify a, b and c correctly, with their signs, from any quadratic equation.
  • Use the quadratic formula step by step and simplify the roots, including surds.
  • Choose between factorisation and the quadratic formula.

Try to factorise x2−6x+4x^2 - 6x + 4x2−6x+4. You need two numbers with product 4 and sum −6-6−6. Whole numbers? −1-1−1 and −4-4−4 add to −5-5−5; −2-2−2 and −2-2−2 add to −4-4−4. Nothing works — because the roots are 3+53 + \sqrt{5}3+5​ and 3−53 - \sqrt{5}3−5​, which no one could guess. The quadratic formula finds roots like these every time, with no guessing at all.

The formula

For any quadratic equation ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 (with a≠0a \neq 0a=0), the roots are

x=−b±b2−4ac2ax = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}x=2a−b±b2−4ac​​Swipe to see the whole equation →

provided b2−4ac≥0b^2 - 4ac \geq 0b2−4ac≥0.

  • The ±\pm± means "do it twice": once with +++, once with −-−. That gives the two roots.
  • The expression under the square root, b2−4acb^2 - 4acb2−4ac, is called the discriminant, written DDD. Topic 5 is all about it.
  • The whole top, −b±D-b \pm \sqrt{D}−b±D​, is divided by 2a2a2a — not just the square root.
  • If D<0D < 0D<0 there is no real square root, so the equation has no real roots. Stop there.

You do not need to prove the formula for the board exam. If you are curious where it comes from, see the optional section at the end of this topic.

The method — six steps

  1. Standard form. Bring every term to the left so the right side is 0.
  2. Write aaa, bbb, ccc with their signs in a small table.
  3. Find D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac first, on its own line. Put negative numbers in brackets: (−7)2=49(-7)^2 = 49(−7)2=49.
  4. If D<0D < 0D<0: write "no real roots" and stop. Otherwise find D\sqrt{D}D​ (simplify it if it is not a perfect square).
  5. Substitute into x=−b±D2ax = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}x=2a−b±D​​. Remember: −b-b−b is the opposite of bbb. If b=−7b = -7b=−7, then −b=+7-b = +7−b=+7.
  6. Split into the +++ root and the −-− root, simplify each, and check by substituting.

Finding DDD first keeps the formula line short and tells you straight away what kind of answer to expect: when aaa, bbb and ccc are whole numbers, a perfect-square DDD gives rational roots and any other positive DDD gives surds.

Factorisation or formula?

Use factorisation when… Use the formula when…
the numbers are small and a split is easy to spot no split comes to mind within about 30 seconds
c=0c = 0c=0 (take xxx out) or b=0b = 0b=0 (x2=x^2 =x2= a number) DDD is not a perfect square (surd roots)
the question says "by factorisation" the question says "by the quadratic formula", or the coefficients are large

Both methods always give the same roots. If aaa, bbb, ccc are whole numbers and DDD is a perfect square, the equation can be factorised — but the formula may still be quicker.

Worked example 4

Question. Solve x2−6x+4=0x^2 - 6x + 4 = 0x2−6x+4=0. Give the roots in simplest surd form.

Common mistakes!

  • Wrong sign of bbb or ccc is one of the four most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter. Most of these errors disappear if you write the standard form and the aaa, bbb, ccc table before anything else.

  • Reading bbb and ccc before writing standard form (3x2+2=7x3x^2 + 2 = 7x3x2+2=7x → "b=7b = 7b=7")

    Terms change sign when they cross the equals sign.

    Rewrite as 3x2−7x+2=03x^2 - 7x + 2 = 03x2−7x+2=0 first; then b=−7b = -7b=−7, c=2c = 2c=2.

  • −b-b−b written as −7-7−7 when b=−7b = -7b=−7

    −b-b−b means "the opposite of bbb".

    If b=−7b = -7b=−7, then −b=+7-b = +7−b=+7.

  • (−4)2=−16(-4)^2 = -16(−4)2=−16

    A negative number squared is positive.

    Use brackets: (−4)2=16(-4)^2 = 16(−4)2=16. b2b^2b2 is never negative.

  • D=16−240D = 16 - 240D=16−240 when c=−15c = -15c=−15

    −4ac=−4(4)(−15)=+240-4ac = -4(4)(-15) = +240−4ac=−4(4)(−15)=+240.

    Write −4(a)(c)-4(a)(c)−4(a)(c) with brackets and multiply the signs carefully.

  • Dividing only D\sqrt{D}D​ by 2a2a2a, or dividing by aaa

    The fraction bar is under the whole top, and it is 2a2a2a.

    x=−b±D2ax = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}x=2a−b±D​​: work out the top first, then divide by 2a2a2a.

  • 6±252=3±25\dfrac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = 3 \pm 2\sqrt{5}26±25​​=3±25​

    Every term on top must be divided.

    3±53 \pm \sqrt{5}3±5​.

Key points

  • Quadratic formula: x=−b±b2−4ac2ax = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}x=2a−b±b2−4ac​​, valid when b2−4ac≥0b^2 - 4ac \geq 0b2−4ac≥0.
  • Routine: standard form → aaa, bbb, ccc with signs → DDD first → D\sqrt{D}D​ → substitute → split ±\pm± → simplify → check.
  • −b-b−b is the opposite of bbb; (−b)2(-b)^2(−b)2 is positive; 2a2a2a divides the whole top.
  • DDD not a perfect square → surd roots; simplify D\sqrt{D}D​ and divide every term by 2a2a2a.
  • Factorise when a split is easy; otherwise use the formula. Both give the same roots.
