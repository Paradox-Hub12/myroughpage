By the end you can…
  • Expand brackets and use the identities (a ± b)² and (a + b)(a − b).

  • Simplify square roots (surds) such as √72 = 6√2.
Why this lesson exists

Most marks lost in this chapter are lost to Class 9 algebra — a wrong sign while expanding, a middle term split the wrong way, or a square root left unsimplified. This short lesson rebuilds those three skills. If you already have them, prove it below and go straight to Topic 3.

Work through this toolkit — it takes about 20 minutes. Then try the questions again.

A recipe card for 4 people says "2 cups rice + 3 cups water". For 4 times as many people you multiply both items by 4 — not just the first. Expanding brackets is the same idea: the number outside multiplies every term inside. Most algebra slips come from forgetting one term.

  1. Expanding brackets

Multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second, then collect like terms:

(x+3)(2x−5)=2x2−5x+6x−15=2x2+x−15(x + 3)(2x - 5) = 2x^2 - 5x + 6x - 15 = 2x^2 + x - 15(x+3)(2x−5)=2x2−5x+6x−15=2x2+x−15Swipe to see the whole equation →

Three identities save time:

Identity Example
(a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2(a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2 (x+4)2=x2+8x+16(x + 4)^2 = x^2 + 8x + 16(x+4)2=x2+8x+16
(a−b)2=a2−2ab+b2(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2(a−b)2=a2−2ab+b2 (3x−1)2=9x2−6x+1(3x - 1)^2 = 9x^2 - 6x + 1(3x−1)2=9x2−6x+1
(a+b)(a−b)=a2−b2(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2(a+b)(a−b)=a2−b2 (x+7)(x−7)=x2−49(x + 7)(x - 7) = x^2 - 49(x+7)(x−7)=x2−49

(a+b)2(a + b)^2(a+b)2 is not a2+b2a^2 + b^2a2+b2 — the middle term 2ab2ab2ab is the one students forget.

  1. Splitting the middle term

To factorise ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c:

  1. Multiply a×ca \times ca×c. Call it the product.
  2. Find two numbers ppp and qqq with p×q=acp \times q = acp×q=ac and p+q=bp + q = bp+q=b.
  3. Rewrite bxbxbx as px+qxpx + qxpx+qx.
  4. Group in pairs and take out the common factor from each pair.
  5. The two brackets must be identical; take that bracket out.

The sign rule tells you where to look before you start:

Sign of acacac Sign of bbb The two numbers are…
+ + both positive
+ − both negative
− + or − one positive, one negative; the bigger one takes the sign of bbb

  1. When aaa is negative or the coefficients are fractions

  • Negative aaa: take out −1-1−1 first. −x2+3x+10=−(x2−3x−10)-x^2 + 3x + 10 = -(x^2 - 3x - 10)−x2+3x+10=−(x2−3x−10), then factorise the bracket.
  • Fractions: in an equation, multiply every term by the LCM of the denominators to clear them. x2−56x+16=0x^2 - \dfrac{5}{6}x + \dfrac{1}{6} = 0x2−65​x+61​=0 becomes 6x2−5x+1=06x^2 - 5x + 1 = 06x2−5x+1=0 (multiply by 6).
  • Common factor: take it out first. 4x2+8x−12=4(x2+2x−3)4x^2 + 8x - 12 = 4(x^2 + 2x - 3)4x2+8x−12=4(x2+2x−3) — smaller numbers, fewer mistakes.

  1. Simplifying square roots (surds)

The rule mn=m×n\sqrt{mn} = \sqrt{m} \times \sqrt{n}mn​=m​×n​ lets you pull out perfect squares.

  1. Split the number into (largest perfect square) × (what is left): 72=36×272 = 36 \times 272=36×2.
  2. Take the square root of the perfect square: 72=36×2=62\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36} \times \sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}72​=36​×2​=62​.

Also useful: m×m=m\sqrt{m} \times \sqrt{m} = mm​×m​=m, and like surds add like terms: 35+25=553\sqrt{5} + 2\sqrt{5} = 5\sqrt{5}35​+25​=55​ (just as 3y+2y=5y3y + 2y = 5y3y+2y=5y). To simplify 6±252\dfrac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2}26±25​​, divide every term on top by 2: 3±53 \pm \sqrt{5}3±5​.

Common mistakes!

  • Splitting the middle term is one of the four most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • Using ccc as the product instead of a×ca \times ca×c

    For 2x2+11x+122x^2 + 11x + 122x2+11x+12 you need product 24, not 12.

    Always multiply a×ca \times ca×c first.

  • Right product, wrong signs

    +10+10+10 and −9-9−9 give −90-90−90, but their sum is +1+1+1, not −1-1−1.

    Check both conditions: product =ac= ac=ac and sum =b= b=b. Use the sign rule.

  • Brackets that don't match after grouping

    3x(2x−1)−1(2x+1)3x(2x - 1) - 1(2x + 1)3x(2x−1)−1(2x+1) cannot be factorised further.

    When you take out a negative, flip every sign inside; the two brackets must be identical.

  • (a+b)2=a2+b2(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2(a+b)2=a2+b2

    The middle term 2ab2ab2ab is missing.

    (a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2(a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2.

  • Not taking out a common factor first

    4x2+8x−124x^2 + 8x - 124x2+8x−12 is harder than 4(x2+2x−3)4(x^2 + 2x - 3)4(x2+2x−3).

    Look for a common factor before splitting.

  • 6+252=3+25\dfrac{6 + 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = 3 + 2\sqrt{5}26+25​​=3+25​

    The denominator divides every term on top.

    Divide each term: 3+53 + \sqrt{5}3+5​.

  • Splitting the middle term: find ppp, qqq with pq=acpq = acpq=ac and p+q=bp + q = bp+q=b; group; the brackets must match.
  • Use the sign rule: acacac negative → opposite signs, the bigger takes the sign of bbb.
  • Negative aaa → take out −1-1−1. Fractions in an equation → multiply through by the LCM.
  • Surds: pull out the largest perfect square, 72=62\sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2}72​=62​; divide every term on top by the denominator.
