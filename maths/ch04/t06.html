Topic 6 of 6

Word problems

About 75 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Model day-to-day situations as quadratic equations (numbers, ages, area, right triangles, speed–distance–time, cost and production).
  • Solve the equation and reject roots that do not fit the situation, giving the reason.
  • Use the discriminant to decide whether a situation is possible.

A quadratic equation always offers you two answers. Real life usually wants one. A garden path cannot be 22 m wide if the garden is only 20 m across; nobody is −32-32−32 years old. The real skill in word problems is not the algebra — it is writing the right equation and then asking, "Which answer actually makes sense?"

The four-step routine

Use it for every word problem, and write each step in your answer — examiners give marks for each one.

  1. Let xxx be … — say exactly what xxx stands for, with units ("Let the speed of the bus be xxx km/h"). Write every other unknown in terms of xxx.
  2. Write the equation — translate the key sentence into algebra, then bring it to standard form.
  3. Solve — factorise or use the formula.
  4. Reject what doesn't fit — with a reason, then answer the question in words, with units.

When to reject a root

Reject a value if it makes… Example
a length, area, speed, time or age negative width =−19= -19=−19 m
a count (people, articles, toys) negative or not a whole number −1211-\dfrac{12}{11}−1112​ articles
another quantity in the problem negative or impossible path 22 m wide in a 20 m park → lawn width 20−44<020 - 44 < 020−44<0

But don't reject too much. If the question just says "numbers" (not "natural numbers" or "positive integers"), a negative root can be perfectly valid — then both answers count.

Two quick reminders

  • Pythagoras' theorem: in a right triangle, (hypotenuse)² = (base)² + (perpendicular)². An angle in a semicircle is a right angle, so a point on a circle joined to the ends of a diameter forms a right triangle.
  • Speed, distance, time: time=distancespeed\text{time} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}time=speeddistance​. Make a small table with one row for each journey; write each time as a fraction; "takes 1 hour less" means (slower time) − (faster time) =1= 1=1.

When the equation has no real roots

If D<0D < 0D<0, no real number fits the conditions, so the situation is impossible. That is a complete answer — say so clearly, with the value of DDD as your reason.

Numbers (keep both answers)

Given: difference 5, product 204. Asked: the two numbers.

Concept used: the four-step routine. Why it applies: the product of two unknowns gives a quadratic.

  1. 1

  2. 2

    Equation: their product is 204.

  3. 3

    Expand the left side.

  4. 4

    Subtract 204 from both sides.

    x2+5x−204=0x^2 + 5x - 204 = 0x2+5x−204=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Solve by the formula: a=1a = 1a=1, b=5b = 5b=5, c=−204c = -204c=−204.

  6. 6

    841=29\sqrt{841} = 29841​=29.

    x=−5±292x = \dfrac{-5 \pm 29}{2}x=2−5±29​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    The two roots.

    x=242=12orx=−342=−17x = \dfrac{24}{2} = 12 \quad\text{or}\quad x = \dfrac{-34}{2} = -17x=224​=12orx=2−34​=−17Swipe to see the whole equation →

Worked example 2

Ages

Question. Ananya's uncle is 30 years older than her. Two years ago, the product of their ages was 136. Find their present ages.

Given: age gap 30 years; product of ages 2 years ago = 136. Asked: present ages.

Concept used: four-step routine; ages two years ago are each 2 less. Why it applies: a product of two unknown ages.

  1. 1

    Let Ananya's present age be xxx years. Then her uncle's present age is (x+30)(x + 30)(x+30) years.

  2. 2

    Two years ago their ages were (x−2)(x - 2)(x−2) and (x+28)(x + 28)(x+28) years.

  3. 3

    Equation:

    (x−2)(x+28)=136(x - 2)(x + 28) = 136(x−2)(x+28)=136Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Expand.

    x2+28x−2x−56=136x^2 + 28x - 2x - 56 = 136x2+28x−2x−56=136Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Collect like terms.

    x2+26x−56=136x^2 + 26x - 56 = 136x2+26x−56=136Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Subtract 136 from both sides.

    x2+26x−192=0x^2 + 26x - 192 = 0x2+26x−192=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Solve: product −192-192−192, sum 262626 → 323232 and −6-6−6.

    x2+32x−6x−192=0x^2 + 32x - 6x - 192 = 0x2+32x−6x−192=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Group.

    x(x+32)−6(x+32)=0x(x + 32) - 6(x + 32) = 0x(x+32)−6(x+32)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    Factorise.

    (x−6)(x+32)=0(x - 6)(x + 32) = 0(x−6)(x+32)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  10. 10

    So x=6x = 6x=6 or x=−32x = -32x=−32.

  11. 11

    Reject x=−32x = -32x=−32: an age cannot be negative. Also check that x=6x = 6x=6 makes sense two years ago: 6−2=4>06 - 2 = 4 > 06−2=4>0 ✔.

  12. Check: two years ago: 4×34=1364 \times 34 = 1364×34=136 ✔.

  13. Answer Ananya is 6 years old and her uncle is 36 years old.

    Common trap: Writing the ages two years ago as (x−2)(x - 2)(x−2) and (x+30)(x + 30)(x+30). Both people were 2 years younger: (x+30)−2=x+28(x + 30) - 2 = x + 28(x+30)−2=x+28.

Worked example 3

Area with a path (reject a positive root)

Question. A rectangular park is 30 m long and 20 m wide. A path of uniform width runs along the inside of its four edges. The lawn left in the middle has an area of 336 m². Find the width of the path.

Given: park 30 m × 20 m; lawn area 336 m². Asked: width of the path.

Concept used: the lawn is a smaller rectangle; four-step routine. Why it applies: both lawn dimensions depend on the unknown width.

  1. 1

    Let the width of the path be xxx m. The path takes xxx m from each side, so the lawn is (30−2x)(30 - 2x)(30−2x) m long and (20−2x)(20 - 2x)(20−2x) m wide.

  2. 2

    Equation:

    (30−2x)(20−2x)=336(30 - 2x)(20 - 2x) = 336(30−2x)(20−2x)=336Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Expand.

    600−60x−40x+4x2=336600 - 60x - 40x + 4x^2 = 336600−60x−40x+4x2=336Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Rearrange into standard form.

    4x2−100x+264=04x^2 - 100x + 264 = 04x2−100x+264=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Divide every term by 4.

    x2−25x+66=0x^2 - 25x + 66 = 0x2−25x+66=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Solve: product 66, sum −25-25−25 → −22-22−22 and −3-3−3.

    (x−3)(x−22)=0(x - 3)(x - 22) = 0(x−3)(x−22)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    So x=3x = 3x=3 or x=22x = 22x=22.

  8. 8

    Reject x=22x = 22x=22: the lawn's width would be 20−2(22)=−2420 - 2(22) = -2420−2(22)=−24 m, which is impossible. (Both roots are positive, so you must test them in the situation.)

  9. Check: lawn =(30−6)×(20−6)=24×14=336= (30 - 6) \times (20 - 6) = 24 \times 14 = 336=(30−6)×(20−6)=24×14=336 m² ✔.

  10. Answer The path is 3 m wide.

    Common trap: Using (30−x)(20−x)(30 - x)(20 - x)(30−x)(20−x). A path along all four edges takes the width off both ends of each side.

Worked example 4

Right triangle in a circle

Question. A lamp post is to be put up on the boundary of a circular garden of diameter 25 m, so that the difference between its distances from two gates P and Q, at the two ends of a diameter, is 17 m. Is this possible? If so, how far from each gate should it be?

Given: diameter PQ = 25 m; difference of distances = 17 m. Asked: whether possible, and the distances.

Concept used: angle in a semicircle is 90°, Pythagoras' theorem, and DDD to test possibility. Why it applies: the lamp post L, P and Q form a right triangle with hypotenuse PQ.

  1. 1

    Let the distance from gate Q be xxx m (the shorter one). Then the distance from gate P is (x+17)(x + 17)(x+17) m.

  2. 2

    ∠PLQ=90°\angle PLQ = 90°∠PLQ=90° (angle in a semicircle), so by Pythagoras' theorem:

    x2+(x+17)2=252x^2 + (x + 17)^2 = 25^2x2+(x+17)2=252Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Expand (x+17)2(x + 17)^2(x+17)2.

    x2+x2+34x+289=625x^2 + x^2 + 34x + 289 = 625x2+x2+34x+289=625Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Collect like terms.

    2x2+34x+289=6252x^2 + 34x + 289 = 6252x2+34x+289=625Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Subtract 625 from both sides.

    2x2+34x−336=02x^2 + 34x - 336 = 02x2+34x−336=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Divide by 2.

    x2+17x−168=0x^2 + 17x - 168 = 0x2+17x−168=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Possible? D=172−4(1)(−168)=289+672=961>0D = 17^2 - 4(1)(-168) = 289 + 672 = 961 > 0D=172−4(1)(−168)=289+672=961>0, so real roots exist.

  8. 8

    Solve: 961=31\sqrt{961} = 31961​=31.

    x=−17±312x = \dfrac{-17 \pm 31}{2}x=2−17±31​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    So x=7x = 7x=7 or x=−24x = -24x=−24.

  10. 10

    Reject x=−24x = -24x=−24: a distance cannot be negative.

  11. Check: 72+242=49+576=625=2527^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25^272+242=49+576=625=252 ✔, and 24−7=1724 - 7 = 1724−7=17 ✔.

  12. Answer Yes, it is possible. The lamp post should be 7 m from gate Q and 24 m from gate P.

    Common trap: Forgetting why the triangle is right-angled. Write "angle in a semicircle is 90°" — it is the reason that earns the Pythagoras step.

Worked example 5

Speed, distance and time

Question. An express bus covers 240 km at a uniform speed. If its speed were 20 km/h more, it would take 1 hour less for the same journey. Find its usual speed.

Given: distance 240 km; extra speed 20 km/h; time saved 1 h. Asked: usual speed.

Concept used: time=distancespeed\text{time} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}time=speeddistance​, four-step routine. Why it applies: the two times differ by 1 hour.

  1. 1

    Let the usual speed be xxx km/h. The faster speed is (x+20)(x + 20)(x+20) km/h.

  2. 2

    Times: usual =240x= \dfrac{240}{x}=x240​ h; faster =240x+20= \dfrac{240}{x + 20}=x+20240​ h. The usual time is longer by 1 h.

    240x−240x+20=1\dfrac{240}{x} - \dfrac{240}{x + 20} = 1x240​−x+20240​=1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Combine the fractions over the common denominator x(x+20)x(x + 20)x(x+20).

    240(x+20)−240xx(x+20)=1\dfrac{240(x + 20) - 240x}{x(x + 20)} = 1x(x+20)240(x+20)−240x​=1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Simplify the top: 240x+4800−240x=4800240x + 4800 - 240x = 4800240x+4800−240x=4800.

    4800x(x+20)=1\dfrac{4800}{x(x + 20)} = 1x(x+20)4800​=1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Multiply both sides by x(x+20)x(x + 20)x(x+20).

    4800=x2+20x4800 = x^2 + 20x4800=x2+20xSwipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Standard form.

    x2+20x−4800=0x^2 + 20x - 4800 = 0x2+20x−4800=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Solve: product −4800-4800−4800, sum 20 → 808080 and −60-60−60.

    (x+80)(x−60)=0(x + 80)(x - 60) = 0(x+80)(x−60)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    So x=60x = 60x=60 or x=−80x = -80x=−80.

  9. 9

    Reject x=−80x = -80x=−80: a speed cannot be negative.

  10. Check: at 60 km/h the trip takes 24060=4\dfrac{240}{60} = 460240​=4 h; at 80 km/h it takes 24080=3\dfrac{240}{80} = 380240​=3 h. Difference 1 h ✔.

  11. Answer The usual speed of the bus is 60 km/h.

    Common trap: Writing time as x240\dfrac{x}{240}240x​ (speed ÷ distance), or subtracting the times the wrong way round. The slower journey takes longer, so (slower time) − (faster time) = 1.

Worked example 6

Cost and quantity

Question. A school spent ₹600 on notebooks to give as prizes. If each notebook had cost ₹5 less, the school could have bought 10 more notebooks for the same ₹600. How many notebooks did it buy, and at what price each?

Given: total ₹600; ₹5 cheaper → 10 more notebooks. Asked: the number and the price.

  1. 1

  3. 3

    600(x+10)−600xx(x+10)=5\dfrac{600(x + 10) - 600x}{x(x + 10)} = 5x(x+10)600(x+10)−600x​=5Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

  5. 5

Worked example 7

When the situation is impossible

Question. Is it possible to design a rectangular hall with a perimeter of 40 m and an area of 120 m²? Justify your answer.

Given: perimeter 40 m, area 120 m². Asked: whether such a rectangle exists.

Concept used: the discriminant decides whether real roots exist. Why it applies: if no real length works, no such hall exists.

  1. 1

    Let the length be xxx m. Perimeter 40 m means length + breadth =20= 20=20 m, so the breadth is (20−x)(20 - x)(20−x) m.

  2. 2

    Equation: area = 120.

    x(20−x)=120x(20 - x) = 120x(20−x)=120Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Expand.

    20x−x2=12020x - x^2 = 12020x−x2=120Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Standard form (move everything to the right, or multiply by −1-1−1).

    x2−20x+120=0x^2 - 20x + 120 = 0x2−20x+120=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Find DDD.

    D=(−20)2−4(1)(120)=400−480=−80D = (-20)^2 - 4(1)(120) = 400 - 480 = -80D=(−20)2−4(1)(120)=400−480=−80Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    D<0D < 0D<0, so there are no real roots: no real length satisfies both conditions.

  7. Check: the largest rectangle with perimeter 40 m is the 10 m × 10 m square, with area 100 m². An area of 120 m² is more than the maximum, so it cannot happen ✔.

  8. Answer No, it is not possible, because the equation x2−20x+120=0x^2 - 20x + 120 = 0x2−20x+120=0 has D=−80<0D = -80 < 0D=−80<0, so it has no real roots.

    Common trap: Thinking you have made a mistake when D<0D < 0D<0 and changing a sign to "fix" it. In a "Is it possible?" question, D<0D < 0D<0 is a perfectly good answer.

Common mistakes!

  • Word problems are one of the four most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter — usually in setting up, and in choosing which root to keep.

  • Not saying what xxx is

    The examiner cannot follow your equation; you lose the first mark.

    Always start: "Let xxx be … (units)".

  • time=speeddistance\text{time} = \dfrac{\text{speed}}{\text{distance}}time=distancespeed​

    Time is distance divided by speed.

    Use a table: time =distancespeed= \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}=speeddistance​.

  • Subtracting times the wrong way round

    The slower journey takes longer.

    (slower time) − (faster time) = the time saved.

  • Mixing minutes and hours

    30 minutes is 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ h, not 30.

    Convert everything to the same unit first.

  • Keeping a root that makes no sense (negative length, path wider than the park, fraction of a person)

    The situation rules it out.

    Test each root in the situation and give the reason for rejecting.

  • Rejecting a valid negative "number"

    The question may allow negatives.

    Reject only when the context or the question ("positive", "natural") says so.

  • Answering xxx when the question asks for something else

    xxx may be the width when the question wants the length, or the number when it wants the price.

    Re-read the question; answer every part in words, with units.

Key points

  • Routine: Let xxx be … → equation → solve → reject what doesn't fit (with a reason) → answer in words with units.
  • Reject roots that make any quantity in the situation negative or impossible — and keep valid negative numbers.
  • Pythagoras: (hypotenuse)² = (base)² + (perpendicular)²; an angle in a semicircle is 90°.
  • Speed–time: time =distancespeed= \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}=speeddistance​; (slower time) − (faster time) = time saved; convert minutes to hours.
  • D<0D < 0D<0 means the situation is impossible — a complete, correct answer.
