Chapter 4 · Unit II

Quadratic Equations

6 topicsAbout 7–8 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

A family plans a kitchen garden of a fixed area. A bus driver wants to know how much faster to drive to save an hour. A school needs a path of the right width around its lawn. Each time, two unknown quantities get multiplied together — and the result is an equation with an x2x^2x2 in it, a quadratic equation.

This chapter teaches you to recognise these equations, to solve them in two reliable ways (factorisation and the quadratic formula), to predict what kind of answer you will get before solving (the discriminant), and to turn everyday situations into equations and pick the answer that actually makes sense. In the board exam this chapter is part of the Algebra unit (20 marks shared with Polynomials, Linear Equations and Arithmetic Progressions), and its word problems appear as 5-mark questions in both 2025-26 sample papers.
