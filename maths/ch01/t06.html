Proving sums and products are irrational

About 55 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Prove numbers like 3 + 2√5, 7√5, 1/√2, 6 + √2 and √2 − √5 irrational by contradiction.
  • Use the facts that rational ± irrational, (non-zero rational) × irrational and irrational ÷ (non-zero rational) are irrational.
  • Recognise that irrational + irrational may be rational (counter-example).

You now know that 5\sqrt{5}5​ is irrational. What about 3+253 + 2\sqrt{5}3+25​? It feels obvious that adding 3 and doubling cannot make 5\sqrt{5}5​ "tidy". But feelings are not proofs — and some combinations of irrational numbers really do come out tidy: (5+2)+(3−2)=8(5 + \sqrt{2}) + (3 - \sqrt{2}) = 8(5+2​)+(3−2​)=8. In this topic you will learn a short, reliable way to prove the first kind of statement, and learn to spot the second kind.

Rational numbers stay rational

Quick reminder

Add, subtract or multiply two rational numbers and the answer is rational: 12+23=76\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{7}{6}21​+32​=67​. Divide by a non-zero rational and the answer is still rational. Fractions of integers combine into fractions of integers.

This is the fact you use at the key moment of every proof in this topic. If a and b are integers (with b≠0b \neq 0b=0), then expressions such as a−5b3b\dfrac{a - 5b}{3b}3ba−5b​ or a4b\dfrac{a}{4b}4ba​ are rational — they are made from integers by +++, −-−, ×\times×, ÷\div÷ only.

Four facts about mixing rational and irrational numbers

If r is rational and s is irrational, then:

  1. r+sr + sr+s and r−sr - sr−s are irrational;
  2. r×sr \times sr×s is irrational, provided r≠0r \neq 0r=0;
  3. sr\dfrac{s}{r}rs​ is irrational, provided r≠0r \neq 0r=0;
  4. rs\dfrac{r}{s}sr​ is irrational, provided r≠0r \neq 0r=0.

Why the first is true: suppose r+sr + sr+s were rational, say t. Then s=t−rs = t - rs=t−r, a rational minus a rational — rational. But s is irrational. Contradiction. The others work the same way. The condition r≠0r \neq 0r=0 matters: 0×2=00 \times \sqrt{2} = 00×2​=0 is rational.

The method: isolate the root

To prove that a number like 5+325 + 3\sqrt{2}5+32​ is irrational:

  1. Assume it is rational: 5+32=ab5 + 3\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{a}{b}5+32​=ba​, with a, b integers, b≠0b \neq 0b=0.
  2. Isolate the root — rearrange until 2\sqrt{2}2​ stands alone on one side.
  3. Say why the other side is rational: "since a and b are integers, a−5b3b\dfrac{a - 5b}{3b}3ba−5b​ is rational."
  4. Contradiction: this says 2\sqrt{2}2​ is rational — but 2\sqrt{2}2​ is irrational.
  5. Conclude: so 5+325 + 3\sqrt{2}5+32​ is irrational.

This proof is shorter than the one in Topic 5, because the hard work — showing 2\sqrt{2}2​ itself is irrational — is already done. You may write "a and b co-prime" here too; it does no harm, but this proof does not need it.

What you are allowed to use. The step "but 5\sqrt{5}5​ is irrational" needs that fact to be known. Board questions often supply it: "Given that 5\sqrt{5}5​ is irrational, prove …". If a question does not say "given", you may still quote it as a known result: write "5\sqrt{5}5​ is irrational (the square root of a prime number is irrational)". NCERT's examples and the CBSE marking scheme do exactly this. Prove p\sqrt{p}p​ first only when the question asks for it, e.g. "Prove that 7\sqrt{7}7​ is irrational. Hence prove …". What you may never do is assume the thing you are proving: "3+253 + 2\sqrt{5}3+25​ is irrational because 5\sqrt{5}5​ is irrational" is a statement, not a proof.

Two irrationals together: anything can happen

The four facts need one rational number. When both numbers are irrational, there is no rule:

  • (5+2)+(3−2)=8(5 + \sqrt{2}) + (3 - \sqrt{2}) = 8(5+2​)+(3−2​)=8 — irrational + irrational = rational;
  • 3×12=36=6\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{12} = \sqrt{36} = 63​×12​=36​=6 — irrational × irrational = rational;
  • 3+7\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}3​+7​ — irrational + irrational = irrational (Worked example 4 proves it with the squaring method below).

So never write "irrational + irrational is irrational" as a reason.

When there are two roots: square once

For a number such as 3+7\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}3​+7​, isolating does not remove both roots at once. Assume it equals a rational r, move one root across, and square:

Quick reminder (squaring)

(ab)2=a2b(a\sqrt{b})^2 = a^2 b(ab​)2=a2b, so (25)2=4×5=20(2\sqrt{5})^2 = 4 \times 5 = 20(25​)2=4×5=20 (not 10). And (x−y)2=x2−2xy+y(x - \sqrt{y})^2 = x^2 - 2x\sqrt{y} + y(x−y​)2=x2−2xy​+y.

After squaring, only one root is left, and you can isolate it as before. Worked example 4 shows the layout. This kind of question appears in the Standard paper and in the NCERT Exemplar.

How the marks are given

In the 2026-27 sample-paper marking schemes, a 3-mark "prove that … is irrational" answer is marked in three parts. About 1 mark is for the assumption and the line that isolates the root (such as 3=a−2b3b\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{a - 2b}{3b}3​=3ba−2b​). About 1 mark is for the reason: "since a and b are integers, a−2b3b\dfrac{a - 2b}{3b}3ba−2b​ is rational". The last 1 mark is for the contradiction ("but 3\sqrt{3}3​ is irrational") and the conclusion. The algebra is short; the marks are in the reasons. You can see the full marking schemes in the sample-paper section of this chapter.

Worked example 1

A sum: 5+325 + 3\sqrt{2}5+32​

Question. Given that 2\sqrt{2}2​ is irrational, prove that 5+325 + 3\sqrt{2}5+32​ is irrational.

Given: 2\sqrt{2}2​ is irrational. To prove: 5+325 + 3\sqrt{2}5+32​ is irrational.

Concept used: contradiction; isolate the root. Why it applies: if 5+325 + 3\sqrt{2}5+32​ were rational, 2\sqrt{2}2​ would be rational too.

  1. 1

    Assume the opposite. Suppose 5+325 + 3\sqrt{2}5+32​ is rational. Then there are integers a and b, b≠0b \neq 0b=0, with

    5+32=ab5 + 3\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{a}{b}5+32​=ba​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Subtract 5 from both sides.

    32=ab−53\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{a}{b} - 532​=ba​−5Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Write the right side as one fraction.

    32=a−5bb3\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{a - 5b}{b}32​=ba−5b​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Divide both sides by 3.

    2=a−5b3b\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{a - 5b}{3b}2​=3ba−5b​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Why the right side is rational: a and b are integers, so a−5ba - 5ba−5b and 3b3b3b are integers, and 3b≠03b \neq 03b=0. So a−5b3b\dfrac{a - 5b}{3b}3ba−5b​ is rational.

  6. 6

    Contradiction. Step 4 then says 2\sqrt{2}2​ is rational — but we are given that 2\sqrt{2}2​ is irrational.

  7. 7

    Conclude. The assumption was false.

    Hence 5+325 + 3\sqrt{2}5+32​ is irrational.

    Common trap: In step 4, dividing only aaa by 3 and writing a3b−5\dfrac{a}{3b} - 53ba​−5. Divide the whole right side by 3.

Worked example 2

A product: 434\sqrt{3}43​

Question. Prove that 434\sqrt{3}43​ is irrational, using the fact that 3\sqrt{3}3​ is irrational.

Given: 3\sqrt{3}3​ is irrational. To prove: 434\sqrt{3}43​ is irrational.

Concept used: contradiction; (non-zero rational) × irrational. Why it applies: dividing by 4 would turn a rational 434\sqrt{3}43​ into a rational 3\sqrt{3}3​.

  1. 1

    Assume the opposite. Suppose 434\sqrt{3}43​ is rational:

    43=ab4\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{a}{b}43​=ba​Swipe to see the whole equation →

    where a and b are integers and b≠0b \neq 0b=0.

  2. 2

    Divide both sides by 4.

    3=a4b\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{a}{4b}3​=4ba​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Why the right side is rational: a and 4b4b4b are integers and 4b≠04b \neq 04b=0, so a4b\dfrac{a}{4b}4ba​ is rational.

  4. 4

    Contradiction. Then 3\sqrt{3}3​ would be rational — but 3\sqrt{3}3​ is irrational.

  5. 5

    Conclude. The assumption is false.

    Hence 434\sqrt{3}43​ is irrational.

    Common trap: Multiplying instead of dividing (3=4ab\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{4a}{b}3​=b4a​). To undo "× 4", divide by 4.

Worked example 3

A quotient: 27\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{7}}7​2​

Question. Given that 7\sqrt{7}7​ is irrational, prove that 27\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{7}}7​2​ is irrational.

Given: 7\sqrt{7}7​ is irrational. To prove: 27\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{7}}7​2​ is irrational.

Concept used: contradiction; rearrange to get 7\sqrt{7}7​ alone. Why it applies: turning the fraction upside down brings 7\sqrt{7}7​ to the top.

  1. 1

    Assume the opposite. Suppose 27\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{7}}7​2​ is rational:

    27=ab\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{7}} = \dfrac{a}{b}7​2​=ba​Swipe to see the whole equation →

    where a and b are integers and b≠0b \neq 0b=0. Also a≠0a \neq 0a=0, because 27\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{7}}7​2​ is not zero.

  2. 2

    Cross-multiply.

    2b=a72b = a\sqrt{7}2b=a7​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Divide both sides by a (allowed, since a≠0a \neq 0a=0).

    7=2ba\sqrt{7} = \dfrac{2b}{a}7​=a2b​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Why the right side is rational: 2b and a are integers and a≠0a \neq 0a=0, so 2ba\dfrac{2b}{a}a2b​ is rational.

  5. 5

    Contradiction. Then 7\sqrt{7}7​ would be rational — but it is irrational.

  6. 6

    Conclude. The assumption is false.

    Hence 27\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{7}}7​2​ is irrational.

    Optional alternative: rationalise first: 27=277\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{7}} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{7}}{7}7​2​=727​​, and then prove 277\dfrac{2}{7}\sqrt{7}72​7​ irrational as in Worked example 2.

    Common trap: Forgetting to say a≠0a \neq 0a=0. You divide by a in step 3, and dividing by zero is never allowed.

Worked example 4

Two roots: 3+7\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}3​+7​

Question. Prove that 3+7\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}3​+7​ is irrational. (You may use the fact that 3\sqrt{3}3​ is irrational.)

Given: 3\sqrt{3}3​ is irrational. To prove: 3+7\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}3​+7​ is irrational.

Concept used: contradiction; move one root across and square. Why it applies: squaring removes one of the two roots.

  1. 1

    Assume the opposite. Suppose 3+7\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}3​+7​ is rational; call it r. Then r is rational and r>0r > 0r>0.

    3+7=r\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7} = r3​+7​=rSwipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Subtract 3\sqrt{3}3​ from both sides.

    7=r−3\sqrt{7} = r - \sqrt{3}7​=r−3​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Square both sides.

    7=r2−2r3+37 = r^2 - 2r\sqrt{3} + 37=r2−2r3​+3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Move 2r32r\sqrt{3}2r3​ to the left and 7 to the right.

    2r3=r2+3−72r\sqrt{3} = r^2 + 3 - 72r3​=r2+3−7Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Simplify.

    2r3=r2−42r\sqrt{3} = r^2 - 42r3​=r2−4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Divide by 2r2r2r (allowed, since r>0r > 0r>0).

    3=r2−42r\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{r^2 - 4}{2r}3​=2rr2−4​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Why the right side is rational: r is rational, so r2−4r^2 - 4r2−4 and 2r2r2r are rational, and 2r≠02r \neq 02r=0. So r2−42r\dfrac{r^2 - 4}{2r}2rr2−4​ is rational.

  8. 8

    Contradiction. Then 3\sqrt{3}3​ would be rational — but it is irrational.

  9. 9

    Conclude. The assumption is false.

    Hence 3+7\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}3​+7​ is irrational.

    Common trap: Squaring the two roots separately: "(3+7)2=3+7=10(\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7})^2 = 3 + 7 = 10(3​+7​)2=3+7=10". The middle term 2212\sqrt{21}221​ is missing.

Worked example 5

Irrational + irrational can be rational

Question. Give examples to show that (a) the sum of two irrational numbers can be rational; (b) the product of two irrational numbers can be rational; (c) the sum of two irrational numbers can also be irrational.

Given: nothing. Asked: three examples.

Concept used: a counter-example disproves a general claim. Why it applies: the rules in this topic need one rational number; with two irrationals, either result can happen.

  1. 1

    (a)

    5+25 + \sqrt{2}5+2​ and 3−23 - \sqrt{2}3−2​ are both irrational (rational ± irrational).

  2. 2

    Add them: the 2\sqrt{2}2​ terms cancel.

    (5+2)+(3−2)=8(5 + \sqrt{2}) + (3 - \sqrt{2}) = 8(5+2​)+(3−2​)=8Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    8 is rational.

  4. 4

    (b)

    3\sqrt{3}3​ and 12\sqrt{12}12​ are both irrational (3 and 12 are not perfect squares).

  5. 5

    Multiply them.

    3×12=36=6\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{12} = \sqrt{36} = 63​×12​=36​=6Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    6 is rational.

  7. 7

    (c)

    3\sqrt{3}3​ and 7\sqrt{7}7​ are both irrational, and their sum 3+7\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}3​+7​ is irrational (Worked example 4).

  8. Check: (b) 12=23\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}12​=23​, so 3×23=2×3=6\sqrt{3} \times 2\sqrt{3} = 2 \times 3 = 63​×23​=2×3=6 ✔.

  9. Answer (a) (5+2)+(3−2)=8(5 + \sqrt{2}) + (3 - \sqrt{2}) = 8(5+2​)+(3−2​)=8 (b) 3×12=6\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{12} = 63​×12​=6 (c) 3+7\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}3​+7​ is irrational. So "irrational + irrational" (or "×") has no fixed answer.

    Common trap: Using "irrational + irrational = irrational" as a reason in a proof. It is false in general, so it earns no marks.

Quick check · 3 of 3True/False with justification1 mark

True or false? Justify. "The product of two irrational numbers is always irrational."

Answer: False.

Working: 2\sqrt{2}2​ and 8\sqrt{8}8​ are irrational, but 2×8=16=4\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{8} = \sqrt{16} = 42​×8​=16​=4, which is rational.

The rule "(non-zero rational) × irrational is irrational" needs one rational factor.

Common mistakes!

  • These continue the irrationality-proof mistakes of Topic 5 — one of the four most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • "3+253 + 2\sqrt{5}3+25​ is irrational because 5\sqrt{5}5​ is irrational" (no working)

    This assumes what is to be proved.

    Assume it equals ab\dfrac{a}{b}ba​, isolate 5\sqrt{5}5​, reach a contradiction.

  • Leaving out "since a and b are integers, … is rational"

    It is the reason the contradiction works — and it carries a mark.

    Always write that line.

  • Dividing by a (or r) without saying it is not zero

    Division by zero is not allowed.

    State a≠0a \neq 0a=0 (or r>0r > 0r>0) before dividing.

  • (25)2=10(2\sqrt{5})^2 = 10(25​)2=10

    Square both factors: 22×5=202^2 \times 5 = 2022×5=20.

    Write (25)2=4×5(2\sqrt{5})^2 = 4 \times 5(25​)2=4×5.

  • (3+7)2=3+7(\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7})^2 = 3 + 7(3​+7​)2=3+7

    The middle term is missing.

    (x+y)2=x2+2xy+y2(x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2(x+y)2=x2+2xy+y2.

  • "Irrational + irrational is irrational"

    False: (5+2)+(3−2)=8(5 + \sqrt{2}) + (3 - \sqrt{2}) = 8(5+2​)+(3−2​)=8.

    The rules need one rational number.

  • Sign slip when isolating: 11=a−7b2b\sqrt{11} = \dfrac{a - 7b}{2b}11​=2ba−7b​ from 7−211=ab7 - 2\sqrt{11} = \dfrac{a}{b}7−211​=ba​

    Dividing by −2-2−2 changes every sign.

    Check by substituting back.

Key points

  • Sums, differences, products and quotients (by non-zero numbers) of rationals are rational.
  • If r is rational and s irrational: r±sr \pm sr±s is irrational; rsrsrs, sr\dfrac{s}{r}rs​ and rs\dfrac{r}{s}sr​ are irrational when r≠0r \neq 0r=0.
  • Method: assume =ab= \dfrac{a}{b}=ba​ → isolate the root → "since a, b are integers, this is rational" → contradiction with the known irrational → conclude.
  • Use a fact like "5\sqrt{5}5​ is irrational" only if it is given or already proved; never assume what you are proving.
  • Two irrationals can give a rational: (5+2)+(3−2)=8(5 + \sqrt{2}) + (3 - \sqrt{2}) = 8(5+2​)+(3−2​)=8, 3×12=6\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{12} = 63​×12​=6.
  • With two roots, move one across and square once.
