Wrap-up

HCF or LCM? Rational or irrational?

About 35 minutes

Questions from this chapter come in a small number of familiar shapes. This page gives you a card for recognising each shape quickly, the proof template on one sheet, and five mixed problems that make you choose the method yourself.

The "Which method?" card

If the question… Do this Where you learnt it
says express as a product of primes Factor tree or repeated division → standard form (ascending primes, exponents) → multiply back to check Topic 2
asks whether knk^nkn can end in 0 (or 5) Factorise k. Ending in 0 needs both 2 and 5; raising to a power adds no new primes Topic 2
asks you to show a number is composite Take out a common factor: 7×9×11+9=9×787 \times 9 \times 11 + 9 = 9 \times 787×9×11+9=9×78 Topic 2
asks for HCF and LCM Prime-power table: HCF = smallest power of common primes; LCM = largest power of every prime Topic 3
gives the HCF (or LCM) of two numbers and asks for the other HCF × LCM = product — for two numbers only Topic 3
tells a story Cutting into the largest equal parts → HCF; waiting for cycles to line up → LCM; then answer the question asked Topic 4
has remainders Largest divisor: subtract remainders, then HCF. Smallest number: LCM + remainder (or LCM − k) Topic 4
says prove p\sqrt{p}p​ is irrational Seven-stage template: assume → ab\dfrac{a}{b}ba​ co-prime → square → p divides a → p divides b → contradiction → conclude Topic 5

Mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

From HCF and product to the numbers

Question. The HCF of two numbers is 16 and their product is 3 072. Find their LCM, and find all possible pairs of such numbers.

Given: HCF = 16, product = 3 072. Asked: the LCM and the possible pairs.

Concept used: HCF × LCM = product (two numbers); both numbers are multiples of the HCF. Why it applies: there are two numbers, and 16 divides each of them.

  1. 1

    LCM from the product rule.

    LCM=307216=192\text{LCM} = \dfrac{3072}{16} = 192LCM=163072​=192Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Write the numbers as 16x16x16x and 16y16y16y, where x and y are co-prime (otherwise the HCF would be bigger than 16).

    16x×16y=307216x \times 16y = 307216x×16y=3072Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Divide both sides by 256.

    xy=12xy = 12xy=12Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Co-prime pairs with product 12: (1, 12) and (3, 4). The pair (2, 6) is not co-prime.

  5. 5

    Multiply back by 16.

    (16,192)and(48,64)(16, 192) \quad \text{and} \quad (48, 64)(16,192)and(48,64)Swipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: HCF(48, 64) = 16 and LCM(48, 64) = 192 ✔; HCF(16, 192) = 16 and LCM = 192 ✔.

  7. Answer LCM = 192192192; the numbers are 16 and 192 or 48 and 64.

    Common trap: Including (32, 96) from xy=2×6xy = 2 \times 6xy=2×6. HCF(32, 96) = 32, not 16 — x and y must be co-prime.

Mixed example 2

Increased by a number

Question. Find the smallest number which, when increased by 13, is exactly divisible by 18, 24 and 32.

Given: divisors 18, 24, 32; the number + 13 is divisible by all. Asked: the number.

Concept used: LCM, then subtract. Why it applies: "number + 13" is the smallest common multiple of 18, 24 and 32.

  1. 1

    Factorise.

    18=2×32,24=23×3,32=2518 = 2 \times 3^2, \qquad 24 = 2^3 \times 3, \qquad 32 = 2^518=2×32,24=23×3,32=25Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    LCM: largest powers 252^525 and 323^232.

    LCM=25×32=288\text{LCM} = 2^5 \times 3^2 = 288LCM=25×32=288Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    The number + 13 = 288, so subtract 13.

    288−13=275288 - 13 = 275288−13=275Swipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: 275+13=288=18×16=24×12=32×9275 + 13 = 288 = 18 \times 16 = 24 \times 12 = 32 \times 9275+13=288=18×16=24×12=32×9 ✔.

  5. Answer 275275275

    Common trap: Adding 13 to the LCM (301). That is the pattern for a remainder of 13, not "increased by 13".

Mixed example 3

Ending in 0, with a twist

Question. (a) Can 42n42^n42n end with the digit 0 for any natural number n? (b) Can 42n×2542^n \times 2542n×25 end with the digit 0?

Given: two expressions. Asked: can either end in 0?

Concept used: a number ends in 0 exactly when its prime factorisation contains both 2 and 5. Why it applies: we can read the primes of each expression.

  1. 1

    (a)

    Factorise: 42=2×3×742 = 2 \times 3 \times 742=2×3×7.

    42n=2n×3n×7n42^n = 2^n \times 3^n \times 7^n42n=2n×3n×7nSwipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    There is a 2 but no 5, so 42n42^n42n never ends in 0.

  3. 3

    (b)

    Factorise: 25=5225 = 5^225=52.

    42n×25=2n×3n×52×7n42^n \times 25 = 2^n \times 3^n \times 5^2 \times 7^n42n×25=2n×3n×52×7nSwipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Now there is a 2 and a 5 (for every n≥1n \geq 1n≥1), so the number is divisible by 10: it always ends in 0.

  5. Check: n=1n = 1n=1: 42×25=105042 \times 25 = 105042×25=1050 ✔ ends in 0. 421=4242^1 = 42421=42, 422=176442^2 = 1764422=1764 — no 0 ✔.

  6. Answer (a) No, never. (b) Yes — for every natural number n.

Mixed example 4

A difference with a root

Question. Given that 11\sqrt{11}11​ is irrational, prove that 311−23\sqrt{11} - 2311​−2 is irrational.

Given: 11\sqrt{11}11​ is irrational. To prove: 311−23\sqrt{11} - 2311​−2 is irrational.

Concept used: contradiction; isolate the root (Topic 6). Why it applies: the number is a rational multiple of 11\sqrt{11}11​, shifted by a rational.

  1. 1

    Assume the opposite. Suppose 311−23\sqrt{11} - 2311​−2 is rational:

    311−2=ab3\sqrt{11} - 2 = \dfrac{a}{b}311​−2=ba​Swipe to see the whole equation →

    where a and b are integers and b≠0b \neq 0b=0.

  2. 2

    Add 2 to both sides.

    311=ab+2=a+2bb3\sqrt{11} = \dfrac{a}{b} + 2 = \dfrac{a + 2b}{b}311​=ba​+2=ba+2b​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Divide both sides by 3.

    11=a+2b3b\sqrt{11} = \dfrac{a + 2b}{3b}11​=3ba+2b​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Since a and b are integers and 3b≠03b \neq 03b=0, a+2b3b\dfrac{a + 2b}{3b}3ba+2b​ is rational. So 11\sqrt{11}11​ would be rational.

  5. 5

    Contradiction — 11\sqrt{11}11​ is irrational. So the assumption is false.

    Hence 311−23\sqrt{11} - 2311​−2 is irrational.

    Common trap: Subtracting 2 in step 2 (getting a−2ba - 2ba−2b). To undo "− 2", add 2.

Mixed example 5

Sum and HCF given

Question. The sum of two numbers is 288 and their HCF is 24. How many pairs of such numbers are possible? List them.

Given: sum 288, HCF 24. Asked: the possible pairs.

Concept used: both numbers are multiples of the HCF, 24x24x24x and 24y24y24y, with x and y co-prime. Why it applies: if x and y shared a factor, the HCF would be bigger than 24.

  1. 1

    Write the sum.

    24x+24y=28824x + 24y = 28824x+24y=288Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Divide by 24.

    x+y=12x + y = 12x+y=12Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    List pairs with x≤yx \leq yx≤y and x+y=12x + y = 12x+y=12: (1, 11), (2, 10), (3, 9), (4, 8), (5, 7), (6, 6).

  4. 4

    Keep only co-prime pairs: (1, 11) and (5, 7). The others share a factor (2, 3 or 6).

  5. 5

    Multiply back by 24.

    (24,264)and(120,168)(24, 264) \quad \text{and} \quad (120, 168)(24,264)and(120,168)Swipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: 24+264=28824 + 264 = 28824+264=288, HCF(24, 264) = 24 ✔; 120+168=288120 + 168 = 288120+168=288, HCF(120, 168) = 24 ✔.

  7. Answer 2 pairs: 24 and 264; 120 and 168.

    Common trap: Counting all six pairs from step 3. For (2, 10) the numbers would be 48 and 240, whose HCF is 48, not 24.
