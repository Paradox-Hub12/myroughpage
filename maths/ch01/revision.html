The whole chapter on one page

Primes — a prime has exactly two factors; 1 is neither prime nor composite; 2 is the only even prime. To test for a prime, divide by primes up to the square root.

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic — every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order of the factors. Write it in standard form: primes in ascending order, with exponents, e.g. 1260=22×32×5×71260 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5 \times 71260=22×32×5×7. Multiply back to check; every factor must be prime.

Ending in 0 — a number ends in 0 only if its prime factorisation contains both 2 and 5. knk^nkn has exactly the same primes as k, so 6n6^n6n, 14n14^n14n, 15n15^n15n, 25n25^n25n never end in 0, while 30n30^n30n always does.

Composite? — show a factor other than 1 and the number, usually by taking out a common factor: 7×9×11+9=9×787 \times 9 \times 11 + 9 = 9 \times 787×9×11+9=9×78.

HCF and LCM — factorise, then: HCF = smallest power of each common prime; LCM = largest power of every prime. Works the same for three numbers. HCF ≤ smallest number; LCM ≥ largest; HCF divides LCM.

Product rule — for two numbers only: HCF(a,b)×LCM(a,b)=a×b\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times bHCF(a,b)×LCM(a,b)=a×b. For three numbers it is not true in general (4, 6, 10: 2×60≠2402 \times 60 \neq 2402×60=240), so never use it.

Word problems — cutting into the largest equal parts → HCF; waiting for cycles to line up → LCM. Then answer the question asked (number of pieces, clock time, number of rooms).

Irrational numbers — cannot be written as pq\dfrac{p}{q}qp​; their decimals never end or repeat. If a prime p divides a2a^2a2, then p divides a (primes only: 4 divides 36 but not 6).

Proofs — p\sqrt{p}p​: assume ab\dfrac{a}{b}ba​ with a, b co-prime → p b2=a2p\,b^2 = a^2pb2=a2 → p divides a → p divides b → contradiction. r+spr + s\sqrt{p}r+sp​: assume ab\dfrac{a}{b}ba​ → isolate p\sqrt{p}p​ → "a, b integers, so rational" → contradiction.

Mixing — rational ± irrational is irrational; (non-zero rational) × or ÷ irrational is irrational; irrational + irrational can be rational: (5+2)+(3−2)=8(5 + \sqrt{2}) + (3 - \sqrt{2}) = 8(5+2​)+(3−2​)=8.

Not in this year's syllabus: Euclid's division algorithm and decimal expansions of rational numbers.
