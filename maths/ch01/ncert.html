Exercise 1.1, Q1 (i)140 as a product of primes1 mark

Express each number as a product of its prime factors:
(i) 140

Given: 140. Asked: its prime factorisation.

Concept used: repeated division by the smallest prime (Topic 2). Why it applies: 140 is composite (it is even).

  1. 1

    140 is even: divide by 2.

    140÷2=70140 \div 2 = 70140÷2=70Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    70 is even: divide by 2.

    70÷2=3570 \div 2 = 3570÷2=35Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    35 ends in 5: divide by 5.

    35÷5=735 \div 5 = 735÷5=7Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    7 is prime.

    140=2×2×5×7=22×5×7140 = 2 \times 2 \times 5 \times 7 = 2^2 \times 5 \times 7140=2×2×5×7=22×5×7Swipe to see the whole equation →

  5. Check: 4×5×7=1404 \times 5 \times 7 = 1404×5×7=140 ✔.

  6. Answer 140=22×5×7140 = 2^2 \times 5 \times 7140=22×5×7

    Common trap: Writing 140=4×5×7140 = 4 \times 5 \times 7140=4×5×7 — 4 is composite, so this is not a prime factorisation.

(i) 26 and 91

Given: 26 and 91. Asked: LCM, HCF and the verification.

Concept used: prime-power table; HCF × LCM = product for two numbers (Topic 3). Why it applies: the question asks for exactly these.

  1. 1

    Factorise.

    26=2×13,91=7×1326 = 2 \times 13, \qquad 91 = 7 \times 1326=2×13,91=7×13Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Common prime: 13.

    HCF=13\text{HCF} = 13HCF=13Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Largest power of every prime: 2, 7, 13.

    LCM=2×7×13=182\text{LCM} = 2 \times 7 \times 13 = 182LCM=2×7×13=182Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Product of the numbers.

    26×91=236626 \times 91 = 236626×91=2366Swipe to see the whole equation →

    Product of LCM and HCF.

    182×13=2366182 \times 13 = 2366182×13=2366Swipe to see the whole equation →

    The two products are equal, so LCM × HCF = product of the numbers — verified.

Exercise 1.1, Q5Can 6n6^n6n end with 0?2 marks

Check whether 6n6^n6n can end with the digit 0 for any natural number n.

Given: the numbers 6n6^n6n, n natural. Asked: can any of them end in 0?

Concept used: a number ends in 0 only if its prime factorisation contains both 2 and 5; uniqueness of prime factorisation (Topic 2). Why it applies: we can find every prime in 6n6^n6n.

  1. 1

    Suppose some 6n6^n6n ended in 0. Then it would be divisible by 10, so its prime factorisation would contain the prime 5.

  2. 2

    Factorise 6n6^n6n.

    6n=(2×3)n=2n×3n6^n = (2 \times 3)^n = 2^n \times 3^n6n=(2×3)n=2n×3nSwipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, this factorisation is unique, so the only primes in 6n6^n6n are 2 and 3. The prime 5 does not occur.

  4. 4

    So 6n6^n6n is not divisible by 5, and cannot end in 0.

  5. Check: 6,36,216,12966, 36, 216, 12966,36,216,1296 — every power of 6 ends in 6 ✔.

  6. Answer No. 6n=2n×3n6^n = 2^n \times 3^n6n=2n×3n has no factor 5, so there is no natural number n for which 6n6^n6n ends with the digit 0.

    Common trap: "6n6^n6n is even, so it can end in 0." Ending in 0 needs a factor 5 as well as a 2.

Given: Sonia's round: 18 min; Ravi's round: 12 min; same start. Asked: the first time both are back at the start.

Concept used: LCM (Topic 4). Why it applies: each is at the start only at multiples of their own round time; we want the first common multiple.

  1. 1

    Factorise.

    18=2×32,12=22×318 = 2 \times 3^2, \qquad 12 = 2^2 \times 318=2×32,12=22×3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    LCM: largest powers 222^222 and 323^232.

    LCM(18,12)=22×32=36\text{LCM}(18, 12) = 2^2 \times 3^2 = 36LCM(18,12)=22×32=36Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    They next meet at the starting point after 36 minutes.

  4. Check: in 36 minutes Sonia completes 36÷18=236 \div 18 = 236÷18=2 rounds and Ravi 36÷12=336 \div 12 = 336÷12=3 rounds — both whole numbers ✔. No smaller time works: multiples of 18 are 18, 36, …, and 18 is not a multiple of 12.

  5. Answer After 36 minutes36 \text{ minutes}36 minutes

    Common trap: Using the HCF (6 minutes). After 6 minutes neither has finished a round.
