Topic 3 of 6

HCF and LCM by prime factorisation

About 55 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find the HCF and LCM of two or three numbers from their prime factorisations.
  • Use HCF × LCM = product of two numbers, and know that it fails for three numbers.
  • Solve problems with unknown exponents or missing values (e.g. HCF given, find the LCM; find k).

Listing factors works for 12 and 18. It is hopeless for 1 050 and 1 386 — you would be writing lists for ten minutes. With prime factorisation, you can find the highest common factor and the lowest common multiple of those two numbers in about a minute, and be sure you are right. The trick is a small table.

What HCF and LCM mean

  • The HCF (highest common factor) of two or more numbers is the largest number that divides all of them exactly. HCF(12, 18) = 6.
  • The LCM (lowest common multiple) is the smallest positive number that all of them divide into exactly. LCM(12, 18) = 36.

So the HCF is never bigger than the smallest number, and the LCM is never smaller than the largest number. Keep this as a quick sense-check.

Reading HCF and LCM from the factorisations

Factorise each number, then put the powers of each prime in a table — one column per prime, one row per number:

222 333 777 111111
84=22×3×784 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 784=22×3×7 222^222 313^131 717^171 —
198=2×32×11198 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 11198=2×32×11 212^121 323^232 — 11111^1111
HCF: smallest power of each common prime 212^121 313^131 — —
LCM: largest power of every prime 222^222 323^232 717^171 11111^1111

So HCF(84,198)=2×3=6\text{HCF}(84, 198) = 2 \times 3 = 6HCF(84,198)=2×3=6 and LCM(84,198)=22×32×7×11=2772\text{LCM}(84, 198) = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7 \times 11 = 2772LCM(84,198)=22×32×7×11=2772.

The two rules

HCF = product of the smallest power of each prime that is common to all the numbers. LCM = product of the largest power of every prime that appears in any of the numbers.

Why the rules work

A factor of 84 can contain at most two 2s; a factor of 198 can contain at most one 2. A number that divides both can therefore contain only one 2 — the smaller power. A prime that is missing from one of the numbers (7 or 11 here) cannot appear in a common factor at all.

A multiple of 84 must contain at least 222^222, and a multiple of 198 must contain at least 323^232 and an 11. A common multiple needs all of these at once, so it must take the larger power of every prime. The lowest common multiple takes exactly those and nothing more.

Three numbers: the same table

For three numbers, add a third row. The HCF uses only primes found in all three rows; the LCM uses every prime in any row, at its highest power. Worked example 3 shows the layout.

HCF × LCM = product — for two numbers only

Multiply the HCF and LCM of 84 and 198: 6×2772=16 6326 \times 2772 = 16\,6326×2772=16632, and 84×198=16 63284 \times 198 = 16\,63284×198=16632. This always happens for two numbers:

HCF(a,b)×LCM(a,b)=a×b\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times bHCF(a,b)×LCM(a,b)=a×bSwipe to see the whole equation →

Why? For each prime, one number has the smaller power and the other has the larger. The HCF takes the smaller, the LCM takes the larger, so between them they use each power exactly once — just like a×ba \times ba×b. This gives a quick way to find the LCM when the HCF is known:

LCM(a,b)=a×bHCF(a,b)\text{LCM}(a, b) = \dfrac{a \times b}{\text{HCF}(a, b)}LCM(a,b)=HCF(a,b)a×b​Swipe to see the whole equation →

For three numbers it is not true in general. Take 4, 6 and 10: HCF = 2 and LCM = 60, so HCF × LCM = 120, but 4×6×10=2404 \times 6 \times 10 = 2404×6×10=240. With three numbers, the middle power of a prime is used by neither the HCF nor the LCM. So the two sides match only in the special case where no two of the numbers share a prime (for example 8, 9 and 25: 1×1800=18001 \times 1800 = 18001×1800=1800). You cannot rely on being in that special case, so never use the product rule to find an HCF or LCM of three numbers. Use the table.

Three more facts worth knowing

  • The HCF of two numbers always divides their LCM. (So numbers with HCF 12 and LCM 150 cannot exist — 12 does not divide 150.)
  • If two numbers have no common prime, their HCF is 1. They are called co-prime, and their LCM is simply their product.
  • If a power is unknown, compare the two sides prime by prime: for an LCM, the given power of each prime must equal the larger of the two powers; for an HCF, the smaller.

Setting out your answer

In the exam, write each factorisation on its own line, with the same primes one under the other, so the smallest and largest powers are easy to see. Then write two clear lines: "HCF = … = 18" and "LCM = … = 1584", each showing the powers you chose. If the question says verify, finish with both products worked out in full — "18×1584=28 51218 \times 1584 = 28\,51218×1584=28512 and 144×198=28 512144 \times 198 = 28\,512144×198=28512" — and the word "verified". Marking schemes usually give marks for the factorisations and for each correct result (in a 2-mark question, typically 1 mark for the factorisations and ½ for each result), so a neat layout protects marks even if one multiplication slips.

Worked example 1

HCF and LCM of two numbers

Question. Find the HCF and LCM of 144 and 198 by prime factorisation, and verify that HCF × LCM = product of the numbers.

Given: 144 and 198. Asked: HCF, LCM, and the check.

Concept used: the two rules (smallest power of common primes; largest power of every prime). Why it applies: both numbers are easy to factorise.

  1. 1

    Factorise 144 (144=16×9144 = 16 \times 9144=16×9).

    144=24×32144 = 2^4 \times 3^2144=24×32Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Factorise 198 (198=2×99=2×9×11198 = 2 \times 99 = 2 \times 9 \times 11198=2×99=2×9×11).

    198=2×32×11198 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 11198=2×32×11Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Common primes: 2 and 3. Smallest powers: 212^121 and 323^232.

    HCF=2×32=18\text{HCF} = 2 \times 3^2 = 18HCF=2×32=18Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    All primes: 2, 3, 11. Largest powers: 242^424, 323^232, 11111^1111.

    LCM=24×32×11=16×99=1584\text{LCM} = 2^4 \times 3^2 \times 11 = 16 \times 99 = 1584LCM=24×32×11=16×99=1584Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Product of the numbers.

    144×198=28 512144 \times 198 = 28\,512144×198=28512Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Product of HCF and LCM.

    18×1584=28 51218 \times 1584 = 28\,51218×1584=28512Swipe to see the whole equation →

  7. Check: the two products are equal ✔. Also HCF 18 ≤ 144 and LCM 1584 ≥ 198 ✔.

  8. Answer HCF=18,LCM=1584\text{HCF} = 18, \quad \text{LCM} = 1584HCF=18,LCM=1584; HCF × LCM = 28 512 = 144 × 198 ✔

    Common trap: Leaving 11 out of the LCM because "it is not common". Common primes are for the HCF; the LCM takes every prime.

Worked example 3

Three numbers

Question. Find the HCF and LCM of 48, 60 and 90.

Given: three numbers. Asked: HCF and LCM.

  3. 3

    Every prime: 2, 3, 5. Largest powers: 242^424 (from 48), 323^232 (from 90), 515^151.

    LCM=24×32×5\text{LCM} = 2^4 \times 3^2 \times 5LCM=24×32×5Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Multiply: 16×9=14416 \times 9 = 14416×9=144, 144×5=720144 \times 5 = 720144×5=720.

    LCM=720\text{LCM} = 720LCM=720Swipe to see the whole equation →

Worked example 4

Does HCF × LCM = product for three numbers?

Question. Riya claims that HCF(8,12,20)×LCM(8,12,20)=8×12×20\text{HCF}(8, 12, 20) \times \text{LCM}(8, 12, 20) = 8 \times 12 \times 20HCF(8,12,20)×LCM(8,12,20)=8×12×20. Test her claim.

Given: 8, 12, 20. Asked: is the claim true?

Concept used: find HCF and LCM by the table, then compare. Why it applies: a single counter-example is enough to show a claim is false.

  1. 1

    Factorise.

    Only 2 is in all three; smallest power 222^222.

    HCF=22=4\text{HCF} = 2^2 = 4HCF=22=4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Largest powers: 232^323, 313^131, 515^151.

    LCM=23×3×5=120\text{LCM} = 2^3 \times 3 \times 5 = 120LCM=23×3×5=120Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Left side.

    HCF×LCM=4×120=480\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM} = 4 \times 120 = 480HCF×LCM=4×120=480Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Right side.

    8×12×20=19208 \times 12 \times 20 = 19208×12×20=1920Swipe to see the whole equation →

Unknown exponent; find k

Common mistakes!

  • Using HCF × LCM = product for three numbers is one of the four most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter. It gives a wrong LCM (or HCF) with no warning sign.

  • LCM(a,b,c)=a×b×cHCF(a,b,c)\text{LCM}(a, b, c) = \dfrac{a \times b \times c}{\text{HCF}(a, b, c)}LCM(a,b,c)=HCF(a,b,c)a×b×c​

    The product rule holds only for two numbers (Worked example 4).

    For three numbers, use the prime-power table.

  • Largest power in the HCF, smallest in the LCM

    The rules are swapped.

    HCF → smallest power; LCM → Largest power.

  • Leaving a prime out of the LCM because it is "not common"

    The LCM must be divisible by every number.

    LCM: every prime that appears anywhere.

  • Putting a non-common prime into the HCF

    A prime missing from one number cannot divide it.

    HCF: only primes found in all the numbers.

  • HCF bigger than the smallest number, or LCM smaller than the largest

    Impossible by definition.

    Always do this one-second sense-check.

Key points

  • Factorise, then use the table: HCF = smallest power of each common prime; LCM = largest power of every prime.
  • The same table works for three numbers; "common" means common to all of them.
  • For two numbers only: HCF(a,b)×LCM(a,b)=a×b\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times bHCF(a,b)×LCM(a,b)=a×b, so LCM=a×bHCF\text{LCM} = \dfrac{a \times b}{\text{HCF}}LCM=HCFa×b​.
  • For three numbers HCF × LCM is not the product (e.g. 4, 6, 10: 2×60≠2402 \times 60 \neq 2402×60=240).
  • The HCF always divides the LCM; co-prime numbers have HCF 1 and LCM = product.
