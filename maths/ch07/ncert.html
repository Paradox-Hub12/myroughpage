(i) (2, 3), (4, 1)

Given: the points (2,3)(2, 3)(2,3) and (4,1)(4, 1)(4,1). Asked: the distance between them.

    Label: (x1,y1)=(2,3)(x_1, y_1) = (2, 3)(x1​,y1​)=(2,3), (x2,y2)=(4,1)(x_2, y_2) = (4, 1)(x2​,y2​)=(4,1).

  2. 2

    Substitute in brackets.

    d=(4−2)2+(1−3)2d = \sqrt{(4 - 2)^2 + (1 - 3)^2}d=(4−2)2+(1−3)2​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Work out the brackets: 4−2=24 - 2 = 24−2=2 and 1−3=−21 - 3 = -21−3=−2.

    d=22+(−2)2=4+4=8d = \sqrt{2^2 + (-2)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 4} = \sqrt{8}d=22+(−2)2​=4+4​=8​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Simplify: 8=4×2=22\sqrt{8} = \sqrt{4 \times 2} = 2\sqrt{2}8​=4×2​=22​.

  5. Check: on a grid, the segment goes 2 across and 2 down — the diagonal of a 2 × 2 square, which is 222\sqrt{2}22​ ✔.

Exercise 7.1, Q1 (iii)Distance between (a, b) and (−a, −b)2 marks
  1. Find the distance between the following pairs of points :

(iii) (a, b), (– a, – b)

Given: the points (a,b)(a, b)(a,b) and (−a,−b)(-a, -b)(−a,−b). Asked: the distance.

Concept used: the distance formula with letters (Topic 2, Worked example 3). Why it applies: letters follow the same rules as numbers.

  1. 1

    Label: (x1,y1)=(a,b)(x_1, y_1) = (a, b)(x1​,y1​)=(a,b), (x2,y2)=(−a,−b)(x_2, y_2) = (-a, -b)(x2​,y2​)=(−a,−b).

  2. 2

    First bracket: x2−x1=−a−a=−2ax_2 - x_1 = -a - a = -2ax2​−x1​=−a−a=−2a.

  3. 3

    Second bracket: y2−y1=−b−b=−2by_2 - y_1 = -b - b = -2by2​−y1​=−b−b=−2b.

  4. 4

    Square and add: (−2a)2+(−2b)2=4a2+4b2=4(a2+b2)(-2a)^2 + (-2b)^2 = 4a^2 + 4b^2 = 4(a^2 + b^2)(−2a)2+(−2b)2=4a2+4b2=4(a2+b2).

  5. 5

    Take the root: 4(a2+b2)=2a2+b2\sqrt{4(a^2 + b^2)} = 2\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}4(a2+b2)​=2a2+b2​.

  6. Check: put a=3a = 3a=3, b=4b = 4b=4: the points are (3,4)(3, 4)(3,4) and (−3,−4)(-3, -4)(−3,−4), the distance is 36+64=10\sqrt{36 + 64} = 1036+64​=10, and the formula gives 29+16=2×5=102\sqrt{9 + 16} = 2 \times 5 = 1029+16​=2×5=10 ✔.

  7. Answer 2a2+b22\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}2a2+b2​ units

    Common trap: Writing 4a2+4b2=2a+2b\sqrt{4a^2 + 4b^2} = 2a + 2b4a2+4b2​=2a+2b. The root of a sum is not the sum of the roots.

Exercise 7.1, Q3Are (1, 5), (2, 3) and (−2, −11) collinear?3 marks
  1. Determine if the points (1, 5), (2, 3) and (– 2, – 11) are collinear.

  3. 3

    AC=(−2−1)2+(−11−5)2=9+256=265AC = \sqrt{(-2 - 1)^2 + (-11 - 5)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 256} = \sqrt{265}AC=(−2−1)2+(−11−5)2​=9+256​=265​.

  4. 4

    Approximately: AB≈2.24AB \approx 2.24AB≈2.24, BC≈14.56BC \approx 14.56BC≈14.56, AC≈16.28AC \approx 16.28AC≈16.28. The largest is AC.

  5. 5

Exercise 7.1, Q4An isosceles triangle?2 marks
  1. Check whether (5, – 2), (6, 4) and (7, – 2) are the vertices of an isosceles triangle.

Given: A(5,−2)A(5, -2)A(5,−2), B(6,4)B(6, 4)B(6,4), C(7,−2)C(7, -2)C(7,−2). Asked: is △ABC isosceles?

Concept used: a triangle is isosceles if two of its sides are equal (Topic 3). Why it applies: we can find all three sides.

  1. 1

    AB=(6−5)2+(4−(−2))2=1+36=37AB = \sqrt{(6 - 5)^2 + (4 - (-2))^2} = \sqrt{1 + 36} = \sqrt{37}AB=(6−5)2+(4−(−2))2​=1+36​=37​.

  2. 2

    BC=(7−6)2+(−2−4)2=1+36=37BC = \sqrt{(7 - 6)^2 + (-2 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 36} = \sqrt{37}BC=(7−6)2+(−2−4)2​=1+36​=37​.

  3. 3

    AC=(7−5)2+(−2−(−2))2=4+0=2AC = \sqrt{(7 - 5)^2 + (-2 - (-2))^2} = \sqrt{4 + 0} = 2AC=(7−5)2+(−2−(−2))2​=4+0​=2.

  4. 4

    AB=BCAB = BCAB=BC, so the triangle is isosceles.

  5. Check: the three points are not collinear (37+2>37\sqrt{37} + 2 > \sqrt{37}37​+2>37​), so they really form a triangle; and B lies above the mid-point (6,−2)(6, -2)(6,−2) of AC, as the apex of an isosceles triangle should ✔.

  6. Answer Yes — AB=BC=37AB = BC = \sqrt{37}AB=BC=37​, so the points are the vertices of an isosceles triangle.

    Common trap: 4−(−2)4 - (-2)4−(−2) written as 2. It is 4+2=64 + 2 = 64+2=6.
Exercise 7.1, Q5Champa and Chameli: is ABCD a square?3 marks
  1. In a classroom, 4 friends are seated at the points A, B, C and D as shown in Fig. 7.8. Champa and Chameli walk into the class and after observing for a few minutes Champa asks Chameli, "Don't you think ABCD is a square?" Chameli disagrees. Using distance formula, find which of them is correct.

(Fig. 7.8 is a grid with "Columns" 0 to 10 along the bottom and "Rows" 0 to 10 up the side. The four friends sit at A(3, 4), B(6, 7), C(9, 4) and D(6, 1).)
Print graph paper

Given: A(3,4)A(3, 4)A(3,4), B(6,7)B(6, 7)B(6,7), C(9,4)C(9, 4)C(9,4), D(6,1)D(6, 1)D(6,1). Asked: is ABCD a square?

Concept used: the shape checker — four equal sides and equal diagonals (Topic 3). Why it applies: six distances decide the shape.

  1. 1

    AB=(6−3)2+(7−4)2=9+9=18=32AB = \sqrt{(6 - 3)^2 + (7 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 9} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}AB=(6−3)2+(7−4)2​=9+9​=18​=32​.

  2. 2

    BC=(9−6)2+(4−7)2=9+9=32BC = \sqrt{(9 - 6)^2 + (4 - 7)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 9} = 3\sqrt{2}BC=(9−6)2+(4−7)2​=9+9​=32​.

  3. 3

    CD=(6−9)2+(1−4)2=9+9=32CD = \sqrt{(6 - 9)^2 + (1 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 9} = 3\sqrt{2}CD=(6−9)2+(1−4)2​=9+9​=32​.

  4. 4

    DA=(3−6)2+(4−1)2=9+9=32DA = \sqrt{(3 - 6)^2 + (4 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 9} = 3\sqrt{2}DA=(3−6)2+(4−1)2​=9+9​=32​.

  5. 5

    Diagonal AC=(9−3)2+(4−4)2=36=6AC = \sqrt{(9 - 3)^2 + (4 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{36} = 6AC=(9−3)2+(4−4)2​=36​=6.

  6. 6

    Diagonal BD=(6−6)2+(1−7)2=36=6BD = \sqrt{(6 - 6)^2 + (1 - 7)^2} = \sqrt{36} = 6BD=(6−6)2+(1−7)2​=36​=6.

  7. 7

    Four equal sides and equal diagonals: ABCD is a square.

  8. Check: AB2+BC2=18+18=36=AC2AB^2 + BC^2 = 18 + 18 = 36 = AC^2AB2+BC2=18+18=36=AC2, so the angle at B is 90° ✔.

  9. Answer ABCD is a square, so Champa is correct.

    Common trap: Stopping after the four sides. Equal sides alone would allow a rhombus; the equal diagonals are what prove the square.
