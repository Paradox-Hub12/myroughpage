Wrap-up

The formula card, the shape checker, the axis card and mixed problems

About 30 minutes

Everything in this chapter comes from two formulas: the distance formula (Pythagoras on a grid) and the section formula (similar triangles on a grid). This page puts them on one card with the two checklists you need most, then gives four mixed problems in the styles of the sample papers — a shape with its area, a ratio with an unknown coordinate, a point on an axis, and a question where only one of two values is allowed.

The shape checker

Find all four sides and both diagonals (vertices in the given order).

Sides Diagonals Shape
all four equal equal square
all four equal not equal rhombus
opposite sides equal equal rectangle
opposite sides equal not equal parallelogram

Triangle: three equal sides — equilateral; two — isosceles; none — scalene; (longest side)² = sum of the other two squares — right-angled. Three points: collinear when the two smaller distances add up to the largest.

The axis card

  • On the x-axis: (x,0)(x, 0)(x,0). On the y-axis: (0,y)(0, y)(0,y).
  • The x-axis cuts a segment where y=0y = 0y=0; the y-axis where x=0x = 0x=0.
  • From the x-axis the distance is ∣y∣\lvert y \rvert∣y∣; from the y-axis it is ∣x∣\lvert x \rvert∣x∣.
  • A ratio of internal division is positive; a negative k means "not between".

Mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

A square and its area

Question. Show that A(1, −2), B(5, 1), C(2, 5) and D(−2, 2), taken in order, are the vertices of a square, and find its area.

Given: four vertices in order. Asked: prove a square; find the area.

Concept used: the shape checker (four equal sides and equal diagonals); area of a square =(side)2= (\text{side})^2=(side)2. Why it applies: six distances decide the shape.

  1. 1

    AB=(5−1)2+(1−(−2))2=16+9=5AB = \sqrt{(5 - 1)^2 + (1 - (-2))^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = 5AB=(5−1)2+(1−(−2))2​=16+9​=5.

  2. 2

    BC=(2−5)2+(5−1)2=9+16=5BC = \sqrt{(2 - 5)^2 + (5 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5BC=(2−5)2+(5−1)2​=9+16​=5.

  3. 3

    CD=(−2−2)2+(2−5)2=16+9=5CD = \sqrt{(-2 - 2)^2 + (2 - 5)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = 5CD=(−2−2)2+(2−5)2​=16+9​=5.

  4. 4

    DA=(1−(−2))2+(−2−2)2=9+16=5DA = \sqrt{(1 - (-2))^2 + (-2 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5DA=(1−(−2))2+(−2−2)2​=9+16​=5.

  5. 5

    AC=(2−1)2+(5−(−2))2=1+49=50=52AC = \sqrt{(2 - 1)^2 + (5 - (-2))^2} = \sqrt{1 + 49} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}AC=(2−1)2+(5−(−2))2​=1+49​=50​=52​.

  6. 6

    BD=(−2−5)2+(2−1)2=49+1=50=52BD = \sqrt{(-2 - 5)^2 + (2 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{49 + 1} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}BD=(−2−5)2+(2−1)2​=49+1​=50​=52​.

  7. 7

    Four equal sides and equal diagonals: ABCD is a square.

  8. 8

    Area =52=25= 5^2 = 25=52=25 square units.

  9. Check: a square is also a rhombus, so 12×d1×d2=12×52×52=12×50=25\dfrac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 5\sqrt{2} \times 5\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 50 = 2521​×d1​×d2​=21​×52​×52​=21​×50=25 ✔.

  10. Answer Square of side 5 units; area 25 square units.

    Common trap: Writing the area as side × diagonal, or forgetting to show the diagonals before saying "square".

Mixed example 2

A ratio and an unknown coordinate

Question. The point P(2, y) lies on the segment joining A(−4, 3) and B(6, −7). Find the ratio in which P divides AB, and the value of y.

Given: P(2, y) on AB. Asked: AP:PBAP : PBAP:PB and y.

Concept used: the k : 1 form; the known x-coordinate gives k, then the y-coordinate gives y. Why it applies: x of P is known, y is not.

  1. 1

    Let the ratio be k:1k : 1k:1. Then x=6k+(−4)k+1=6k−4k+1x = \dfrac{6k + (-4)}{k + 1} = \dfrac{6k - 4}{k + 1}x=k+16k+(−4)​=k+16k−4​.

  2. 2

    Set it equal to 2: 6k−4k+1=2\dfrac{6k - 4}{k + 1} = 2k+16k−4​=2.

  3. 3

    Multiply by k+1k + 1k+1: 6k−4=2k+26k - 4 = 2k + 26k−4=2k+2.

  4. 4

    4k=64k = 64k=6, so k=32k = \dfrac{3}{2}k=23​. The ratio is 3:23 : 23:2 (positive, so P is between A and B).

  5. 5

    Now y with the ratio 3 : 2.

    y=3×(−7)+2×33+2=−21+65=−155=−3y = \dfrac{3 \times (-7) + 2 \times 3}{3 + 2} = \dfrac{-21 + 6}{5} = \dfrac{-15}{5} = -3y=3+23×(−7)+2×3​=5−21+6​=5−15​=−3Swipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: the x-coordinate with the ratio 3 : 2: 3×6+2×(−4)5=18−85=2\dfrac{3 \times 6 + 2 \times (-4)}{5} = \dfrac{18 - 8}{5} = 253×6+2×(−4)​=518−8​=2 ✔.

  7. Answer 3:23 : 23:2; y=−3y = -3y=−3

    Common trap: Writing the ratio as 32:1\dfrac{3}{2} : 123​:1 in the final answer, or as 2:32 : 32:3. Clear the fraction and keep the order: k:1=3:2k : 1 = 3 : 2k:1=3:2.

Mixed example 3

A point on the x-axis

Question. Find the point on the x-axis which is equidistant from A(−1, 4) and B(5, 2).

Given: the point is on the x-axis and PA=PBPA = PBPA=PB. Asked: the point.

Concept used: axis card — the point is (x,0)(x, 0)(x,0); PA2=PB2PA^2 = PB^2PA2=PB2. Why it applies: only x is unknown.

  1. 1

    Let the point be P(x,0)P(x, 0)P(x,0).

  2. 2

    PA2=PB2PA^2 = PB^2PA2=PB2.

    (x+1)2+(0−4)2=(x−5)2+(0−2)2(x + 1)^2 + (0 - 4)^2 = (x - 5)^2 + (0 - 2)^2(x+1)2+(0−4)2=(x−5)2+(0−2)2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Expand.

    x2+2x+1+16=x2−10x+25+4x^2 + 2x + 1 + 16 = x^2 - 10x + 25 + 4x2+2x+1+16=x2−10x+25+4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Cancel x2x^2x2: 2x+17=−10x+292x + 17 = -10x + 292x+17=−10x+29.

  5. 5

    12x=1212x = 1212x=12, so x=1x = 1x=1.

  6. 6

    The point is P(1,0)P(1, 0)P(1,0).

  7. Check: PA=(1+1)2+42=4+16=20PA = \sqrt{(1 + 1)^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{4 + 16} = \sqrt{20}PA=(1+1)2+42​=4+16​=20​ and PB=(1−5)2+22=16+4=20PB = \sqrt{(1 - 5)^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{16 + 4} = \sqrt{20}PB=(1−5)2+22​=16+4​=20​ ✔.

Mixed example 4

Two values, one allowed

Question. The point Q(4, y) is 5 units from P(1, −2), and Q lies in the fourth quadrant. Find y.

Given: PQ=5PQ = 5PQ=5; Q in quadrant IV. Asked: y.

Concept used: the distance formula gives two values; the quadrant decides which one is allowed. Why it applies: in quadrant IV, y<0y < 0y<0.

  1. 1

    (4−1)2+(y−(−2))2=5\sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (y - (-2))^2} = 5(4−1)2+(y−(−2))2​=5.

  2. 2

    Square: 9+(y+2)2=259 + (y + 2)^2 = 259+(y+2)2=25.

  3. 3

    (y+2)2=16(y + 2)^2 = 16(y+2)2=16, so y+2=4y + 2 = 4y+2=4 or y+2=−4y + 2 = -4y+2=−4.

  4. 4

    y=2y = 2y=2 or y=−6y = -6y=−6.

  5. 5

    In the fourth quadrant y is negative, so y=2y = 2y=2 is not allowed. y=−6y = -6y=−6.

  6. Check: Q(4,−6)Q(4, -6)Q(4,−6): PQ=32+(−4)2=25=5PQ = \sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5PQ=32+(−4)2​=25​=5 ✔, and (4,−6)(4, -6)(4,−6) has x positive, y negative — quadrant IV ✔.

  7. Answer y=−6y = -6y=−6

    Common trap: Writing only y=2y = 2y=2 (the first value found) without looking at the condition. Find both values first, then use the condition to choose — and say why the other is rejected.
