Topic 4 of 5

The section formula

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Derive the section formula for internal division and use it to find the point dividing a segment in a given ratio.
  • Find the mid-point of a segment, the points of trisection, and the points dividing a segment into four equal parts.
  • Find the ratio in which a point divides a segment (including the k : 1 method) and the ratio in which an axis divides a segment.

Engineers building a straight metro line want a ventilation shaft one-third of the way from one station to the next. A town planner wants a bus stop one-quarter of the way along a straight road. A town planner wants a bus stop one-quarter of the way along a straight road. Both need the same thing: the coordinates of a point that cuts a segment in a given ratio. The section formula gives them in one line — as long as the ratio is written the right way round.

What "divides AB in the ratio m₁ : m₂" means

A point P on the segment AB divides it internally in the ratio m1:m2m_1 : m_2m1​:m2​ if

APPB=m1m2\dfrac{AP}{PB} = \dfrac{m_1}{m_2}PBAP​=m2​m1​​Swipe to see the whole equation →

So m1m_1m1​ is the part next to A and m2m_2m2​ is the part next to B. P lies between A and B. For example, dividing AB in the ratio 1 : 2 puts P one-third of the way from A to B; dividing it in 2 : 1 puts P two-thirds of the way.

Where the formula comes from

Quick reminder (Chapter 6): two triangles with two pairs of equal angles are similar (AA), and then their sides are proportional.

Let A(x1,y1)A(x_1, y_1)A(x1​,y1​), B(x2,y2)B(x_2, y_2)B(x2​,y2​) and let P(x,y)P(x, y)P(x,y) divide AB in the ratio m1:m2m_1 : m_2m1​:m2​. Draw a horizontal line through A and one through P, and vertical lines through P and B, to make two small right triangles: one from A to P and one from P to B. Their hypotenuses AP and PB lie on the same line, so their angles are equal and the triangles are similar (AA). Their sides are therefore in the ratio AP:PB=m1:m2AP : PB = m_1 : m_2AP:PB=m1​:m2​:

  • horizontal sides: x−x1x2−x=m1m2\dfrac{x - x_1}{x_2 - x} = \dfrac{m_1}{m_2}x2​−xx−x1​​=m2​m1​​;
  • vertical sides: y−y1y2−y=m1m2\dfrac{y - y_1}{y_2 - y} = \dfrac{m_1}{m_2}y2​−yy−y1​​=m2​m1​​.

Cross-multiplying the first: m2(x−x1)=m1(x2−x)m_2(x - x_1) = m_1(x_2 - x)m2​(x−x1​)=m1​(x2​−x), so m2x−m2x1=m1x2−m1xm_2 x - m_2 x_1 = m_1 x_2 - m_1 xm2​x−m2​x1​=m1​x2​−m1​x, so (m1+m2)x=m1x2+m2x1(m_1 + m_2)x = m_1 x_2 + m_2 x_1(m1​+m2​)x=m1​x2​+m2​x1​. In the same way for y:

P(x,y)=(m1x2+m2x1m1+m2,m1y2+m2y1m1+m2)P(x, y) = \left( \dfrac{m_1 x_2 + m_2 x_1}{m_1 + m_2}, \dfrac{m_1 y_2 + m_2 y_1}{m_1 + m_2} \right)P(x,y)=(m1​+m2​m1​x2​+m2​x1​​,m1​+m2​m1​y2​+m2​y1​​)Swipe to see the whole equation →

This is the section formula.

How to remember the order — "cross over": m1m_1m1​ is written next to A, but it multiplies B's coordinates; m2m_2m2​ is next to B, but multiplies A's coordinates. (The bigger part of the ratio pulls P towards the other end.)

The fixed routine:

  1. Write the ratio next to the segment: A ——m1m_1m1​—— P ——m2m_2m2​—— B.
  2. Cross over: x=m1xB+m2xAm1+m2x = \dfrac{m_1 x_B + m_2 x_A}{m_1 + m_2}x=m1​+m2​m1​xB​+m2​xA​​, and the same for y.
  3. Check: P must lie between A and B (its x lies between xAx_AxA​ and xBx_BxB​, and so does its y).

Special cases

  • Mid-point (ratio 1 : 1): (x1+x22,y1+y22)\left( \dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)(2x1​+x2​​,2y1​+y2​​) — add the coordinates and halve.
  • Points of trisection (three equal parts): divide in 1 : 2 and in 2 : 1.
  • Four equal parts: divide in 1 : 3, find the mid-point (1 : 1), and divide in 3 : 1.
  • The k : 1 form. Dividing every part by m2m_2m2​ gives the ratio k:1k : 1k:1 with k=m1m2k = \dfrac{m_1}{m_2}k=m2​m1​​, and
P=(kx2+x1k+1,ky2+y1k+1)P = \left( \dfrac{k x_2 + x_1}{k + 1}, \dfrac{k y_2 + y_1}{k + 1} \right)P=(k+1kx2​+x1​​,k+1ky2​+y1​​)Swipe to see the whole equation →

This is the easiest form when the ratio is unknown: there is only one unknown, k.

Finding the ratio

  1. Let the ratio be k:1k : 1k:1 and write P with the k : 1 form.
  2. Set one coordinate equal to the known value of P and solve for k.
  3. Use the other coordinate to check (or to find an unknown in it).
  4. k must be positive. A negative k means the point is not between A and B, so the segment is not divided internally.
  5. Write the ratio as k:1k : 1k:1 and clear any fraction: k=32k = \dfrac{3}{2}k=23​ gives 3:23 : 23:2.

An axis dividing a segment (the axis card again): the point where AB meets the x-axis has y=0y = 0y=0, so set the y-coordinate of the k : 1 point equal to 0. For the y-axis, set the x-coordinate equal to 0.

Worked example 1

Dividing a segment in a ratio

Worked example 5

Finding the ratio (the k : 1 method)

Question. In what ratio does the point (1,−1)(1, -1)(1,−1) divide the segment joining A(−3, 5) and B(3, −4)?

Given: P(1, −1) on AB. Asked: AP:PBAP : PBAP:PB.

Concept used: let the ratio be k:1k : 1k:1; P is (kx2+x1k+1,ky2+y1k+1)\left( \dfrac{k x_2 + x_1}{k + 1}, \dfrac{k y_2 + y_1}{k + 1} \right)(k+1kx2​+x1​​,k+1ky2​+y1​​). Why it applies: only one unknown, k.

  1. 1

    Let the ratio be k:1k : 1k:1. The x-coordinate of the dividing point is

    k×3+1×(−3)k+1=3k−3k+1\dfrac{k \times 3 + 1 \times (-3)}{k + 1} = \dfrac{3k - 3}{k + 1}k+1k×3+1×(−3)​=k+13k−3​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    This must equal 1, the x-coordinate of P.

    3k−3k+1=1\dfrac{3k - 3}{k + 1} = 1k+13k−3​=1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Multiply both sides by k+1k + 1k+1.

    3k−3=k+13k - 3 = k + 13k−3=k+1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    2k=42k = 42k=4, so k=2k = 2k=2. The ratio is 2:12 : 12:1.

  5. 5

    Check with y: 2×(−4)+52+1=−8+53=−33=−1\dfrac{2 \times (-4) + 5}{2 + 1} = \dfrac{-8 + 5}{3} = \dfrac{-3}{3} = -12+12×(−4)+5​=3−8+5​=3−3​=−1 — this matches P's y-coordinate ✔.

  6. Check: k is positive, so P is between A and B ✔; and from step 5 both coordinates agree.

  7. Answer 2:12 : 12:1

    Common trap: Skipping step 5. If the y-coordinate does not match, the point is not on AB at all and there is no such ratio.

Worked example 7

When the axis does not cut the segment

Question. Does the y-axis divide the segment joining A(2, 3) and B(5, −1) internally? Explain.

Given: A(2, 3), B(5, −1). Asked: whether the y-axis cuts AB between A and B.

Concept used: on the y-axis x=0x = 0x=0; an internal ratio must be positive. Why it applies: a negative k means the point is outside the segment.

  1. 1

    Let the ratio be k:1k : 1k:1. The x-coordinate of the dividing point is 5k+2k+1\dfrac{5k + 2}{k + 1}k+15k+2​.

  2. 2

    On the y-axis, x=0x = 0x=0: 5k+2k+1=0\dfrac{5k + 2}{k + 1} = 0k+15k+2​=0, so 5k+2=05k + 2 = 05k+2=0 and k=−25k = -\dfrac{2}{5}k=−52​.

  3. 3

    k is negative, so there is no point of AB on the y-axis between A and B.

  4. Check: both A and B have positive x-coordinates (2 and 5), so both lie to the right of the y-axis — the whole segment is on one side ✔.

  5. Answer No — k comes out negative (−25-\dfrac{2}{5}−52​); the y-axis does not meet the segment AB.

    Common trap: Writing "the ratio is 2 : 5" by dropping the minus sign. A ratio of internal division can never be negative.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers name the section formula order (ratio direction), the axis condition and keeping a ratio that cannot be right among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • x=m1x1+m2x2m1+m2x = \dfrac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2}{m_1 + m_2}x=m1​+m2​m1​x1​+m2​x2​​

    m1m_1m1​ is the part next to A; it must multiply B's coordinate.

    Write A —m1m_1m1​— P —m2m_2m2​— B and cross over.

  • Reading "divides PQ in 3 : 1" as PR:RQ=1:3PR : RQ = 1 : 3PR:RQ=1:3

    The first number goes with the first-named end.

    PR:RQ=3:1PR : RQ = 3 : 1PR:RQ=3:1 — R is nearer Q.

  • Mid-point by subtracting: x2−x12\dfrac{x_2 - x_1}{2}2x2​−x1​​

    That is half of the change, not the middle point.

    Add and halve: x1+x22\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}2x1​+x2​​.

  • For the x-axis, setting x=0x = 0x=0

    On the x-axis it is yyy that is 0.

    x-axis → y=0y = 0y=0; y-axis → x=0x = 0x=0.

  • Keeping a negative k as a ratio

    A negative k means the point is not between the ends.

    Internal division needs k>0k > 0k>0. If k is negative, say the segment is not divided internally.

  • Finding k from x and never checking y

    The point may not be on the segment at all.

    Always check the other coordinate.
