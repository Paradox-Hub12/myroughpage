Topic 3 of 5

Using distances: collinear points and shapes

About 50 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Decide whether three points are collinear by comparing the sum of two distances with the third.
  • Name the triangle formed by three points (scalene, isosceles, equilateral, right-angled).
  • Name the quadrilateral formed by four points (square, rhombus, rectangle, parallelogram, or none) with full reasons.

Four friends sit at four desks in a classroom. One says, "We make a square!" Another says, "No — it only looks like one." Who is right? With the distance formula you never have to trust your eyes: six distances settle the argument. This topic turns the formula into a tool for checking whether points lie in a line and for naming the shape they make.

Are three points collinear?

Points are collinear if they lie on one straight line. For three points A, B, C, work out all three distances AB, BC and AC.

  • If the two smaller distances add up exactly to the largest, the points are collinear (the middle point lies between the other two).
  • If the two smaller distances add up to more than the largest, the points are not collinear: they form a triangle.

Simplify the roots first, so that like roots can be added: 25+35=552\sqrt{5} + 3\sqrt{5} = 5\sqrt{5}25​+35​=55​. Compare the distances themselves, not their squares — AB2+BC2=AC2AB^2 + BC^2 = AC^2AB2+BC2=AC2 is a test for a right angle, not for a straight line.

Naming a triangle

Work out the three sides.

If… the triangle is…
all three sides are equal equilateral
exactly two sides are equal isosceles
no two sides are equal scalene
the square of the longest side = the sum of the squares of the other two right-angled (converse of Pythagoras' theorem; the right angle is opposite the longest side)

A triangle can be two of these at once: for example, a right-angled isosceles triangle.

Naming a quadrilateral — the shape checker

Take the vertices in the order given (ABCD means sides AB, BC, CD, DA and diagonals AC, BD). Work out all four sides and both diagonals, then use the card:

Sides Diagonals Shape
all four equal equal square
all four equal not equal rhombus (not a square)
opposite sides equal (AB = CD, BC = DA) equal rectangle
opposite sides equal not equal parallelogram (not a rectangle)

Three warnings:

  1. Four equal sides alone do not make a square — a rhombus also has four equal sides. You need the diagonals too.
  2. Check that no three of the points are collinear. If three of them lie on one line, the four points do not form a quadrilateral at all.
  3. Sketch the points in the given order. The sides AB, BC, CD, DA must go round the shape without crossing; only then do these tests name the shape.

Worked example 1

Collinear points

Question. Show that the points A(−1, −4), B(1, 0) and C(4, 6) are collinear.

Given: three points. Asked: to show they lie on one line.

Concept used: the sum of the two smaller distances equals the largest. Why it applies: if B lies between A and C on one line, AB+BC=ACAB + BC = ACAB+BC=AC.

  1. 1

    AB=(1−(−1))2+(0−(−4))2=22+42=4+16=20=25AB = \sqrt{(1 - (-1))^2 + (0 - (-4))^2} = \sqrt{2^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{4 + 16} = \sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}AB=(1−(−1))2+(0−(−4))2​=22+42​=4+16​=20​=25​.

  2. 2

    BC=(4−1)2+(6−0)2=9+36=45=35BC = \sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (6 - 0)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 36} = \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5}BC=(4−1)2+(6−0)2​=9+36​=45​=35​.

  3. 3

    AC=(4−(−1))2+(6−(−4))2=25+100=125=55AC = \sqrt{(4 - (-1))^2 + (6 - (-4))^2} = \sqrt{25 + 100} = \sqrt{125} = 5\sqrt{5}AC=(4−(−1))2+(6−(−4))2​=25+100​=125​=55​.

  4. 4

    Add the two smaller distances: AB+BC=25+35=55=ACAB + BC = 2\sqrt{5} + 3\sqrt{5} = 5\sqrt{5} = ACAB+BC=25​+35​=55​=AC.

  5. 5

    So A, B and C are collinear, with B between A and C.

  6. Check: from A to B, x goes up 2 and y goes up 4; from B to C, x goes up 3 and y goes up 6 — the same "up 2 for every 1 across" both times, so the path does not bend ✔.

  7. Answer AB+BC=ACAB + BC = ACAB+BC=AC, so the points are collinear.

    Common trap: Leaving the roots as 20+45\sqrt{20} + \sqrt{45}20​+45​ and comparing with 125\sqrt{125}125​, or adding them as 65\sqrt{65}65​. Simplify first; only like roots can be added.

Worked example 2

Not collinear

Question. Are the points P(−2, 1), Q(1, 3) and R(4, 2) collinear?

Given: three points. Asked: collinear or not.

Concept used: compare the sum of the two smaller distances with the largest. Why it applies: collinear needs exact equality.

  1. 1

    PQ=(1−(−2))2+(3−1)2=9+4=13PQ = \sqrt{(1 - (-2))^2 + (3 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4} = \sqrt{13}PQ=(1−(−2))2+(3−1)2​=9+4​=13​, about 3.61.

  2. 2

    QR=(4−1)2+(2−3)2=9+1=10QR = \sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (2 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 1} = \sqrt{10}QR=(4−1)2+(2−3)2​=9+1​=10​, about 3.16.

  3. 3

    PR=(4−(−2))2+(2−1)2=36+1=37PR = \sqrt{(4 - (-2))^2 + (2 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 1} = \sqrt{37}PR=(4−(−2))2+(2−1)2​=36+1​=37​, about 6.08.

  4. 4

    The largest is PR. The two smaller add to about 3.61+3.16=6.773.61 + 3.16 = 6.773.61+3.16=6.77, which is more than 6.08.

  5. 5

    PQ+QR>PRPQ + QR > PRPQ+QR>PR, so the points are not collinear; they form a triangle.

  6. Check: from P to Q the path goes up 2, but from Q to R it goes down 1 — the path bends at Q ✔.

  7. Answer No — PQ+QR≠PRPQ + QR \neq PRPQ+QR=PR, so P, Q, R are not collinear.

    Common trap: Checking only two distances ("PQ is about the same as QR, so they are in a line"). Collinearity needs all three distances and the sum test.

Worked example 3

An isosceles triangle

Question. Show that A(1, 1), B(5, 4) and C(1, 6) are the vertices of an isosceles triangle. Is it also right-angled?

Given: three points. Asked: the type of triangle.

Concept used: compare the three sides; for a right angle, compare squares. Why it applies: the type depends only on the side lengths.

  1. 1

    AB=(5−1)2+(4−1)2=16+9=25=5AB = \sqrt{(5 - 1)^2 + (4 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5AB=(5−1)2+(4−1)2​=16+9​=25​=5.

  2. 2

    BC=(1−5)2+(6−4)2=16+4=20=25BC = \sqrt{(1 - 5)^2 + (6 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 4} = \sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}BC=(1−5)2+(6−4)2​=16+4​=20​=25​.

  3. 3

    AC=(1−1)2+(6−1)2=0+25=5AC = \sqrt{(1 - 1)^2 + (6 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{0 + 25} = 5AC=(1−1)2+(6−1)2​=0+25​=5.

  4. 4

    AB=AC=5AB = AC = 5AB=AC=5 and BC is different, so △ABC is isosceles.

  5. 5

    Right angle? The longest side is 5 (AB or AC). Compare squares: AC2=25AC^2 = 25AC2=25, but AB2+BC2=25+20=45AB^2 + BC^2 = 25 + 20 = 45AB2+BC2=25+20=45. Neither 5 works as a hypotenuse, so there is no right angle.

  6. Check: A and C have the same x-coordinate, so AC is vertical — count 5 squares from y = 1 to y = 6 ✔; AB is a 3, 4, 5 right triangle on the grid (4 across, 3 up), so AB = 5 ✔.

  7. Answer Isosceles (AB=AC=5AB = AC = 5AB=AC=5 units); not right-angled.

    Common trap: Stopping after two sides: "AB = 5 and AC = 5, so isosceles" is fine, but "equilateral" needs all three equal — always find the third side.

Worked example 4

A right-angled triangle

Question. Show that P(0, 4), Q(2, 0) and R(8, 3) form a right-angled triangle. At which vertex is the right angle?

Given: three points. Asked: prove a right angle and find where it is.

Concept used: the converse of Pythagoras' theorem. Why it applies: if the square of the longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two, the angle opposite the longest side is 90°.

  1. 1

    PQ2=(2−0)2+(0−4)2=4+16=20PQ^2 = (2 - 0)^2 + (0 - 4)^2 = 4 + 16 = 20PQ2=(2−0)2+(0−4)2=4+16=20.

  2. 2

    QR2=(8−2)2+(3−0)2=36+9=45QR^2 = (8 - 2)^2 + (3 - 0)^2 = 36 + 9 = 45QR2=(8−2)2+(3−0)2=36+9=45.

  3. 3

    PR2=(8−0)2+(3−4)2=64+1=65PR^2 = (8 - 0)^2 + (3 - 4)^2 = 64 + 1 = 65PR2=(8−0)2+(3−4)2=64+1=65.

  4. 4

    The longest side is PR. PQ2+QR2=20+45=65=PR2PQ^2 + QR^2 = 20 + 45 = 65 = PR^2PQ2+QR2=20+45=65=PR2.

  5. 5

    By the converse of Pythagoras' theorem, the angle opposite PR — the angle at Q — is 90°.

  6. Check: plot P, Q and R on squared paper and fit the corner of a page into the angle at Q: it fits exactly ✔. (Also, the sides 20\sqrt{20}20​, 45\sqrt{45}45​, 65\sqrt{65}65​ are all different, so the triangle is a scalene right triangle.)

  7. Answer PQ2+QR2=PR2PQ^2 + QR^2 = PR^2PQ2+QR2=PR2, so △PQR is right-angled at Q.

    Common trap: Naming the right angle at the wrong vertex. The right angle is opposite the longest side: PR is opposite Q.

Worked example 5

A square

Question. Show that A(2, −1), B(5, 1), C(3, 4) and D(0, 2), taken in order, are the vertices of a square.
Print graph paper

Given: four points in order. Asked: prove ABCD is a square.

Concept used: the shape checker — four equal sides and equal diagonals. Why it applies: a rhombus also has four equal sides; only the diagonals tell a square apart.

  1. 1

    AB=(5−2)2+(1−(−1))2=9+4=13AB = \sqrt{(5 - 2)^2 + (1 - (-1))^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4} = \sqrt{13}AB=(5−2)2+(1−(−1))2​=9+4​=13​.

  2. 2

    BC=(3−5)2+(4−1)2=4+9=13BC = \sqrt{(3 - 5)^2 + (4 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9} = \sqrt{13}BC=(3−5)2+(4−1)2​=4+9​=13​.

  3. 3

    CD=(0−3)2+(2−4)2=9+4=13CD = \sqrt{(0 - 3)^2 + (2 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4} = \sqrt{13}CD=(0−3)2+(2−4)2​=9+4​=13​.

  4. 4

    DA=(2−0)2+(−1−2)2=4+9=13DA = \sqrt{(2 - 0)^2 + (-1 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9} = \sqrt{13}DA=(2−0)2+(−1−2)2​=4+9​=13​.

  5. 5

    Diagonal AC=(3−2)2+(4−(−1))2=1+25=26AC = \sqrt{(3 - 2)^2 + (4 - (-1))^2} = \sqrt{1 + 25} = \sqrt{26}AC=(3−2)2+(4−(−1))2​=1+25​=26​.

  6. 6

    Diagonal BD=(0−5)2+(2−1)2=25+1=26BD = \sqrt{(0 - 5)^2 + (2 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 1} = \sqrt{26}BD=(0−5)2+(2−1)2​=25+1​=26​.

  7. 7

    All four sides are equal and the diagonals are equal, so ABCD is a square.

  8. Check: AB2+BC2=13+13=26=AC2AB^2 + BC^2 = 13 + 13 = 26 = AC^2AB2+BC2=13+13=26=AC2, so the angle at B is 90° — a rhombus with a right angle is a square ✔.

  9. Answer AB=BC=CD=DA=13AB = BC = CD = DA = \sqrt{13}AB=BC=CD=DA=13​ and AC=BD=26AC = BD = \sqrt{26}AC=BD=26​, so ABCD is a square.

    Common trap: Stopping after step 4 and writing "square". Four equal sides prove only a rhombus.

Worked example 6

A rhombus that is not a square

Question. Name the quadrilateral formed by A(−1, 1), B(2, 3), C(5, 1) and D(2, −1), taken in order. Give reasons.
Print graph paper

Given: four points in order. Asked: the name of ABCD, with reasons.

Concept used: the shape checker. Why it applies: the six distances decide the shape.

  1. 1

    AB=(2−(−1))2+(3−1)2=9+4=13AB = \sqrt{(2 - (-1))^2 + (3 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4} = \sqrt{13}AB=(2−(−1))2+(3−1)2​=9+4​=13​.

  2. 2

    BC=(5−2)2+(1−3)2=9+4=13BC = \sqrt{(5 - 2)^2 + (1 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4} = \sqrt{13}BC=(5−2)2+(1−3)2​=9+4​=13​.

  3. 3

    CD=(2−5)2+(−1−1)2=9+4=13CD = \sqrt{(2 - 5)^2 + (-1 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4} = \sqrt{13}CD=(2−5)2+(−1−1)2​=9+4​=13​.

  4. 4

    DA=(−1−2)2+(1−(−1))2=9+4=13DA = \sqrt{(-1 - 2)^2 + (1 - (-1))^2} = \sqrt{9 + 4} = \sqrt{13}DA=(−1−2)2+(1−(−1))2​=9+4​=13​.

  5. 5

    Diagonal AC=(5−(−1))2+(1−1)2=36=6AC = \sqrt{(5 - (-1))^2 + (1 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{36} = 6AC=(5−(−1))2+(1−1)2​=36​=6.

  6. 6

    Diagonal BD=(2−2)2+(−1−3)2=16=4BD = \sqrt{(2 - 2)^2 + (-1 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{16} = 4BD=(2−2)2+(−1−3)2​=16​=4.

  7. 7

    Four equal sides, but the diagonals are not equal (6≠46 \neq 46=4): ABCD is a rhombus, not a square.

  8. Check: if it were a square, AB2+BC2AB^2 + BC^2AB2+BC2 would equal AC2AC^2AC2; but 13+13=26≠3613 + 13 = 26 \neq 3613+13=26=36, so the angle at B is not 90° ✔.

  9. Answer A rhombus: AB=BC=CD=DA=13AB = BC = CD = DA = \sqrt{13}AB=BC=CD=DA=13​, but AC=6≠BD=4AC = 6 \neq BD = 4AC=6=BD=4.

    Common trap: Calling it a square because "all four sides are equal". This is the most common wrong answer in shape questions.

Worked example 7

A parallelogram

Question. Name the quadrilateral formed by A(−2, −1), B(4, 0), C(5, 3) and D(−1, 2), taken in order. Give reasons.
Print graph paper

Given: four points in order. Asked: the name of ABCD.

Concept used: the shape checker. Why it applies: sides and diagonals decide the shape.

  1. 1

    AB=(4−(−2))2+(0−(−1))2=36+1=37AB = \sqrt{(4 - (-2))^2 + (0 - (-1))^2} = \sqrt{36 + 1} = \sqrt{37}AB=(4−(−2))2+(0−(−1))2​=36+1​=37​.

  2. 2

    BC=(5−4)2+(3−0)2=1+9=10BC = \sqrt{(5 - 4)^2 + (3 - 0)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10}BC=(5−4)2+(3−0)2​=1+9​=10​.

  3. 3

    CD=(−1−5)2+(2−3)2=36+1=37CD = \sqrt{(-1 - 5)^2 + (2 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 1} = \sqrt{37}CD=(−1−5)2+(2−3)2​=36+1​=37​.

  4. 4

    DA=(−2−(−1))2+(−1−2)2=1+9=10DA = \sqrt{(-2 - (-1))^2 + (-1 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10}DA=(−2−(−1))2+(−1−2)2​=1+9​=10​.

  5. 5

    Opposite sides are equal: AB=CDAB = CDAB=CD and BC=DABC = DABC=DA.

  6. 6

    Diagonals: AC=(5−(−2))2+(3−(−1))2=49+16=65AC = \sqrt{(5 - (-2))^2 + (3 - (-1))^2} = \sqrt{49 + 16} = \sqrt{65}AC=(5−(−2))2+(3−(−1))2​=49+16​=65​ and BD=(−1−4)2+(2−0)2=25+4=29BD = \sqrt{(-1 - 4)^2 + (2 - 0)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 4} = \sqrt{29}BD=(−1−4)2+(2−0)2​=25+4​=29​.

  7. 7

    The diagonals are not equal, so it is not a rectangle. ABCD is a parallelogram.

  8. Check: the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other: mid-point of AC is (−2+52,−1+32)=(32,1)\left(\dfrac{-2 + 5}{2}, \dfrac{-1 + 3}{2}\right) = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}, 1\right)(2−2+5​,2−1+3​)=(23​,1), and mid-point of BD is (4+(−1)2,0+22)=(32,1)\left(\dfrac{4 + (-1)}{2}, \dfrac{0 + 2}{2}\right) = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}, 1\right)(24+(−1)​,20+2​)=(23​,1) — the same point ✔ (Topic 4 explains mid-points).

  9. Answer A parallelogram: AB=CD=37AB = CD = \sqrt{37}AB=CD=37​, BC=DA=10BC = DA = \sqrt{10}BC=DA=10​, and AC≠BDAC \neq BDAC=BD.

    Common trap: Comparing the wrong pairs — AB with BC (adjacent sides) instead of AB with CD (opposite sides). Draw a quick sketch and pair the sides that do not touch.

To prove that ABCD is a square using distances, it is enough to show that:

A rhombus also has four equal sides.
Equal sides make it a rhombus; equal diagonals then make the rhombus a square.
A rectangle (and some other shapes) also has equal diagonals.
This proves only a parallelogram.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that naming a quadrilateral without full checks is one of the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • "Four equal sides, so a square"

    A rhombus also has four equal sides.

    Find both diagonals: equal → square; unequal → rhombus.

  • Finding only the four sides of a "rectangle"

    A parallelogram also has equal opposite sides.

    Find both diagonals: equal → rectangle.

  • Comparing adjacent sides (AB with BC) for a parallelogram

    A parallelogram's equal sides are opposite (AB with CD).

    Sketch ABCD; pair the sides that do not touch.

  • Ignoring the order of the vertices

    "ABCD" fixes which segments are sides and which are diagonals.

    Sides AB, BC, CD, DA; diagonals AC, BD.

  • Not checking for three points in a line

    Then there is no quadrilateral at all.

    If one distance equals the sum of two others among three of the points, say "no quadrilateral".

  • Collinearity from two distances only, or from squares

    AB2+BC2=AC2AB^2 + BC^2 = AC^2AB2+BC2=AC2 tests a right angle, not a straight line.

    Find all three distances; test AB+BC=ACAB + BC = ACAB+BC=AC.

Key points

  • Collinear: the two smaller distances add up exactly to the largest. Simplify roots before adding.
  • Triangle: compare the three sides (equilateral / isosceles / scalene); compare squares for a right angle, which is opposite the longest side.
  • Quadrilateral: take the vertices in order; find all four sides and both diagonals.
  • Square = four equal sides + equal diagonals. Rhombus = four equal sides, unequal diagonals. Rectangle = opposite sides equal + equal diagonals. Parallelogram = opposite sides equal, unequal diagonals.
  • Three of the four points in a line → no quadrilateral.
