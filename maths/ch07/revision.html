The coordinate plane — a point is (x,y)(x, y)(x,y): x is the sideways move from the y-axis, y is the up-down move from the x-axis. Quadrants I–IV (anticlockwise): (+,+)(+, +)(+,+), (−,+)(-, +)(−,+), (−,−)(-, -)(−,−), (+,−)(+, -)(+,−). On the x-axis a point is (x,0)(x, 0)(x,0); on the y-axis, (0,y)(0, y)(0,y).

Distance formula — PQ=(x2−x1)2+(y2−y1)2PQ = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}PQ=(x2​−x1​)2+(y2​−y1​)2​: Pythagoras' theorem in the right triangle whose two other sides are the differences of the coordinates. From the origin: x2+y2\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}x2+y2​. From the x-axis: ∣y∣\lvert y \rvert∣y∣; from the y-axis: ∣x∣\lvert x \rvert∣x∣. Substitute in brackets; a square is never negative.

Using distances — an unknown coordinate: square both sides; usually two values — keep both unless a condition rules one out. Equidistant: PA2=PB2PA^2 = PB^2PA2=PB2 gives a linear relation (the perpendicular bisector of AB). Collinear: the two smaller distances add up exactly to the largest. Triangles: compare the three sides; a right angle when (longest side)² = sum of the other two squares. Quadrilaterals: find all four sides and both diagonals.

Section formula — P divides AB internally in m1:m2m_1 : m_2m1​:m2​ (so AP:PB=m1:m2AP : PB = m_1 : m_2AP:PB=m1​:m2​): P=(m1x2+m2x1m1+m2,m1y2+m2y1m1+m2)P = \left( \dfrac{m_1 x_2 + m_2 x_1}{m_1 + m_2}, \dfrac{m_1 y_2 + m_2 y_1}{m_1 + m_2} \right)P=(m1​+m2​m1​x2​+m2​x1​​,m1​+m2​m1​y2​+m2​y1​​) — cross over. Mid-point: add and halve. Trisection: 1 : 2 and 2 : 1. Unknown ratio: k:1k : 1k:1, solve with one coordinate, check with the other; k must be positive. An axis cuts AB where the other coordinate is 0.

Applications — parallelogram ABCD: mid-point of AC = mid-point of BD, so D=A+C−BD = A + C - BD=A+C−B. Diameter: the centre is its mid-point. Rhombus area =12d1d2= \dfrac{1}{2} d_1 d_2=21​d1​d2​. Median: mid-point of the opposite side, then the distance.

Not in the 2026-27 syllabus: area of a triangle from coordinates; division of a segment externally.
