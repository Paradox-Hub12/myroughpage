Chapter 7 · Unit III

Coordinate Geometry

5 topicsAbout 7 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

A map app can tell you that a shop is 2.4 km away in a straight line; a pilot reads a position as two numbers; a game moves a character to the point half-way between two others. Behind each of these is a pair of numbers, (x,y)(x, y)(x,y), and two formulas. In this chapter you learn how far apart two points are without measuring, where to stand to divide a path in a given ratio, and how to prove what shape four points make — all by calculation on a grid.

In the board exam this chapter is Unit III, Coordinate Geometry (6 marks), with the same content in Mathematics Standard and Mathematics Basic. Each of the four sample papers for 2025-26 and 2026-27 gives it exactly 6 marks: the 2026-27 Standard paper asks three 1-mark questions (a perpendicular bisector, a median, an assertion–reason on a parallelogram) and a 3-mark "in what ratio does the x-axis divide…"; the 2026-27 Basic paper asks a 1-mark section-formula question, a 2-mark parallelogram or equidistant-point question and a 3-mark ratio question; both 2025-26 papers ask two 1-mark questions and a 4-mark case study.

What you'll learn

  • Plot points in all four quadrants and on the axes, use Pythagoras' theorem on a grid, square negative numbers without sign slips, and simplify square roots. Take the 5-question skip-check first — score 5 out of 5 and go straight to Topic 2.
  • Find the distance between two points (also with letters), from the origin and from an axis; find an unknown coordinate (and keep both values); find points equidistant from two points. Includes an interactive coordinate explorer.
  • Decide whether three points are collinear, name the triangle three points make, and name a quadrilateral with full reasons — square, rhombus, rectangle, parallelogram or none.
  • Find the point dividing a segment in a given ratio, the mid-point and the points of trisection; find the ratio in which a point — or an axis — divides a segment.
  • Find the fourth vertex of a parallelogram and the other end of a diameter, the area of a rhombus and the length of a median, and solve real-life grid problems and case studies.
  • Use the formula card, the shape checker and the axis card, and solve mixed problems in sample-paper styles.
