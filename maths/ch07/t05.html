Topic 5 of 5

Applications and mixed problems

About 45 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find a missing vertex of a parallelogram (its diagonals bisect each other) and the other end of a diameter of a circle.
  • Find the area of a rhombus from its diagonals, and the length of a median of a triangle (mid-point, then distance).
  • Solve real-life grid problems (seats, sports grounds, maps) and sample-paper style case studies.

A surveyor knows three corners of a rectangular plot and must mark the fourth. A gardener knows the centre of a round flower bed and one point on its edge, and needs the point exactly opposite. A sports teacher wants to place a flag half-way between two others. Each of these jobs is one of the two formulas you already know — used together and in the right order.

The missing vertex of a parallelogram

Quick reminder (Class 9): the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other — they cut each other at their common mid-point.

So for a parallelogram ABCD (vertices in order), the diagonals are AC and BD, and

mid-point of AC=mid-point of BD\text{mid-point of } AC = \text{mid-point of } BDmid-point of AC=mid-point of BDSwipe to see the whole equation →

If three vertices are known, this gives the fourth. For example, with D unknown: xA+xC2=xB+xD2\dfrac{x_A + x_C}{2} = \dfrac{x_B + x_D}{2}2xA​+xC​​=2xB​+xD​​, so xD=xA+xC−xBx_D = x_A + x_C - x_BxD​=xA​+xC​−xB​, and in the same way yD=yA+yC−yBy_D = y_A + y_C - y_ByD​=yA​+yC​−yB​.

Order matters. The diagonals join opposite vertices: A with C, and B with D. Pairing A with B (a side) gives a wrong point.

The ends of a diameter

The centre of a circle is the mid-point of every diameter. If the centre O and one end A are known, the other end B satisfies xA+xB2=xO\dfrac{x_A + x_B}{2} = x_O2xA​+xB​​=xO​, so xB=2xO−xAx_B = 2x_O - x_AxB​=2xO​−xA​ (and the same for y).

Rhombus area from its diagonals

Quick reminder (Class 8): the area of a rhombus is 12×d1×d2\dfrac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_221​×d1​×d2​, where d1d_1d1​ and d2d_2d2​ are its diagonals. Find the two diagonals with the distance formula. (First check that the shape is a rhombus — four equal sides — if the question does not say so.)

The length of a median

A median joins a vertex of a triangle to the mid-point of the opposite side. So: (1) find the mid-point of that side; (2) find the distance from the vertex to it.

Real-life grids

Many questions describe a classroom, a playground or a map with rows and columns, or with distances along two perpendicular lines. Choose the origin and the axes, write each place as a pair of coordinates (columns across as x, rows up as y, unless the question says otherwise), and then use the formulas. Keep the units (metres, kilometres) in the answer.

Area of a triangle from coordinates is not in the 2026-27 syllabus, so it is left out of this chapter.

The fourth vertex of a parallelogram

Question. A(−2, 1), B(3, −1) and C(6, 3) are three vertices of a parallelogram ABCD, taken in order. Find the fourth vertex D.
Print graph paper

Given: A, B, C; ABCD is a parallelogram (in order). Asked: D.

Concept used: the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other. Why it applies: AC and BD are the diagonals, so they have the same mid-point.

  1. 1

    Let D be (x,y)(x, y)(x,y).

  2. 2

    Mid-point of AC: (−2+62,1+32)=(2,2)\left( \dfrac{-2 + 6}{2}, \dfrac{1 + 3}{2} \right) = (2, 2)(2−2+6​,21+3​)=(2,2).

  3. 3

    Mid-point of BD: (3+x2,−1+y2)\left( \dfrac{3 + x}{2}, \dfrac{-1 + y}{2} \right)(23+x​,2−1+y​).

  4. 4

    They are the same point. Compare the x-coordinates.

    3+x2=2\dfrac{3 + x}{2} = 223+x​=2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Multiply by 2: 3+x=43 + x = 43+x=4, so x=1x = 1x=1.

  6. 6

    Compare the y-coordinates.

    −1+y2=2\dfrac{-1 + y}{2} = 22−1+y​=2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Multiply by 2: −1+y=4-1 + y = 4−1+y=4, so y=5y = 5y=5.

  8. 8

    So D is (1,5)(1, 5)(1,5).

  9. Check: opposite sides must be equal: AB=25+4=29AB = \sqrt{25 + 4} = \sqrt{29}AB=25+4​=29​ and CD=25+4=29CD = \sqrt{25 + 4} = \sqrt{29}CD=25+4​=29​ ✔; BC=9+16=5BC = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5BC=9+16​=5 and DA=9+16=5DA = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5DA=9+16​=5 ✔.

  10. Answer D(1, 5)

    Common trap: Pairing the wrong vertices: "mid-point of AB = mid-point of CD" gives D=A+B−C=(−5,−3)D = A + B - C = (-5, -3)D=A+B−C=(−5,−3), which is not a vertex of this parallelogram. Only opposite vertices are joined by a diagonal.

Worked example 2

The other end of a diameter

Question. The centre of a circle is O(1,−2)O(1, -2)O(1,−2) and one end of a diameter is A(−3,1)A(-3, 1)A(−3,1). Find the other end B, and the radius.

Given: centre O, one end A. Asked: B and the radius.

Concept used: the centre is the mid-point of the diameter AB. Why it applies: every diameter passes through the centre and is bisected by it.

  1. 1

    Let B be (x,y)(x, y)(x,y). The mid-point of AB is O.

    −3+x2=11+y2=−2\dfrac{-3 + x}{2} = 1 \qquad \dfrac{1 + y}{2} = -22−3+x​=121+y​=−2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Multiply by 2: −3+x=2-3 + x = 2−3+x=2 and 1+y=−41 + y = -41+y=−4.

  3. 3

    So x=5x = 5x=5 and y=−5y = -5y=−5: B is (5,−5)(5, -5)(5,−5).

  4. 4

    Radius OA=(−3−1)2+(1−(−2))2=16+9=25=5OA = \sqrt{(-3 - 1)^2 + (1 - (-2))^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5OA=(−3−1)2+(1−(−2))2​=16+9​=25​=5 units.

  5. Check: OB=(5−1)2+(−5−(−2))2=16+9=5OB = \sqrt{(5 - 1)^2 + (-5 - (-2))^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = 5OB=(5−1)2+(−5−(−2))2​=16+9​=5 — B is also on the circle ✔.

  6. Answer B(5, −5); radius 5 units.

    Common trap: Taking the mid-point of O and A, (1+(−3)2,−2+12)=(−1,−12)\left( \dfrac{1 + (-3)}{2}, \dfrac{-2 + 1}{2} \right) = \left( -1, -\dfrac{1}{2} \right)(21+(−3)​,2−2+1​)=(−1,−21​). O is the middle, not an end.

Worked example 3

The area of a rhombus

Question. Show that A(−3, 1), B(2, 3), C(7, 1) and D(2, −1), taken in order, form a rhombus, and find its area.

Given: four vertices in order. Asked: show it is a rhombus; find the area.

Concept used: four equal sides make a rhombus; area =12×d1×d2= \dfrac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2=21​×d1​×d2​. Why it applies: the diagonals are AC and BD.

  1. 1

    AB=(2−(−3))2+(3−1)2=25+4=29AB = \sqrt{(2 - (-3))^2 + (3 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 4} = \sqrt{29}AB=(2−(−3))2+(3−1)2​=25+4​=29​.

  2. 2

    BC=(7−2)2+(1−3)2=25+4=29BC = \sqrt{(7 - 2)^2 + (1 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 4} = \sqrt{29}BC=(7−2)2+(1−3)2​=25+4​=29​.

  3. 3

    CD=(2−7)2+(−1−1)2=25+4=29CD = \sqrt{(2 - 7)^2 + (-1 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 4} = \sqrt{29}CD=(2−7)2+(−1−1)2​=25+4​=29​.

  4. 4

    DA=(−3−2)2+(1−(−1))2=25+4=29DA = \sqrt{(-3 - 2)^2 + (1 - (-1))^2} = \sqrt{25 + 4} = \sqrt{29}DA=(−3−2)2+(1−(−1))2​=25+4​=29​.

  5. 5

    All four sides are equal, so ABCD is a rhombus.

  6. 6

    Diagonals: AC=(7−(−3))2+(1−1)2=100=10AC = \sqrt{(7 - (-3))^2 + (1 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10AC=(7−(−3))2+(1−1)2​=100​=10 and BD=(2−2)2+(−1−3)2=16=4BD = \sqrt{(2 - 2)^2 + (-1 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{16} = 4BD=(2−2)2+(−1−3)2​=16​=4.

  7. 7

    Area =12×10×4=20= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 4 = 20=21​×10×4=20 square units.

  8. Check: the diagonals are unequal (10≠410 \neq 410=4), so it is a rhombus and not a square ✔; and the rhombus is made of four right triangles with legs 5 and 2, each of area 12×5×2=5\dfrac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 2 = 521​×5×2=5, total 4×5=204 \times 5 = 204×5=20 ✔.

  9. Answer A rhombus (AB=BC=CD=DA=29AB = BC = CD = DA = \sqrt{29}AB=BC=CD=DA=29​); area 20 square units.

    Common trap: Multiplying the diagonals without the 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ (giving 40), or multiplying two sides (29×29=29\sqrt{29} \times \sqrt{29} = 2929​×29​=29).

Worked example 4

The length of a median

Question. The vertices of △ABC are A(4, −3), B(−2, 5) and C(6, 1). Find the length of the median from A.

Given: the three vertices. Asked: the median AM.

Concept used: a median joins a vertex to the mid-point of the opposite side. Why it applies: the side opposite A is BC.

  1. 1

    M is the mid-point of BC.

    M=(−2+62,5+12)=(42,62)=(2,3)M = \left( \dfrac{-2 + 6}{2}, \dfrac{5 + 1}{2} \right) = \left( \dfrac{4}{2}, \dfrac{6}{2} \right) = (2, 3)M=(2−2+6​,25+1​)=(24​,26​)=(2,3)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Distance AM.

    AM=(2−4)2+(3−(−3))2=4+36=40=210AM = \sqrt{(2 - 4)^2 + (3 - (-3))^2} = \sqrt{4 + 36} = \sqrt{40} = 2\sqrt{10}AM=(2−4)2+(3−(−3))2​=4+36​=40​=210​Swipe to see the whole equation →

  3. Check: from B(−2, 5) to M(2, 3) is 4 across and 2 down; from M to C(6, 1) is again 4 across and 2 down — equal steps, so M is half-way along BC ✔.

  4. Answer AM=210AM = 2\sqrt{10}AM=210​ units

    Common trap: Using the mid-point of the wrong side (AB or AC). The median from A goes to the side opposite A.

Worked example 5

A trip on a town map

Question. On a town map, with 1 unit = 1 km, a school is at S(2, 3) and a library is at L(8, 11). Riya cycles in a straight line from the school towards the library and stops for juice one-quarter of the way along. (a) Where is the juice shop? (b) How far has she cycled, and how far is she from the library?
Print graph paper

Given: S(2, 3), L(8, 11); the shop is 14\dfrac{1}{4}41​ of the way from S. Asked: the shop's position and two distances.

Concept used: "one-quarter of the way from S" means the ratio 1:31 : 31:3; the distance formula. Why it applies: SJ is 1 part and JL is 3 parts of SL.

  1. 1

    The shop J divides SL in the ratio 1 : 3 (1 part from S, 3 parts to L).

  2. 2

    x=1×8+3×21+3=8+64=144=3.5x = \dfrac{1 \times 8 + 3 \times 2}{1 + 3} = \dfrac{8 + 6}{4} = \dfrac{14}{4} = 3.5x=1+31×8+3×2​=48+6​=414​=3.5.

  3. 3

    y=1×11+3×31+3=11+94=204=5y = \dfrac{1 \times 11 + 3 \times 3}{1 + 3} = \dfrac{11 + 9}{4} = \dfrac{20}{4} = 5y=1+31×11+3×3​=411+9​=420​=5.

  4. 4

    (a) The shop is at J(3.5,5)J(3.5, 5)J(3.5,5).

  5. 5

    SL=(8−2)2+(11−3)2=36+64=100=10SL = \sqrt{(8 - 2)^2 + (11 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10SL=(8−2)2+(11−3)2​=36+64​=100​=10 km.

  6. 6

    (b) She has cycled 14×10=2.5\dfrac{1}{4} \times 10 = 2.541​×10=2.5 km, and she is 10−2.5=7.510 - 2.5 = 7.510−2.5=7.5 km from the library.

  7. Check: SJ=1.52+22=2.25+4=6.25=2.5SJ = \sqrt{1.5^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{2.25 + 4} = \sqrt{6.25} = 2.5SJ=1.52+22​=2.25+4​=6.25​=2.5 km ✔.

  8. Answer (a) (3.5,5)(3.5, 5)(3.5,5) (b) 2.5 km cycled; 7.5 km still to go.

    Common trap: Using the ratio 1:41 : 41:4. "One-quarter of the way" splits the whole into 4 parts: 1 part done and 3 parts left, so the ratio is 1:31 : 31:3.

Common mistakes!

  • "Mid-point of AB = mid-point of CD" for parallelogram ABCD

    AB and CD are sides, not diagonals.

    Diagonals join opposite vertices: mid-point of AC = mid-point of BD.

  • Ignoring "taken in order"

    The order decides which vertices are opposite.

    Sketch the vertices in the given order before pairing them.

  • Finding the mid-point of the centre and the known end

    The centre is the mid-point.

    Other end =2×= 2 \times=2× centre −-− known end.

  • Rhombus area =d1×d2= d_1 \times d_2=d1​×d2​

    The formula has a half.

    Area =12×d1×d2= \dfrac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2=21​×d1​×d2​.

  • Median to the mid-point of the wrong side

    A median goes to the opposite side.

    Median from A → mid-point of BC.

  • "One-quarter of the way" as the ratio 1 : 4

    The parts are 1 done and 3 left.

    1n\dfrac{1}{n}n1​ of the way from A → ratio 1:(n−1)1 : (n - 1)1:(n−1).

Key points

  • Parallelogram ABCD (in order): mid-point of AC = mid-point of BD, so D=A+C−BD = A + C - BD=A+C−B (coordinate by coordinate).
  • The centre of a circle is the mid-point of every diameter: other end =2×= 2 \times=2× centre −-− known end.
  • Rhombus area =12×d1×d2= \dfrac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2=21​×d1​×d2​; check the four sides first if the shape is not given.
  • Median: mid-point of the opposite side, then the distance.
  • In grid problems, choose the axes, write each place as (x,y)(x, y)(x,y), and keep the units.
  • Area of a triangle from coordinates is not in the 2026-27 syllabus.
