The whole chapter on one page

Words — the line of sight joins the observer's eye to the point viewed. The angle of elevation is between the line of sight and the horizontal when the point is above the eye; the angle of depression when it is below. Both are measured from the horizontal, never from the vertical. Towers, poles and walls are vertical, so they make right angles with the ground.

The depression rule — the angle of depression from the top equals the angle of elevation from the ground point (alternate angles between two horizontals). Move it into the triangle at the ground point; the angle of the triangle at the top is 90°−θ90° - \theta90°−θ.

One right triangle — draw → name the sides from the known angle → pick the ratio: height & ground distance → tan; height & slant length (ladder, rope, string, line of sight) → sin; ground distance & slant length → cos → write it with letters, then numbers. With an eye height, the triangle starts at the eye: add the eye height at the end. An angle from two lengths: work out tan θ and read 30°, 45° or 60°. The Sun's altitude is the angle at the tip of the shadow.

Two right triangles — find the shared side. Shared height h: ground distances h3h\sqrt{3}h3​ (30°), hhh (45°), h3\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}}3​h​ (60°); same side → the given length is the difference, opposite sides → the sum; an object at a fixed height → its height above the eye is shared. Shared distance d: heights d3\dfrac{d}{\sqrt{3}}3​d​, ddd, d3d\sqrt{3}d3​; something on top → difference of heights; from a roof up and down → tower = part above + roof height.

Surds and decimals — c3=c33\dfrac{c}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{c\sqrt{3}}{3}3​c​=3c3​​; c3−1=c(3+1)2\dfrac{c}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \dfrac{c(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{2}3​−1c​=2c(3​+1)​; exact answer first, decimal last, with the value of 3\sqrt{3}3​ the question gives.

Syllabus limits: at most two right triangles; angles of elevation and depression only 30°, 45° and 60°.
