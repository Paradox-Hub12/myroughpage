Section 9.1, page 134The angles in Fig. 8.31 mark

Now, you may identify the lines of sight, and the angles so formed in Fig. 8.3. Are they angles of elevation or angles of depression?

(Fig. 8.3 of Chapter 8, described: a hot air balloon at A and, later, at B, at about the same height; a girl and her mother stand on the ground at the bottom left and look up at it. Dotted lines go from them to A and to B, and vertical dotted lines come down from A and B to the ground, each with a right-angle mark.)

Given: the girl on the ground and the balloon at A and then at B. Asked: the lines of sight, and the name of the angles they make.

Concept used: the line of sight joins the eye to the point viewed; the angle is one of elevation when the point is above the horizontal through the eye (Topic 2). Why it applies: the balloon is high in the sky, above the girl's eye.

  1. 1

    The lines of sight are the two lines from the girl's eye to the balloon: one to A and one to B.

  2. 2

    Each line of sight makes an angle with the horizontal through her eye (the ground line in the figure).

  3. 3

    A and B are both above that horizontal — she raises her head to look at the balloon.

  4. 4

    So both angles are angles of elevation. (B is farther away than A along the ground at a similar height, so the angle to B is the smaller one.)

  5. Check: each line of sight, the vertical from the balloon and the ground make a right triangle with the right angle at the ground — exactly the figure of a height problem with an angle of elevation ✔.

  6. Answer The lines of sight are from the girl's eye to A and to B; both angles are angles of elevation.

    Common trap: Calling them angles of depression because the dotted lines are drawn "coming down" from the balloon. The angle is named from the observer: the girl looks up.

Exercise 9.1, Q1The circus artist's rope2 marks
  1. A circus artist is climbing a 20 m long rope, which is tightly stretched and tied from the top of a vertical pole to the ground. Find the height of the pole, if the angle made by the rope with the ground level is 30° (see Fig. 9.11).

(Fig. 9.11, described: a vertical pole AB with A at the top and B on the ground (right angle at B); the rope AC goes from A down to C on the ground, labelled 20 m; the angle at C between the rope and the ground is 30°.)

Given: rope AC=20AC = 20AC=20 m (tight, so straight); ∠ACB=30°\angle ACB = 30°∠ACB=30°. Asked: the height AB.

Concept used: height and slant length → sin (Topic 3). Why it applies: the rope is the hypotenuse of the right triangle ABC.

  1. 1

    In right triangle ABC (right-angled at B), AB is opposite the 30° angle and AC is the hypotenuse.

    sin⁡30°=ABAC\sin 30° = \dfrac{AB}{AC}sin30°=ACAB​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Substitute.

    12=AB20\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{AB}{20}21​=20AB​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Multiply by 20: AB=10AB = 10AB=10.

  4. Check: at 30° the side opposite is half the hypotenuse — half of 20 m is 10 m ✔.

  5. Answer The pole is 10 m high.

    Common trap: Using tan⁡30°=AB20\tan 30° = \dfrac{AB}{20}tan30°=20AB​, which treats the rope as if it lay along the ground and gives 203\dfrac{20}{\sqrt{3}}3​20​ m.
Exercise 9.1, Q2The broken tree3 marks
  1. A tree breaks due to storm and the broken part bends so that the top of the tree touches the ground making an angle 30° with it. The distance between the foot of the tree to the point where the top touches the ground is 8 m. Find the height of the tree.

Given: the standing part is vertical; the broken part slants down to the ground at 30°; the top lands 8 m from the foot. Asked: the height of the tree before it broke.

Concept used: one right triangle; the height of the tree = standing part + broken part. tan gives the standing part, cos gives the broken (slant) part (Topic 3). Why it applies: the standing part, the ground and the broken part form a right triangle with the right angle at the foot.

  1. 1

    Let B be the foot, A the point where the tree broke and C the point where the top touches the ground. Then AB is the standing part, AC the broken part, BC=8BC = 8BC=8 m and ∠ACB=30°\angle ACB = 30°∠ACB=30°.

  2. 2

    Standing part (opposite and adjacent → tan):

    tan⁡30°=ABBC  ⇒  13=AB8  ⇒  AB=83\tan 30° = \dfrac{AB}{BC} \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{AB}{8} \;\Rightarrow\; AB = \dfrac{8}{\sqrt{3}}tan30°=BCAB​⇒3​1​=8AB​⇒AB=3​8​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Broken part (adjacent and hypotenuse → cos):

    cos⁡30°=BCAC  ⇒  32=8AC  ⇒  AC=163\cos 30° = \dfrac{BC}{AC} \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{8}{AC} \;\Rightarrow\; AC = \dfrac{16}{\sqrt{3}}cos30°=ACBC​⇒23​​=AC8​⇒AC=3​16​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Height of the tree =AB+AC=83+163=243= AB + AC = \dfrac{8}{\sqrt{3}} + \dfrac{16}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{24}{\sqrt{3}}=AB+AC=3​8​+3​16​=3​24​.

  5. 5

    Rationalise: 243=2433=83\dfrac{24}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{24\sqrt{3}}{3} = 8\sqrt{3}3​24​=3243​​=83​.

  6. Check: at 30° the side opposite is half the hypotenuse: AB=83AB = \dfrac{8}{\sqrt{3}}AB=3​8​ is half of AC=163AC = \dfrac{16}{\sqrt{3}}AC=3​16​ ✔. Decimal: 83≈13.868\sqrt{3} \approx 13.8683​≈13.86 m.

  7. Answer The tree was 838\sqrt{3}83​ m (about 13.86 m) high.

    Common trap: Giving only AB, the part still standing. The tree's height is the standing part plus the broken part that now lies slanting.
Exercise 9.1, Q3Two slides in a park3 marks
  1. A contractor plans to install two slides for the children to play in a park. For the children below the age of 5 years, she prefers to have a slide whose top is at a height of 1.5 m, and is inclined at an angle of 30° to the ground, whereas for elder children, she wants to have a steep slide at a height of 3m, and inclined at an angle of 60° to the ground. Given: slide 1: height 1.5 m, angle 30°; slide 2: height 3 m, angle 60°. Asked: the length of each slide.

Concept used: height and slant length → sin (Topic 3). Why it applies: each slide is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose vertical side is the height of its top.

  1. 1

    Slide 1: let its length be L1L_1L1​. The height 1.5 m is opposite the 30° angle.

    sin⁡30°=1.5L1\sin 30° = \dfrac{1.5}{L_1}sin30°=L1​1.5​Swipe to see the whole equation →
    12=1.5L1  ⇒  L1=3\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1.5}{L_1} \;\Rightarrow\; L_1 = 321​=L1​1.5​⇒L1​=3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Slide 2: let its length be L2L_2L2​. The height 3 m is opposite the 60° angle.

    sin⁡60°=3L2  ⇒  32=3L2  ⇒  L2=63\sin 60° = \dfrac{3}{L_2} \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{3}{L_2} \;\Rightarrow\; L_2 = \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}}sin60°=L2​3​⇒23​​=L2​3​⇒L2​=3​6​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Rationalise: L2=633=23L_2 = \dfrac{6\sqrt{3}}{3} = 2\sqrt{3}L2​=363​​=23​.

  4. Check: each slide is longer than its height (3>1.53 > 1.53>1.5 and 23≈3.46>32\sqrt{3} \approx 3.46 > 323​≈3.46>3) ✔; the steep 60° slide is only a little longer than its height, as a steep slide should be ✔.

  5. Answer 3 m for the younger children; 232\sqrt{3}23​ m (about 3.46 m) for the elder children.

    Common trap: Using tan: tan⁡60°=3L2\tan 60° = \dfrac{3}{L_2}tan60°=L2​3​ gives 3\sqrt{3}3​ m — shorter than the height, which is impossible for a slide.

  1. The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground, which is 30 m away from the foot of the tower, is 30°. Find the height of the tower.

Concept used: height and ground distance → tan (Topic 3). Why it applies: the tower is vertical, so the tower, the ground and the line of sight make a right triangle.

  2. 2

    AB=303AB = \dfrac{30}{\sqrt{3}}AB=3​30​.

  3. 3

    Rationalise: AB=3033=103AB = \dfrac{30\sqrt{3}}{3} = 10\sqrt{3}AB=3303​​=103​.

  4. Check: a 30° angle means the tower is shorter than the distance: 103≈17.32<3010\sqrt{3} \approx 17.32 < 30103​≈17.32<30 ✔.

  5. Answer 10310\sqrt{3}103​ m (about 17.32 m)

Exercise 9.1, Q6The boy walks towards the building3 marks
  1. A 1.5 m tall boy is standing at some distance from a 30 m tall building. The angle of elevation from his eyes to the top of the building increases from 30° to 60° as he walks towards the building. Find the distance he walked towards the building.

Given: eye height 1.5 m (taken as the boy's height); building 30 m; angles 30° then 60°. Asked: the distance walked.

Concept used: the triangles start at the eye; shared height above the eye; the walk is the difference of the two horizontal distances (Topics 3 and 4). Why it applies: both positions look at the same top, on the same side.

  1. 1

    Height of the top above the boy's eyes: 30−1.5=28.530 - 1.5 = 28.530−1.5=28.5 m.

  2. 2

    Let E₁ and E₂ be his eyes at the first and second positions and M the point on the building at eye level.

  3. 3

    First position (30°): tan⁡30°=28.5E1M\tan 30° = \dfrac{28.5}{E_1M}tan30°=E1​M28.5​, so E1M=28.53E_1M = 28.5\sqrt{3}E1​M=28.53​.

  4. 4

    Second position (60°): tan⁡60°=28.5E2M\tan 60° = \dfrac{28.5}{E_2M}tan60°=E2​M28.5​, so E2M=28.53E_2M = \dfrac{28.5}{\sqrt{3}}E2​M=3​28.5​.

  5. 5

    Distance walked:

    E1E2=28.53−28.53=28.5×3−28.53=573E_1E_2 = 28.5\sqrt{3} - \dfrac{28.5}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{28.5 \times 3 - 28.5}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{57}{\sqrt{3}}E1​E2​=28.53​−3​28.5​=3​28.5×3−28.5​=3​57​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Rationalise: 5733=193\dfrac{57\sqrt{3}}{3} = 19\sqrt{3}3573​​=193​.

  7. Check: with 3≈1.732\sqrt{3} \approx 1.7323​≈1.732: E1M≈49.36E_1M \approx 49.36E1​M≈49.36 m and E2M≈16.45E_2M \approx 16.45E2​M≈16.45 m; the difference is about 32.91 m, and 19×1.732=32.90819 \times 1.732 = 32.90819×1.732=32.908 ✔.

  8. Answer 19319\sqrt{3}193​ m (about 32.91 m)

    Common trap: Using 30 m as the vertical side. The angles are measured from the eyes, so the vertical side is 30−1.5=28.530 - 1.5 = 28.530−1.5=28.5 m (with 30 m the answer would be 20320\sqrt{3}203​ m).

  1. From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and the top of a transmission tower fixed at the top of a 20 m high building are 45° and 60° respectively. Find the height of the tower.

Given: building 20 m; angle to the bottom of the tower (= top of the building) 45°; to the top of the tower 60°. Asked: the height of the tower.

Concept used: shared horizontal distance; the tower is the difference of the two heights (Topic 5). Why it applies: both tops are on one vertical line.

  1. 1

    Let BC be the building (BC=20BC = 20BC=20 m), CD the tower and P the point, PB=dPB = dPB=d.

  2. 2

    In right triangle PBC: tan⁡45°=20d\tan 45° = \dfrac{20}{d}tan45°=d20​, so d=20d = 20d=20.

  3. 3

    In right triangle PBD: tan⁡60°=BDd\tan 60° = \dfrac{BD}{d}tan60°=dBD​, so BD=203BD = 20\sqrt{3}BD=203​.

  4. 4

    Tower: CD=BD−BC=203−20=20(3−1)CD = BD - BC = 20\sqrt{3} - 20 = 20(\sqrt{3} - 1)CD=BD−BC=203​−20=20(3​−1).

  5. Check: 20(3−1)≈14.6420(\sqrt{3} - 1) \approx 14.6420(3​−1)≈14.64 m, and 20+14.64=34.64≈20320 + 14.64 = 34.64 \approx 20\sqrt{3}20+14.64=34.64≈203​ ✔.

  6. Answer 20(3−1)20(\sqrt{3} - 1)20(3​−1) m (about 14.64 m)

    Common trap: Giving 20320\sqrt{3}203​ m — the height of the tower's top above the ground, not the tower.

    In right triangle ABD: tan⁡60°=h+1.6d\tan 60° = \dfrac{h + 1.6}{d}tan60°=dh+1.6​, so h+1.6=d3=h3h + 1.6 = d\sqrt{3} = h\sqrt{3}h+1.6=d3​=h3​.

    Rationalise: h=1.6(3+1)2=0.8(3+1)h = \dfrac{1.6(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{2} = 0.8(\sqrt{3} + 1)h=21.6(3​+1)​=0.8(3​+1).

  1. The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of the tower is 30° and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is 60°. If the tower is 50 m high, find the height of the building.

Concept used: the distance between the feet is shared (Topic 5). Why it applies: each triangle has its right angle at the foot of one structure and its angle at the foot of the other.

  1. 1

    Let AB be the tower (AB=50AB = 50AB=50 m), CD the building, and BD=dBD = dBD=d the distance between their feet.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    In right triangle CDB (30° at B, the tower's foot): tan⁡30°=CDd\tan 30° = \dfrac{CD}{d}tan30°=dCD​, so CD=d3=503×3=503CD = \dfrac{d}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{50}{\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{50}{3}CD=3​d​=3​×3​50​=350​.

  4. 4

    503=1623\dfrac{50}{3} = 16\dfrac{2}{3}350​=1632​.

  5. Check: the tower is seen at the bigger angle from the same distance, so it is the taller — exactly 3 times the building, since tan⁡60°=3tan⁡30°\tan 60° = 3\tan 30°tan60°=3tan30°: 3×503=503 \times \dfrac{50}{3} = 503×350​=50 ✔.

  6. Answer 503\dfrac{50}{3}350​ m, that is 162316\dfrac{2}{3}1632​ m

    Common trap: Attaching the 30° to the tower: tan⁡30°=50d\tan 30° = \dfrac{50}{d}tan30°=d50​. The 30° is the angle at the tower's foot, looking at the building's top.

  1. From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. Determine the height of the tower.

Given: building 7 m; elevation of the tower's top 60°; depression of its foot 45°. Asked: the height of the tower.

Concept used: the depression gives the shared distance; the tower = part above the roof + part below (Topic 5). Why it applies: the horizontal through the roof cuts the tower into two parts.

  1. 1

    Let AB be the building (A the roof), CD the tower (C its top, D its foot) and AE the horizontal from A to the tower; AE=BDAE = BDAE=BD and ED=AB=7ED = AB = 7ED=AB=7 m.

  2. 2

    Depression 45° to D, so ∠ADB=45°\angle ADB = 45°∠ADB=45° (alternate angles): tan⁡45°=7BD\tan 45° = \dfrac{7}{BD}tan45°=BD7​, so BD=7BD = 7BD=7 and AE=7AE = 7AE=7.

  3. 3

    In right triangle AEC: tan⁡60°=CEAE\tan 60° = \dfrac{CE}{AE}tan60°=AECE​, so CE=73CE = 7\sqrt{3}CE=73​.

  4. 4

    Tower: CD=CE+ED=73+7=7(3+1)CD = CE + ED = 7\sqrt{3} + 7 = 7(\sqrt{3} + 1)CD=CE+ED=73​+7=7(3​+1).

  5. Check: 7(3+1)≈19.127(\sqrt{3} + 1) \approx 19.127(3​+1)≈19.12 m; the part above the roof, 73≈12.127\sqrt{3} \approx 12.1273​≈12.12 m, is 3\sqrt{3}3​ times the distance 7 m, as a 60° angle requires ✔.

  6. Answer 7(3+1)7(\sqrt{3} + 1)7(3​+1) m (about 19.12 m)

    Common trap: Giving 737\sqrt{3}73​ m — only the part of the tower above the roof.

Exercise 9.1, Q15The car on the highway3 marks
  1. A straight highway leads to the foot of a tower. A man standing at the top of the tower observes a car at an angle of depression of 30°, which is approaching the foot of the tower with a uniform speed. Six seconds later, the angle of depression of the car is found to be 60°. Find the time taken by the car to reach the foot of the tower from this point.

Given: angles of depression 30° then 60°, 6 seconds apart; uniform speed. Asked: the time from the second position to the foot.

Concept used: shared height h; distances in terms of h; at a uniform speed, time is in proportion to distance (Topic 4). Why it applies: the car moves along the ground line through the foot of the tower.

  1. 1

    Let AB be the tower (AB=hAB = hAB=h), D the first position (30°) and C the second (60°). By alternate angles, ∠ADB=30°\angle ADB = 30°∠ADB=30° and ∠ACB=60°\angle ACB = 60°∠ACB=60°.

  2. 2

    BD=htan⁡30°=h3BD = \dfrac{h}{\tan 30°} = h\sqrt{3}BD=tan30°h​=h3​ and BC=htan⁡60°=h3BC = \dfrac{h}{\tan 60°} = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}}BC=tan60°h​=3​h​.

  3. 3

    In 6 s the car covers DC=h3−h3=3h−h3=2h3DC = h\sqrt{3} - \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3h - h}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{2h}{\sqrt{3}}DC=h3​−3​h​=3​3h−h​=3​2h​.

  4. 4

    It still has to cover BC=h3BC = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}}BC=3​h​, which is half of DC.

  5. 5

    At a uniform speed, half the distance takes half the time: 62=3\dfrac{6}{2} = 326​=3 s.

  6. Check: speed =2h3÷6=h33= \dfrac{2h}{\sqrt{3}} \div 6 = \dfrac{h}{3\sqrt{3}}=3​2h​÷6=33​h​ per second; time for BC =h3÷h33=3= \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}} \div \dfrac{h}{3\sqrt{3}} = 3=3​h​÷33​h​=3 s ✔ — the h cancels, so the answer does not depend on the height.

  7. Answer 3 seconds

    Common trap: Answering 6 s ("the angle doubled in 6 s, so…") or 12 s. Angles do not change at a steady rate; compare the distances.
