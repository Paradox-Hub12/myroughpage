Topic 5 of 5

Two right triangles with a shared distance

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Solve problems with one object on top of another seen from one point — a flagstaff, a statue on a pedestal, a tower on a building.
  • Solve problems with the angle of elevation of a top and the angle of depression of a foot seen from one building, and with two towers seen from each other's foot.
  • Solve sample-paper-style multi-part problems, including a line-of-sight (slant) distance and a Pythagoras step.

From the window of your classroom you can see the water tank on the next building. You look up to the top of the tank and down to the foot of the building it stands on. Two angles, one window, and only one horizontal distance between you and that building. In Topic 4 two triangles shared a height; here they share a horizontal distance — and that distance is usually the first thing to find.

The shared-distance method

Both right triangles have the same horizontal side d: the distance from the observer to the line of the vertical object(s).

  1. Draw both triangles. Mark the shared distance d once.
  2. Write each height in terms of d, from tan⁡θ=heightd\tan \theta = \dfrac{\text{height}}{d}tanθ=dheight​:
Angle Height reached
30° d3\dfrac{d}{\sqrt{3}}3​d​
45° ddd
60° d3d\sqrt{3}d3​
  1. Use the extra fact: the given length is a difference of the two heights (a flagstaff, a statue or a tower on top of something) or a sum (one height above the observer's level and one below it).
  2. Solve, rationalise, and give the decimal last.

Find d first when you can. If one triangle has a known height, it gives d at once; then the other triangle gives the unknown height.

Three standard figures

  • Something on top of something (seen from one point on the ground): the lower top at the smaller angle, the higher top at the bigger angle; the length of the thing on top is the difference of the two heights.
  • From the top of a building, the top and the foot of another: the angle of depression of the foot gives d (the building's height is the side opposite it); the angle of elevation of the top gives the part of the other tower above your level; the tower's height is that part plus the height of your building.
  • Two towers, each seen from the foot of the other: the distance between the feet is the shared side; the taller tower is the one seen at the bigger angle.

Slant lengths and Pythagoras

Some sample-paper questions give or ask for a line-of-sight distance (the slant distance from the eye to the object) instead of a ground distance. Then the ratio is sin or cos (Topic 3). A question may also ask for a length that joins two tops — such as a wire from one roof to another: once you know the horizontal gap and the difference in height, finish with Pythagoras' theorem (in a right triangle, hypotenuse2=one side2+other side2\text{hypotenuse}^2 = \text{one side}^2 + \text{other side}^2hypotenuse2=one side2+other side2).

Worked example 1

A tower on top of a building

Question. A mobile tower stands on the flat roof of a building 16 m high. From a point P on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the building is 30°, and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is 60°. Find the height of the tower and the distance of P from the building.

Given: building 16 m; angles 30° (to the roof) and 60° (to the tower top). Asked: the height of the tower; the distance BP.

Concept used: shared distance BP; the tower is the difference of the two heights. Why it applies: both tops are on one vertical line above B.

  1. 1

    Let BC be the building (BC=16BC = 16BC=16 m), CA the tower, and P the point, with BP=dBP = dBP=d m.

  2. 2

    In right triangle PBC: tan⁡30°=BCBP\tan 30° = \dfrac{BC}{BP}tan30°=BPBC​, so 13=16d\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{16}{d}3​1​=d16​ and d=163d = 16\sqrt{3}d=163​.

  3. 3

    In right triangle PBA: tan⁡60°=BABP\tan 60° = \dfrac{BA}{BP}tan60°=BPBA​, so BA=d3=163×3=48BA = d\sqrt{3} = 16\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 48BA=d3​=163​×3​=48.

  4. 4

    Height of the tower: CA=BA−BC=48−16=32CA = BA - BC = 48 - 16 = 32CA=BA−BC=48−16=32 m.

  5. Check: from the top A, tan⁡(angle at P)=48163=33=3\tan(\text{angle at P}) = \dfrac{48}{16\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}tan(angle at P)=163​48​=3​3​=3​, which is tan⁡60°\tan 60°tan60° ✔.

  6. Answer the tower is 32 m high; P is 16316\sqrt{3}163​ m (about 27.71 m) from the building.

    Common trap: Giving 48 m as the height of the tower. 48 m is the height of the tower's top above the ground; the tower itself is the part above the roof.

Worked example 2

A statue on a pedestal

Question. A statue 2.4 m tall stands on a pedestal. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the pedestal is 30° and the angle of elevation of the top of the statue is 45°. Find the height of the pedestal. (Take 3=1.732\sqrt{3} = 1.7323​=1.732.)

Given: statue 2.4 m; angles 30° (pedestal top) and 45° (statue top). Asked: the height of the pedestal.

Concept used: shared distance d; the statue is the difference of the two heights. Why it applies: both tops are above the same foot.

  1. 1

    Let the pedestal be BC (BC=hBC = hBC=h m), the statue CD (CD=2.4CD = 2.4CD=2.4 m) and P the point, with PB=dPB = dPB=d.

  2. 2

    In right triangle PBC: tan⁡30°=hd\tan 30° = \dfrac{h}{d}tan30°=dh​, so d=h3d = h\sqrt{3}d=h3​.

  3. 3

    In right triangle PBD: tan⁡45°=h+2.4d\tan 45° = \dfrac{h + 2.4}{d}tan45°=dh+2.4​, so d=h+2.4d = h + 2.4d=h+2.4.

  4. 4

    Both are d: h3=h+2.4h\sqrt{3} = h + 2.4h3​=h+2.4.

  5. 5

    So h(3−1)=2.4h(\sqrt{3} - 1) = 2.4h(3​−1)=2.4, and h=2.43−1=2.4(3+1)2=1.2(3+1)h = \dfrac{2.4}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \dfrac{2.4(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{2} = 1.2(\sqrt{3} + 1)h=3​−12.4​=22.4(3​+1)​=1.2(3​+1).

  6. 6

    Decimal: 1.2×2.732=3.27841.2 \times 2.732 = 3.27841.2×2.732=3.2784, about 3.28 m.

  7. Check: d=h+2.4=5.6784d = h + 2.4 = 5.6784d=h+2.4=5.6784 m and h3=3.2784×1.732≈5.678h\sqrt{3} = 3.2784 \times 1.732 \approx 5.678h3​=3.2784×1.732≈5.678 m — the two triangles give the same distance ✔.

  8. Answer 1.2(3+1)1.2(\sqrt{3} + 1)1.2(3​+1) m, about 3.28 m

    Common trap: Writing tan⁡45°=2.4d\tan 45° = \dfrac{2.4}{d}tan45°=d2.4​ — using only the statue as the height of the bigger triangle. Its vertical side runs from the ground to the top of the statue: h+2.4h + 2.4h+2.4.

Worked example 3

Up to the top, down to the foot

Question. From the top of a house 9 m high, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 30°. Find the height of the tower and the distance between the house and the tower.

Given: house 9 m; elevation 60°; depression 30°. Asked: the height of the tower; the distance between them.

Concept used: the depression angle gives the shared distance; the elevation gives the part of the tower above the roof; the tower is the sum of the two parts. Why it applies: the horizontal through the roof cuts the tower into a part above and a part below.

  1. 1

    Let AB be the house (A the roof), CD the tower (C the top, D the foot), and AE the horizontal from A to the tower, so AE=BD=dAE = BD = dAE=BD=d and ED=AB=9ED = AB = 9ED=AB=9 m.

  2. 2

    The angle of depression of D from A is 30°, so ∠ADB=30°\angle ADB = 30°∠ADB=30° (alternate angles). In right triangle ABD: tan⁡30°=ABBD=9d\tan 30° = \dfrac{AB}{BD} = \dfrac{9}{d}tan30°=BDAB​=d9​, so d=93d = 9\sqrt{3}d=93​.

  3. 3

    In right triangle AEC (right-angled at E), ∠CAE=60°\angle CAE = 60°∠CAE=60°: tan⁡60°=CEAE\tan 60° = \dfrac{CE}{AE}tan60°=AECE​, so CE=93×3=27CE = 9\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 27CE=93​×3​=27.

  4. 4

    Height of the tower: CD=CE+ED=27+9=36CD = CE + ED = 27 + 9 = 36CD=CE+ED=27+9=36 m.

  5. Check: put the answers back. The top C is 36−9=2736 - 9 = 2736−9=27 m above the roof and 939\sqrt{3}93​ m away: 2793=33=3=tan⁡60°\dfrac{27}{9\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3} = \tan 60°93​27​=3​3​=3​=tan60° ✔. The foot D is 9 m below the roof and 939\sqrt{3}93​ m away: 993=13=tan⁡30°\dfrac{9}{9\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \tan 30°93​9​=3​1​=tan30° ✔.

  6. Answer the tower is 36 m high; the distance is 939\sqrt{3}93​ m (about 15.59 m).

    Common trap: Answering 27 m — forgetting the 9 m of the tower below the roof level.

Worked example 4

Two towers seen from each other's foot

Question. Two towers stand on level ground. The angle of elevation of the top of the first tower from the foot of the second is 60°, and the angle of elevation of the top of the second tower from the foot of the first is 30°. The first tower is 36 m high. Find the distance between the towers and the height of the second tower.

Given: first tower 36 m; angles 60° and 30°. Asked: the distance; the height of the second tower.

Concept used: the distance between the feet is shared by both triangles. Why it applies: each triangle has its right angle at the foot of one tower and its angle at the foot of the other.

  1. 1

    Let AB be the first tower (AB=36AB = 36AB=36 m), CD the second, and BD=dBD = dBD=d.

  2. 2

    In right triangle ABD (angle 60° at D): tan⁡60°=ABBD\tan 60° = \dfrac{AB}{BD}tan60°=BDAB​, so 3=36d\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{36}{d}3​=d36​ and d=363=123d = \dfrac{36}{\sqrt{3}} = 12\sqrt{3}d=3​36​=123​.

  3. 3

    In right triangle CDB (angle 30° at B): tan⁡30°=CDBD\tan 30° = \dfrac{CD}{BD}tan30°=BDCD​, so CD=1233=12CD = \dfrac{12\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = 12CD=3​123​​=12.

  4. Check: the first tower is seen at the bigger angle from the same distance, so it should be the taller: 36 m against 12 m ✔ — exactly 3 times, as tan⁡60°=3tan⁡30°\tan 60° = 3\tan 30°tan60°=3tan30° ✔.

  5. Answer distance 12312\sqrt{3}123​ m (about 20.78 m); second tower 12 m

    Common trap: Putting both angles at the same foot — writing tan⁡30°=36d\tan 30° = \dfrac{36}{d}tan30°=d36​ for the first tower. Each angle belongs to the triangle of the tower being looked at.

Worked example 5

A line-of-sight distance and a second observer

Question. Ravi, standing on level ground, sees a drone at an angle of elevation of 45°; the drone is 60260\sqrt{2}602​ m away from him in a straight line. Meera stands on the flat roof of a building 24 m high on the other side of the drone, and sees the same drone at an angle of elevation of 30°. Find the distance between the drone and Meera. (Ignore the heights of Ravi's and Meera's eyes.)

Given: Ravi's line-of-sight distance 60260\sqrt{2}602​ m at 45°; roof 24 m; Meera's angle 30°. Asked: the line-of-sight distance from Meera to the drone.

Concept used: with a slant length, use sin (Topic 3): first the drone's height from Ravi's triangle, then Meera's triangle above the roof. Why it applies: the drone's height is shared; both distances asked or given are slant lengths.

  1. 1

    Let R be Ravi, D the drone and N the point on the ground below it. In right triangle RND: sin⁡45°=DNRD\sin 45° = \dfrac{DN}{RD}sin45°=RDDN​.

    12=DN602  ⇒  DN=60\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{DN}{60\sqrt{2}} \;\Rightarrow\; DN = 602​1​=602​DN​⇒DN=60Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Meera is at M on the roof, 24 m up. Let K be the point on DN level with the roof. Then DK=DN−KN=60−24=36DK = DN - KN = 60 - 24 = 36DK=DN−KN=60−24=36 m.

  3. 3

    In right triangle MKD (right-angled at K), ∠DMK=30°\angle DMK = 30°∠DMK=30° and MD is the hypotenuse: sin⁡30°=DKMD\sin 30° = \dfrac{DK}{MD}sin30°=MDDK​.

    12=36MD  ⇒  MD=72\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{36}{MD} \;\Rightarrow\; MD = 7221​=MD36​⇒MD=72Swipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: at 30° the side opposite is half the hypotenuse: 36 m is half of 72 m ✔. Ravi's horizontal distance is also 60 m (at 45° the height and the ground distance are equal), and 602+602=602\sqrt{60^2 + 60^2} = 60\sqrt{2}602+602​=602​ ✔.

  5. Answer 72 m

    Common trap: Using tan with the slant length, tan⁡45°=DN602\tan 45° = \dfrac{DN}{60\sqrt{2}}tan45°=602​DN​, which gives DN=602DN = 60\sqrt{2}DN=602​ m. "60260\sqrt{2}602​ m away in a straight line" is the hypotenuse.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that not finding the shared distance first, and adding or subtracting the wrong heights, are common reasons for lost marks here.

  • The thing on top (statue, flagstaff) used as the whole height of the bigger triangle

    The bigger triangle reaches from the ground to the top of the thing on top.

    Bigger triangle's height = lower height + length on top.

  • The tower's height given as only the part above the roof

    The horizontal through the roof cuts the tower in two.

    Tower = part above the roof + height of the roof.

  • Two different letters for the two distances when they are the same distance

    Both triangles stand on the same horizontal side.

    Mark d once; write both heights in terms of d.

  • tan used with a line-of-sight distance ("200 m away in a straight line")

    A straight-line distance from the eye is the hypotenuse.

    sin with the height; cos with the ground distance.

  • The roof height not subtracted when the observer is on a roof

    Meera's triangle starts at her level, not at the ground.

    Height above the roof = height of the object − height of the roof.

Key points

  • Two triangles with a shared horizontal distance d: heights d3\dfrac{d}{\sqrt{3}}3​d​ at 30°, d at 45°, d3d\sqrt{3}d3​ at 60°.
  • Something on top of something: its length is the difference of the two heights.
  • From a roof, up to a top and down to a foot: the depression gives d; tower = part above + part below.
  • Two towers seen from each other's foot: the distance between the feet is shared.
  • A straight-line (slant) distance → sin or cos; a line joining two tops → Pythagoras at the end.
