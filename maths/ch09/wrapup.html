Wrap-up

The values card, the ratio picker, the two-triangle patterns and mixed problems

About 30 minutes

Every question in this chapter is the same three moves: draw the figure, find the right triangle(s) and the shared side, use one ratio per triangle. This page puts the three cards you need on one sheet and then gives four mixed problems in the styles of the sample papers — an angle from lengths, a car at a steady speed, a building seen from a tower with 3=1.73\sqrt{3} = 1.733​=1.73, and a three-part case.

The ratio picker

The two sides in the question Ratio
height and ground distance tan
height and slant length (ladder, string, rope, line of sight) sin

Angles of depression move to the ground point (alternate angles). With an eye height, the triangle starts at the eye: add the eye height at the end.

The two-triangle patterns

Pattern Shared side The given length is…
walking towards a tower; two shadows; two ships on one side the height h the difference of the ground distances
two banks of a river; two poles across a road the height h the sum of the ground distances
balloon, drone or aircraft at a fixed height its height above the eye distance travelled = difference
statue, flagstaff or tower on top of something the distance d its length = difference of the heights
from a roof: top and foot of another tower the distance d tower = part above the roof + roof height

Mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

An angle, with an eye height

Question. A girl whose eyes are 1.6 m above the ground stands 434\sqrt{3}43​ m from the foot of a flagpole 13.6 m high. Find the angle of elevation of the top of the flagpole from her eyes.

Given: eye height 1.6 m; distance 434\sqrt{3}43​ m; flagpole 13.6 m. Asked: the angle of elevation.

Concept used: the triangle starts at the eye; tan = (height above the eye) ÷ (horizontal distance); then read the table. Why it applies: the angle is measured along the horizontal through the eye.

  1. 1

    Height of the top above her eyes: 13.6−1.6=1213.6 - 1.6 = 1213.6−1.6=12 m.

  2. 2

    Horizontal distance from her eyes to the pole: 434\sqrt{3}43​ m.

  3. 3

    Let θ be the angle of elevation.

    tan⁡θ=1243=33=3\tan \theta = \dfrac{12}{4\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}tanθ=43​12​=3​3​=3​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    tan⁡60°=3\tan 60° = \sqrt{3}tan60°=3​, so θ=60°\theta = 60°θ=60°.

  5. Check: the horizontal distance (43≈6.94\sqrt{3} \approx 6.943​≈6.9 m) is less than the height above the eye (12 m), so the angle must be more than 45° ✔.

  6. Answer 60°

    Common trap: Using the whole 13.6 m: tan⁡θ=13.643\tan \theta = \dfrac{13.6}{4\sqrt{3}}tanθ=43​13.6​ is not a table value — a sign that the eye height was forgotten.

Mixed example 2

A bus at a steady speed

Question. From the top of a tower 40 m high, a man sees a bus moving at a steady speed straight towards the foot of the tower. The angle of depression of the bus is 30°, and 2 minutes later it is 45°. Find (a) the speed of the bus in metres per minute (b) how much longer the bus will take to reach the foot of the tower. (Take 3=1.732\sqrt{3} = 1.7323​=1.732.)

Given: height 40 m; angles of depression 30° then 45°, 2 minutes apart. Asked: the speed; the remaining time.

Concept used: shared height; the distance covered is the difference of the two ground distances; speed = distance ÷ time. Why it applies: both positions are on the same side, in a line with the foot.

  1. 1

    Let AB be the tower (AB=40AB = 40AB=40 m), D the first position (30°) and C the second (45°). By alternate angles ∠ADB=30°\angle ADB = 30°∠ADB=30° and ∠ACB=45°\angle ACB = 45°∠ACB=45°.

  2. 2

    tan⁡30°=40BD\tan 30° = \dfrac{40}{BD}tan30°=BD40​, so BD=403BD = 40\sqrt{3}BD=403​; tan⁡45°=40BC\tan 45° = \dfrac{40}{BC}tan45°=BC40​, so BC=40BC = 40BC=40.

  3. 3

    Distance covered in 2 minutes: DC=403−40=40(3−1)DC = 40\sqrt{3} - 40 = 40(\sqrt{3} - 1)DC=403​−40=40(3​−1).

  4. 4

    (a) Speed =40(3−1)2=20(3−1)= \dfrac{40(\sqrt{3} - 1)}{2} = 20(\sqrt{3} - 1)=240(3​−1)​=20(3​−1) m per minute; 20×0.732=14.6420 \times 0.732 = 14.6420×0.732=14.64 m per minute.

  5. 5

    (b) Remaining distance BC=40BC = 40BC=40 m. Time =4020(3−1)=23−1=2(3+1)2=3+1= \dfrac{40}{20(\sqrt{3} - 1)} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \dfrac{2(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{2} = \sqrt{3} + 1=20(3​−1)40​=3​−12​=22(3​+1)​=3​+1 minutes, that is 2.732 minutes.

  6. Check: in 2.732 minutes at 14.64 m per minute the bus covers 2.732×14.64≈40.02.732 \times 14.64 \approx 40.02.732×14.64≈40.0 m — exactly BC ✔.

  7. Answer (a) 20(3−1)20(\sqrt{3} - 1)20(3​−1) m per minute, about 14.64 m per minute (b) (3+1)(\sqrt{3} + 1)(3​+1) minutes, about 2.73 minutes

    Common trap: Dividing the whole distance BD=403BD = 40\sqrt{3}BD=403​ m by 2 minutes for the speed. In 2 minutes the bus covers only DC, the difference.

Mixed example 3

A building seen from a tower

Question. The angles of depression of the top and the bottom of a building 24 m high, from the top of a tower, are 30° and 60° respectively. Find the height of the tower and the horizontal distance between the tower and the building. (Use 3=1.73\sqrt{3} = 1.733​=1.73.)

Given: building 24 m; angles of depression 30° (top) and 60° (bottom). Asked: the height of the tower; the distance.

Concept used: shared horizontal distance d; the tower is higher than the building by the part seen at 30°. Why it applies: the building's top and foot are on one vertical line, at the same distance from the tower.

  1. 1

    Let the tower be PQ (P the top, height H m), the building AB (A the top, B the foot) and QB=dQB = dQB=d.

  2. 2

    Depression 60° to B: in right triangle PQB, tan⁡60°=Hd\tan 60° = \dfrac{H}{d}tan60°=dH​, so H=d3H = d\sqrt{3}H=d3​.

  3. 3

    Depression 30° to A: the top of the tower is H−24H - 24H−24 m above A, so tan⁡30°=H−24d\tan 30° = \dfrac{H - 24}{d}tan30°=dH−24​, and H−24=d3H - 24 = \dfrac{d}{\sqrt{3}}H−24=3​d​.

  4. 4

    Subtract: d3−d3=24d\sqrt{3} - \dfrac{d}{\sqrt{3}} = 24d3​−3​d​=24. Multiply by 3\sqrt{3}3​: 3d−d=2433d - d = 24\sqrt{3}3d−d=243​, so d=123d = 12\sqrt{3}d=123​.

  5. 5

    H=123×3=36H = 12\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 36H=123​×3​=36 m.

  6. 6

    Decimal: d=12×1.73=20.76d = 12 \times 1.73 = 20.76d=12×1.73=20.76 m.

  7. Check: the top of the tower is 36−24=1236 - 24 = 1236−24=12 m above A, and 12123=13=tan⁡30°\dfrac{12}{12\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \tan 30°123​12​=3​1​=tan30° ✔.

  8. Answer tower 36 m; distance 12312\sqrt{3}123​ m, that is 20.76 m

    Common trap: Writing tan⁡30°=24d\tan 30° = \dfrac{24}{d}tan30°=d24​ — the 30° triangle's vertical side is the part of the tower above the building, H−24H - 24H−24, not the building.

Mixed example 4

Case: the clock tower

Question. A clock tower in a town square is 25.2 m tall. Asha, whose eyes are 1.2 m above the ground, stands on the level square and looks at the top of the tower.
(i) What is the angle of elevation of the top if she stands 24 m from the foot of the tower? (1)
(ii) How far from the foot should she stand to see the top at an angle of elevation of 60°? (1)
(iii) (A) From the position in (i) she walks straight back, away from the tower, until the angle of elevation is 30°. How far does she walk? (2)

  1. OR
    (iii) (B) Her friend stands where the angle of elevation is 30°, on the same side as the 60° position, in a line with the foot of the tower. How far apart are the 60° position and the friend? (2)
    (Take 3=1.732\sqrt{3} = 1.7323​=1.732.)

    Given: tower 25.2 m; eye height 1.2 m. Asked: four answers.

    Concept used: the triangle starts at the eye; the top is 25.2−1.2=2425.2 - 1.2 = 2425.2−1.2=24 m above her eyes; the shared-height method for (iii). Why it applies: every position looks at the same top from the same eye height.

  2. 1

    Height of the top above her eyes: 25.2−1.2=2425.2 - 1.2 = 2425.2−1.2=24 m.

  3. 2

    (i) tan⁡θ=2424=1\tan \theta = \dfrac{24}{24} = 1tanθ=2424​=1, so θ=45°\theta = 45°θ=45°.

  4. 3

    (ii) tan⁡60°=24x\tan 60° = \dfrac{24}{x}tan60°=x24​, so x=243=83x = \dfrac{24}{\sqrt{3}} = 8\sqrt{3}x=3​24​=83​ m, about 13.86 m.

  5. 4

    (iii)(A) At 30°: tan⁡30°=24y\tan 30° = \dfrac{24}{y}tan30°=y24​, so y=243y = 24\sqrt{3}y=243​ m. She walks 243−24=24(3−1)24\sqrt{3} - 24 = 24(\sqrt{3} - 1)243​−24=24(3​−1) m; 24×0.732=17.56824 \times 0.732 = 17.56824×0.732=17.568, about 17.57 m.

  6. 5

    (iii)(B) 243−83=16324\sqrt{3} - 8\sqrt{3} = 16\sqrt{3}243​−83​=163​ m; 16×1.732=27.71216 \times 1.732 = 27.71216×1.732=27.712, about 27.71 m.

  7. Check: (i) 45° because the horizontal distance (24 m) equals the height above the eye (24 m) ✔; (iii)(A) she ends 24+17.57=41.5724 + 17.57 = 41.5724+17.57=41.57 m from the tower, and 24×1.732=41.56824 \times 1.732 = 41.56824×1.732=41.568 ✔.

  8. Answer (i) 45° (ii) 838\sqrt{3}83​ m, about 13.86 m (iii)(A) 24(3−1)24(\sqrt{3} - 1)24(3​−1) m, about 17.57 m (iii)(B) 16316\sqrt{3}163​ m, about 27.71 m

    Common trap: In (i), using 25.2 m against 24 m: tan⁡θ=25.224\tan \theta = \dfrac{25.2}{24}tanθ=2425.2​ is not a table value — the eye height was not subtracted.
