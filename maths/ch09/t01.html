Toolkit: the values you need, surds and decimals

About 30 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Recall sin, cos and tan of 30°, 45° and 60°, and choose the ratio that links two given sides of a right triangle.
  • Simplify and rationalise surd expressions such as 24/√3 and 30/(√3 − 1), and solve equations such as h√3 = h + 18.
  • Give a decimal answer using the value of √3 that the question gives, rounding only at the end.
Why this lesson exists

Every question in this chapter ends in one of three ways: a table value such as tan⁡60°=3\tan 60° = \sqrt{3}tan60°=3​, a division by 3\sqrt{3}3​ or by 3−1\sqrt{3} - 13​−1, or a decimal with 3≈1.732\sqrt{3} \approx 1.7323​≈1.732. When an answer goes wrong, the slip is very often here — not in the picture. If you already have these skills, prove it below and go straight to Topic 2.

A surveyor stands 50 m from a temple tower and points a simple angle-finder at its top. She reads 60° — and in less than a minute she knows that the top is about 87 m above her angle-finder. She did not climb anything. She used one value from a table, tan⁡60°=3\tan 60° = \sqrt{3}tan60°=3​, and one multiplication. This chapter is full of such calculations. Half an hour here makes every one of them quick and safe.

  1. The only values you need

In this chapter the angles are always 30°, 45° or 60°, and you mostly need three ratios. From Chapter 8, for an acute angle of a right triangle:

sin⁡θ=oppositehypotenuse\sin \theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}sinθ=hypotenuseopposite​Swipe to see the whole equation →
cos⁡θ=adjacenthypotenuse\cos \theta = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}cosθ=hypotenuseadjacent​Swipe to see the whole equation →
tan⁡θ=oppositeadjacent\tan \theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}tanθ=adjacentopposite​Swipe to see the whole equation →
30°30°30° 45°45°45° 60°60°60°
sin⁡\sinsin 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ 12\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}2​1​ 32\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}23​​
cos⁡\coscos 32\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}23​​ 12\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}2​1​ 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​
tan⁡\tantan 13\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}3​1​ 111 3\sqrt{3}3​

Three facts that save marks:

  • tan grows with the angle: tan⁡30°=13≈0.58\tan 30° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.58tan30°=3​1​≈0.58, tan⁡45°=1\tan 45° = 1tan45°=1, tan⁡60°=3≈1.73\tan 60° = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.73tan60°=3​≈1.73. A steeper line of sight means a bigger tan.
  • At 45° the two shorter sides are equal (tan 45° = 1): the height equals the ground distance.
  • sin 30° = cos 60° = 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​: at 30° the side opposite is half the hypotenuse.

Which ratio? Look at the two sides the question links, measured from the angle you know:

  • opposite and adjacent (height and ground distance) → tan;
  • opposite and hypotenuse → sin;
  • adjacent and hypotenuse → cos.

  1. Multiplying and dividing by 3\sqrt{3}3​

  • 3×3=3\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 33​×3​=3.
  • Rationalise a single root: multiply top and bottom by 3\sqrt{3}3​. For example 243=2433=83\dfrac{24}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{24\sqrt{3}}{3} = 8\sqrt{3}3​24​=3243​​=83​.
  • A quick rule: dividing by 3\sqrt{3}3​ is the same as multiplying by 33\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}33​​.

  1. Denominators 3−1\sqrt{3} - 13​−1 and 3+1\sqrt{3} + 13​+1

Two-triangle problems often end with c3−1\dfrac{c}{\sqrt{3} - 1}3​−1c​. Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate 3+1\sqrt{3} + 13​+1 (same two terms, the other sign):

(3−1)(3+1)=(3)2−12(\sqrt{3} - 1)(\sqrt{3} + 1) = (\sqrt{3})^2 - 1^2(3​−1)(3​+1)=(3​)2−12Swipe to see the whole equation →
=3−1=2= 3 - 1 = 2=3−1=2Swipe to see the whole equation →

So c3−1=c(3+1)2\dfrac{c}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \dfrac{c(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{2}3​−1c​=2c(3​+1)​, and in the same way c3+1=c(3−1)2\dfrac{c}{\sqrt{3} + 1} = \dfrac{c(\sqrt{3} - 1)}{2}3​+1c​=2c(3​−1)​.

  1. Solving equations with 3\sqrt{3}3​

An equation such as h3=h+18h\sqrt{3} = h + 18h3​=h+18 is linear in h. Bring the h terms together, take h out as a common factor, then divide:

h3−h=18h\sqrt{3} - h = 18h3​−h=18Swipe to see the whole equation →
h(3−1)=18h(\sqrt{3} - 1) = 18h(3​−1)=18Swipe to see the whole equation →
h=183−1h = \dfrac{18}{\sqrt{3} - 1}h=3​−118​Swipe to see the whole equation →

and rationalise. If the equation has 13\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}3​1​ in it, multiply every term by 3\sqrt{3}3​ first.

  1. Decimals — only at the end

  • Keep the answer exact (9(3+1)9(\sqrt{3} + 1)9(3​+1) m) until the last line.
  • If the question says "take 3=1.732\sqrt{3} = 1.7323​=1.732" (or 1.73), use that value, and only then round. A question that gives no value expects the exact answer — a decimal is extra.
  • Rationalise before putting in the decimal: 9×2.7329 \times 2.7329×2.732 is easy, 18÷0.73218 \div 0.73218÷0.732 is not — and with a 3-decimal value of 3\sqrt{3}3​ the two can differ in the second decimal place.

A denominator 3−1\sqrt{3} - 13​−1

Solving h3=h+18h\sqrt{3} = h + 18h3​=h+18

Key points

  • You only need the 30°, 45°, 60° values; tan is 13\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}3​1​, 111, 3\sqrt{3}3​ — it grows with the angle.
