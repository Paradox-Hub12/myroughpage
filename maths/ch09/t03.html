Topic 3 of 5

Heights and distances with one right triangle

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Choose the right ratio — tan for a height and a ground distance, sin or cos when a slant length (ladder, rope, string, line of sight) is involved.
  • Find a height, a distance or a slant length from one right triangle, including the observer's eye height.
  • Find an angle of elevation or depression from two lengths.

How tall is the tallest building in your town? You could ask the builder — or you could walk away from it with a long tape until the top looks exactly "half-way up the sky" (45°), and then measure how far you walked: the height of the top above your eyes is the same as that distance. Every question in this topic is a version of that idea: one right triangle, one known side, one angle from the table.

The four-line method

  1. Draw the figure with the routine from Topic 2, and find the right triangle that has the known side and the side you want.
  2. Name the sides from the known angle: opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse.

(Reminder: the hypotenuse is the longest side, opposite the right angle; the opposite side faces the known angle; the adjacent side is the other side that touches it.)
3. Pick the ratio that has the known side and the wanted side — and nothing else:

The observer's eye height

If the observer's eyes are e metres above the ground, the right triangle sits on the horizontal through the eye. The trigonometry gives only the part of the height above eye level; then

height=part above eye level+e\text{height} = \text{part above eye level} + eheight=part above eye level+eSwipe to see the whole equation →

Finding an angle

If two sides are known, work out the ratio and read the angle from the table: tan⁡θ=3\tan \theta = \sqrt{3}tanθ=3​ means θ=60°\theta = 60°θ=60°, tan⁡θ=1\tan \theta = 1tanθ=1 means 45°, and tan⁡θ=13\tan \theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}tanθ=3​1​ means 30°. In this syllabus the angle asked for is always one of these three.

A quick sense check

  • At 45° the height and the ground distance are equal.
  • A steeper angle (60°) means you are closer than the height; a flatter angle (30°) means you are farther away than the height.
  • A slant length (ladder, string) is always longer than the height it reaches.

Visuals

The heights-and-distances explorer lets you drag an observer along the ground: the angle of elevation snaps to 30°, 45° or 60°, and the triangle, the tan ratio and the working change together.

Worked examples

Worked example 1

A height from a distance

Question. From a point on level ground 18 m from the foot of a vertical tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is 60°. Find the height of the tower. (Take 3=1.732\sqrt{3} = 1.7323​=1.732.)

Given: distance 18 m; angle of elevation 60°. Asked: the height.

Concept used: tan links the height (opposite) and the ground distance (adjacent). Why it applies: the tower is vertical, so the tower, the ground and the line of sight make a right triangle.

  1. 1

    Let AB be the tower (A the top, B the foot) and C the point, with BC=18BC = 18BC=18 m and ∠ACB=60°\angle ACB = 60°∠ACB=60°. Let AB=hAB = hAB=h m.

  2. 2

    In right triangle ABC (right-angled at B), AB is opposite the 60° angle and BC is adjacent to it.

    tan⁡60°=ABBC\tan 60° = \dfrac{AB}{BC}tan60°=BCAB​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Substitute.

    3=h18\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{h}{18}3​=18h​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Multiply both sides by 18: h=183h = 18\sqrt{3}h=183​.

  5. 5

    Decimal: 18×1.732=31.17618 \times 1.732 = 31.17618×1.732=31.176, about 31.18 m.

  6. Check: 60° is steeper than 45°, so the height must be more than the distance 18 m — and 31.18 m is ✔.

  7. Answer 18318\sqrt{3}183​ m, about 31.18 m

    Common trap: Using tan⁡60°=13\tan 60° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}tan60°=3​1​ (the 30° value): that gives 183=63\dfrac{18}{\sqrt{3}} = 6\sqrt{3}3​18​=63​ m, less than the distance — impossible for a 60° angle.

Worked example 2

A distance from a height (angle of depression)

Question. From the top of a lighthouse 45 m high above sea level, the angle of depression of a boat is 30°. How far is the boat from the foot of the lighthouse? (Take 3=1.732\sqrt{3} = 1.7323​=1.732.)

Given: height 45 m; angle of depression 30°. Asked: the distance of the boat.

Concept used: alternate angles move the 30° to the boat; tan links the height and the distance. Why it applies: the horizontal at the top is parallel to the sea.

  1. 1

    Let AB be the lighthouse (A the top, B the foot) and C the boat. The angle of depression of C from A is 30°, so ∠ACB=30°\angle ACB = 30°∠ACB=30° (alternate angles).

  2. 2

    In right triangle ABC, AB is opposite the 30° angle at C, and BC is adjacent.

    tan⁡30°=ABBC\tan 30° = \dfrac{AB}{BC}tan30°=BCAB​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Substitute.

    13=45BC\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{45}{BC}3​1​=BC45​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Cross-multiply: BC=453BC = 45\sqrt{3}BC=453​.

  5. 5

    Decimal: 45×1.732=77.9445 \times 1.732 = 77.9445×1.732=77.94 m.

  6. Check: a shallow angle (30°) means the boat is far away — farther than the height: 77.94 m is more than 45 m ✔.

  7. Answer 45345\sqrt{3}453​ m, about 77.94 m

    Common trap: Writing tan⁡30°=BCAB\tan 30° = \dfrac{BC}{AB}tan30°=ABBC​ (adjacent over opposite) — the ratio upside down. That gives BC=453=153BC = \dfrac{45}{\sqrt{3}} = 15\sqrt{3}BC=3​45​=153​ m.

Worked example 3

A ladder (sin and tan)

Question. A ladder leans against a vertical wall and makes an angle of 60° with the level ground. Its top reaches a window 9 m above the ground. Find (a) the length of the ladder (b) how far the foot of the ladder is from the wall.

Given: height reached 9 m; angle with the ground 60°. Asked: the length of the ladder; the distance of its foot from the wall.

Concept used: the ladder is the hypotenuse: height and ladder → sin; height and ground distance → tan. Why it applies: wall, ground and ladder form a right triangle with the right angle at the foot of the wall.

  1. 1

    Let the wall be AB (A the window, B its foot) and C the foot of the ladder, so AC is the ladder and ∠ACB=60°\angle ACB = 60°∠ACB=60°, AB=9AB = 9AB=9 m.

  2. 2

    (a) AB is opposite the 60° angle and AC is the hypotenuse.

    sin⁡60°=ABAC  ⇒  32=9AC\sin 60° = \dfrac{AB}{AC} \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{9}{AC}sin60°=ACAB​⇒23​​=AC9​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Cross-multiply: 3×AC=18\sqrt{3} \times AC = 183​×AC=18, so AC=183=1833=63AC = \dfrac{18}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{18\sqrt{3}}{3} = 6\sqrt{3}AC=3​18​=3183​​=63​.

  4. 4

    (b) AB is opposite and BC is adjacent.

    tan⁡60°=ABBC  ⇒  3=9BC\tan 60° = \dfrac{AB}{BC} \;\Rightarrow\; \sqrt{3} = \dfrac{9}{BC}tan60°=BCAB​⇒3​=BC9​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    So BC=93=933=33BC = \dfrac{9}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{9\sqrt{3}}{3} = 3\sqrt{3}BC=3​9​=393​​=33​.

  6. Check: Pythagoras: BC2+AB2=(33)2+92=27+81=108BC^2 + AB^2 = (3\sqrt{3})^2 + 9^2 = 27 + 81 = 108BC2+AB2=(33​)2+92=27+81=108 and AC2=(63)2=36×3=108AC^2 = (6\sqrt{3})^2 = 36 \times 3 = 108AC2=(63​)2=36×3=108 ✔.

  7. Answer (a) 636\sqrt{3}63​ m (about 10.39 m) (b) 333\sqrt{3}33​ m (about 5.20 m)

    Common trap: Using tan for the ladder in (a): tan⁡60°=9AC\tan 60° = \dfrac{9}{AC}tan60°=AC9​ treats the ladder as if it lay along the ground, and gives 333\sqrt{3}33​ m — shorter than the height it reaches, which is impossible.

Worked example 4

A kite string

Question. A kite is flying on a string 90 m long. The string is tied to a point on the ground and makes an angle of 30° with the ground. Assuming there is no slack in the string, find the height of the kite above the ground.

Given: string 90 m (a straight slant length); angle 30°. Asked: the height of the kite.

Concept used: height and slant length → sin. Why it applies: the string is the hypotenuse of the right triangle (string, ground, vertical drop from the kite).

  1. 1

    Let K be the kite, O the point where the string is tied and N the point on the ground directly below K. Then OK=90OK = 90OK=90 m, ∠KON=30°\angle KON = 30°∠KON=30° and ∠KNO=90°\angle KNO = 90°∠KNO=90°. Let KN=hKN = hKN=h m.

  2. 2

    KN is opposite the 30° angle; OK is the hypotenuse.

    sin⁡30°=KNOK  ⇒  12=h90\sin 30° = \dfrac{KN}{OK} \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{h}{90}sin30°=OKKN​⇒21​=90h​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Multiply by 90: h=45h = 45h=45.

  4. Check: at 30° the side opposite is half the hypotenuse (Topic 1): half of 90 m is 45 m ✔.

  5. Answer 45 m

    Common trap: Using tan⁡30°=h90\tan 30° = \dfrac{h}{90}tan30°=90h​, as if 90 m were measured along the ground; it gives 30330\sqrt{3}303​ m.

Worked example 5

Adding the eye height

Question. A man whose eyes are 1.4 m above the ground stands 26.6 m from a building. The angle of elevation of the top of the building from his eyes is 45°. Find the height of the building.

Given: eye height 1.4 m; distance 26.6 m; angle 45°. Asked: the height of the building.

Concept used: the triangle starts at the eye; tan gives the part above eye level; then add the eye height. Why it applies: the angle is measured along the horizontal through the eye.

  1. 1

    Let PQ be the building, B the man's feet and E his eye (BE=1.4BE = 1.4BE=1.4 m). Draw the horizontal EM to the building, so EM=BQ=26.6EM = BQ = 26.6EM=BQ=26.6 m and MQ=1.4MQ = 1.4MQ=1.4 m.

  2. 2

    In right triangle EMP (right-angled at M), ∠PEM=45°\angle PEM = 45°∠PEM=45°; PM is opposite and EM is adjacent.

    tan⁡45°=PMEM  ⇒  1=PM26.6\tan 45° = \dfrac{PM}{EM} \;\Rightarrow\; 1 = \dfrac{PM}{26.6}tan45°=EMPM​⇒1=26.6PM​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    So PM=26.6PM = 26.6PM=26.6 m.

  4. 4

    Height of the building =PM+MQ=26.6+1.4=28= PM + MQ = 26.6 + 1.4 = 28=PM+MQ=26.6+1.4=28 m.

  5. Check: at 45° the height above the eye equals the horizontal distance (26.6 m) ✔, and the building must be taller than that by the eye height ✔.

  6. Answer 28 m

    Common trap: Answering 26.6 m — the eye height has been forgotten. Or subtracting it: 26.6−1.4=25.226.6 - 1.4 = 25.226.6−1.4=25.2 m. The eye is above the ground, so the part below eye level is added.

Worked example 6

Finding the angle

Question. A building is 24 m tall. A point on the level ground is 838\sqrt{3}83​ m from its foot. Find the angle of elevation of the top of the building from this point.

Given: height 24 m; distance 838\sqrt{3}83​ m. Asked: the angle of elevation.

Concept used: tan of the angle = height ÷ ground distance; then read the table. Why it applies: both shorter sides of the right triangle are known.

  1. 1

    Let θ be the angle of elevation.

    tan⁡θ=heightdistance=2483\tan \theta = \dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{distance}} = \dfrac{24}{8\sqrt{3}}tanθ=distanceheight​=83​24​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Simplify: 2483=33=333=3\dfrac{24}{8\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{3} = \sqrt{3}83​24​=3​3​=333​​=3​.

  3. 3

    tan⁡60°=3\tan 60° = \sqrt{3}tan60°=3​, so θ=60°\theta = 60°θ=60°.

  4. Check: the distance 83≈13.98\sqrt{3} \approx 13.983​≈13.9 m is less than the height 24 m, so the angle must be more than 45° ✔.

  5. Answer 60°

    Common trap: Simplifying 33\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}}3​3​ to 13\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}3​1​ (the 3s "cancelled" wrongly) and answering 30°. 33=3×33=3\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}3​3​=3​3​×3​​=3​.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that choosing the wrong ratio, mixing up the table values and forgetting the eye height are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • tan used for a ladder, rope or kite string

    The slant length is the hypotenuse; tan does not contain it.

    Height & slant → sin; ground & slant → cos.

  • tan⁡30°=distanceheight\tan 30° = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{height}}tan30°=heightdistance​

    The ratio is upside down.

    tan = opposite ÷ adjacent, from the angle you know.

  • tan⁡60°=13\tan 60° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}tan60°=3​1​ or tan⁡60°=32\tan 60° = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}tan60°=23​​

    Table values mixed up (the first is tan 30°, the second sin 60°).

    tan: 13\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}3​1​, 111, 3\sqrt{3}3​ for 30°, 45°, 60°.

  • Answer stops at the part above eye level

    The eye is above the ground.

    Height = part above the eye + eye height.

  • 33=13\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}3​3​=3​1​

    3 is not cancelled by 3\sqrt{3}3​.

    33=3\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}3​3​=3​.

Key points

  • Four lines: draw → name the sides from the known angle → pick the ratio → write it with letters, then numbers.
  • Height & ground distance → tan; height & slant → sin; ground & slant → cos.
  • With an eye height: height = (part above eye level) + eye height.
  • An angle from two lengths: work out tan θ and read 30°, 45° or 60°.
  • Sense check: 45° → height = distance; 60° → closer than the height; 30° → farther.
