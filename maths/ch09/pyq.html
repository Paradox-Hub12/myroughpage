This section currently holds the heights-and-distances items from the CBSE Mathematics Sample Question Papers 2026-27 and 2025-26 — Mathematics Standard (041) and Mathematics Basic (241) — answered with their official marking schemes (newest first). Past year board questions, and the "Exam focus" analysis, will be added once the papers are sourced.

Both 2026-27 sample papers state that there is no change in the question paper design and assessment pattern for 2026-27, so the 2025-26 items remain good practice. Standard and Basic share the same syllabus, so every student should try all nine items. This chapter and Chapter 8 together make Unit V, Trigonometry, worth 12 marks in every paper. This chapter's share: 6 marks in the 2026-27 Standard paper (a 1-mark MCQ and a 5-mark question with an internal choice), 6 marks in the 2026-27 Basic paper (a 1-mark MCQ — a ladder problem solved by Pythagoras — and a 5-mark question with an internal choice), 4 marks in the 2025-26 Standard paper (a case study) and 5 marks in the 2025-26 Basic paper (a 5-mark question with an internal choice). Every 5-mark question gives 1 mark for a correct figure. None of these items has a separate version for visually impaired candidates.

Each item has two parts:

  • Understand it — the full step-by-step explanation: Given / Asked → Concept used (and why) → steps → Check → answer → Common trap.
  • Write it in the exam — the answer as you should write it, following the official marking scheme, with marks shown. Where the scheme gives only a total for a group of lines, the split shown is inferred and marked so.

Question: A flying kite is tied to a point on the ground with the help of a string. The string makes an angle θ with the ground level such that tan⁡θ=125\tan \theta = \dfrac{12}{5}tanθ=512​. If the length of the string is 52 m, then the height of the kite above the ground is:

Given: tan⁡θ=125\tan \theta = \dfrac{12}{5}tanθ=512​; string 52 m (the hypotenuse). Asked: the height h.

Concept used: from one ratio, draw the triangle and find the others (Chapter 8); then height & slant length → sin (Topic 3). Why it applies: the angle here is not 30°, 45° or 60°, so we build sin⁡θ\sin \thetasinθ from tan⁡θ\tan \thetatanθ.

  1. 1

    tan⁡θ=125\tan \theta = \dfrac{12}{5}tanθ=512​: take opposite =12k= 12k=12k and adjacent =5k= 5k=5k.

  2. 2

    Hypotenuse by Pythagoras: (12k)2+(5k)2=169k2=13k\sqrt{(12k)^2 + (5k)^2} = \sqrt{169k^2} = 13k(12k)2+(5k)2​=169k2​=13k.

  3. 3

    So sin⁡θ=12k13k=1213\sin \theta = \dfrac{12k}{13k} = \dfrac{12}{13}sinθ=13k12k​=1312​.

  4. 4

    sin⁡θ=h52\sin \theta = \dfrac{h}{52}sinθ=52h​, so h=52×1213=48h = 52 \times \dfrac{12}{13} = 48h=52×1312​=48 m.

  5. Check: the ground distance is 52×513=2052 \times \dfrac{5}{13} = 2052×135​=20 m, and 4820=125\dfrac{48}{20} = \dfrac{12}{5}2048​=512​ ✔; also 202+482=400+2304=2704=52220^2 + 48^2 = 400 + 2304 = 2704 = 52^2202+482=400+2304=2704=522 ✔.

    Common trap: Using tan with the string: 52×125=124.852 \times \dfrac{12}{5} = 124.852×512​=124.8 m — higher than the string is long. Option (D) 50 m is close to the string and is a guess.
tan⁡θ=125⇒sin⁡θ=1213\tan \theta = \dfrac{12}{5} \Rightarrow \sin \theta = \dfrac{12}{13}tanθ=512​⇒sinθ=1312​; height =52×1213=48= 52 \times \dfrac{12}{13} = 48=52×1312​=48 m. Answer: (C) 48 m

Question: A ladder 15 m long reaches a window 12 m above the ground. The distance of the foot of the ladder from the base of the wall is

Given: ladder (hypotenuse) 15 m; height 12 m. Asked: the distance x of the foot from the wall.

Concept used: Pythagoras' theorem. Why it applies: wall, ground and ladder form a right triangle, and two sides are known — no angle is given.

  1. 1

    152=x2+12215^2 = x^2 + 12^2152=x2+122.

  2. 2

    225=x2+144225 = x^2 + 144225=x2+144, so x2=81x^2 = 81x2=81.

  3. 3

    x=9x = 9x=9 m (a length is positive).

  4. Check: 9, 12, 15 is 3 × (3, 4, 5), a right-triangle triple ✔.

    Common trap: Subtracting the lengths (15−12=315 - 12 = 315−12=3) or adding the squares (225+144\sqrt{225 + 144}225+144​). The ladder is the longest side: subtract the squares.
(Hypotenuse)2=(Base)2+(Perpendicular)2⇒152=(Base)2+122⇒(\text{Hypotenuse})^2 = (\text{Base})^2 + (\text{Perpendicular})^2 \Rightarrow 15^2 = (\text{Base})^2 + 12^2 \Rightarrow(Hypotenuse)2=(Base)2+(Perpendicular)2⇒152=(Base)2+122⇒ Base =9= 9=9 m. Answer: (B) 9 m

If the ambulance is moving at a constant speed then, find the total time taken by the ambulance to reach the site of accident.

(Note: this is the same kind of problem as NCERT Exercise 9.1 Q15, with minutes instead of seconds.)

Given: drone at A above the site B (height h); angles of depression 30° (ambulance at D) then 60° (at C), 12 minutes apart; constant speed. Asked: the total time from D to B.

  3. 3

  5. 5

  6. 6

  7. Check: BD=h3=18xBD = h\sqrt{3} = 18xBD=h3​=18x, and at x m per minute that takes 18 minutes ✔.
