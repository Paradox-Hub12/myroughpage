Toolkit: lines and rearranging

About 30 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Recognise a linear equation in two variables and write it in the form ax + by + c = 0.
  • Make a table of values and plot the straight line of a linear equation.
  • Rearrange an equation to make x or y the subject, and clear fractions and decimals.
Why this lesson exists

This chapter uses three skills from Classes 8 and 9 again and again: writing an equation neatly as ax+by+c=0ax + by + c = 0ax+by+c=0, drawing its straight line from a table of values, and rearranging it to make x or y the subject. When a question in this chapter goes wrong, the slip is usually in one of these — a sign lost while moving a term, or a fraction cleared from only one term. If you already have these skills, prove it below and go straight to Topic 2.

Work through this toolkit — it takes about 30 minutes. Then try the questions again.

Priya buys some pens and some notebooks — 10 items in all. How many of each? It could be 3 pens and 7 notebooks, or 6 and 4, or 0 and 10… With x pens and y notebooks, the one equation x+y=10x + y = 10x+y=10 has many answers. Plot them all and something neat happens: they line up on a straight line. One equation in two unknowns gives a whole line of answers. In this chapter you will see that two such equations usually pin down a single answer — the point where two lines cross.

  1. Linear equations in two variables

A linear equation in two variables can be written as

ax+by+c=0ax + by + c = 0ax+by+c=0Swipe to see the whole equation →

where a, b and c are real numbers and a and b are not both zero. "Linear" means each variable appears only to the power 1, and the variables are not multiplied together.

  • A solution is a pair of values (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) that makes the equation true. (3,7)(3, 7)(3,7) is a solution of x+y=10x + y = 10x+y=10 because 3+7=103 + 7 = 103+7=10.
  • There are infinitely many solutions, and their points all lie on one straight line: the graph of the equation. Every point on the line is a solution, and every solution is a point on the line.

Writing an equation in the form ax+by+c=0ax + by + c = 0ax+by+c=0. Bring every term to the left side. A term that crosses the "=" changes its sign. If there are fractions or decimals, multiply every term by the LCM of the denominators (or by 10 or 100). Then read a, b and c with their signs.

Special lines. y=4y = 4y=4 is 0x+1y−4=00x + 1y - 4 = 00x+1y−4=0: a horizontal line through (0,4)(0, 4)(0,4). x=−2x = -2x=−2 is 1x+0y+2=01x + 0y + 2 = 01x+0y+2=0: a vertical line through (−2,0)(-2, 0)(−2,0). A coefficient may be 0 — but not both a and b.

  1. From a table of values to a line

  1. Make y the subject if that helps, or choose x-values that give whole-number y-values.
  2. Find three points. Two fix the line; the third is your check — if it does not land on the same straight line, there is an arithmetic slip.
  3. A quick pair of points: the intercepts. Put x=0x = 0x=0 to find where the line meets the y-axis, and y=0y = 0y=0 to find where it meets the x-axis.
  4. Choose a scale (often 1 cm = 1 unit), plot the points, join them with a ruler, extend the line beyond them, and label it with its equation.

  1. Rearranging: making x or y the subject

Do the same thing to both sides, one move at a time. To divide, divide every term. For example, from 2x−5y=42x - 5y = 42x−5y=4:

−5y=4−2x⇒y=4−2x−5=2x−45-5y = 4 - 2x \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad y = \dfrac{4 - 2x}{-5} = \dfrac{2x - 4}{5}−5y=4−2x⇒y=−54−2x​=52x−4​Swipe to see the whole equation →

To clear fractions first, multiply every term on both sides by the LCM of the denominators.

Worked example 1

Writing equations in the form ax + by + c = 0

Question. Write each equation in the form ax+by+c=0ax + by + c = 0ax+by+c=0 and state a, b and c: (a) 5x=3y+45x = 3y + 45x=3y+4 (b) y=2x3−1y = \dfrac{2x}{3} - 1y=32x​−1 (c) 0.4x−1.5=0.3y0.4x - 1.5 = 0.3y0.4x−1.5=0.3y (d) y=4y = 4y=4.

Given: four equations. Asked: each in general form, with a, b, c.

Concept used: move every term to the left (a term changes sign when it crosses "="); clear fractions and decimals. Why it applies: the general form needs everything on one side and whole-number coefficients are easiest to read.

  1. 1

    (a)

    Subtract 3y3y3y from both sides.

    5x−3y=45x - 3y = 45x−3y=4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Subtract 4 from both sides.

    5x−3y−4=05x - 3y - 4 = 05x−3y−4=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    (b)

    Multiply every term by 3.

    3y=2x−33y = 2x - 33y=2x−3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract 3y3y3y from both sides and write the x-term first.

    2x−3y−3=02x - 3y - 3 = 02x−3y−3=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    (c)

    Multiply every term by 10.

    4x−15=3y4x - 15 = 3y4x−15=3ySwipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Subtract 3y3y3y from both sides.

    4x−3y−15=04x - 3y - 15 = 04x−3y−15=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    (d)

    Subtract 4 from both sides; there is no x-term, so its coefficient is 0.

    0x+1y−4=00x + 1y - 4 = 00x+1y−4=0Swipe to see the whole equation →

  8. Check: each general form should have the same solutions as the original. Try (2,2)(2, 2)(2,2) in (a): 5(2)=105(2) = 105(2)=10 and 3(2)+4=103(2) + 4 = 103(2)+4=10 ✔; general form 10−6−4=010 - 6 - 4 = 010−6−4=0 ✔. Try (3,1)(3, 1)(3,1) in (b): 2(3)3−1=1\dfrac{2(3)}{3} - 1 = 132(3)​−1=1 ✔; general form 6−3−3=06 - 3 - 3 = 06−3−3=0 ✔.

Answer (a) 5x−3y−4=05x - 3y - 4 = 05x−3y−4=0; a=5a = 5a=5, b=−3b = -3b=−3, c=−4c = -4c=−4 (b) 2x−3y−3=02x - 3y - 3 = 02x−3y−3=0; a=2a = 2a=2, b=−3b = -3b=−3, c=−3c = -3c=−3 (c) 4x−3y−15=04x - 3y - 15 = 04x−3y−15=0; a=4a = 4a=4, b=−3b = -3b=−3, c=−15c = -15c=−15 (d) 0x+y−4=00x + y - 4 = 00x+y−4=0; a=0a = 0a=0, b=1b = 1b=1, c=−4c = -4c=−4

    Common trap: In (c), multiplying only the left side by 10 and writing 4x−15=0.3y4x - 15 = 0.3y4x−15=0.3y. Every term, on both sides, must be multiplied.

Worked example 2

Tables of values and plotting

Question. Draw the graphs of (a) 2x+y=52x + y = 52x+y=5 and (b) x−2y=2x - 2y = 2x−2y=2, using a table of three points for each.

Worked example 3

Rearranging, with fractions

Question. (a) Make y the subject of 3x−4y=103x - 4y = 103x−4y=10. (b) Clear the fractions in x3−y4=12\dfrac{x}{3} - \dfrac{y}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}3x​−4y​=21​, then make x the subject.

Given: two equations. Asked: as stated.

Concept used: do the same to both sides, one move at a time; multiply by the LCM to clear fractions. Why it applies: these are exactly the moves used in the substitution method (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    (a)

    Subtract 3x3x3x from both sides.

  2. 2

    y=10−3x−4y = \dfrac{10 - 3x}{-4}y=−410−3x​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Multiply the top and bottom by −1-1−1 to tidy the signs.

    y=3x−104y = \dfrac{3x - 10}{4}y=43x−10​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    (b)

    The LCM of 3, 4 and 2 is 12. Multiply every term by 12.

    4x−3y=64x - 3y = 64x−3y=6Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Add 3y3y3y to both sides.

    4x=3y+64x = 3y + 64x=3y+6Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Divide every term by 4.

    x=3y+64x = \dfrac{3y + 6}{4}x=43y+6​Swipe to see the whole equation →

Common mistakes!

  • 3x=2y−73x = 2y - 73x=2y−7 becomes 3x+2y−7=03x + 2y - 7 = 03x+2y−7=0

    Terms that cross "=" change sign.

    3x−2y+7=03x - 2y + 7 = 03x−2y+7=0.

  • Clearing fractions in one term only

    Multiplying one side (or one term) changes the equation.

    Multiply every term on both sides by the LCM.

  • Dividing one term only: 2x=9−5y⇒x=9−5y22x = 9 - 5y \Rightarrow x = 9 - \dfrac{5y}{2}2x=9−5y⇒x=9−25y​

    The 9 must be divided by 2 as well.

    x=9−5y2x = \dfrac{9 - 5y}{2}x=29−5y​.

  • Plotting (5,0)(5, 0)(5,0) for the point (0,5)(0, 5)(0,5)

    The first coordinate is always x.

    Read (x,y)(x, y)(x,y): across first, then up or down.

  • Drawing a line through two points only, with a slip

    A wrong point still gives a straight line.

    Plot a third point as a check.

Key points

  • A linear equation in two variables: ax+by+c=0ax + by + c = 0ax+by+c=0, with a and b not both zero. Its graph is a straight line.
  • Every solution is a point on the line, and every point on the line is a solution.
  • General form: move every term to one side (signs change), clear fractions by multiplying every term by the LCM, then read a, b, c with signs.
  • Table of values: three points (one as a check); intercepts come from x=0x = 0x=0 and y=0y = 0y=0.
  • y=ky = ky=k is a horizontal line (a=0a = 0a=0); x=kx = kx=k is a vertical line (b=0b = 0b=0).
