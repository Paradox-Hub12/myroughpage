Chapter 3 · Unit II

Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

6 topicsAbout 7 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Two mobile plans, two taxi fares, two bills at the canteen: whenever two facts link the same two unknowns, you have a pair of linear equations. Each equation is a straight line, and the answer is where the two lines meet — or, if they never meet, the facts cannot both be true. This chapter shows you three ways to find that meeting point (a graph, substitution and elimination), and a ten-second test that tells you, from the numbers alone, whether there is one answer, none, or infinitely many.

In the board exam this chapter is part of Unit II, Algebra (20 marks, shared with Polynomials, Quadratic Equations and Arithmetic Progressions), in both Mathematics Standard and Mathematics Basic. In the 2026-27 sample papers it supplies a 1-mark item and a 4-mark case study in Standard (5 marks), and two 1-mark items and a 3-mark question in Basic (5 marks).
