Topic 2 of 6

The graphical method

About 50 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Represent a pair of linear equations in two variables graphically.
  • Find the solution of a pair of linear equations from the point of intersection of their lines.
  • Classify a pair as intersecting, parallel or coincident from its graph; find the vertices and the area of the triangle formed by two lines and an axis.

Two mobile data plans: Plan A costs ₹ 100 a month plus ₹ 10 for each GB; Plan B costs ₹ 40 plus ₹ 25 for each GB. For x GB the monthly bill y is y=100+10xy = 100 + 10xy=100+10x on Plan A and y=40+25xy = 40 + 25xy=40+25x on Plan B. Draw both lines on one graph and they cross at (4,140)(4, 140)(4,140): at 4 GB both plans cost ₹ 140. Below 4 GB, Plan B is cheaper; above it, Plan A. The crossing point is the one answer that fits both equations — and that is what "solving a pair of equations" means.

A pair of linear equations

Two linear equations in the same two variables form a pair of linear equations. In general form:

a1x+b1y+c1=0a2x+b2y+c2=0a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0 \qquad a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0a1​x+b1​y+c1​=0a2​x+b2​y+c2​=0Swipe to see the whole equation →

A solution of the pair is a pair of values (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) that satisfies both equations at once. On a graph, it is a point that lies on both lines.

Three ways two lines can meet

Two straight lines drawn on the same graph can do only one of three things:

The lines… Points common to both Solutions of the pair Name of the pair
intersect (cross at one point) exactly one exactly one (a unique solution) consistent
are parallel (never meet) none no solution inconsistent
are coincident (the same line) every point of the line infinitely many solutions dependent (and consistent)

So: consistent means "has at least one solution"; inconsistent means "has none". A dependent pair is always consistent.

The graphical method, step by step

  1. Make a table of values with three points for each equation (Topic 1).
  2. Draw both lines on the same axes with the same scale. Label each line with its equation.
  3. Look at the lines: intersecting, parallel or coincident?
  4. If they intersect, read the coordinates of the point of intersection. That is the solution.
  5. Check by substituting the point into both equations. A reading that does not satisfy both is not the solution, however close it looks.

The graphical method shows the picture clearly, but it gives an exact answer only when the point of intersection is easy to read. For an answer like (197,37)\left(\dfrac{19}{7}, \dfrac{3}{7}\right)(719​,73​) you need the algebraic methods of Topics 4 and 5.

Triangles formed by two lines and an axis

Two intersecting lines and the x-axis usually form a triangle (unless the lines meet on the x-axis or one of them is parallel to it).

Quick reminder

Area of a triangle =12×base×height= \dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}=21​×base×height, where the height is measured perpendicular to the base.

  • Vertices: the point where the lines meet, and the two points where the lines cut the x-axis (put y=0y = 0y=0 in each equation).
  • Base: the distance between the two x-axis points.
  • Height: the distance of the meeting point from the x-axis, which is its y-coordinate (without the sign).

With the y-axis instead, use the y-intercepts (put x=0x = 0x=0) for the base, and the x-coordinate of the meeting point for the height.

Solving a pair graphically

Question. Solve graphically: 2x+y=82x + y = 82x+y=8 and x−y=1x - y = 1x−y=1.
Print graph paper

Given: two linear equations. Asked: their solution, found from a graph.

Concept used: the solution is the point common to both lines. Why it applies: a point on both lines satisfies both equations.

  1. 1

    Make a table for 2x+y=82x + y = 82x+y=8, i.e. y=8−2xy = 8 - 2xy=8−2x.

    x 0 2 4
    y=8−2xy = 8 - 2xy=8−2x 8 4 0
  2. 2

    Make a table for x−y=1x - y = 1x−y=1, i.e. y=x−1y = x - 1y=x−1.

    x 0 1 3
    y=x−1y = x - 1y=x−1 −1 0 2
  3. 3

    Plot both sets of points on the same axes (1 cm = 1 unit) and draw the two lines.

  4. 4

    The lines cross at one point. Read it: (3,2)(3, 2)(3,2).

  5. Check: substitute (3,2)(3, 2)(3,2) in both equations: 2(3)+2=82(3) + 2 = 82(3)+2=8 ✔ and 3−2=13 - 2 = 13−2=1 ✔.

  6. Answer The lines intersect at (3,2)(3, 2)(3,2), so x=3x = 3x=3, y=2y = 2y=2. The pair is consistent with a unique solution.

Worked example 2

A parallel pair and a coincident pair

Question. Draw the graphs and say how many solutions each pair has: (a) 3x+y=33x + y = 33x+y=3 and 6x+2y=126x + 2y = 126x+2y=12 (b) x−3y=3x - 3y = 3x−3y=3 and 2x−6y=62x - 6y = 62x−6y=6.
Print graph paper

Given: two pairs of equations. Asked: the picture and the number of solutions.

Concept used: parallel lines → no solution; coincident lines → infinitely many. Why it applies: we read the case from the graph.

  1. 1

    (a)

    Table for 3x+y=33x + y = 33x+y=3, i.e. y=3−3xy = 3 - 3xy=3−3x: points (0,3)(0, 3)(0,3), (1,0)(1, 0)(1,0), (2,−3)(2, -3)(2,−3).

  2. 2

    Table for 6x+2y=126x + 2y = 126x+2y=12, i.e. y=6−3xy = 6 - 3xy=6−3x: points (0,6)(0, 6)(0,6), (2,0)(2, 0)(2,0), (1,3)(1, 3)(1,3).

  3. 3

    Plot and draw. The lines slant the same way and stay the same distance apart: they are parallel.

  4. 4

    (b)

    Table for x−3y=3x - 3y = 3x−3y=3: points (0,−1)(0, -1)(0,−1), (3,0)(3, 0)(3,0), (6,1)(6, 1)(6,1).

  5. 5

    Table for 2x−6y=62x - 6y = 62x−6y=6: the same three points work, e.g. 2(6)−6(1)=62(6) - 6(1) = 62(6)−6(1)=6.

  6. 6

    Both equations give the same line: the lines are coincident.

  7. Check: (a) Every point on the first line has 3x+y=33x + y = 33x+y=3, so 6x+2y=2(3x+y)=66x + 2y = 2(3x + y) = 66x+2y=2(3x+y)=6, never 12 — no point is on both ✔. (b) 2x−6y=62x - 6y = 62x−6y=6 is x−3y=3x - 3y = 3x−3y=3 multiplied by 2, so every solution of one is a solution of the other ✔.

  8. Answer (a) Parallel lines: no solution (inconsistent). (b) Coincident lines: infinitely many solutions (dependent, consistent).

    Common trap: In (b), drawing two lines through different points and reporting "one solution" where they seem to cross. If one equation is a multiple of the other, the lines are the same.

The triangle formed with an axis

Quick check · 1 of 3MCQ1 mark

The graphs of a pair of linear equations are two parallel lines. The pair has:

Parallel lines never meet, so no point lies on both: the pair is inconsistent.
One solution needs the lines to cross at one point.
Infinitely many needs the lines to be the same line (coincident).
Two different straight lines meet at most once; a pair of linear equations never has exactly two solutions.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers see graphs — plotting, reading the point of intersection and the triangle area — as one of the four commonest reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • Plotting only two points per line

    A slip in one point still gives a straight line, through the wrong place.

    Plot three points; the third is a check.

  • Different scales for the two lines, or unlabelled lines

    The lines will not meet in the right place, and the examiner cannot tell which is which.

    Same axes, same scale; write each equation on its line.

  • Reading the intersection "roughly"

    A point close to the crossing is not the solution.

    Substitute the point into both equations before you write the answer.

  • Taking a point from one table as the solution

    It lies on one line only.

    The solution is the point common to both lines.

  • Using a slanting side as the height of the triangle

    Area needs the perpendicular height.

    With the x-axis, height = y-coordinate of the meeting point.

  • Using the y-intercepts when the triangle is with the x-axis

    The base must lie on the axis named in the question.

    x-axis → put y=0y = 0y=0; y-axis → put x=0x = 0x=0.

Key points

  • A solution of a pair is a point on both lines.
  • Intersecting lines → exactly one solution (consistent). Parallel lines → no solution (inconsistent). Coincident lines → infinitely many solutions (dependent, consistent).
  • Graphical method: three points per line, same axes and scale, read the intersection, check it in both equations.
  • Triangle with the x-axis: vertices = meeting point and the two x-intercepts; area =12×= \dfrac{1}{2} \times=21​× (distance between the x-intercepts) ×\times× (y-coordinate of the meeting point, without its sign).
  • With the y-axis, swap the roles: y-intercepts for the base, x-coordinate for the height.
