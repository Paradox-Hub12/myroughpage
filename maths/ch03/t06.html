Topic 6 of 6

Situational problems

About 60 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Form a pair of linear equations from a situation (ages, money and cost, digits, fractions, geometry, fixed and per-unit charges).
  • Solve the pair and check that the answer fits the situation.

A gym says: "Pay a joining fee once, then the same fee every month." Asha paid ₹ 4300 for 5 months; Ravi paid ₹ 7300 for 10 months. What is the joining fee? Neither bill tells you on its own — each one mixes two unknowns. Put the two bills together and the answer drops out. Many everyday puzzles work like this: two unknowns, two facts, one pair of equations.

The four-step routine

Use it for every situational problem, and write each step in your answer — marks are given for each one.

  1. Let … — say exactly what x and y stand for, with units: "Let the cost of one notebook be ₹ x", not "let x be the notebook".
  2. Write two equations — one for each fact in the question.
  3. Solve — by substitution or elimination (Topic 5's "Which method?" list).
  4. Answer the question asked, in words with units — and check the answer against the story, not just against your equations. If your equations are wrong, the check in the equations will still pass; the story check catches it.

Translating words into algebra

Words Algebra
"a is 4 more than b" a=b+4a = b + 4a=b+4
"a is 3 times b" a=3ba = 3ba=3b
"5 years ago, A was 3 times as old as B" A−5=3(B−5)A - 5 = 3(B - 5)A−5=3(B−5) — subtract 5 from both ages
"in 4 years, A will be twice as old as B" A+4=2(B+4)A + 4 = 2(B + 4)A+4=2(B+4)
a two-digit number with tens digit x and units digit y 10x+y10x + y10x+y
the number with its digits reversed 10y+x10y + x10y+x
a fraction with numerator x and denominator y xy\dfrac{x}{y}yx​; "add 1 to both" gives x+1y+1\dfrac{x + 1}{y + 1}y+1x+1​
perimeter of a rectangle 2(l+w)2(l + w)2(l+w)
a fixed charge f plus r for each unit total =f+r×(number of units)= f + r \times (\text{number of units})=f+r×(number of units)

Sense checks

  • Ages, lengths, costs and counts must be positive (a count must also be a whole number).
  • Digits must be whole numbers from 0 to 9, and the tens digit of a two-digit number cannot be 0.
  • Re-read each sentence of the question with your answer: does it come out true?

Worked example 1

Ages

Question. Six years ago, Meera's mother was five times as old as Meera. Twelve years from now, her mother will be twice as old as Meera. Find their present ages.

Given: two facts about ages at two different times. Asked: the present ages.

Concept used: the four-step routine; "x years ago" subtracts x from both ages. Why it applies: each fact compares the two ages at the same moment.

  1. 1

    Let Meera's mother's present age be m years and Meera's present age be d years.

  2. 2

    Six years ago both ages were 6 less, and the mother's age was 5 times Meera's.

    m−6=5(d−6)m - 6 = 5(d - 6)m−6=5(d−6)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Expand the bracket.

    m−6=5d−30m - 6 = 5d - 30m−6=5d−30Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract 5d5d5d from both sides.

    m−5d−6=−30m - 5d - 6 = -30m−5d−6=−30Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Add 6 to both sides.

    m−5d=−24(1)m - 5d = -24 \qquad (1)m−5d=−24(1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Twelve years from now both ages will be 12 more.

    m+12=2(d+12)m + 12 = 2(d + 12)m+12=2(d+12)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Expand the bracket.

    m+12=2d+24m + 12 = 2d + 24m+12=2d+24Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Subtract 2d2d2d from both sides.

    m−2d+12=24m - 2d + 12 = 24m−2d+12=24Swipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    Subtract 12 from both sides.

    m−2d=12(2)m - 2d = 12 \qquad (2)m−2d=12(2)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  10. 10

    Subtract (1) from (2), term by term.

    (m−m)+(−2d+5d)=12−(−24)(m - m) + (-2d + 5d) = 12 - (-24)(m−m)+(−2d+5d)=12−(−24)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  11. 11

    Simplify.

    3d=363d = 363d=36Swipe to see the whole equation →
  12. 12

    Divide by 3.

    d=12d = 12d=12Swipe to see the whole equation →
  13. 13

    Back-substitute into (2).

    m−24=12m - 24 = 12m−24=12Swipe to see the whole equation →
  14. 14

    Add 24 to both sides.

    m=36m = 36m=36Swipe to see the whole equation →

  15. Check (story): six years ago they were 30 and 6, and 30=5×630 = 5 \times 630=5×6 ✔; in twelve years they will be 48 and 24, and 48=2×2448 = 2 \times 2448=2×24 ✔.

  16. Answer Meera is 12 years old and her mother is 36 years old.

    Common trap: Writing "six years ago" as m−6=5dm - 6 = 5dm−6=5d (subtracting 6 from the mother's age only). That gives m−5d=6m - 5d = 6m−5d=6, and the pair then gives d=2d = 2d=2, m=16m = 16m=16 — a mother 14 years older than her daughter, and the story check fails.

Worked example 2

Money and cost

Question. 3 notebooks and 2 pens cost ₹ 110, and 5 notebooks and 4 pens cost ₹ 190. Find the cost of one notebook and of one pen.

Given: two bills. Asked: the cost of each item.

Concept used: the four-step routine; elimination. Why it applies: doubling the first bill matches the number of pens.

  1. 1

    Let one notebook cost ₹ n and one pen cost ₹ p.

  2. 2

    Write the two equations.

    3n+2p=110(1)3n + 2p = 110 \qquad (1)3n+2p=110(1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
    5n+4p=190(2)5n + 4p = 190 \qquad (2)5n+4p=190(2)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Multiply every term of (1) by 2.

    6n+4p=220(3)6n + 4p = 220 \qquad (3)6n+4p=220(3)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract (2) from (3), term by term.

    (6n−5n)+(4p−4p)=220−190(6n - 5n) + (4p - 4p) = 220 - 190(6n−5n)+(4p−4p)=220−190Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Simplify.

    n=30n = 30n=30Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Back-substitute into (1).

    90+2p=11090 + 2p = 11090+2p=110Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Subtract 90 from both sides.

    2p=202p = 202p=20Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Divide by 2.

    p=10p = 10p=10Swipe to see the whole equation →

  9. Check (story): 3 notebooks and 2 pens: 90+20=11090 + 20 = 11090+20=110 ✔; 5 notebooks and 4 pens: 150+40=190150 + 40 = 190150+40=190 ✔.

  10. Answer A notebook costs ₹ 30 and a pen costs ₹ 10.

    Common trap: Letting x be "the notebooks" and y be "the pens" without saying "the cost of one". Then it is easy to answer "30 notebooks", which is not what was asked.

Worked example 3

Digits

Question. A two-digit number is 7 times the sum of its digits. If 27 is subtracted from the number, the digits are reversed. Find the number.

Given: two facts about a two-digit number. Asked: the number.

Concept used: a number with tens digit x and units digit y is 10x+y10x + y10x+y; reversed it is 10y+x10y + x10y+x. Why it applies: the question compares the number with its digits and with its reverse.

  1. 1

    Let the tens digit be x and the units digit be y. The number is 10x+y10x + y10x+y.

  2. 2

    "7 times the sum of its digits":

    10x+y=7(x+y)10x + y = 7(x + y)10x+y=7(x+y)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Expand the bracket.

    10x+y=7x+7y10x + y = 7x + 7y10x+y=7x+7ySwipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract 7x7x7x from both sides.

    3x+y=7y3x + y = 7y3x+y=7ySwipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Subtract 7y7y7y from both sides.

    3x−6y=03x - 6y = 03x−6y=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Divide by 3.

    x−2y=0(1)x - 2y = 0 \qquad (1)x−2y=0(1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    "Subtract 27 and the digits are reversed":

    10x+y−27=10y+x10x + y - 27 = 10y + x10x+y−27=10y+xSwipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Subtract x from both sides.

    9x+y−27=10y9x + y - 27 = 10y9x+y−27=10ySwipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    Subtract 10y10y10y from both sides.

    9x−9y−27=09x - 9y - 27 = 09x−9y−27=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  10. 10

    Add 27 to both sides.

    9x−9y=279x - 9y = 279x−9y=27Swipe to see the whole equation →
  11. 11

    Divide by 9.

    x−y=3(2)x - y = 3 \qquad (2)x−y=3(2)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  12. 12

    Subtract (1) from (2), term by term.

    (x−x)+(−y+2y)=3−0(x - x) + (-y + 2y) = 3 - 0(x−x)+(−y+2y)=3−0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  13. 13

    Simplify.

    y=3y = 3y=3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  14. 14

    Back-substitute into (2).

    x−3=3x - 3 = 3x−3=3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  15. 15

    Add 3 to both sides.

    x=6x = 6x=6Swipe to see the whole equation →

  16. Check (story): the number is 63; the sum of its digits is 9, and 7×9=637 \times 9 = 637×9=63 ✔; 63−27=3663 - 27 = 3663−27=36, which is 63 reversed ✔. Both digits are whole numbers from 0 to 9 ✔.

  17. Answer The number is 63.

    Common trap: Writing the number as x+yx + yx+y. That is the sum of the digits, not the number: 63 is 10×6+310 \times 6 + 310×6+3, not 6+36 + 36+3.

Worked example 4

A fraction

Question. A fraction becomes 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ when 1 is added to both its numerator and its denominator, and it becomes 13\dfrac{1}{3}31​ when 1 is subtracted from both. Find the fraction.

Given: two facts about a fraction. Asked: the fraction.

Concept used: write the fraction as xy\dfrac{x}{y}yx​; cross-multiply each fact into a linear equation. Why it applies: each fact is an equality of two fractions.

  1. 1

    Let the fraction be xy\dfrac{x}{y}yx​.

  2. 2

    First fact:

    x+1y+1=12\dfrac{x + 1}{y + 1} = \dfrac{1}{2}y+1x+1​=21​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Cross-multiply.

    2x+2=y+12x + 2 = y + 12x+2=y+1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract y from both sides.

    2x−y+2=12x - y + 2 = 12x−y+2=1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Subtract 2 from both sides.

    2x−y=−1(1)2x - y = -1 \qquad (1)2x−y=−1(1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Second fact:

    x−1y−1=13\dfrac{x - 1}{y - 1} = \dfrac{1}{3}y−1x−1​=31​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Cross-multiply.

    3x−3=y−13x - 3 = y - 13x−3=y−1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Subtract y from both sides.

    3x−y−3=−13x - y - 3 = -13x−y−3=−1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    Add 3 to both sides.

    3x−y=2(2)3x - y = 2 \qquad (2)3x−y=2(2)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  10. 10

    Subtract (1) from (2), term by term.

    (3x−2x)+(−y+y)=2−(−1)(3x - 2x) + (-y + y) = 2 - (-1)(3x−2x)+(−y+y)=2−(−1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  11. 11

    Simplify.

    x=3x = 3x=3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  12. 12

    Back-substitute into (1).

    6−y=−16 - y = -16−y=−1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  13. 13

    Add y to both sides.

    6=y−16 = y - 16=y−1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  14. 14

    Add 1 to both sides.

    y=7y = 7y=7Swipe to see the whole equation →

  15. Check (story): 3+17+1=48=12\dfrac{3 + 1}{7 + 1} = \dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{1}{2}7+13+1​=84​=21​ ✔ and 3−17−1=26=13\dfrac{3 - 1}{7 - 1} = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}7−13−1​=62​=31​ ✔.

  16. Answer The fraction is 37\dfrac{3}{7}73​.

    Common trap: Adding 1 to the numerator only: x+1y=12\dfrac{x + 1}{y} = \dfrac{1}{2}yx+1​=21​. Read the question for both numerator and denominator.

Worked example 5

Geometry: a rectangle

Question. The length of a rectangular garden is 3 m more than twice its width. Its perimeter is 48 m. Find its length and width.

Given: a relation between length and width; the perimeter. Asked: length and width.

Concept used: perimeter =2(l+w)= 2(l + w)=2(l+w); substitution. Why it applies: the first fact already gives l in terms of w.

  1. 1

    Let the length be l m and the width be w m.

  2. 2

    First fact.

    l=2w+3(1)l = 2w + 3 \qquad (1)l=2w+3(1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Perimeter: 2(l+w)=482(l + w) = 482(l+w)=48. Divide by 2.

    l+w=24(2)l + w = 24 \qquad (2)l+w=24(2)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Substitute (1) into (2).

    (2w+3)+w=24(2w + 3) + w = 24(2w+3)+w=24Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Collect the w-terms.

    3w+3=243w + 3 = 243w+3=24Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Subtract 3 from both sides.

    3w=213w = 213w=21Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Divide by 3.

    w=7w = 7w=7Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Back-substitute into (1).

    l=14+3=17l = 14 + 3 = 17l=14+3=17Swipe to see the whole equation →

  9. Check (story): 17=2×7+317 = 2 \times 7 + 317=2×7+3 ✔ and 2(17+7)=482(17 + 7) = 482(17+7)=48 ✔; both lengths are positive ✔.

  10. Answer Length 17 m, width 7 m.

    Common trap: Using l+w=48l + w = 48l+w=48 (half the perimeter taken as the whole). That gives w=15w = 15w=15, l=33l = 33l=33, and then the perimeter would be 96 m.

Worked example 6

A fixed charge plus a charge per unit

Question. A gym charges a one-time joining fee plus a fixed fee for each month. Asha paid ₹ 4300 for 5 months and Ravi paid ₹ 7300 for 10 months. Find the joining fee and the monthly fee. How much would 12 months cost?

Given: two totals for different numbers of months. Asked: the two fees, and the total for 12 months.

Concept used: total =f+r×(number of months)= f + r \times (\text{number of months})=f+r×(number of months); elimination. Why it applies: both totals contain the same joining fee.

  1. 1

    Let the joining fee be ₹ f and the monthly fee be ₹ r.

  2. 2

    Write the two equations.

    f+5r=4300(1)f + 5r = 4300 \qquad (1)f+5r=4300(1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
    f+10r=7300(2)f + 10r = 7300 \qquad (2)f+10r=7300(2)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Subtract (1) from (2), term by term.

    (f−f)+(10r−5r)=7300−4300(f - f) + (10r - 5r) = 7300 - 4300(f−f)+(10r−5r)=7300−4300Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Simplify.

    5r=30005r = 30005r=3000Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Divide by 5.

    r=600r = 600r=600Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Back-substitute into (1).

    f+3000=4300f + 3000 = 4300f+3000=4300Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Subtract 3000 from both sides.

    f=1300f = 1300f=1300Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    For 12 months.

    f+12r=1300+7200=8500f + 12r = 1300 + 7200 = 8500f+12r=1300+7200=8500Swipe to see the whole equation →

  9. Check (story): Asha: 1300+5×600=43001300 + 5 \times 600 = 43001300+5×600=4300 ✔; Ravi: 1300+10×600=73001300 + 10 \times 600 = 73001300+10×600=7300 ✔.

  10. Answer Joining fee ₹ 1300, monthly fee ₹ 600; 12 months cost ₹ 8500.

    Common trap: Stopping after finding f and r. The question also asks for the cost of 12 months — answer every part of the question.

Quick check · 1 of 3MCQ1 mark

A two-digit number has tens digit x and units digit y. The number formed by reversing its digits is:

After reversing, y is the tens digit (worth 10y10y10y) and x is the units digit.
This is the original number, not the reversed one.
This is the sum of the digits.
yxyxyx means y multiplied by x, not "the digits y then x".
Quick check · 2 of 3MCQ1 mark

"Five years ago, A was three times as old as B." With present ages A and B years, this is:

Five years ago both ages were 5 less; then A's age was 3 times B's.
The 3 must multiply B's whole age five years ago, (B−5)(B - 5)(B−5).
This subtracts 5 from one side only and mixes present and past ages.
The 3 is on the wrong side: this says B was three times as old as A.
Quick check · 3 of 3Short answer2 marks

A taxi charges a fixed amount plus a charge for each km. An 8 km ride costs ₹ 140 and a 14 km ride costs ₹ 230. Find the fixed charge and the charge per km.

Answer: Fixed charge ₹ 20; ₹ 15 per km.

Working: Let the fixed charge be ₹ f and the charge per km ₹ r. f+8r=140f + 8r = 140f+8r=140 and f+14r=230f + 14r = 230f+14r=230. Subtracting, 6r=906r = 906r=90, so r=15r = 15r=15; then f=140−120=20f = 140 - 120 = 20f=140−120=20. Check: 20+14×15=23020 + 14 \times 15 = 23020+14×15=230 ✔.

The fixed charge is the same in both rides, so subtracting the equations removes it.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers see word problems as one of the four commonest reasons for lost marks in this chapter. Most errors happen before any algebra — in step 1 or step 2.

  • "Let x be the notebooks"

    Unclear: the number of notebooks, or the cost of one?

    "Let the cost of one notebook be ₹ x."

  • A two-digit number written as x+yx + yx+y

    x+yx + yx+y is the sum of the digits.

    The number is 10x+y10x + y10x+y; reversed, 10y+x10y + x10y+x.

  • "5 years ago" applied to one age only

    Both people were 5 years younger.

    A−5=3(B−5)A - 5 = 3(B - 5)A−5=3(B−5).

  • "A is 4 more than B" written as A+4=BA + 4 = BA+4=B

    That makes B the larger.

    A=B+4A = B + 4A=B+4. Test with numbers: if B = 10, A = 14.

  • Half the perimeter used as the perimeter

    Perimeter of a rectangle is 2(l+w)2(l + w)2(l+w).

    Write the formula first, then substitute.

  • Checking only in the equations

    If an equation was formed wrongly, the check still passes.

    Re-read every sentence of the story with your answer.

  • Answering only part of the question

    Marks are lost for the missing part.

    Underline every "find" in the question and answer each one.

Key points

  • Routine: Let (with meaning and units) → two equations → solve → answer in words + story check.
  • Ages: "n years ago/hence" changes both ages by n.
  • Digits: number =10x+y= 10x + y=10x+y, reversed =10y+x= 10y + x=10y+x; digits are whole numbers 0–9.
  • Fractions: xy\dfrac{x}{y}yx​; cross-multiply each fact.
  • Fixed + per-unit: total =f+r×= f + r \times=f+r× units; subtracting two totals removes f.
  • Check the answer against the story, not only against your equations.
