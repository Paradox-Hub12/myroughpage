Wrap-up

The three cases, the method card and mixed problems

About 35 minutes

Everything in this chapter comes back to one picture: two straight lines, which cross, run parallel, or lie on top of each other. This page puts the cards you need on one sheet, then gives five mixed problems that join the graph, the ratios and the algebra.

Mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

Two unknown coefficients

Question. For which values of a and b does the pair x+3y=5x + 3y = 5x+3y=5 and (a−1)x+(a+b)y=15(a - 1)x + (a + b)y = 15(a−1)x+(a+b)y=15 have infinitely many solutions?

Given: a1=1a_1 = 1a1​=1, b1=3b_1 = 3b1​=3, c1=−5c_1 = -5c1​=−5; a2=a−1a_2 = a - 1a2​=a−1, b2=a+bb_2 = a + bb2​=a+b, c2=−15c_2 = -15c2​=−15. Asked: a and b.

Concept used: infinitely many solutions ⇔ a1a2=b1b2=c1c2\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2} = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}a2​a1​​=b2​b1​​=c2​c1​​. Why it applies: the lines must coincide.

  1. 1

    The constant ratio is known.

    c1c2=−5−15=13\dfrac{c_1}{c_2} = \dfrac{-5}{-15} = \dfrac{1}{3}c2​c1​​=−15−5​=31​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Set a1a2=13\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{1}{3}a2​a1​​=31​.

    1a−1=13\dfrac{1}{a - 1} = \dfrac{1}{3}a−11​=31​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Cross-multiply.

    a−1=3a - 1 = 3a−1=3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Add 1 to both sides.

    a=4a = 4a=4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Set b1b2=13\dfrac{b_1}{b_2} = \dfrac{1}{3}b2​b1​​=31​.

    3a+b=13\dfrac{3}{a + b} = \dfrac{1}{3}a+b3​=31​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Cross-multiply.

    a+b=9a + b = 9a+b=9Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Substitute a=4a = 4a=4.

    4+b=94 + b = 94+b=9Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Subtract 4 from both sides.

    b=5b = 5b=5Swipe to see the whole equation →

  9. Check: with a=4a = 4a=4, b=5b = 5b=5 the second equation is 3x+9y=153x + 9y = 153x+9y=15, exactly 3 times x+3y=5x + 3y = 5x+3y=5 — coincident ✔.

  10. Answer a=4a = 4a=4, b=5b = 5b=5

    Common trap: Using only a1a2=b1b2\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}a2​a1​​=b2​b1​​, which gives one equation in two unknowns. The constant ratio supplies the number 13\dfrac{1}{3}31​ that fixes both.

Mixed example 2

Solve, then use the solution

Question. Solve 3x+2y=73x + 2y = 73x+2y=7 and x−y=4x - y = 4x−y=4. Hence find m if the solution also satisfies y=mx+2y = mx + 2y=mx+2.

Given: a pair, and a third line through the solution. Asked: x, y, then m.

Concept used: substitution; then a point on a line satisfies its equation. Why it applies: x has coefficient 1 in the second equation.

  1. 1

    From x−y=4x - y = 4x−y=4, add y to both sides.

    x=y+4x = y + 4x=y+4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Substitute into 3x+2y=73x + 2y = 73x+2y=7.

    3(y+4)+2y=73(y + 4) + 2y = 73(y+4)+2y=7Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Expand the bracket.

    3y+12+2y=73y + 12 + 2y = 73y+12+2y=7Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Collect the y-terms.

    5y+12=75y + 12 = 75y+12=7Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Subtract 12 from both sides.

    5y=−55y = -55y=−5Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Divide by 5.

    y=−1y = -1y=−1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Back-substitute: x=−1+4=3x = -1 + 4 = 3x=−1+4=3.

  8. 8

    Put (3,−1)(3, -1)(3,−1) into y=mx+2y = mx + 2y=mx+2.

    −1=3m+2-1 = 3m + 2−1=3m+2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    Subtract 2 from both sides.

    −3=3m-3 = 3m−3=3mSwipe to see the whole equation →
  10. 10

    Divide by 3.

    m=−1m = -1m=−1Swipe to see the whole equation →

  11. Check: 9−2=79 - 2 = 79−2=7 ✔, 3−(−1)=43 - (-1) = 43−(−1)=4 ✔, and y=−x+2y = -x + 2y=−x+2 at x=3x = 3x=3 gives −1-1−1 ✔.

  12. Answer x=3x = 3x=3, y=−1y = -1y=−1; m=−1m = -1m=−1

    Common trap: Dropping the "+ 2" and writing −1=3m-1 = 3m−1=3m, which gives m=−13m = -\dfrac{1}{3}m=−31​. Substitute into the whole equation y=mx+2y = mx + 2y=mx+2.

Mixed example 3

A triangle without drawing

Question. Without drawing, find the vertices and the area of the triangle formed by the lines 2x−y=22x - y = 22x−y=2 and x+y=7x + y = 7x+y=7 and the y-axis.

Given: two lines; the y-axis. Asked: vertices and area.

Concept used: vertices = meeting point + the two y-intercepts; height = x-coordinate of the meeting point. Why it applies: the base lies on the y-axis.

  1. 1

    Meeting point: add the equations to eliminate y.

    3x=93x = 93x=9Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Divide by 3.

    x=3x = 3x=3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Back-substitute into x+y=7x + y = 7x+y=7.

    3+y=73 + y = 73+y=7Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract 3 from both sides: y=4y = 4y=4. The meeting point is (3,4)(3, 4)(3,4).

  5. 5

    On the y-axis, x=0x = 0x=0. For 2x−y=22x - y = 22x−y=2 this gives −y=2-y = 2−y=2, so y=−2y = -2y=−2: the point (0,−2)(0, -2)(0,−2).

  6. 6

    For x+y=7x + y = 7x+y=7 it gives y=7y = 7y=7: the point (0,7)(0, 7)(0,7).

  7. 7

    Base =7−(−2)=9= 7 - (-2) = 9=7−(−2)=9 units; height = x-coordinate of (3,4)(3, 4)(3,4) = 3 units.

  8. 8

    Area.

    12×9×3=272\dfrac{1}{2} \times 9 \times 3 = \dfrac{27}{2}21​×9×3=227​Swipe to see the whole equation →

  9. Check: (3,4)(3, 4)(3,4): 6−4=26 - 4 = 26−4=2 ✔ and 3+4=73 + 4 = 73+4=7 ✔. Shoelace check with (0,−2)(0, -2)(0,−2), (0,7)(0, 7)(0,7), (3,4)(3, 4)(3,4) also gives 272\dfrac{27}{2}227​ ✔.

  10. Answer Vertices (3,4)(3, 4)(3,4), (0,−2)(0, -2)(0,−2), (0,7)(0, 7)(0,7); area 272=13.5\dfrac{27}{2} = 13.5227​=13.5 square units.

    Common trap: Using 4 (the y-coordinate) as the height. For a base on the y-axis, the height is the horizontal distance, the x-coordinate.

Mixed example 4

Can the story be true?

Question. A shopkeeper says: "2 pens and 3 pencils cost ₹ 40, and 4 pens and 6 pencils cost ₹ 90." Can both statements be true? Explain.

Given: two bills. Asked: whether a price for a pen and a pencil can fit both.

Concept used: the ratio test. Why it applies: "can both be true?" asks whether the pair is consistent.

  1. 1

    Let a pen cost ₹ x and a pencil ₹ y: 2x+3y=402x + 3y = 402x+3y=40 and 4x+6y=904x + 6y = 904x+6y=90.

  2. 2

    Ratios: 24=12\dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}42​=21​, 36=12\dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}63​=21​, 4090=49\dfrac{40}{90} = \dfrac{4}{9}9040​=94​.

  3. 3

    12=12≠49\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2} \neq \dfrac{4}{9}21​=21​=94​: the lines are parallel, so there is no solution.

  4. Check: the second order is exactly twice the first, so it should cost 2×40=802 \times 40 = 802×40=80, not 90 ✔.

  5. Answer No. The pair is inconsistent, so no prices fit both statements — one of the bills must be wrong.

    Common trap: Trying to solve, getting 0=100 = 100=10 (or a similar false line), and deciding the working is wrong. The false statement is the answer.

Mixed example 5

Two candidates for k

Question. Find k so that the pair x+ky=2x + ky = 2x+ky=2 and kx+4y=4kx + 4y = 4kx+4y=4 has no solution.

Given: a1=1a_1 = 1a1​=1, b1=kb_1 = kb1​=k, c1=−2c_1 = -2c1​=−2; a2=ka_2 = ka2​=k, b2=4b_2 = 4b2​=4, c2=−4c_2 = -4c2​=−4. Asked: k.

Concept used: no solution ⇔ a1a2=b1b2≠c1c2\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}a2​a1​​=b2​b1​​=c2​c1​​. Why it applies: the lines must be parallel.

  1. 1

    Set the first two ratios equal.

    1k=k4\dfrac{1}{k} = \dfrac{k}{4}k1​=4k​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Cross-multiply.

    k2=4k^2 = 4k2=4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Take square roots: k=2k = 2k=2 or k=−2k = -2k=−2.

  4. 4

    Test k=2k = 2k=2: ratios 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​, 24=12\dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}42​=21​, −2−4=12\dfrac{-2}{-4} = \dfrac{1}{2}−4−2​=21​ — all equal, so the lines coincide. Reject k=2k = 2k=2.

  5. 5

    Test k=−2k = -2k=−2: ratios −12-\dfrac{1}{2}−21​, −24=−12\dfrac{-2}{4} = -\dfrac{1}{2}4−2​=−21​, −2−4=12\dfrac{-2}{-4} = \dfrac{1}{2}−4−2​=21​ — the third is different, so the lines are parallel ✔.

  6. Check: with k=−2k = -2k=−2: x−2y=2x - 2y = 2x−2y=2 and −2x+4y=4-2x + 4y = 4−2x+4y=4. Multiplying the first by −2-2−2 gives −2x+4y=−4-2x + 4y = -4−2x+4y=−4, which cannot also equal 4 ✔.

  7. Answer k=−2k = -2k=−2

    Common trap: Answering "k=±2k = \pm 2k=±2". Each candidate must pass the test b1b2≠c1c2\dfrac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}b2​b1​​=c2​c1​​; k=2k = 2k=2 gives infinitely many solutions, not none.
