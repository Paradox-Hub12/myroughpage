Chapter 6 · Unit IV

Triangles

5 topicsAbout 8 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

A photograph and its enlargement, a map and the city it shows, a stick and a tower with their shadows: each pair has the same shape in different sizes. Such figures are similar, and similar triangles let you find lengths you cannot reach — the height of a tower, the width of a river — from a few lengths you can measure. This chapter shows exactly when two triangles are similar, and proves the theorem that makes it all work: a line parallel to one side of a triangle cuts the other two sides in the same ratio.

In the board exam this chapter is part of Unit IV, Geometry (15 marks, shared with Circles), in both Mathematics Standard and Mathematics Basic. In all four sample papers for 2025-26 and 2026-27, the proof of the Basic Proportionality Theorem is set as a 5-mark question (in Standard, followed by a short application). The 2026-27 sample papers also ask a 1-mark perimeter question in both papers, a 2-mark similarity question in Standard (6 + 2 = 8 marks in all from this chapter), and a 1-mark similar-or-congruent question and a 1-mark assertion–reason in Basic (5 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 8 marks).

What you'll learn

  • Split a length in a ratio, tell part-to-part from part-to-whole, use angles made by parallel lines, and use "same base, same parallels → equal area". Take the 5-question skip-check first — score 5 out of 5 and go straight to Topic 2.
  • Say what makes two figures similar, explain why congruent figures are similar but not the other way round, give counter-examples (square and rectangle, square and rhombus), and find the scale factor and missing sides.
  • State and prove BPT, use it in all its forms, use the converse to test whether a line is parallel, and solve trapezium problems. Includes an interactive similarity explorer.
  • State AA, SSS and SAS, choose the right one (and spot when none applies), write the correspondence in the right order, and prove triangles similar with a reason for every line.
  • Find heights from shadows and lamp-posts, use the ratios of perimeters, medians and altitudes, and solve trapezium and right-triangle problems.
  • Use the criterion chooser, the matching card and the BPT proof skeleton, and solve mixed problems in sample-paper styles.
