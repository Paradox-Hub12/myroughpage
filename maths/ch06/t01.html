Toolkit: ratios, parallel lines and areas

About 30 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Work with ratios and proportions — cross-multiply and solve for an unknown.
  • Use angle facts for parallel lines (corresponding and alternate angles) and the angle sum of a triangle.
  • Use area = ½ × base × height and "same base, same parallels → equal area".
Why this lesson exists

Every page of this chapter uses three skills from Classes 7–9: splitting a length in a ratio, reading angles made by parallel lines, and finding the area of a triangle. When a triangles answer goes wrong, the slip is very often here — a ratio taken "part to whole" instead of "part to part", or co-interior angles treated as equal. If you already have these skills, prove it below and go straight to Topic 2.

Work through this toolkit — it takes about 30 minutes. Then try the questions again.

A photo shop prints the same picture as a passport photo and as a poster. How does the machine keep the face from looking stretched? It makes every length grow by the same factor — a ratio. This chapter is about such "same shape, different size" figures, and every fact in it is a statement about ratios, about parallel lines, or (in one proof) about areas of triangles. Half an hour here makes the rest of the chapter much easier.

  1. Ratios: parts and wholes

A ratio compares two quantities: AD:DB=2:3AD : DB = 2 : 3AD:DB=2:3 means that if AD is 2 equal parts, DB is 3 of the same parts. It can be written as a fraction, ADDB=23\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{2}{3}DBAD​=32​.

  • The whole AB is 2+3=52 + 3 = 52+3=5 parts. So ADAB=25\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{2}{5}ABAD​=52​ and DBAB=35\dfrac{DB}{AB} = \dfrac{3}{5}ABDB​=53​.
  • A part-to-part ratio (ADDB\dfrac{AD}{DB}DBAD​) and a part-to-whole ratio (ADAB\dfrac{AD}{AB}ABAD​) are different numbers. Always say which one you are using.

A proportion says two ratios are equal: ab=cd\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}ba​=dc​. To solve it, cross-multiply: a×d=b×ca \times d = b \times ca×d=b×c. This works whenever b and d are not 0.

  1. Angles and parallel lines

When a line (a transversal) cuts two parallel lines:

  • corresponding angles are equal ("F-shape");
  • alternate interior angles are equal ("Z-shape");
  • co-interior angles (same side, between the lines) add up to 180° ("C-shape") — they are equal only when each is 90°.

The converse is also true: if corresponding angles (or alternate angles) are equal, the lines are parallel. And in every triangle the three angles add up to 180°.

  1. Area of a triangle

Area of a triangle =12×base×height= \dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}=21​×base×height, where the height is the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the line of the base. Any side can be the base.

Same base, same parallels. Two triangles on the same base whose third vertices lie on a line parallel to that base have the same height — so they have equal areas, however different they look. Topic 3's proof rests on exactly this fact.

Worked example 1

Splitting a length in a ratio

Question. (a) Divide a segment PQ of length 35 cm at R so that PR:RQ=3:4PR : RQ = 3 : 4PR:RQ=3:4. (b) D lies on AB with AD:DB=5:2AD : DB = 5 : 2AD:DB=5:2 and DB=6DB = 6DB=6 cm. Find AD and AB.

Given: two ratios. Asked: the lengths.

Concept used: a ratio m:nm : nm:n splits the whole into m+nm + nm+n equal parts. Why it applies: the whole length (or one part) is known, so one part can be found.

  1. 1

    (a)

    Number of parts: 3+4=73 + 4 = 73+4=7.

  2. 2

    One part: 35÷7=535 \div 7 = 535÷7=5 cm.

  3. 3

    PR=3×5=15PR = 3 \times 5 = 15PR=3×5=15 cm and RQ=4×5=20RQ = 4 \times 5 = 20RQ=4×5=20 cm.

  4. 4

    (b)

    DB is 2 parts, so one part is 6÷2=36 \div 2 = 36÷2=3 cm.

  5. 5

    AD=5×3=15AD = 5 \times 3 = 15AD=5×3=15 cm.

  6. 6

    AB=AD+DB=15+6=21AB = AD + DB = 15 + 6 = 21AB=AD+DB=15+6=21 cm.

  7. Check: (a) 15+20=3515 + 20 = 3515+20=35 ✔ and 1520=34\dfrac{15}{20} = \dfrac{3}{4}2015​=43​ ✔. (b) 156=52\dfrac{15}{6} = \dfrac{5}{2}615​=25​ ✔.

  8. Answer (a) PR=15PR = 15PR=15 cm, RQ=20RQ = 20RQ=20 cm (b) AD=15AD = 15AD=15 cm, AB=21AB = 21AB=21 cm

    Common trap: In (a), finding PR as 35×3435 \times \dfrac{3}{4}35×43​. The 4 counts the parts of RQ only; the whole has 7 parts.

Worked example 2

Part to part and part to whole

Question. E is a point on AC with AEEC=45\dfrac{AE}{EC} = \dfrac{4}{5}ECAE​=54​. Find (a) AEAC\dfrac{AE}{AC}ACAE​ (b) ECAC\dfrac{EC}{AC}ACEC​ (c) AE, if AC=27AC = 27AC=27 cm.

Given: AE:EC=4:5AE : EC = 4 : 5AE:EC=4:5. Asked: two part-to-whole ratios and AE.

Concept used: the whole is the sum of the parts. Why it applies: AC is made of AE and EC.

  1. 1

    AE is 4 parts and EC is 5 parts, so AC is 4+5=94 + 5 = 94+5=9 parts.

  2. 2

    (a) AEAC=49\dfrac{AE}{AC} = \dfrac{4}{9}ACAE​=94​.

  3. 3

    (b) ECAC=59\dfrac{EC}{AC} = \dfrac{5}{9}ACEC​=95​.

  4. 4

    (c) One part is 27÷9=327 \div 9 = 327÷9=3 cm, so AE=4×3=12AE = 4 \times 3 = 12AE=4×3=12 cm.

  5. Check: EC=5×3=15EC = 5 \times 3 = 15EC=5×3=15 cm, and 12+15=2712 + 15 = 2712+15=27 ✔; 1215=45\dfrac{12}{15} = \dfrac{4}{5}1512​=54​ ✔.

  6. Answer (a) 49\dfrac{4}{9}94​ (b) 59\dfrac{5}{9}95​ (c) AE=12AE = 12AE=12 cm

    Common trap: Writing AEAC=45\dfrac{AE}{AC} = \dfrac{4}{5}ACAE​=54​. That is the part-to-part ratio; the whole AC has 9 parts.

Worked example 3

Solving a proportion with an unknown

Question. Solve x+12x+1=35\dfrac{x + 1}{2x + 1} = \dfrac{3}{5}2x+1x+1​=53​.

Given: a proportion. Asked: x.

Concept used: cross-multiplication, ab=cd⇒ad=bc\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d} \Rightarrow ad = bcba​=dc​⇒ad=bc. Why it applies: it removes the fractions in one move.

  1. 1

    Cross-multiply.

    5(x+1)=3(2x+1)5(x + 1) = 3(2x + 1)5(x+1)=3(2x+1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Expand both brackets.

    5x+5=6x+35x + 5 = 6x + 35x+5=6x+3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Subtract 5x from both sides.

    5=x+35 = x + 35=x+3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract 3 from both sides.

    x=2x = 2x=2Swipe to see the whole equation →

  5. Check: 2+12×2+1=35\dfrac{2 + 1}{2 \times 2 + 1} = \dfrac{3}{5}2×2+12+1​=53​ ✔.

  6. Answer x=2x = 2x=2

    Common trap: Multiplying only the first term of a bracket: 5x+1=6x+15x + 1 = 6x + 15x+1=6x+1 gives x=0x = 0x=0. Put brackets round every numerator and denominator before cross-multiplying.

Worked example 4

Angles made by parallel lines

Question. AB and CD are parallel lines (B and D on the same side). A transversal meets AB at P and CD at Q, and ∠BPQ=58°\angle BPQ = 58°∠BPQ=58°. Find ∠PQC\angle PQC∠PQC and ∠PQD\angle PQD∠PQD.

Given: AB∥CDAB \parallel CDAB∥CD, ∠BPQ=58°\angle BPQ = 58°∠BPQ=58°. Asked: ∠PQC\angle PQC∠PQC, ∠PQD\angle PQD∠PQD.

Concept used: alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles add to 180°. Why it applies: PQ is a transversal of two parallel lines.

  1. 1

    ∠BPQ\angle BPQ∠BPQ and ∠PQC\angle PQC∠PQC lie on opposite sides of PQ, between the parallels: they are alternate angles.

    ∠PQC=∠BPQ=58°\angle PQC = \angle BPQ = 58°∠PQC=∠BPQ=58°Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    ∠BPQ\angle BPQ∠BPQ and ∠PQD\angle PQD∠PQD lie on the same side of PQ, between the parallels: they are co-interior.

    ∠PQD=180°−58°=122°\angle PQD = 180° - 58° = 122°∠PQD=180°−58°=122°Swipe to see the whole equation →

  3. Check: ∠PQC+∠PQD=58°+122°=180°\angle PQC + \angle PQD = 58° + 122° = 180°∠PQC+∠PQD=58°+122°=180° — they make a straight angle on CD ✔.

  4. Answer ∠PQC=58°\angle PQC = 58°∠PQC=58°, ∠PQD=122°\angle PQD = 122°∠PQD=122°

    Common trap: Saying ∠PQD=58°\angle PQD = 58°∠PQD=58° because "angles on parallel lines are equal". Only corresponding and alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles add to 180°.

Worked example 5

A line parallel to one side of a triangle

Question. In △ABC, D lies on AB and E lies on AC with DE∥BCDE \parallel BCDE∥BC. ∠ABC=64°\angle ABC = 64°∠ABC=64° and ∠ACB=51°\angle ACB = 51°∠ACB=51°. Find ∠ADE\angle ADE∠ADE, ∠AED\angle AED∠AED and ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC.

Given: DE∥BCDE \parallel BCDE∥BC, ∠B=64°\angle B = 64°∠B=64°, ∠C=51°\angle C = 51°∠C=51°. Asked: three angles.

Concept used: corresponding angles; the angle sum of a triangle. Why it applies: AB and AC are transversals of the parallel lines DE and BC.

  1. 1

    AB cuts the parallels DE and BC; ∠ADE\angle ADE∠ADE and ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC are corresponding angles.

    ∠ADE=64°\angle ADE = 64°∠ADE=64°Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    AC cuts the same parallels; ∠AED\angle AED∠AED and ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB are corresponding angles.

    ∠AED=51°\angle AED = 51°∠AED=51°Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Angle sum in △ABC.

    ∠BAC=180°−64°−51°=65°\angle BAC = 180° - 64° - 51° = 65°∠BAC=180°−64°−51°=65°Swipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: angle sum in △ADE: 65°+64°+51°=180°65° + 64° + 51° = 180°65°+64°+51°=180° ✔.

  5. Answer ∠ADE=64°\angle ADE = 64°∠ADE=64°, ∠AED=51°\angle AED = 51°∠AED=51°, ∠BAC=65°\angle BAC = 65°∠BAC=65°

    Common trap: None of the angles of the small triangle ADE are new: it has exactly the same three angles as △ABC. Topic 4 shows why that makes the two triangles similar.

Worked example 6

Same base, same parallels

Question. In △ABC, D is on AB and E is on AC with DE∥BCDE \parallel BCDE∥BC. DE=6DE = 6DE=6 cm and the distance between the parallel lines DE and BC is 4 cm. Find the areas of △BDE and △CDE, and say why they are equal.

Given: DE∥BCDE \parallel BCDE∥BC, DE=6DE = 6DE=6 cm, gap between the lines 4 cm. Asked: ar(BDE)\text{ar}(BDE)ar(BDE) and ar(CDE)\text{ar}(CDE)ar(CDE).

Concept used: area =12×base×height= \dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}=21​×base×height. Why it applies: both triangles have base DE, and their third vertices B and C lie on the line BC, which is parallel to DE.

  1. 1

    In △BDE take DE as the base. B is on BC, so its perpendicular distance from DE is the gap between the lines, 4 cm.

    ar(BDE)=12×6×4=12 cm2\text{ar}(BDE) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4 = 12 \text{ cm}^2ar(BDE)=21​×6×4=12 cm2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    In △CDE the base is again DE, and C is also on BC, so the height is again 4 cm.

    ar(CDE)=12×6×4=12 cm2\text{ar}(CDE) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4 = 12 \text{ cm}^2ar(CDE)=21​×6×4=12 cm2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    They are equal because the triangles are on the same base DE and between the same parallels DE and BC.

  4. Check: a different pair of numbers would change both areas together — the reason in step 3 does not depend on the numbers ✔.

  5. Answer Both areas are 12 cm²: same base DE, same parallels DE and BC.

    Common trap: Saying "the areas are equal because the triangles look the same". They need not look alike at all (here one leans left, the other right). The reason is "same base, same parallels".

Common mistakes!

  • AD:DB=2:3AD : DB = 2 : 3AD:DB=2:3, so AD=23AD = \dfrac{2}{3}AD=32​ of AB

    3 is the number of parts in DB, not in AB.

    AB has 2+3=52 + 3 = 52+3=5 parts: AD=25AD = \dfrac{2}{5}AD=52​ of AB.

  • Treating ADDB\dfrac{AD}{DB}DBAD​ and ADAB\dfrac{AD}{AB}ABAD​ as the same

    One is part-to-part, the other part-to-whole.

    Write which ratio you mean; convert with "whole = sum of parts".

  • Co-interior angles taken as equal

    They add to 180° (equal only if both are 90°).

    F- and Z-angles are equal; C-angles add to 180°.

  • Using a slanting side as the "height"

    The height must be perpendicular to the base.

    Drop a perpendicular from the vertex to the line of the base.

  • "Equal areas because they look alike"

    Shape does not decide area here.

    Give the reason: same base and between the same parallels.

Key points

  • m:nm : nm:n splits a whole into m+nm + nm+n parts; part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios are different numbers.
  • Solve a proportion by cross-multiplying, with brackets round every numerator and denominator.
  • Parallel lines: corresponding and alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • Area of a triangle =12×base×height= \dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}=21​×base×height (height perpendicular to the base).
  • Triangles on the same base and between the same parallels have equal areas.
