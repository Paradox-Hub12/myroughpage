Sample paper and case-study questions

16 questions from official CBSE sources. Each answer has two views: Understand it (every step) and Write it in the exam (set out as you would write it, within the word limit).

Past board papers not yet available — to be added. This section currently holds the Triangles items from the CBSE Mathematics Sample Question Papers 2026-27 and 2025-26 — Mathematics Standard (041) and Mathematics Basic (241) — answered with their official marking schemes (newest first). Past year board questions, and the "Exam focus" analysis, will be added once the papers are sourced.

Both 2026-27 sample papers state that there is no change in the question paper design and assessment pattern for 2026-27, so the 2025-26 items remain good practice. Standard and Basic share the same syllabus, so every student should try all sixteen items. In each paper this chapter is part of Unit IV, Geometry (15 marks, shared with Circles). The proof of the Basic Proportionality Theorem is a 5-mark question in all four papers — in the two Standard papers it is followed by a short application; in the two Basic papers the proof alone carries the 5 marks. Two items here are alternatives for visually impaired candidates: 2026-27 Standard Q22 (a separate question, given in full below) and 2025-26 Basic Q10 (the same question with the figure's lengths written in words).

Each item has two parts:

  • Understand it — the full step-by-step explanation: Given / Asked → Concept used (and why) → steps → Check → answer → Common trap.
  • Write it in the exam — the answer as you should write it, following the official marking scheme, with marks shown. Where the scheme gives only a total for a group of lines, the split shown is inferred and marked so.

Question: The perimeters of two similar triangles are 56 cm and 70 cm respectively. If one side of the first triangle is 14 cm, then the corresponding side of the second triangle (in cm) is:

Given: perimeters 56 cm and 70 cm; a side of the first triangle is 14 cm. Asked: the matching side of the second.

Concept used: perimeters of similar triangles are in the same ratio as corresponding sides (Topic 5). Why it applies: the triangles are similar.

  1. 1

    5670=14x\dfrac{56}{70} = \dfrac{14}{x}7056​=x14​.

  2. 2

    Cross-multiply: 56x=98056x = 98056x=980, so x=17.5x = 17.5x=17.5 → option D.

  3. Check: 7056=1.25\dfrac{70}{56} = 1.255670​=1.25 and 14×1.25=17.514 \times 1.25 = 17.514×1.25=17.5 ✔.

    Why the other options are wrong: (A), (B) and (C) are all less than 14 cm, but the second triangle has the bigger perimeter, so its side must be longer than 14 cm.
    Common trap: Inverting the ratio: 14×5670=11.214 \times \dfrac{56}{70} = 11.214×7056​=11.2 cm makes the side shrink although the perimeter grows.
Ratio of perimeters = ratio of corresponding sides: 5670=14x⇒x=17.5\dfrac{56}{70} = \dfrac{14}{x} \Rightarrow x = 17.57056​=x14​⇒x=17.5. (D)
