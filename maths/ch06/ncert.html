Exercise 6.1

Exercise 6.1, Q1 (i)All circles are …1 mark

Fill in the blanks using the correct word given in brackets :
(i) All circles are ________. (congruent, similar)

Given: the two words "congruent" and "similar". Asked: the one that is true of all circles.

Concept used: similar = same shape; congruent = same shape and same size (Topic 2). Why it applies: circles can have different radii.

  1. 1

    Every circle has the same shape: any circle is an enlargement of any other.

  2. 2

    Circles with different radii are not the same size, so not all circles are congruent.

  3. Check: a circle of radius 2 cm and one of radius 5 cm cannot be placed one on top of the other, but one is a 2.5 times enlargement of the other ✔.

  4. Answer All circles are similar.

    Common trap: Choosing "congruent" — that would need every circle to have the same radius.
Exercise 6.1, Q1 (ii)All squares are …1 mark

Fill in the blanks using the correct word given in brackets :
(ii) All squares are ________. (similar, congruent)

Given: the words "similar" and "congruent". Asked: the one true of all squares.

Concept used: similar polygons need equal angles and proportional sides (Topic 2). Why it applies: both conditions can be checked for any two squares.

  1. 1

    Every angle of every square is 90°, so corresponding angles are equal.

  2. 2

    A square of side a and one of side b have every side ratio equal to ba\dfrac{b}{a}ab​, so the sides are proportional.

  3. 3

    Squares of different sides are not the same size, so they are not all congruent.

  4. Check: a 3 cm square and a 6 cm square: all angles 90°, all ratios 2 ✔.

  5. Answer All squares are similar.

    Common trap: Thinking "squares are all the same", so congruent. Only squares with the same side are congruent.
Exercise 6.1, Q1 (iii)All … triangles are similar1 mark

Fill in the blanks using the correct word given in brackets :
(iii) All ________ triangles are similar. (isosceles, equilateral)

Given: the words "isosceles" and "equilateral". Asked: the kind of triangle that is always similar to another of the same kind.

Concept used: equal angles and proportional sides (Topic 2); a counter-example shows "not always". Why it applies: we test each kind.

  1. 1

    Equilateral: every angle is 60° and the three side ratios are all ba\dfrac{b}{a}ab​ — always similar.

  2. 2

    Isosceles: sides 5, 5, 6 and 5, 5, 8 are both isosceles, but 55≠86\dfrac{5}{5} \neq \dfrac{8}{6}55​=68​ — not similar. So "isosceles" is wrong.

  3. Check: two equilateral triangles of sides 2 cm and 7 cm have all ratios 72\dfrac{7}{2}27​ ✔.

  4. Answer All equilateral triangles are similar.

    Common trap: Choosing "isosceles" because isosceles triangles "look alike". Their angles can be anything (as long as two are equal).
Exercise 6.1, Q1 (iv)Two polygons of the same number of sides are similar, if …1 mark

Fill in the blanks using the correct word given in brackets :
(iv) Two polygons of the same number of sides are similar, if (a) their corresponding angles are ________ and (b) their corresponding sides are ________. (equal, proportional)

Given: the words "equal" and "proportional". Asked: the two conditions for similar polygons.

Concept used: the definition of similar polygons (Topic 2). Why it applies: this is that definition.

  1. 1

    Angles do not change in an enlargement, so corresponding angles must be equal.

  2. 2

    Every length is multiplied by the same scale factor, so corresponding sides must be proportional.

  3. Check: a square and a rectangle satisfy (a) but not (b) and are not similar — both conditions are needed ✔.

  4. Answer (a) equal (b) proportional

    Common trap: Swapping the words: sides of similar polygons are usually not equal.
Exercise 6.1, Q2 (i)Examples of similar figures1 mark

Give two different examples of pair of
(i) similar figures.

Given: — Asked: two pairs of figures that are similar.

Concept used: similar = same shape, any size (Topic 2). Why it applies: we need pairs where one is an enlargement of the other.

  1. 1

    A circle of radius 3 cm and a circle of radius 5 cm (all circles are similar).

  2. 2

    An equilateral triangle of side 4 cm and an equilateral triangle of side 10 cm (all angles 60°; every side ratio 2.5).

  3. Check: in each pair, one figure is an exact enlargement of the other ✔.

  4. Answer (1) Two circles of radii 3 cm and 5 cm. (2) Two equilateral triangles of sides 4 cm and 10 cm. (Other correct pairs: two squares; a photograph and its enlargement; a map and a bigger map of the same place.)

    Common trap: Giving two congruent figures only — they are similar, but the question is meant to show figures of different sizes.
Exercise 6.1, Q2 (ii)Examples of non-similar figures1 mark

Give two different examples of pair of
(ii) non-similar figures.

Given: — Asked: two pairs of figures that are not similar.

Concept used: a pair is not similar if the angles differ or the sides are not in one ratio (Topic 2). Why it applies: each example breaks one condition.

  1. 1

    A square of side 4 cm and a rectangle 4 cm × 6 cm: angles equal, sides not in one ratio (44≠64\dfrac{4}{4} \neq \dfrac{6}{4}44​=46​).

  2. 2

    A square of side 5 cm and a rhombus of side 5 cm with an angle of 60°: sides in one ratio, angles not equal.

  3. Check: each pair fails exactly one of the two conditions ✔.

  4. Answer (1) A 4 cm square and a 4 cm × 6 cm rectangle. (2) A 5 cm square and a rhombus of side 5 cm with angles 60° and 120°. (Also correct: a circle and a square; a triangle and a square.)

    Common trap: Giving two figures of different sizes but the same shape (two circles, say). Different size does not make figures non-similar.
Exercise 6.1, Q3Are the quadrilaterals similar?2 marks

State whether the following quadrilaterals are similar or not:

Fig. 6.8 (described): a quadrilateral PQRS with every side marked 1.5 cm and no right angles marked (it is drawn slanting, like a rhombus), and a square ABCD with every side marked 3 cm and a right angle marked at each corner.

Given: PQRS: all sides 1.5 cm, angles not right angles; ABCD: all sides 3 cm, all angles 90°. Asked: similar or not.

Concept used: both conditions for similar polygons (Topic 2). Why it applies: the sides are proportional, so the angles decide.

  1. 1

    Sides: every ratio is 31.5=2\dfrac{3}{1.5} = 21.53​=2, so the sides are in one ratio.

  2. 2

    Angles: ABCD has four right angles; PQRS (a rhombus drawn slanting) does not.

  3. 3

    The corresponding angles are not equal, so the quadrilaterals are not similar.

  4. Check: this is exactly the square-and-rhombus counter-example of Topic 2 ✔.

  5. Answer No, they are not similar: the sides are proportional but the angles are not equal.

    Common trap: Answering "yes" from the side ratio alone.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. ABPQ=BCQR\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{BC}{QR}PQAB​=QRBC​ and ∠B=∠Q\angle B = \angle Q∠B=∠Q △ABC ~ △PQR (corresponding sides proportional, corresponding angles equal)
    2. BC=2BDBC = 2BDBC=2BD and QR=2QMQR = 2QMQR=2QM AD and PM are medians
    3. ABPQ=2BD2QM=BDQM\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{2BD}{2QM} = \dfrac{BD}{QM}PQAB​=2QM2BD​=QMBD​ Steps 1 and 2
    4. △ABD ~ △PQM SAS similarity criterion: ∠B=∠Q\angle B = \angle Q∠B=∠Q, and the sides forming them are proportional (step 3)
    5. ABPQ=ADPM\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{AD}{PM}PQAB​=PMAD​ Corresponding sides of similar triangles. Hence proved.

  2. Check: Topic 5, Worked example 4: ABPQ=96\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{9}{6}PQAB​=69​ and ADPM=7.55\dfrac{AD}{PM} = \dfrac{7.5}{5}PMAD​=57.5​ — both 1.5 ✔.

  3. Answer ABPQ=ADPM\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{AD}{PM}PQAB​=PMAD​.

    Common trap: Writing "AA" in step 4. Only one angle pair is known; the median gives a side ratio, so the criterion is SAS.
