Topic 4 of 5

Criteria for similarity of triangles

About 75 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • State the AAA/AA, SSS and SAS similarity criteria (without proof) and choose the right one.
  • Write the correspondence correctly and prove two triangles similar with a reason for every line.
  • Use similarity to find unknown sides and angles.

A surveyor cannot measure across a river, but she can measure angles and short distances on her own bank. If she can show that a small triangle she drew is similar to a big triangle across the river, one multiplication gives the river's width. To use that, she needs a quick test for similarity — without measuring all six parts of both triangles. That test is what this topic is about.

Triangles are special

For quadrilaterals, equal angles and proportional sides were both needed (Topic 2). For triangles, one condition brings the other with it, and even less is enough. Two triangles ABC and DEF are similar, written △ABC ~ △DEF, if:

  • AAA (or AA): their corresponding angles are equal. Since the angles of a triangle add up to 180°, two pairs of equal angles are enough — the third pair is then equal too.
  • SSS: all three pairs of corresponding sides are in the same ratio: ABDE=BCEF=CAFD\dfrac{AB}{DE} = \dfrac{BC}{EF} = \dfrac{CA}{FD}DEAB​=EFBC​=FDCA​.
  • SAS: one pair of angles is equal, ∠A=∠D\angle A = \angle D∠A=∠D, and the sides that form those angles are in the same ratio: ABDE=ACDF\dfrac{AB}{DE} = \dfrac{AC}{DF}DEAB​=DFAC​.

The syllabus asks you to state these criteria and use them; their proofs are not required.

The SAS warning. The equal angle must be the angle between the two sides you compare (the "included" angle). One equal angle with two proportional sides that do not form it is not a criterion at all — such triangles may or may not be similar (Worked example 3 (b) shows a pair that is not).

Quick reminder (Class 9). The congruence criteria SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS match two triangles exactly. The similarity criteria are their "any size" versions: equal sides become sides in one ratio.

The matching card

Similarity is only useful if you match the right parts.

  1. Find the equal angles and write the vertex matching: e.g. A↔NA \leftrightarrow NA↔N, B↔MB \leftrightarrow MB↔M, C↔LC \leftrightarrow LC↔L.
  2. Write the triangles in that order: △ABC ~ △NML.
  3. Write the ratio line by reading the letters in the same positions: first two letters, last two letters, first and last:
ABNM=BCML=CALN\dfrac{AB}{NM} = \dfrac{BC}{ML} = \dfrac{CA}{LN}NMAB​=MLBC​=LNCA​Swipe to see the whole equation →

The proof routine

"In △… and △…: (angle or side facts, each with a reason) → so △… ~ △… (criterion) → so ……=……\dfrac{\ldots}{\ldots} = \dfrac{\ldots}{\ldots}……​=……​ (corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional)." Every line needs a reason. Do not write "DE ∥ BC" as a reason unless it is given or proved — check it with the converse of BPT (Topic 3).

Optional — not for the exam

NCERT adds, in "A note to the reader": "If in two right triangles, hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are proportional to the hypotenuse and one side of the other triangle, then the two triangles are similar. This may be referred to as the RHS Similarity Criterion." It is not one of the criteria listed in the 2026-27 syllabus, so in the exam prove right triangles similar with AA, SSS or SAS.

Not in the 2026-27 syllabus, so left out of this chapter: areas of similar triangles, and proofs of Pythagoras' theorem.

Worked example 1

Which criterion? (angles)

Question. In △ABC, ∠A=48°\angle A = 48°∠A=48° and ∠B=77°\angle B = 77°∠B=77°. In △LMN, ∠L=55°\angle L = 55°∠L=55° and ∠N=48°\angle N = 48°∠N=48°. Are the triangles similar? If so, write the similarity in the correct order and name the criterion.

Given: two angles of each triangle. Asked: similar? the statement and the criterion.

Concept used: angle sum, then AA; the matching card. Why it applies: only angles are given.

  1. 1

    Third angle of △ABC.

    ∠C=180°−48°−77°=55°\angle C = 180° - 48° - 77° = 55°∠C=180°−48°−77°=55°Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Third angle of △LMN.

    ∠M=180°−55°−48°=77°\angle M = 180° - 55° - 48° = 77°∠M=180°−55°−48°=77°Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Match equal angles: A↔NA \leftrightarrow NA↔N (48°), B↔MB \leftrightarrow MB↔M (77°), C↔LC \leftrightarrow LC↔L (55°).

  4. 4

    Two pairs of equal angles, so the triangles are similar (AA): △ABC ~ △NML.

  5. Check: read the statement back: ∠A=∠N=48°\angle A = \angle N = 48°∠A=∠N=48° ✔, ∠B=∠M=77°\angle B = \angle M = 77°∠B=∠M=77° ✔, ∠C=∠L=55°\angle C = \angle L = 55°∠C=∠L=55° ✔.

  6. Answer Yes: △ABC ~ △NML (AA similarity criterion).

    Common trap: Writing △ABC ~ △LMN because the letters are in alphabetical order. That would claim ∠A=∠L\angle A = \angle L∠A=∠L, i.e. 48° = 55°.

Worked example 2

Which criterion? (sides)

Question. In △ABC, AB=4AB = 4AB=4 cm, BC=6BC = 6BC=6 cm, CA=7CA = 7CA=7 cm. In △PQR, PQ=10.5PQ = 10.5PQ=10.5 cm, QR=6QR = 6QR=6 cm, RP=9RP = 9RP=9 cm. Are the triangles similar? Write the correspondence.

Given: three sides of each triangle. Asked: similar? in which order?

Concept used: SSS — match shortest with shortest, middle with middle, longest with longest. Why it applies: only sides are given.

  1. 1

    Order the sides. △ABC: AB=4<BC=6<CA=7AB = 4 < BC = 6 < CA = 7AB=4<BC=6<CA=7. △PQR: QR=6<RP=9<PQ=10.5QR = 6 < RP = 9 < PQ = 10.5QR=6<RP=9<PQ=10.5.

  2. 2

    Ratios in that order.

    QRAB=64=1.5,RPBC=96=1.5,PQCA=10.57=1.5\dfrac{QR}{AB} = \dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5, \quad \dfrac{RP}{BC} = \dfrac{9}{6} = 1.5, \quad \dfrac{PQ}{CA} = \dfrac{10.5}{7} = 1.5ABQR​=46​=1.5,BCRP​=69​=1.5,CAPQ​=710.5​=1.5Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    All three ratios are equal, so the triangles are similar (SSS).

  4. 4

    Find the matching from the sides. AB ↔ QR and BC ↔ RP share B and R, so B↔RB \leftrightarrow RB↔R; then A↔QA \leftrightarrow QA↔Q and C↔PC \leftrightarrow PC↔P.

  5. 5

    △ABC ~ △QRP.

  6. Check: the third pair: CA ↔ PQ (last and first letters) — 10.57=1.5\dfrac{10.5}{7} = 1.5710.5​=1.5 ✔.

  7. Answer Yes: △ABC ~ △QRP (SSS similarity criterion), scale factor 1.5.

    Common trap: Pairing AB with PQ because both are "the first side": 10.54≠66\dfrac{10.5}{4} \neq \dfrac{6}{6}410.5​=66​, and you would wrongly say "not similar".

Worked example 3

Which criterion? (an angle and two sides)

Question. (a) In △DEF, DE=3DE = 3DE=3 cm, DF=5DF = 5DF=5 cm and ∠D=40°\angle D = 40°∠D=40°. In △XYZ, XY=4.5XY = 4.5XY=4.5 cm, XZ=7.5XZ = 7.5XZ=7.5 cm and ∠X=40°\angle X = 40°∠X=40°. Are they similar? (b) Now suppose instead that △XYZ has XY=4.5XY = 4.5XY=4.5 cm, XZ=7.5XZ = 7.5XZ=7.5 cm and ∠Y=40°\angle Y = 40°∠Y=40°. Can SAS be used?

Given: one angle and two sides of each triangle. Asked: similar? which criterion?

Concept used: SAS needs the equal angle between the two proportional sides. Why it applies: this is the only criterion that uses an angle and sides.

  1. 1

    (a)

    Sides that form ∠D\angle D∠D: DE and DF. Sides that form ∠X\angle X∠X: XY and XZ.

  2. 2

    Ratios.

    XYDE=4.53=1.5,XZDF=7.55=1.5\dfrac{XY}{DE} = \dfrac{4.5}{3} = 1.5, \quad \dfrac{XZ}{DF} = \dfrac{7.5}{5} = 1.5DEXY​=34.5​=1.5,DFXZ​=57.5​=1.5Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    ∠D=∠X=40°\angle D = \angle X = 40°∠D=∠X=40° and the sides forming these angles are in one ratio, so △DEF ~ △XYZ (SAS).

  4. 4

    (b)

    ∠Y\angle Y∠Y is formed by YX and YZ — but the sides we know are XY and XZ. The known angle is not between the two known sides.

  5. 5

    SAS cannot be used, and no other criterion fits. In fact, with ∠Y=40°\angle Y = 40°∠Y=40° this triangle has ∠X≈117°\angle X \approx 117°∠X≈117° and ∠Z≈23°\angle Z \approx 23°∠Z≈23°, while △DEF has angles 40°, about 104.5° and about 35.5°. They do not match, so it is not similar to △DEF.

  6. Check: (a) the angle "between" DE and DF is at their common letter D ✔; XY and XZ meet at X ✔.

  7. Answer (a) Yes, △DEF ~ △XYZ (SAS). (b) No — the angle is not included between the sides; SAS does not apply, and this triangle is not similar to △DEF.

    Common trap: Seeing "one angle and two sides in one ratio" and writing SAS without checking where the angle is. Find the common letter of the two sides: the angle must be at that vertex.

Worked example 4

A two-column similarity proof

Question. In △ABC, D lies on AB and E lies on AC, and ∠ADE=∠ACB\angle ADE = \angle ACB∠ADE=∠ACB. (a) Prove that △ADE ~ △ACB. (b) Hence show that AD×AB=AE×ACAD \times AB = AE \times ACAD×AB=AE×AC. (c) If AD=3AD = 3AD=3 cm, AE=4AE = 4AE=4 cm and AC=9AC = 9AC=9 cm, find AB.

Given: ∠ADE=∠ACB\angle ADE = \angle ACB∠ADE=∠ACB. To prove: △ADE ~ △ACB, then AD×AB=AE×ACAD \times AB = AE \times ACAD×AB=AE×AC.

Concept used: AA; the matching card; corresponding sides are proportional. Why it applies: two pairs of equal angles are available — the given one and the common angle A.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. In △ADE and △ACB: ∠DAE=∠CAB\angle DAE = \angle CAB∠DAE=∠CAB Common angle (∠A\angle A∠A)
    2. ∠ADE=∠ACB\angle ADE = \angle ACB∠ADE=∠ACB Given
    3. △ADE ~ △ACB AA similarity criterion (matching A↔AA \leftrightarrow AA↔A, D↔CD \leftrightarrow CD↔C, E↔BE \leftrightarrow BE↔B)
    4. ADAC=AEAB\dfrac{AD}{AC} = \dfrac{AE}{AB}ACAD​=ABAE​ Corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional (first two letters; first and last letters)
    5. AD×AB=AE×ACAD \times AB = AE \times ACAD×AB=AE×AC Cross-multiply step 4. Hence shown.
  2. 6

    (c)

    Substitute in step 5.

    3×AB=4×93 \times AB = 4 \times 93×AB=4×9Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 7

    Divide by 3.

    AB=12 cmAB = 12 \text{ cm}AB=12 cmSwipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: ADAC=39=13\dfrac{AD}{AC} = \dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{1}{3}ACAD​=93​=31​ and AEAB=412=13\dfrac{AE}{AB} = \dfrac{4}{12} = \dfrac{1}{3}ABAE​=124​=31​ ✔.

  5. Answer (a) △ADE ~ △ACB (AA) (b) shown (c) AB=12AB = 12AB=12 cm

    Common trap: Writing △ADE ~ △ABC. Then D would match B — but the given equal angle pairs D with C. With the wrong order, step 4 becomes ADAB=AEAC\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{AE}{AC}ABAD​=ACAE​, which gives AB=274AB = \dfrac{27}{4}AB=427​ cm — a wrong answer.

Worked example 5

Prove, then find a length

Question. In the figure, AB⊥BDAB \perp BDAB⊥BD and ED⊥BDED \perp BDED⊥BD, and the segment AE crosses BD at C (A and E on opposite sides of BD). (a) Prove that △ABC ~ △EDC. (b) If AB=6AB = 6AB=6 m, BC=4BC = 4BC=4 m and CD=10CD = 10CD=10 m, find DE.

Given: ∠ABC=∠EDC=90°\angle ABC = \angle EDC = 90°∠ABC=∠EDC=90°; AE and BD cross at C. To prove: △ABC ~ △EDC; then find DE.

Concept used: AA (right angles and vertically opposite angles), then proportional sides. Why it applies: two pairs of equal angles can be read straight from the figure.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. In △ABC and △EDC: ∠ABC=∠EDC\angle ABC = \angle EDC∠ABC=∠EDC Each is 90° (given perpendiculars)
    2. ∠ACB=∠ECD\angle ACB = \angle ECD∠ACB=∠ECD Vertically opposite angles
    3. △ABC ~ △EDC AA similarity criterion (A↔EA \leftrightarrow EA↔E, B↔DB \leftrightarrow DB↔D, C↔CC \leftrightarrow CC↔C)
    4. ABED=BCDC\dfrac{AB}{ED} = \dfrac{BC}{DC}EDAB​=DCBC​ Corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional
  2. 5

    (b)

    Substitute.

    6DE=410\dfrac{6}{DE} = \dfrac{4}{10}DE6​=104​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 6

    Cross-multiply: 4×DE=604 \times DE = 604×DE=60.

    DE=15 mDE = 15 \text{ m}DE=15 mSwipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: scale factor from △ABC to △EDC is 104=2.5\dfrac{10}{4} = 2.5410​=2.5, and 6×2.5=156 \times 2.5 = 156×2.5=15 ✔.

  5. Answer (a) △ABC ~ △EDC (AA) (b) DE=15DE = 15DE=15 m

    Common trap: Pairing AB with DC because both are "next to the right angle on the left". Match by the angles: AB is opposite ∠C\angle C∠C in △ABC, and ED is opposite ∠C\angle C∠C in △EDC.

Worked example 6

Finding a side from an angle condition

Question. In △PQR, S is a point on QR such that ∠QPS=∠QRP\angle QPS = \angle QRP∠QPS=∠QRP. PQ=10PQ = 10PQ=10 cm, QS=4QS = 4QS=4 cm and PR=20PR = 20PR=20 cm. Find QR and PS.

Finding a side with SAS

Question. In △XYZ and △LMN, ∠X=∠L\angle X = \angle L∠X=∠L, XY=6XY = 6XY=6 cm, XZ=9XZ = 9XZ=9 cm, LM=4LM = 4LM=4 cm, LN=6LN = 6LN=6 cm and YZ=7.5YZ = 7.5YZ=7.5 cm. Find MN.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers name wrong correspondence and choosing the criterion among the four commonest reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • △ABC ~ △LMN "in alphabetical order"

    The order claims which angles are equal.

    Match the equal angles first (matching card), then write the order.

  • Right order, wrong ratio: ABML\dfrac{AB}{ML}MLAB​ for △ABC ~ △NML

    Sides must be read from the same positions.

    First two letters with first two letters: ABNM\dfrac{AB}{NM}NMAB​.

  • SAS with an angle not between the two sides

    That is not a criterion; the triangles need not be similar.

    Find the common letter of the two sides — the angle must be there.

  • "DE ∥ BC" used without being given

    Looking parallel proves nothing.

    Prove it (converse of BPT) or use only the given facts.

  • Writing "AAA" after finding only two equal angles

    Not wrong, but say what you used

    Write "(AA similarity criterion)" — or find the third angle first and then write AAA.

  • Writing "similar" with no criterion

    The criterion is the reason for that line.

    Every line of a proof needs a reason.

Key points

  • AA: two pairs of equal angles. SSS: three pairs of sides in one ratio. SAS: one equal angle and the two sides that form it in one ratio.
  • An angle that is not between the two proportional sides gives no criterion.
  • Matching card: equal angles → vertex matching → statement in that order → ratio line from the same positions.
  • After similarity: corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are proportional.
  • RHS similarity is an optional NCERT note, not for the exam; areas of similar triangles are not in the syllabus.
