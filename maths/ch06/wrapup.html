Wrap-up

The criterion chooser, the matching card, the BPT skeleton and mixed problems

About 40 minutes

Everything in this chapter comes back to one idea: similar triangles have equal angles and proportional sides, and a line parallel to one side of a triangle makes them. This page puts the three tools you use in every question on one sheet, then gives four mixed problems in the styles of the sample papers — a trapezium with a quadratic, BPT followed by similarity, a BPT application with decimals, and an indirect measurement.

The criterion chooser

What you know about the two triangles Criterion
two pairs of equal angles (the third pair follows from the angle sum) AA
all three pairs of sides in one ratio SSS
one pair of equal angles and the two sides that form those angles, in one ratio SAS
one pair of equal angles and two sides in one ratio, but the angle is not formed by those sides no criterion — do not conclude similarity

Before choosing: use only facts that are given or proved. A line that "looks parallel" is not parallel until the converse of BPT (equal ratios) says so.

The matching card

  1. Match the equal angles: write A↔…A \leftrightarrow \ldotsA↔…, B↔…B \leftrightarrow \ldotsB↔…, C↔…C \leftrightarrow \ldotsC↔…
  2. Write the similarity in that order: △ABC ~ △…
  3. Write the ratio line from the same positions — first two letters, last two letters, first and last:
AB…=BC…=CA…\dfrac{AB}{\ldots} = \dfrac{BC}{\ldots} = \dfrac{CA}{\ldots}…AB​=…BC​=…CA​Swipe to see the whole equation →

The BPT proof skeleton

Learn this outline; then write the full proof from it (Topic 3, Worked example 1).

  • Figure: △ABC, DE∥BCDE \parallel BCDE∥BC, D on AB, E on AC; BE and CD joined; DM⊥ACDM \perp ACDM⊥AC, EN⊥ABEN \perp ABEN⊥AB.
  • Given / To prove: DE∥BCDE \parallel BCDE∥BC / ADDB=AEEC\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}DBAD​=ECAE​.
  • Construction: join BE and CD; draw DM⊥ACDM \perp ACDM⊥AC and EN⊥ABEN \perp ABEN⊥AB.
  • (1) ar(ADE)ar(BDE)=12×AD×EN12×DB×EN=ADDB\dfrac{\text{ar}(ADE)}{\text{ar}(BDE)} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2} \times AD \times EN}{\dfrac{1}{2} \times DB \times EN} = \dfrac{AD}{DB}ar(BDE)ar(ADE)​=21​×DB×EN21​×AD×EN​=DBAD​
  • (2) ar(ADE)ar(DEC)=12×AE×DM12×EC×DM=AEEC\dfrac{\text{ar}(ADE)}{\text{ar}(DEC)} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2} \times AE \times DM}{\dfrac{1}{2} \times EC \times DM} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}ar(DEC)ar(ADE)​=21​×EC×DM21​×AE×DM​=ECAE​
  • (3) ar(BDE)=ar(DEC)\text{ar}(BDE) = \text{ar}(DEC)ar(BDE)=ar(DEC) — same base DE, between the same parallels DE and BC.
  • Conclusion: from (1), (2), (3), ADDB=AEEC\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}DBAD​=ECAE​.

Mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

Trapezium diagonals and a quadratic

Question. ABCD is a trapezium with AB∥DCAB \parallel DCAB∥DC, and its diagonals meet at O. If OA=3x−1OA = 3x - 1OA=3x−1, OC=5x−3OC = 5x - 3OC=5x−3, OB=2x+1OB = 2x + 1OB=2x+1 and OD=6x−5OD = 6x - 5OD=6x−5 (in cm), find x and the four lengths.

Given: AB∥DCAB \parallel DCAB∥DC and the four pieces in x. Asked: x.

Concept used: △OAB ~ △OCD (AA, alternate angles), so OAOC=OBOD\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD}OCOA​=ODOB​; then a quadratic (Chapter 4), rejecting a value that makes a length negative. Why it applies: the diagonals of a trapezium are divided in the same ratio.

  1. 1

    In △OAB and △OCD, ∠OAB=∠OCD\angle OAB = \angle OCD∠OAB=∠OCD and ∠OBA=∠ODC\angle OBA = \angle ODC∠OBA=∠ODC (alternate angles), so △OAB ~ △OCD (AA).

  2. 2

    Corresponding sides.

    OAOC=OBOD\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD}OCOA​=ODOB​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Substitute.

    3x−15x−3=2x+16x−5\dfrac{3x - 1}{5x - 3} = \dfrac{2x + 1}{6x - 5}5x−33x−1​=6x−52x+1​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Cross-multiply.

    (3x−1)(6x−5)=(5x−3)(2x+1)(3x - 1)(6x - 5) = (5x - 3)(2x + 1)(3x−1)(6x−5)=(5x−3)(2x+1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Expand both sides.

    18x2−21x+5=10x2−x−318x^2 - 21x + 5 = 10x^2 - x - 318x2−21x+5=10x2−x−3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Bring everything to the left.

    8x2−20x+8=08x^2 - 20x + 8 = 08x2−20x+8=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Divide by 4.

    2x2−5x+2=02x^2 - 5x + 2 = 02x2−5x+2=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Factorise.

    (2x−1)(x−2)=0(2x - 1)(x - 2) = 0(2x−1)(x−2)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    So x=12x = \dfrac{1}{2}x=21​ or x=2x = 2x=2.

  10. 10

    Test x=12x = \dfrac{1}{2}x=21​: OC=5×12−3=−12OC = 5 \times \dfrac{1}{2} - 3 = -\dfrac{1}{2}OC=5×21​−3=−21​, a negative length — reject.

  11. 11

    Take x=2x = 2x=2: OA=5OA = 5OA=5, OC=7OC = 7OC=7, OB=5OB = 5OB=5, OD=7OD = 7OD=7 (cm).

  12. Check: 57=57\dfrac{5}{7} = \dfrac{5}{7}75​=75​ ✔; all four lengths positive ✔.

  13. Answer x=2x = 2x=2; OA=5OA = 5OA=5 cm, OC=7OC = 7OC=7 cm, OB=5OB = 5OB=5 cm, OD=7OD = 7OD=7 cm

    Common trap: Keeping both roots. A length can never be negative (or zero), so every root must be tested in every expression.

Mixed example 2

BPT, then similarity

Question. In △ABC, DE∥BCDE \parallel BCDE∥BC with D on AB and E on AC. AD=3AD = 3AD=3 cm, DB=2DB = 2DB=2 cm and BC=10BC = 10BC=10 cm. Find DE.

Given: DE∥BCDE \parallel BCDE∥BC, AD=3AD = 3AD=3, DB=2DB = 2DB=2, BC=10BC = 10BC=10 (cm). Asked: DE.

Concept used: △ADE ~ △ABC (AA, corresponding angles); then the ratio with the whole side AB. Why it applies: DE is a side of the small triangle and BC is the matching side of the big one.

  1. 1

    ∠ADE=∠ABC\angle ADE = \angle ABC∠ADE=∠ABC and ∠AED=∠ACB\angle AED = \angle ACB∠AED=∠ACB (corresponding angles, DE∥BCDE \parallel BCDE∥BC), so △ADE ~ △ABC (AA).

  2. 2

    Matching sides: DEBC=ADAB\dfrac{DE}{BC} = \dfrac{AD}{AB}BCDE​=ABAD​.

  3. 3

    AB=AD+DB=3+2=5AB = AD + DB = 3 + 2 = 5AB=AD+DB=3+2=5 cm.

  4. 4

    Substitute.

    DE10=35\dfrac{DE}{10} = \dfrac{3}{5}10DE​=53​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Multiply by 10.

    DE=6 cmDE = 6 \text{ cm}DE=6 cmSwipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: DE must be shorter than BC (the small triangle) and 610=35\dfrac{6}{10} = \dfrac{3}{5}106​=53​ ✔.

  7. Answer DE=6DE = 6DE=6 cm

    Common trap: Using DEBC=ADDB\dfrac{DE}{BC} = \dfrac{AD}{DB}BCDE​=DBAD​, which gives DE=15DE = 15DE=15 cm — longer than BC. DB is not a side of either triangle; AB is.

Mixed example 3

A BPT application with decimals

Question. A line drawn parallel to QR meets the sides PQ and PR of △PQR at L and M respectively. If PL=4.2PL = 4.2PL=4.2 cm, PQ=10.5PQ = 10.5PQ=10.5 cm and MR=4.8MR = 4.8MR=4.8 cm, find PM.

Given: LM∥QRLM \parallel QRLM∥QR, PL=4.2PL = 4.2PL=4.2, PQ=10.5PQ = 10.5PQ=10.5, MR=4.8MR = 4.8MR=4.8 (cm). Asked: PM.

Concept used: BPT with the two pieces of each side. Why it applies: MR is a piece of PR, so the part-to-part form fits.

  1. 1

    LQ=PQ−PL=10.5−4.2=6.3LQ = PQ - PL = 10.5 - 4.2 = 6.3LQ=PQ−PL=10.5−4.2=6.3 cm.

  2. 2

    BPT.

    PLLQ=PMMR\dfrac{PL}{LQ} = \dfrac{PM}{MR}LQPL​=MRPM​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Substitute.

    4.26.3=PM4.8\dfrac{4.2}{6.3} = \dfrac{PM}{4.8}6.34.2​=4.8PM​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Simplify: 4.26.3=23\dfrac{4.2}{6.3} = \dfrac{2}{3}6.34.2​=32​.

  5. 5

    Multiply by 4.8.

    PM=23×4.8=3.2 cmPM = \dfrac{2}{3} \times 4.8 = 3.2 \text{ cm}PM=32​×4.8=3.2 cmSwipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: PR=3.2+4.8=8PR = 3.2 + 4.8 = 8PR=3.2+4.8=8, and PLPQ=4.210.5=0.4=3.28=PMPR\dfrac{PL}{PQ} = \dfrac{4.2}{10.5} = 0.4 = \dfrac{3.2}{8} = \dfrac{PM}{PR}PQPL​=10.54.2​=0.4=83.2​=PRPM​ ✔.

  7. Answer PM=3.2PM = 3.2PM=3.2 cm

    Common trap: Using PLPQ=PMMR\dfrac{PL}{PQ} = \dfrac{PM}{MR}PQPL​=MRPM​ (mixed forms), which gives PM=1.92PM = 1.92PM=1.92 cm.

Mixed example 4

Measuring across a river

Question. To find the width PQ of a river, Meera stands at Q on one bank, directly opposite a tree P on the other bank. She walks 36 m along her bank, at right angles to PQ, to a point R and puts a stick there; then she walks on another 12 m to T and turns at right angles away from the river, walking until she reaches a point S from which the stick at R and the tree P are in one straight line. TS is 9 m. Find the width of the river.

Given: PQ⊥QTPQ \perp QTPQ⊥QT, ST⊥QTST \perp QTST⊥QT, R on QT with QR=36QR = 36QR=36 m and RT=12RT = 12RT=12 m, P, R, S collinear, TS=9TS = 9TS=9 m. Asked: PQ.

Concept used: AA (right angles at Q and T; vertically opposite angles at R), then proportional sides. Why it applies: the line PS crosses QT at R, making two right triangles.

  1. 1

    In △PQR and △STR: ∠PQR=∠STR=90°\angle PQR = \angle STR = 90°∠PQR=∠STR=90° and ∠PRQ=∠SRT\angle PRQ = \angle SRT∠PRQ=∠SRT (vertically opposite), so △PQR ~ △STR (AA).

  2. 2

    Matching sides.

    PQST=QRTR\dfrac{PQ}{ST} = \dfrac{QR}{TR}STPQ​=TRQR​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Substitute.

    PQ9=3612=3\dfrac{PQ}{9} = \dfrac{36}{12} = 39PQ​=1236​=3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Multiply by 9.

    PQ=27 mPQ = 27 \text{ m}PQ=27 mSwipe to see the whole equation →

  5. Check: scale factor 3: 9×3=279 \times 3 = 279×3=27 and 12×3=3612 \times 3 = 3612×3=36 ✔.

  6. Answer The river is 27 m wide.

    Common trap: Pairing PQ with TR (both "the side next to the river"): PQ12=369\dfrac{PQ}{12} = \dfrac{36}{9}12PQ​=936​ gives 48 m. Match by angles: PQ is opposite ∠R\angle R∠R in △PQR, ST is opposite ∠R\angle R∠R in △STR.
