Topic 5 of 5

Applications of similar triangles

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Solve real-life problems (heights from shadows, a lamp-post and a person, poles, rivers) with similar triangles.
  • Use the ratios of perimeters, medians and altitudes of similar triangles.
  • Solve multi-step problems that combine BPT and similarity (for example trapezium diagonals, or the perpendicular to the hypotenuse).

How tall is the tallest tree in your school? You cannot climb it with a tape measure — but at 4 o'clock on a sunny afternoon, a metre stick and the tree both cast shadows, and the sun's rays hit them at the same angle. Two similar triangles appear on the ground, and the tree's height drops out of one proportion. Surveyors used exactly this idea of indirect measurement long before satellites.

Shadows and lamps

  • Sun: the sun is so far away that its rays are parallel. A vertical pole and its shadow, and a vertical tower and its shadow (at the same time), make two right triangles with the same angle at the shadow tip — similar by AA.
  • Lamp: a lamp-post, a person and the person's shadow share the same shadow tip: the small triangle (person) sits inside the big triangle (lamp-post), again similar by AA (right angles, common angle at the tip). The big triangle's base is the distance from the post plus the shadow.

In both cases, write the matching first and keep each object with its own shadow:

height of object 1its shadow=height of object 2its shadow\dfrac{\text{height of object 1}}{\text{its shadow}} = \dfrac{\text{height of object 2}}{\text{its shadow}}its shadowheight of object 1​=its shadowheight of object 2​Swipe to see the whole equation →

Perimeters, medians and altitudes

If △ABC ~ △PQR with scale factor k=PQABk = \dfrac{PQ}{AB}k=ABPQ​, then every length in △PQR is k times the matching length in △ABC — not only the sides:

  • the perimeters are in the ratio k (add PQ+QR+RP=k(AB+BC+CA)PQ + QR + RP = k(AB + BC + CA)PQ+QR+RP=k(AB+BC+CA));
  • matching medians and matching altitudes are in the ratio k (Worked example 4 proves it for altitudes; NCERT Exercise 6.3, Q16 does it for medians).

So perimeter(PQR)perimeter(ABC)=PQAB\dfrac{\text{perimeter}(PQR)}{\text{perimeter}(ABC)} = \dfrac{PQ}{AB}perimeter(ABC)perimeter(PQR)​=ABPQ​ — the plain ratio, not its square. (The squared ratio belongs to areas of similar triangles, which are not in the 2026-27 syllabus.)

Multi-step problems

Most longer questions chain two ideas: a parallel line gives equal angles (so similar triangles), and similar triangles give a ratio, which BPT or a second pair of triangles turns into the answer. Draw the figure, mark every given right angle and parallel, and name each pair of triangles before you write ratios.

Worked example 1

Height from shadows

Question. One sunny afternoon, a 2.4 m vertical pole throws a 1.8 m shadow on level ground. At that same moment the shadow of a nearby building is 13.5 m. How tall is the building?

Given: pole 2.4 m, its shadow 1.8 m; building's shadow 13.5 m. Asked: the building's height h.

Concept used: parallel sun rays make equal angles at the shadow tips; with the right angles, the triangles are similar (AA). Why it applies: both objects are vertical and the measurements are at the same time.

  1. 1

    Let AB be the pole, BC its shadow; DE the building, EF its shadow. ∠B=∠E=90°\angle B = \angle E = 90°∠B=∠E=90°, and ∠C=∠F\angle C = \angle F∠C=∠F (the sun's rays make the same angle with the ground).

  2. 2

    △ABC ~ △DEF (AA).

  3. 3

    Each height with its own shadow.

    ABBC=DEEF\dfrac{AB}{BC} = \dfrac{DE}{EF}BCAB​=EFDE​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Substitute.

    2.41.8=h13.5\dfrac{2.4}{1.8} = \dfrac{h}{13.5}1.82.4​=13.5h​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Multiply both sides by 13.5.

    h=2.4×13.51.8=18 mh = \dfrac{2.4 \times 13.5}{1.8} = 18 \text{ m}h=1.82.4×13.5​=18 mSwipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: each height is 43\dfrac{4}{3}34​ of its shadow: 43×1.8=2.4\dfrac{4}{3} \times 1.8 = 2.434​×1.8=2.4 ✔ and 43×13.5=18\dfrac{4}{3} \times 13.5 = 1834​×13.5=18 ✔.

    Common trap: Pairing the pole's height with the building's shadow: 2.413.5=h1.8\dfrac{2.4}{13.5} = \dfrac{h}{1.8}13.52.4​=1.8h​ gives h=0.32h = 0.32h=0.32 m — a building shorter than the pole, though its shadow is longer.

Worked example 2

A boy walking away from a lamp-post

Question. A lamp is fixed 4.8 m above the ground on a vertical post. A boy 1.6 m tall walks away from the foot of the post in a straight line at 1.5 m/s. How long is his shadow after 4 seconds?

Given: lamp-post 4.8 m, boy 1.6 m, speed 1.5 m/s, time 4 s. Asked: the shadow length x.

Concept used: AA (right angles and the common angle at the shadow tip); the big base is "distance walked + shadow". Why it applies: the lamp, the top of the boy's head and the shadow tip are on one straight ray.

  1. 1

    Distance walked: BD=1.5×4=6BD = 1.5 \times 4 = 6BD=1.5×4=6 m. Let AB be the post, CD the boy, E the shadow tip, DE=xDE = xDE=x.

  2. 2

    In △ABE and △CDE: ∠ABE=∠CDE=90°\angle ABE = \angle CDE = 90°∠ABE=∠CDE=90° and ∠E\angle E∠E is common, so △ABE ~ △CDE (AA).

  3. 3

    Ratio of matching sides.

    BEDE=ABCD\dfrac{BE}{DE} = \dfrac{AB}{CD}DEBE​=CDAB​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Substitute (BE=6+xBE = 6 + xBE=6+x).

    6+xx=4.81.6=3\dfrac{6 + x}{x} = \dfrac{4.8}{1.6} = 3x6+x​=1.64.8​=3Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Multiply both sides by x.

    6+x=3x6 + x = 3x6+x=3xSwipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Subtract x from both sides and divide by 2.

    x=3 mx = 3 \text{ m}x=3 mSwipe to see the whole equation →

  7. Check: big base 6+3=96 + 3 = 96+3=9 m and small base 3 m: ratio 3, the same as 4.81.6\dfrac{4.8}{1.6}1.64.8​ ✔.

  8. Answer The shadow is 3 m long.

    Common trap: Using 6x=4.81.6\dfrac{6}{x} = \dfrac{4.8}{1.6}x6​=1.64.8​ — the 6 m is not a side of the big triangle; its base is 6+x6 + x6+x. That slip gives x=2x = 2x=2 m.

Worked example 3

Perimeters of similar triangles

Question. △ABC ~ △DEF. The perimeter of △ABC is 45 cm and the perimeter of △DEF is 60 cm. If AB=12AB = 12AB=12 cm, find DE.

Given: perimeters 45 cm and 60 cm; AB=12AB = 12AB=12 cm. Asked: DE.

Concept used: the ratio of perimeters of similar triangles equals the ratio of corresponding sides. Why it applies: AB and DE are corresponding sides (first two letters).

  1. 1

    If DE=k⋅ABDE = k \cdot ABDE=k⋅AB, EF=k⋅BCEF = k \cdot BCEF=k⋅BC, FD=k⋅CAFD = k \cdot CAFD=k⋅CA, then adding gives perimeter(DEF) =k×= k \times=k× perimeter(ABC).

  2. 2

    So

    DEAB=6045=43\dfrac{DE}{AB} = \dfrac{60}{45} = \dfrac{4}{3}ABDE​=4560​=34​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Multiply.

    DE=43×12=16 cmDE = \dfrac{4}{3} \times 12 = 16 \text{ cm}DE=34​×12=16 cmSwipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: a possible pair: sides 12, 15, 18 (sum 45) and 16, 20, 24 (sum 60) — each ratio 43\dfrac{4}{3}34​ ✔.

  5. Answer DE=16DE = 16DE=16 cm

    Common trap: Squaring the ratio: DEAB=(6045)2\dfrac{DE}{AB} = \left(\dfrac{60}{45}\right)^2ABDE​=(4560​)2 gives DE=643DE = \dfrac{64}{3}DE=364​ cm. Perimeters are lengths, so the ratio is not squared.

Worked example 4

Altitudes and medians of similar triangles

Question. △ABC ~ △PQR. AL and PN are altitudes (L on BC, N on QR), and AD and PM are medians (D, M the mid-points of BC, QR). (a) Prove that ABPQ=ALPN\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{AL}{PN}PQAB​=PNAL​. (b) If AB=9AB = 9AB=9 cm, PQ=6PQ = 6PQ=6 cm, AL=6AL = 6AL=6 cm and AD=7.5AD = 7.5AD=7.5 cm, find PN and PM.

Given: △ABC ~ △PQR; AL⊥BCAL \perp BCAL⊥BC, PN⊥QRPN \perp QRPN⊥QR. To prove: ABPQ=ALPN\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{AL}{PN}PQAB​=PNAL​.

Concept used: AA in △ABL and △PQN, using an angle from the similarity we are given. Why it applies: each altitude makes a right triangle with the matching side.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. ∠B=∠Q\angle B = \angle Q∠B=∠Q △ABC ~ △PQR (corresponding angles are equal)
    2. In △ABL and △PQN: ∠ABL=∠PQN\angle ABL = \angle PQN∠ABL=∠PQN Step 1 (L is on BC, N is on QR)
    3. ∠ALB=∠PNQ\angle ALB = \angle PNQ∠ALB=∠PNQ Each is 90° (AL and PN are altitudes)
    4. △ABL ~ △PQN AA similarity criterion (A↔PA \leftrightarrow PA↔P, B↔QB \leftrightarrow QB↔Q, L↔NL \leftrightarrow NL↔N)
    5. ABPQ=ALPN\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{AL}{PN}PQAB​=PNAL​ Corresponding sides of similar triangles (first two letters; first and last letters). Hence proved.
  2. 6

    (b)

    Scale factor from △ABC to △PQR: PQAB=69=23\dfrac{PQ}{AB} = \dfrac{6}{9} = \dfrac{2}{3}ABPQ​=96​=32​.

  3. 7

    By part (a): PN=23×6=4PN = \dfrac{2}{3} \times 6 = 4PN=32​×6=4 cm.

  4. 8

    Matching medians are in the same ratio as the sides (NCERT Exercise 6.3, Q16 proves it with SAS — see the NCERT solutions): PM=23×7.5=5PM = \dfrac{2}{3} \times 7.5 = 5PM=32​×7.5=5 cm.

  5. Check: ALPN=64=1.5=96=ABPQ\dfrac{AL}{PN} = \dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5 = \dfrac{9}{6} = \dfrac{AB}{PQ}PNAL​=46​=1.5=69​=PQAB​ ✔ and 7.55=1.5\dfrac{7.5}{5} = 1.557.5​=1.5 ✔.

  6. Answer (a) proved (b) PN=4PN = 4PN=4 cm, PM=5PM = 5PM=5 cm

    Common trap: Taking an altitude from a vertex that does not match: the altitude from A in △ABC matches the altitude from P in △PQR, because A ↔ P.

Worked example 5

The perpendicular to the hypotenuse

Question. △ABC is right-angled at B, and BD is perpendicular to the hypotenuse AC. (a) Prove that △ADB ~ △ABC. (b) If AD=3.6AD = 3.6AD=3.6 cm and AC=10AC = 10AC=10 cm, find AB.

Given: ∠ABC=90°\angle ABC = 90°∠ABC=90°, BD⊥ACBD \perp ACBD⊥AC. To prove: △ADB ~ △ABC; then find AB.

Concept used: AA (a right angle and the common angle A), then proportional sides. Why it applies: both triangles contain ∠A\angle A∠A and a right angle.

  1. Statement Reason
    1. In △ADB and △ABC: ∠ADB=∠ABC\angle ADB = \angle ABC∠ADB=∠ABC Each is 90° (BD ⊥ AC; given right angle at B)
    2. ∠DAB=∠BAC\angle DAB = \angle BAC∠DAB=∠BAC Common angle A
    3. △ADB ~ △ABC AA similarity criterion (A↔AA \leftrightarrow AA↔A, D↔BD \leftrightarrow BD↔B, B↔CB \leftrightarrow CB↔C)
    4. ADAB=ABAC\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{AB}{AC}ABAD​=ACAB​ Corresponding sides (first two letters; first and last letters)
    5. AB2=AD×ACAB^2 = AD \times ACAB2=AD×AC Cross-multiply step 4
  2. 6

    (b)

    Substitute.

    AB2=3.6×10=36AB^2 = 3.6 \times 10 = 36AB2=3.6×10=36Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 7

    Take the positive square root (a length).

    AB=6 cmAB = 6 \text{ cm}AB=6 cmSwipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: in the same way △BDC ~ △ABC gives BC2=CD×CA=6.4×10=64BC^2 = CD \times CA = 6.4 \times 10 = 64BC2=CD×CA=6.4×10=64, so BC=8BC = 8BC=8 cm; and 62+82=100=1026^2 + 8^2 = 100 = 10^262+82=100=102, as a right triangle must ✔.

Worked example 6

Trapezium diagonals

Question. ABCD is a trapezium with AB∥DCAB \parallel DCAB∥DC, AB=12AB = 12AB=12 cm and DC=8DC = 8DC=8 cm. Its diagonals AC and BD meet at O. If AC=15AC = 15AC=15 cm and BD=10BD = 10BD=10 cm, find OA, OC, OB and OD.

Given: AB∥DCAB \parallel DCAB∥DC, AB=12AB = 12AB=12, DC=8DC = 8DC=8, AC=15AC = 15AC=15, BD=10BD = 10BD=10 (cm). Asked: the four pieces of the diagonals.

Concept used: AA (alternate angles), then proportional sides; then splitting a length in a ratio (Topic 1). Why it applies: the parallel sides make alternate angles at the diagonals.

  1. 1

    In △OAB and △OCD: ∠OAB=∠OCD\angle OAB = \angle OCD∠OAB=∠OCD and ∠OBA=∠ODC\angle OBA = \angle ODC∠OBA=∠ODC (alternate angles, AB∥DCAB \parallel DCAB∥DC).

  2. 2

    So △OAB ~ △OCD (AA), with O↔OO \leftrightarrow OO↔O, A↔CA \leftrightarrow CA↔C, B↔DB \leftrightarrow DB↔D.

  3. 3

    Ratio line.

    OAOC=OBOD=ABCD=128=32\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD} = \dfrac{AB}{CD} = \dfrac{12}{8} = \dfrac{3}{2}OCOA​=ODOB​=CDAB​=812​=23​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    AC is 3+2=53 + 2 = 53+2=5 parts; one part is 15÷5=315 \div 5 = 315÷5=3 cm, so OA=9OA = 9OA=9 cm and OC=6OC = 6OC=6 cm.

  5. 5

    BD is also 5 parts; one part is 10÷5=210 \div 5 = 210÷5=2 cm, so OB=6OB = 6OB=6 cm and OD=4OD = 4OD=4 cm.

  6. Check: 96=64=128=1.5\dfrac{9}{6} = \dfrac{6}{4} = \dfrac{12}{8} = 1.569​=46​=812​=1.5 ✔; 9+6=159 + 6 = 159+6=15 and 6+4=106 + 4 = 106+4=10 ✔.

  7. Answer OA=9OA = 9OA=9 cm, OC=6OC = 6OC=6 cm, OB=6OB = 6OB=6 cm, OD=4OD = 4OD=4 cm

    Common trap: Writing △OAB ~ △ODC and then OAOD=ABDC\dfrac{OA}{OD} = \dfrac{AB}{DC}ODOA​=DCAB​. The alternate angle at A matches the one at C, so OA pairs with OC.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers name ratio set-up — including pairing in shadow problems — among the four commonest reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • Pole height ÷ tower's shadow

    Each height must stay with its own shadow.

    height1shadow1=height2shadow2\dfrac{\text{height}_1}{\text{shadow}_1} = \dfrac{\text{height}_2}{\text{shadow}_2}shadow1​height1​​=shadow2​height2​​.

  • Lamp-post: using the distance walked as the big base

    The big triangle's base runs from the post to the shadow tip.

    Big base = distance + shadow.

  • Squaring the ratio for perimeters

    Perimeters are lengths; the squared ratio was for areas (not in the syllabus).

    Perimeter ratio = side ratio.

  • Medians or altitudes taken from non-matching vertices

    Only matching medians/altitudes are in the side ratio.

    Median from A in △ABC matches the median from the matching vertex.

  • Pairing OA with OD in a trapezium

    The alternate angles pair A with C.

    △OAB ~ △OCD, so OAOC=OBOD\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD}OCOA​=ODOB​.

Key points

  • Sun: parallel rays → equal angles at the shadow tips → AA. Each height goes with its own shadow.
  • Lamp: one triangle inside another; the big base is distance + shadow.
  • Perimeters, medians and altitudes of similar triangles are in the same ratio as the sides (not squared).
  • Right triangle with BD⊥BD \perpBD⊥ hypotenuse AC: △ADB ~ △ABC ~ △BDC, so AB2=AD×ACAB^2 = AD \times ACAB2=AD×AC.
  • Trapezium with AB∥DCAB \parallel DCAB∥DC: △OAB ~ △OCD, so OAOC=OBOD=ABCD\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OB}{OD} = \dfrac{AB}{CD}OCOA​=ODOB​=CDAB​.
