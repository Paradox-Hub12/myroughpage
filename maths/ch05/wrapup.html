Wrap-up

The formula card, "term or sum?" and mixed problems

About 35 minutes

Everything in this chapter comes from one idea: an AP adds the same number d at every step. This page puts the formulas and the "term or sum?" decision on one sheet, then gives five mixed problems in the styles the sample papers use — a polygon whose angles are in AP, multiples of two numbers, two APs that meet, two unknowns from two facts, and three numbers in AP.

n is always a positive whole number. When n comes from a quadratic, test both roots: reject a negative or fractional root; if both roots are positive whole numbers, check each one against the question (both can be valid).

The "term or sum?" card

First ask: what does one term of my list stand for? Then… Use
the question wants one member of the list ("in the 12th week", "the top row") ana_nan​
the question wants the total of the first n members ("altogether", "in all", "the first 12 weeks") SnS_nSn​
the question wants a position or a count ("in which week?", "how many rows?") solve for n — from ana_nan​ if a term is given, from SnS_nSn​ if a total is given

Careful: 'after' and 'by' can go either way — decide from what one term stands for.

Mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

A polygon whose exterior angles are in AP

Question. The exterior angles of a polygon, taken in increasing order, form an AP. The smallest exterior angle is 9° and each of the others is 6° more than the one before it. How many sides does the polygon have?

Given: exterior angles 9°,15°,21°,…9°, 15°, 21°, \ldots9°,15°,21°,…: a=9a = 9a=9, d=6d = 6d=6. Asked: the number of sides n.

Concept used: the exterior angles of any polygon add up to 360°, so Sn=360S_n = 360Sn​=360; a polygon with n sides has n exterior angles. Why it applies: the total of the angles is known, and n is unknown.

  1. 1

    Substitute into Sn=n2[2a+(n−1)d]S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d].

    360=n2[18+(n−1)×6]360 = \dfrac{n}{2}[18 + (n - 1) \times 6]360=2n​[18+(n−1)×6]Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Simplify the bracket: 18+6n−6=6n+1218 + 6n - 6 = 6n + 1218+6n−6=6n+12.

    360=n2(6n+12)360 = \dfrac{n}{2}(6n + 12)360=2n​(6n+12)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Divide the bracket by 2.

    360=n(3n+6)360 = n(3n + 6)360=n(3n+6)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Expand.

    360=3n2+6n360 = 3n^2 + 6n360=3n2+6nSwipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Divide every term by 3.

    120=n2+2n120 = n^2 + 2n120=n2+2nSwipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Subtract 120 from both sides.

    n2+2n−120=0n^2 + 2n - 120 = 0n2+2n−120=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Factorise (12×10=12012 \times 10 = 12012×10=120 and 12−10=212 - 10 = 212−10=2).

    (n+12)(n−10)=0(n + 12)(n - 10) = 0(n+12)(n−10)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    A polygon cannot have −12-12−12 sides, so n=10n = 10n=10.

  9. Check: the angles are 9°,15°,…,63°9°, 15°, \ldots, 63°9°,15°,…,63° (a10=9+9×6=63a_{10} = 9 + 9 \times 6 = 63a10​=9+9×6=63), and 102(9+63)=5×72=360\dfrac{10}{2}(9 + 63) = 5 \times 72 = 360210​(9+63)=5×72=360 ✔. Every angle is between 0° and 180°, as an exterior angle must be ✔.

  10. Answer The polygon has 10 sides.

    Common trap: Using 180° (the angle sum of a triangle) or the interior-angle sum. The exterior angles of every polygon add up to 360°.

Mixed example 2

Multiples of two numbers

Question. Find the sum of all the numbers between 100 and 400 that are divisible by both 6 and 9.

Given: the range 100 to 400; divisors 6 and 9. Asked: the sum.

Concept used: divisible by both ⇔ divisible by the LCM; the multiples form an AP. Why it applies: first and last terms can be found directly.

  1. 1

    6=2×36 = 2 \times 36=2×3 and 9=329 = 3^29=32, so LCM(6,9)=2×32=18\text{LCM}(6, 9) = 2 \times 3^2 = 18LCM(6,9)=2×32=18.

  2. 2

    First multiple of 18 above 100: 18×6=10818 \times 6 = 10818×6=108. Last below 400: 18×22=39618 \times 22 = 39618×22=396.

  3. 3

    Put l=396l = 396l=396 in l=a+(n−1)dl = a + (n - 1)dl=a+(n−1)d.

    396=108+(n−1)×18396 = 108 + (n - 1) \times 18396=108+(n−1)×18Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract 108 from both sides.

    288=(n−1)×18288 = (n - 1) \times 18288=(n−1)×18Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Divide by 18.

    16=n−116 = n - 116=n−1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Add 1.

    n=17n = 17n=17Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Sum.

    S17=172(108+396)S_{17} = \dfrac{17}{2}(108 + 396)S17​=217​(108+396)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    Add inside the bracket.

    S17=172×504S_{17} = \dfrac{17}{2} \times 504S17​=217​×504Swipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    Halve 504, then multiply by 17.

    S17=17×252=4284S_{17} = 17 \times 252 = 4284S17​=17×252=4284Swipe to see the whole equation →

  10. Check: the multiples are 18×618 \times 618×6 to 18×2218 \times 2218×22, i.e. 17 of them; their sum is 18×(6+7+⋯+22)=18×238=428418 \times (6 + 7 + \cdots + 22) = 18 \times 238 = 428418×(6+7+⋯+22)=18×238=4284 ✔.

  11. Answer 4284

    Common trap: Using 6×9=546 \times 9 = 546×9=54 as the step. That misses numbers such as 126 and 144, which are divisible by both 6 and 9.

Mixed example 3

When do two APs meet?

Question. Two APs are 5,12,19,…5, 12, 19, \ldots5,12,19,… and 137,133,129,…137, 133, 129, \ldots137,133,129,… For which value of n are their nth terms equal? What is that term?

Given: first AP a=5a = 5a=5, d=7d = 7d=7; second AP a=137a = 137a=137, d=−4d = -4d=−4. Asked: n with equal nth terms, and the term.

Concept used: write both nth terms with an=a+(n−1)da_n = a + (n - 1)dan​=a+(n−1)d and set them equal. Why it applies: "the nth terms are equal" is one equation in n.

  1. 1

    Set the two nth terms equal.

    5+(n−1)×7=137+(n−1)(−4)5 + (n - 1) \times 7 = 137 + (n - 1)(-4)5+(n−1)×7=137+(n−1)(−4)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Add 4(n−1)4(n - 1)4(n−1) to both sides.

    5+11(n−1)=1375 + 11(n - 1) = 1375+11(n−1)=137Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Subtract 5 from both sides.

    11(n−1)=13211(n - 1) = 13211(n−1)=132Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Divide by 11.

    n−1=12n - 1 = 12n−1=12Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Add 1.

    n=13n = 13n=13Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    The common term: 5+12×7=895 + 12 \times 7 = 895+12×7=89.

  7. Check: the second AP's 13th term is 137+12×(−4)=137−48=89137 + 12 \times (-4) = 137 - 48 = 89137+12×(−4)=137−48=89 ✔.

  8. Answer The 13th terms are equal; both are 89.

    Common trap: Writing both nth terms with nnn in place of n−1n - 1n−1. That gives 5+7n=137−4n5 + 7n = 137 - 4n5+7n=137−4n, so n=12n = 12n=12 — one position too early.

Mixed example 4

Two facts, two unknowns

Question. In an AP, the 4th term and the 9th term add up to 56, and the 6th term is 26. Find the sum of the first 20 terms.

Given: a4+a9=56a_4 + a_9 = 56a4​+a9​=56, a6=26a_6 = 26a6​=26. Asked: S20S_{20}S20​.

Concept used: write each fact with an=a+(n−1)da_n = a + (n - 1)dan​=a+(n−1)d; solve the pair of linear equations (Chapter 3); then SnS_nSn​. Why it applies: two facts give two equations in a and d.

  1. 1

    a4+a9=(a+3d)+(a+8d)a_4 + a_9 = (a + 3d) + (a + 8d)a4​+a9​=(a+3d)+(a+8d).

    2a+11d=56(1)2a + 11d = 56 \qquad (1)2a+11d=56(1)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    a6=a+5da_6 = a + 5da6​=a+5d.

    a+5d=26(2)a + 5d = 26 \qquad (2)a+5d=26(2)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Multiply (2) by 2.

    2a+10d=52(3)2a + 10d = 52 \qquad (3)2a+10d=52(3)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract (3) from (1).

    d=4d = 4d=4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Put d=4d = 4d=4 into (2).

    a+20=26a + 20 = 26a+20=26Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Subtract 20 from both sides.

    a=6a = 6a=6Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Sum of the first 20 terms.

    S20=202[12+19×4]=10×88=880S_{20} = \dfrac{20}{2}[12 + 19 \times 4] = 10 \times 88 = 880S20​=220​[12+19×4]=10×88=880Swipe to see the whole equation →

  8. Check: a4=6+12=18a_4 = 6 + 12 = 18a4​=6+12=18, a9=6+32=38a_9 = 6 + 32 = 38a9​=6+32=38, and 18+38=5618 + 38 = 5618+38=56 ✔; a6=6+20=26a_6 = 6 + 20 = 26a6​=6+20=26 ✔.

  9. Answer a=6a = 6a=6, d=4d = 4d=4; S20=880S_{20} = 880S20​=880

    Common trap: Writing a4+a9=a13a_4 + a_9 = a_{13}a4​+a9​=a13​. Terms do not add by their positions: a4+a9=2a+11da_4 + a_9 = 2a + 11da4​+a9​=2a+11d, while a13=a+12da_{13} = a + 12da13​=a+12d.

Mixed example 5

Three numbers in AP

Question. Three numbers are in AP. Their sum is 27 and their product is 693. Find the numbers.

Given: sum 27, product 693. Asked: the three numbers.

Concept used: write three numbers in AP as a−da - da−d, aaa, a+da + da+d; then the d's cancel in the sum. Why it applies: this choice makes the sum give a at once.

  1. 1

    Let the numbers be a−da - da−d, aaa, a+da + da+d. Their sum is 3a3a3a.

    3a=273a = 273a=27Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Divide by 3.

    a=9a = 9a=9Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Write the product.

    (9−d)×9×(9+d)=693(9 - d) \times 9 \times (9 + d) = 693(9−d)×9×(9+d)=693Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Divide both sides by 9.

    (9−d)(9+d)=77(9 - d)(9 + d) = 77(9−d)(9+d)=77Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Use (9−d)(9+d)=81−d2(9 - d)(9 + d) = 81 - d^2(9−d)(9+d)=81−d2.

    81−d2=7781 - d^2 = 7781−d2=77Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Add d2d^2d2 and subtract 77 on both sides.

    d2=4d^2 = 4d2=4Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    So d=2d = 2d=2 or d=−2d = -2d=−2.

  8. 8

    d=2d = 2d=2 gives 7,9,117, 9, 117,9,11; d=−2d = -2d=−2 gives 11,9,711, 9, 711,9,7 — the same three numbers in the other order.

  9. Check: 7+9+11=277 + 9 + 11 = 277+9+11=27 ✔ and 7×9×11=6937 \times 9 \times 11 = 6937×9×11=693 ✔.

  10. Answer The numbers are 7, 9 and 11.

    Common trap: Calling the numbers aaa, a+da + da+d, a+2da + 2da+2d. It works, but the equations are much harder; with a−da - da−d, aaa, a+da + da+d the d's cancel in the sum.
