Topic 5 of 5

AP word problems

About 60 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Model daily-life situations (savings, instalments, rows and stacks, prizes, production, races) as APs.
  • Decide whether a question asks for a term or a sum, and answer the question asked, with units and a sense check.

Kabir saves ₹ 40 in week 1 of the year, and every week he saves ₹ 15 more than the week before. Two friends ask him two questions: "How much will you save in the 17th week?" and "How much will you have saved by the end of the 17th week?" The numbers in the two questions are the same, but the answers are very different — ₹ 280 and ₹ 2720. Knowing which one is being asked is half of every AP word problem.

A routine for every word problem

  1. Find the list. Decide what one term stands for (the saving in one week, the logs in one row, one prize) and write the first two or three terms.
  2. Check it is an AP: the same amount is added (or taken away) each time. Write a, d (with its sign) and units.
  3. Term or sum? Use the card below.
  4. Write the formula, substitute, solve. If n comes from a quadratic, test both roots — and test the story.
  5. Answer in words, with units, and do a quick sense check.

Term or sum?

Ask: does the question want one member of my list, or the total of several members?

The question wants… It usually sounds like Use
one term — the value at one position "in the 12th week", "the 20th instalment", "the number of logs in the top row", "the prize for 3rd place" an=a+(n−1)da_n = a + (n - 1)dan​=a+(n−1)d (or the last term l)
a total of the first n terms "altogether", "in all", "the total", "how much in the first 12 weeks", "the total distance run" SnS_nSn​
a position or a count "in which week…?", "how many rows…?", "how many instalments…?" Solve for n — from ana_nan​ if a term is given, from SnS_nSn​ if a total is given

Words such as "after" and "by" can point either way: the "amount after 12 weeks" is a term if each term already is the amount so far, but a sum if each term is one week's deposit. So always decide from step 1 — what one term stands for — not from a single word.

Sense checks

  • A count (rows, weeks, instalments) is a positive whole number.
  • Physical amounts (logs in a row, seats) cannot be negative.
  • If every term is positive and there are two or more terms, the total is bigger than any single term. (When some terms are negative, the total can even be smaller than a term: −8+(−6)+⋯+4=−14-8 + (-6) + \cdots + 4 = -14−8+(−6)+⋯+4=−14.)

Worked example 1

Savings: a term and a total

Question. Kabir saves ₹ 40 in week 1 of the year. Every week after that, he saves ₹ 15 more than in the week before. (a) In which week will his weekly saving be ₹ 280? (b) How much will he have saved altogether in the weeks up to and including that week?

Given: weekly savings (in ₹) 40,55,70,…40, 55, 70, \ldots40,55,70,…: a=40a = 40a=40, d=15d = 15d=15. Asked: (a) n with an=280a_n = 280an​=280 (b) SnS_nSn​ for that n.

Concept used: one term = one week's saving. (a) asks for a position from a term value → ana_nan​. (b) asks for a total → SnS_nSn​. Why it applies: each week adds the same ₹ 15.

  1. 1

    (a)

    Put an=280a_n = 280an​=280.

    280=40+(n−1)×15280 = 40 + (n - 1) \times 15280=40+(n−1)×15Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Subtract 40 from both sides.

    240=(n−1)×15240 = (n - 1) \times 15240=(n−1)×15Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Divide by 15.

    16=n−116 = n - 116=n−1Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Add 1.

    n=17n = 17n=17Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    (b)

    The first and last terms are known, so use Sn=n2(a+l)S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}(a + l)Sn​=2n​(a+l).

    S17=172(40+280)S_{17} = \dfrac{17}{2}(40 + 280)S17​=217​(40+280)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Work it out.

    S17=172×320=17×160=2720S_{17} = \dfrac{17}{2} \times 320 = 17 \times 160 = 2720S17​=217​×320=17×160=2720Swipe to see the whole equation →

  7. Check: (a) 40+16×15=28040 + 16 \times 15 = 28040+16×15=280 ✔. (b) the other formula: 172[80+16×15]=172×320=2720\dfrac{17}{2}[80 + 16 \times 15] = \dfrac{17}{2} \times 320 = 2720217​[80+16×15]=217​×320=2720 ✔. Sense: ₹ 2720 is far more than any single week's saving ✔.

  8. Answer (a) In the 17th week. (b) ₹ 2720 altogether.

    Common trap: Answering (b) with ₹ 280. That is the saving in week 17 alone, a single term.

Worked example 2

Instalments on a loan

Question. Sunita repays a loan of ₹ 19,500 in monthly instalments. The first instalment is ₹ 500, and each instalment is ₹ 50 more than the one before. How many instalments does she pay, and what is the last instalment?

Given: instalments (in ₹) 500,550,600,…500, 550, 600, \ldots500,550,600,…: a=500a = 500a=500, d=50d = 50d=50; total Sn=19500S_n = 19500Sn​=19500. Asked: n and ana_nan​.

Concept used: one term = one instalment; the loan is the total → SnS_nSn​ gives n; then ana_nan​. Why it applies: the instalments grow by a fixed ₹ 50.

  1. 1

    Substitute into Sn=n2[2a+(n−1)d]S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d].

    19500=n2[1000+(n−1)×50]19500 = \dfrac{n}{2}[1000 + (n - 1) \times 50]19500=2n​[1000+(n−1)×50]Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Simplify the bracket: 1000+50n−50=50n+9501000 + 50n - 50 = 50n + 9501000+50n−50=50n+950.

    19500=n2(50n+950)19500 = \dfrac{n}{2}(50n + 950)19500=2n​(50n+950)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Divide the bracket by 2.

    19500=n(25n+475)19500 = n(25n + 475)19500=n(25n+475)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Expand.

    19500=25n2+475n19500 = 25n^2 + 475n19500=25n2+475nSwipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Divide every term by 25.

    780=n2+19n780 = n^2 + 19n780=n2+19nSwipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Subtract 780 from both sides.

    n2+19n−780=0n^2 + 19n - 780 = 0n2+19n−780=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Factorise (39×20=78039 \times 20 = 78039×20=780 and 39−20=1939 - 20 = 1939−20=19).

    (n+39)(n−20)=0(n + 39)(n - 20) = 0(n+39)(n−20)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  8. 8

    n=−39n = -39n=−39 is impossible for a number of instalments, so n=20n = 20n=20.

  9. 9

    Last instalment.

    a20=500+19×50=1450a_{20} = 500 + 19 \times 50 = 1450a20​=500+19×50=1450Swipe to see the whole equation →

  10. Check: 202(500+1450)=10×1950=19500\dfrac{20}{2}(500 + 1450) = 10 \times 1950 = 19500220​(500+1450)=10×1950=19500 ✔.

  11. Answer 20 instalments; the last one is ₹ 1450.

    Common trap: Taking the last instalment as 500+20×50=1500500 + 20 \times 50 = 1500500+20×50=1500, i.e. ₹ 1500 — that would be a 21st instalment.

Worked example 3

A stack with two possible answers

Question. A shopkeeper stacks 150 cans in rows: 18 cans in the bottom row, 17 in the row above it, 16 in the next, and so on. How many rows are there, and how many cans are in the top row?

Given: cans per row (from the bottom) 18,17,16,…18, 17, 16, \ldots18,17,16,…: a=18a = 18a=18, d=−1d = -1d=−1; total Sn=150S_n = 150Sn​=150. Asked: n and the top row ana_nan​.

Concept used: one term = the cans in one row; the total is given → SnS_nSn​ gives n. Why it applies: each row has one can fewer.

  1. 1

    Substitute into Sn=n2[2a+(n−1)d]S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d].

    150=n2[36+(n−1)(−1)]150 = \dfrac{n}{2}[36 + (n - 1)(-1)]150=2n​[36+(n−1)(−1)]Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Simplify the bracket: 36−n+1=37−n36 - n + 1 = 37 - n36−n+1=37−n.

    150=n2(37−n)150 = \dfrac{n}{2}(37 - n)150=2n​(37−n)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Multiply both sides by 2.

    300=n(37−n)300 = n(37 - n)300=n(37−n)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Expand.

    300=37n−n2300 = 37n - n^2300=37n−n2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Bring every term to the left.

    n2−37n+300=0n^2 - 37n + 300 = 0n2−37n+300=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Factorise (12×25=30012 \times 25 = 30012×25=300 and 12+25=3712 + 25 = 3712+25=37).

    (n−12)(n−25)=0(n - 12)(n - 25) = 0(n−12)(n−25)=0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Both roots are positive whole numbers, so test each in the story.

  8. 8

    n=25n = 25n=25: the top row would have a25=18+24×(−1)=−6a_{25} = 18 + 24 \times (-1) = -6a25​=18+24×(−1)=−6 cans — impossible. Reject.

  9. 9

    n=12n = 12n=12: the top row has a12=18+11×(−1)=7a_{12} = 18 + 11 \times (-1) = 7a12​=18+11×(−1)=7 cans ✔.

  10. Check: 122(18+7)=6×25=150\dfrac{12}{2}(18 + 7) = 6 \times 25 = 150212​(18+7)=6×25=150 ✔.

  11. Answer 12 rows, with 7 cans in the top row.

    Common trap: Accepting n=25n = 25n=25 because it also solves the equation. Algebra gives both; the story rejects 25, because rows 19 to 25 would need 0 or a negative number of cans.

Worked example 4

Prizes that go down in equal steps

Question. A sum of ₹ 3,000 is to be given as 8 cash prizes in a quiz. Each prize is ₹ 60 less than the prize just above it. Find the value of each prize.

Given: 8 prizes, d=−60d = -60d=−60, S8=3000S_8 = 3000S8​=3000. Asked: every prize.

Concept used: Sn=n2[2a+(n−1)d]S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d] with a unknown. Why it applies: one term = one prize; the total is the ₹ 3,000.

  1. 1

    Substitute.

    3000=82[2a+7×(−60)]3000 = \dfrac{8}{2}[2a + 7 \times (-60)]3000=28​[2a+7×(−60)]Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Simplify: 82=4\dfrac{8}{2} = 428​=4 and 7×(−60)=−4207 \times (-60) = -4207×(−60)=−420.

    3000=4(2a−420)3000 = 4(2a - 420)3000=4(2a−420)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Divide both sides by 4.

    750=2a−420750 = 2a - 420750=2a−420Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Add 420.

    1170=2a1170 = 2a1170=2aSwipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Divide by 2.

    a=585a = 585a=585Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Subtract ₹ 60 each time: 585, 525, 465, 405, 345, 285, 225, 165.

  7. Check: 82(585+165)=4×750=3000\dfrac{8}{2}(585 + 165) = 4 \times 750 = 300028​(585+165)=4×750=3000 ✔. Every prize is positive ✔.

  8. Answer The prizes are ₹ 585, ₹ 525, ₹ 465, ₹ 405, ₹ 345, ₹ 285, ₹ 225 and ₹ 165.

    Common trap: Taking d=+60d = +60d=+60 ("₹ 60 apart"). Then 4(2a+420)=30004(2a + 420) = 30004(2a+420)=3000 gives a=165a = 165a=165, the smallest prize — the list comes out upside down.

Worked example 5

Production that grows uniformly

Question. A factory made 1,240 bicycles in its 4th year and 1,600 in its 10th year. Each year it makes the same number of bicycles more than the year before. (i) How many bicycles did it make in its first year? (ii) How many will it make in its 15th year? (iii) How many did it make altogether in its first 10 years?

Given: a4=1240a_4 = 1240a4​=1240, a10=1600a_{10} = 1600a10​=1600. Asked: a1a_1a1​, a15a_{15}a15​ and S10S_{10}S10​.

Concept used: two terms give d and a; then a term and a sum. Why it applies: "rises by the same number every year" makes the yearly figures an AP.

  1. 1

    From the 4th to the 10th year there are 6 steps.

    6d=1600−1240=3606d = 1600 - 1240 = 3606d=1600−1240=360Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Divide by 6.

    d=60d = 60d=60Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Use a4=a+3da_4 = a + 3da4​=a+3d.

    1240=a+1801240 = a + 1801240=a+180Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Subtract 180.

    a=1060a = 1060a=1060Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    (ii) a15=1060+14×60=1060+840=1900a_{15} = 1060 + 14 \times 60 = 1060 + 840 = 1900a15​=1060+14×60=1060+840=1900.

  6. 6

    (iii) Use Sn=n2(a+l)S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}(a + l)Sn​=2n​(a+l) with l=a10=1600l = a_{10} = 1600l=a10​=1600.

    S10=102(1060+1600)=5×2660=13300S_{10} = \dfrac{10}{2}(1060 + 1600) = 5 \times 2660 = 13300S10​=210​(1060+1600)=5×2660=13300Swipe to see the whole equation →

  7. Check: a10=1060+9×60=1600a_{10} = 1060 + 9 \times 60 = 1600a10​=1060+9×60=1600 ✔; S10=5[2120+9×60]=5×2660=13300S_{10} = 5[2120 + 9 \times 60] = 5 \times 2660 = 13300S10​=5[2120+9×60]=5×2660=13300 ✔.

  8. Answer (i) 1,060 bicycles (ii) 1,900 bicycles (iii) 13,300 bicycles

    Common trap: For (iii), answering with the 10th year's production (1,600) — a single term, not the total of ten years.

Worked example 6

A fetch-and-return race

Question. In a flag race, a bucket is placed at the start line. The nearest flag is 4 m from the bucket; the other flags stand in one straight line, each 2.5 m beyond the last. There are 8 flags in all. A runner starts at the bucket, runs to the nearest flag, brings it back to the bucket, then runs for the next flag, and so on until all 8 flags are in the bucket. Find the total distance she runs.

Given: flags at 4 m, 6.5 m, 9 m, … from the bucket; 8 flags. Asked: total distance.

Concept used: one term = the distance for one flag (there and back). The question asks for the total → S8S_8S8​. Why it applies: each flag is 2.5 m further, so each round trip is 2×2.5=52 \times 2.5 = 52×2.5=5 m longer.

  1. 1

    Round trips (in m): 2×4=82 \times 4 = 82×4=8, 2×6.5=132 \times 6.5 = 132×6.5=13, 2×9=182 \times 9 = 182×9=18, … So a=8a = 8a=8, d=5d = 5d=5, n=8n = 8n=8.

  2. 2

    Substitute into Sn=n2[2a+(n−1)d]S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d].

    S8=82[16+7×5]S_8 = \dfrac{8}{2}[16 + 7 \times 5]S8​=28​[16+7×5]Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Work it out.

    S8=4×51=204S_8 = 4 \times 51 = 204S8​=4×51=204Swipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: the last flag is 4+7×2.5=21.54 + 7 \times 2.5 = 21.54+7×2.5=21.5 m away, so the last trip is 43 m, and 82(8+43)=4×51=204\dfrac{8}{2}(8 + 43) = 4 \times 51 = 20428​(8+43)=4×51=204 ✔.

  5. Answer She runs 204 m in all.

    Common trap: Counting each flag one way only: 82(4+21.5)=102\dfrac{8}{2}(4 + 21.5) = 10228​(4+21.5)=102 m. The runner goes there and back for every flag.

Quick check · 1 of 3MCQ1 mark

Rina reads 12 pages of a novel on the first day and 3 more pages each day than the day before. How many pages does she read on the 10th day?

This is the total for the first 10 days, S10S_{10}S10​.
This is a+nd=12+10×3a + nd = 12 + 10 \times 3a+nd=12+10×3 — the 11th day.
This is n×dn \times dn×d only; the 12 pages of day 1 are left out.
One term: a10=12+9×3=39a_{10} = 12 + 9 \times 3 = 39a10​=12+9×3=39.
Quick check · 2 of 3Very short answer1 mark

In QC1, how many pages does Rina read altogether in the first 10 days?

Answer: 255 pages

Working: S10=102(12+39)=5×51=255S_{10} = \dfrac{10}{2}(12 + 39) = 5 \times 51 = 255S10​=210​(12+39)=5×51=255.

"Altogether in the first 10 days" asks for a total, so add the ten terms with SnS_nSn​.

Quick check · 3 of 3MCQ1 mark

An auditorium has 15 seats in the front row, and each row has 2 more seats than the row in front of it. Which expression gives the total number of seats in the first 20 rows?

S20=202[2×15+19×2]=10×68=680S_{20} = \dfrac{20}{2}[2 \times 15 + 19 \times 2] = 10 \times 68 = 680S20​=220​[2×15+19×2]=10×68=680.
This is the number of seats in the 20th row — one term.
This multiplies the last row by 20, as if every row were as long as the last.
Inside the bracket it must be (n−1)d=19×2(n - 1)d = 19 \times 2(n−1)d=19×2, not 20×220 \times 220×2.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers see mixing up a term and a sum as one of the four commonest reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • "How many in the 15th row?" answered with S15S_{15}S15​

    The question wants one row: one term.

    Use a15a_{15}a15​.

  • "How much altogether in 15 weeks?" answered with a15a_{15}a15​

    The question wants a total.

    Use S15S_{15}S15​.

  • Deciding from one word such as "after" or "by"

    These words fit both kinds of question.

    First write what one term stands for.

  • Keeping a root that makes a row negative

    The equation allows it; the story does not.

    Find the term at that n and reject it if it is impossible.

  • Prizes "₹ 60 apart" with d=+60d = +60d=+60 when they go down

    The sign of d sets the direction of the list.

    Going down: d=−60d = -60d=−60.

  • Answer without units ("2720")

    The question is about rupees, rows, metres…

    Write "₹ 2720", "12 rows", "204 m".

Key points

  • First decide what one term stands for; then a, d (with sign) and units.
  • One member of the list → ana_nan​. A total → SnS_nSn​. "Which / how many" → solve for n, from ana_nan​ or from SnS_nSn​ depending on what is given.
  • Test every root of a quadratic in n against the story, not just the algebra.
  • Two given terms (e.g. production in two years) → find d from the gap, then a.
  • Answer in words, with units, and check that the answer is sensible.
