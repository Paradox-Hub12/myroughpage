Arithmetic progression (AP) — a list in which every term after the first is the term before it plus the same number d, the common difference. d=ak+1−akd = a_{k+1} - a_kd=ak+1​−ak​ (a term minus the one before it); d can be positive, negative or 0. General form: a,a+d,a+2d,…a, a + d, a + 2d, \ldotsa,a+d,a+2d,… A finite AP has a last term l.

Is it an AP? Check every difference you are given (simplify fractions, surds and algebra first). A constant list is an AP with d=0d = 0d=0. Three consecutive terms a, b, c are in AP exactly when 2b=a+c2b = a + c2b=a+c.

nth term — an=a+(n−1)da_n = a + (n - 1)dan​=a+(n−1)d: from the 1st term to the nth there are n−1n - 1n−1 steps of d. Know three of a, d, n, ana_nan​ → find the fourth. A number is a term only if n comes out a positive whole number. Two terms: aq−ap=(q−p)da_q - a_p = (q - p)daq​−ap​=(q−p)d.

From the end and the middle — kth term from the end =l−(k−1)d= l - (k - 1)d=l−(k−1)d (the (n−k+1)(n - k + 1)(n−k+1)th from the start). Middle term: n odd → the (n+12)\left(\dfrac{n + 1}{2}\right)(2n+1​)th; n even → the (n2)\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)(2n​)th and (n2+1)\left(\dfrac{n}{2} + 1\right)(2n​+1)th.

Sum of the first n terms — derived by writing the sum forwards and backwards and adding: Sn=n2[2a+(n−1)d]=n2(a+l)S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d] = \dfrac{n}{2}(a + l)Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d]=2n​(a+l), where l is the last of these n terms. 1+2+⋯+n=n(n+1)21 + 2 + \cdots + n = \dfrac{n(n + 1)}{2}1+2+⋯+n=2n(n+1)​; 1+3+⋯+(2n−1)=n21 + 3 + \cdots + (2n - 1) = n^21+3+⋯+(2n−1)=n2.

n from a sum — a quadratic in n. Reject a negative or fractional root, with a reason. If both roots are positive whole numbers (the terms change sign), both can be valid — unless the story rules one out.

Going backwards — an=Sn−Sn−1a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}an​=Sn​−Sn−1​ for n≥2n \geq 2n≥2, and a1=S1a_1 = S_1a1​=S1​. Sum of the last k terms =k2(an−k+1+l)=Sn−Sn−k= \dfrac{k}{2}(a_{n-k+1} + l) = S_n - S_{n-k}=2k​(an−k+1​+l)=Sn​−Sn−k​.

Multiples — multiples of m in a range form an AP with d=md = md=m; "multiples of both p and q" = multiples of LCM(p, q).

Word problems — decide what one term stands for; one member → ana_nan​; a total → SnS_nSn​; "which / how many" → solve for n. Units and a sense check.

The question wants… Use

the total of the first n members ("altogether", "in all", "the first 12 weeks") SnS_nSn​
a position or a count ("in which week?", "how many rows?") solve for n — from ana_nan​ if a term is given, from SnS_nSn​ if a total is given

Careful: 'after' and 'by' can go either way — decide from what one term stands for.
