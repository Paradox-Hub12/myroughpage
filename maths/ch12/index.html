Chapter 12 · Unit VI

Surface Areas and Volumes

5 topicsAbout 8 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

A medicine capsule, a tent, a toy top, a water tanker, an ice-cream cone — almost nothing around you is a single cylinder, cone or sphere. Each is two basic solids put together, or a solid with a piece cut out. This chapter shows you how to find the paint, cloth or metal sheet such an object needs (its surface area) and how much it holds or weighs (its volume). The one new idea is simple to say and easy to forget: when two solids are joined, the faces that touch are hidden — they leave the surface, but no space is lost.

In the board exam this chapter is part of Unit VI, Mensuration (10 marks together with Chapter 11, Areas Related to Circles), with the same content in Mathematics Standard and Mathematics Basic. In the four sample papers for 2025-26 and 2026-27 it carries 5 to 6 marks: a 1-mark MCQ (two cubes joined; the height of a cone) and a 5-mark question (a tent with its canvas and cost; a toy with its wood and paint; a hemisphere drilled out of a cube; a cylindrical cavity in a cone; an ice-cream cone; a toy inside a cylinder). The syllabus covers combinations of any two of cubes, cuboids, spheres, hemispheres, cylinders and cones.

Not in the syllabus: converting a solid from one shape to another (melting and recasting, pouring a liquid from one vessel into another) and the frustum of a cone (a bucket shape). Neither is in the 2026-27 book, and neither is taught here.

What you'll learn

  • Find volumes and surface areas of cuboids, cylinders, cones, spheres and hemispheres; find a cone's slant height; change cm³ into litres. Take the 5-question skip-check first — score 5 out of 5 and go straight to Topic 2.
  • Name the hidden faces, read each part's height from the total, and find the surface area of capsules, toys, tents and blocks, and its cost. Includes an interactive solid builder.
  • Take away the opening and add the inside of a hollow; find the inner surface of vessels and the largest solid that can be cut from a cube.
  • Add the volumes of joined solids, subtract cavities and raised bottoms, and change volumes into litres and kilograms.
  • Solve problems with a solid inside a container or in water, work backwards to a height, and solve case studies with cost and wastage.
  • Use the formula card, the hidden-faces card and the add-or-take-away card, and solve mixed problems in sample-paper styles.
