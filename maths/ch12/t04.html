Topic 4 of 5

Volume of combined solids

About 50 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find the volume of two solids joined together (volumes add; nothing is hidden).
  • Find the volume left when a part is removed (a cavity, a depression, a raised bottom), including apparent and actual capacity.
  • Convert volumes into capacity in litres and into mass, and find the air in a building, shed or tent.

Pour water into a mould shaped like a capsule. Every drop goes either into the cylinder part or into one of the round ends — none of it is lost where the parts meet. That is why volume is easier than surface area: when solids are joined, their volumes simply add. When a part is cut away, its volume is taken away. The only skill is to decide which.

  1. Joined: volumes add

When two solids are joined, the faces that touch are hidden from the surface, but no space is lost:

Volume of the new solid = volume of part 1 + volume of part 2.

  1. Cut out: volumes subtract

A hollow, a depression or a hole removes space:

Volume left = volume of the solid − volume of the part removed.

Raised bottom. Some glasses have a solid bump (often a hemisphere) inside the base. The glass looks as if it holds a full cylinder — the apparent capacity — but it really holds less:

Actual capacity = apparent capacity − volume of the bump.

  1. Air inside a building or a tent

The air inside fills the whole hollow shape, so treat it as a solid: a shed is a cuboid plus a half-cylinder; a hall with a dome is a cylinder plus a hemisphere; a tent is a cylinder plus a cone. If machines or people are inside, subtract the space they take.

  1. Litres and mass

  • cm³ → litres: ÷ 1000. m³ → litres: × 1000.
  • Mass === volume × mass of 1 cm³ (in g); then ÷ 1000 for kg.
  • Keep every length in the same unit before you multiply.

  1. Which one — add or take away?

Words in the question Do
surmounted by, mounted on, stuck to, joined, fixed on, with … at the ends add
hollowed out, scooped out, depression, cavity, hole, drilled, raised bottom subtract

Worked example 1

The volume of a toy

Question. A solid toy is a cone of height 9 cm on a hemisphere of radius 6 cm; the cone has the same radius. Find the volume of the toy. (Use π = 3.14.)

Given: r=6r = 6r=6 cm; cone height 9 cm. Asked: the volume.

Concept used: volume = volume of cone + volume of hemisphere. Why it applies: joined solids keep all their space.

  1. Hidden faces: the base of the cone and the flat face of the hemisphere are joined — this hides them from the surface, but both volumes are still counted in full.

  2. 1

    Cone: 13×π×6×6×9=108π\dfrac{1}{3} \times \pi \times 6 \times 6 \times 9 = 108\pi31​×π×6×6×9=108π cm³.

  3. 2

    Hemisphere: 23×π×6×6×6=144π\dfrac{2}{3} \times \pi \times 6 \times 6 \times 6 = 144\pi32​×π×6×6×6=144π cm³.

  4. 3

    Toy: 108π+144π=252π=252×3.14=791.28108\pi + 144\pi = 252\pi = 252 \times 3.14 = 791.28108π+144π=252π=252×3.14=791.28 cm³.

  5. Check: with the short form 13πr2(h+2r)=13×π×36×(9+12)=12π×21=252π\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2(h + 2r) = \dfrac{1}{3} \times \pi \times 36 \times (9 + 12) = 12\pi \times 21 = 252\pi31​πr2(h+2r)=31​×π×36×(9+12)=12π×21=252π ✔.

Worked example 2

How much medicine fits in a capsule

Question. A capsule is a cylinder with a hemisphere at each end. Its total length is 16 mm and its radius is 3 mm. Find the volume of medicine it can hold (take the inner and outer sizes as the same). (Use π = 3.14.)

Given: total length 16 mm; r=3r = 3r=3 mm. Asked: the volume.

Concept used: volume = cylinder + two hemispheres (one sphere); cylinder length =L−2r= L - 2r=L−2r. Why it applies: the parts are joined end to end.

  1. Hidden faces: the two ends of the cylinder and the flat faces of the hemispheres are joined in pairs; the volumes add.

  2. 1

    Cylinder length =16−6=10= 16 - 6 = 10=16−6=10 mm.

  3. 2

    Cylinder: π×3×3×10=90π\pi \times 3 \times 3 \times 10 = 90\piπ×3×3×10=90π mm³.

  4. 3

    Two hemispheres =43×π×27=36π= \dfrac{4}{3} \times \pi \times 27 = 36\pi=34​×π×27=36π mm³.

  5. 4

    Capsule: 90π+36π=126π=126×3.14=395.6490\pi + 36\pi = 126\pi = 126 \times 3.14 = 395.6490π+36π=126π=126×3.14=395.64 mm³.

  6. Check: another route — a cylinder of the full length 16 mm (144π144\pi144π) has two "corners" of space outside the capsule, together a cylinder of length 6 mm minus a sphere: 54π−36π=18π54\pi - 36\pi = 18\pi54π−36π=18π; 144π−18π=126π144\pi - 18\pi = 126\pi144π−18π=126π ✔.

  7. Answer 395.64 mm³

    Common trap: Taking the cylinder as 16 mm long and adding the sphere: 144π+36π=180π144\pi + 36\pi = 180\pi144π+36π=180π — the ends are counted twice.

Worked example 3

The air in a shed

Question. A storage shed has a floor 14 m wide and 20 m long. Its walls are 6 m high, and its roof is a half-cylinder whose diameter is the 14 m width, running along the 20 m length. Find the volume of air in the empty shed, in m³ and in litres. A machine taking up 250 m³ is then put inside; how much air is left? (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: cuboid 20 m × 14 m × 6 m; half-cylinder radius 7 m, length 20 m; machine 250 m³. Asked: the air, empty and with the machine.

Concept used: volume = cuboid + half of a cylinder. Why it applies: the air fills both parts of one open space.

  1. Hidden faces: the top of the cuboid and the flat face of the half-cylinder are the same open plane inside the shed — the two spaces join, and their volumes add.

  2. 1

    Cuboid =20×14×6=1680= 20 \times 14 \times 6 = 1680=20×14×6=1680 m³.

  3. 2

    Half-cylinder =12×227×7×7×20=12×3080=1540= \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 \times 20 = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 3080 = 1540=21​×722​×7×7×20=21​×3080=1540 m³.

  4. 3

    Air =1680+1540=3220= 1680 + 1540 = 3220=1680+1540=3220 m³ =3220×1000=3 220 000= 3220 \times 1000 = 3\,220\,000=3220×1000=3220000 litres.

  5. 4

    With the machine: 3220−250=29703220 - 250 = 29703220−250=2970 m³.

  6. Check: the shed has the same end face all along its length: end face =14×6+12×227×7×7=84+77=161= 14 \times 6 + \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = 84 + 77 = 161=14×6+21​×722​×7×7=84+77=161 m², and 161×20=3220161 \times 20 = 3220161×20=3220 m³ ✔.

  7. Answer 3220 m³ =3 220 000= 3\,220\,000=3220000 litres; 2970 m³ with the machine

    Common trap: Using 14 m as the radius of the roof — the 14 m width is its diameter.

Worked example 4

Apparent and actual capacity

Question. A juice glass is a cylinder of inner diameter 12 cm and height 15 cm, but its bottom has a raised hemispherical part of radius 6 cm. Find the apparent capacity and the actual capacity of the glass. (Use π = 3.14.)

Given: r=6r = 6r=6 cm; h=15h = 15h=15 cm; raised hemisphere r=6r = 6r=6 cm. Asked: apparent and actual capacity.

Concept used: apparent =πr2h= \pi r^2 h=πr2h; actual = apparent − volume of the bump. Why it applies: the bump fills part of the cylinder.

  1. Hidden faces: the hemisphere's flat face is joined to the inside of the glass's base, so the bump sits inside the cylinder's space and takes away from it.

  2. 1

    Apparent capacity =3.14×6×6×15=1695.6= 3.14 \times 6 \times 6 \times 15 = 1695.6=3.14×6×6×15=1695.6 cm³.

  3. 2

    Bump =23×3.14×6×6×6=452.16= \dfrac{2}{3} \times 3.14 \times 6 \times 6 \times 6 = 452.16=32​×3.14×6×6×6=452.16 cm³.

  4. 3

    Actual capacity =1695.6−452.16=1243.44= 1695.6 - 452.16 = 1243.44=1695.6−452.16=1243.44 cm³ (about 1.24 litres).

  5. Check: in one step, πr2(h−2r3)=3.14×36×(15−4)=3.14×396=1243.44\pi r^2\left(h - \dfrac{2r}{3}\right) = 3.14 \times 36 \times (15 - 4) = 3.14 \times 396 = 1243.44πr2(h−32r​)=3.14×36×(15−4)=3.14×396=1243.44 cm³ ✔.

  6. Answer apparent 1695.6 cm³; actual 1243.44 cm³

    Common trap: Adding the bump — the glass would then hold more than it appears to, 2147.76 cm³.

Worked example 5

A block with a hole, and its mass

Question. A wooden block is a cuboid 20 cm × 12 cm × 6 cm. A cylindrical hole of radius 3.5 cm is drilled right through its 6 cm thickness. One cm³ of the wood has a mass of 0.8 g. Find the volume of wood left and its mass. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: cuboid 20 × 12 × 6 (cm); hole r=3.5r = 3.5r=3.5 cm, length 6 cm; 0.8 g per cm³. Asked: the volume and the mass.

Concept used: volume left = cuboid − cylinder; mass = volume × 0.8 g. Why it applies: the hole removes a cylinder of wood.

  1. Hidden faces: two circles of radius 3.5 cm are cut out of the top and bottom faces; for the volume, the whole cylinder of wood is removed.

  2. 1

    Cuboid =20×12×6=1440= 20 \times 12 \times 6 = 1440=20×12×6=1440 cm³.

  3. 2

    Hole =227×3.5×3.5×6=38.5×6=231= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 3.5 \times 6 = 38.5 \times 6 = 231=722​×3.5×3.5×6=38.5×6=231 cm³.

  4. 3

    Wood left =1440−231=1209= 1440 - 231 = 1209=1440−231=1209 cm³.

  5. 4

    Mass =1209×0.8=967.2= 1209 \times 0.8 = 967.2=1209×0.8=967.2 g.

  6. Check: mass of the full block 1440×0.8=11521440 \times 0.8 = 11521440×0.8=1152 g; mass of the wood removed 231×0.8=184.8231 \times 0.8 = 184.8231×0.8=184.8 g; 1152−184.8=967.21152 - 184.8 = 967.21152−184.8=967.2 g ✔.

  7. Answer 1209 cm³; 967.2 g

    Common trap: Adding the hole (1671 cm³) — a hole takes wood away.

Worked example 6

The mass of a metal post

Question. A solid metal post is a cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 1 m with a hemisphere of radius 7 cm on top. One cm³ of the metal has a mass of 7.8 g. Find the mass of the post in kilograms, to two decimal places. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=7r = 7r=7 cm; cylinder height 1 m = 100 cm; 7.8 g per cm³. Asked: the mass in kg.

Concept used: volume = cylinder + hemisphere; mass = volume × 7.8 g. Why it applies: the two parts are joined and made of the same metal.

  1. Hidden faces: the top of the cylinder and the flat face of the hemisphere are joined; both volumes count.

  2. 1

    Height in cm: 1 m = 100 cm.

  3. 2

    Cylinder =227×7×7×100=15 400= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 \times 100 = 15\,400=722​×7×7×100=15400 cm³.

  4. 3

    Hemisphere =23×227×7×7×7=21563≈718.67= \dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 \times 7 = \dfrac{2156}{3} \approx 718.67=32​×722​×7×7×7=32156​≈718.67 cm³.

  5. 4

    Volume =15 400+21563=48 3563= 15\,400 + \dfrac{2156}{3} = \dfrac{48\,356}{3}=15400+32156​=348356​ cm³.

  6. 5

    Mass =48 3563×7.8=125 725.6= \dfrac{48\,356}{3} \times 7.8 = 125\,725.6=348356​×7.8=125725.6 g ≈125.73\approx 125.73≈125.73 kg.

  7. Check: πr2(h+2r3)=154×(100+143)=154×3143=48 3563\pi r^2\left(h + \dfrac{2r}{3}\right) = 154 \times \left(100 + \dfrac{14}{3}\right) = 154 \times \dfrac{314}{3} = \dfrac{48\,356}{3}πr2(h+32r​)=154×(100+314​)=154×3314​=348356​ cm³ ✔.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that adding a volume that should be taken away, using the sphere's formula for a hemisphere and mixing units and litres are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • Hollow, hole or raised bottom: volume added

    These remove space.

    Subtract the removed part.

  • Joined solid: "hidden faces" taken off the volume

    Only surfaces hide; no space is lost.

    Volumes of joined parts simply add.

  • Hemisphere volume 43πr3\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^334​πr3

    That is a sphere.

    23πr3\dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^332​πr3.

  • Capsule: cylinder of the full length + a sphere

    The ends are counted twice.

    Cylinder length =L−2r= L - 2r=L−2r.

  • Metres and centimetres in one formula

    The answer comes out far too small or too big (in Worked example 6, about 873 cm³ instead of about 16 119 cm³).

    Change every length to one unit first.

  • cm³ → litres by ÷ 100; m³ → litres by ÷ 1000

    1 litre = 1000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1000 litres.

    cm³ ÷ 1000; m³ × 1000.

Key points

  • Joined solids: add the volumes — faces hide, space does not.
  • Hollows, holes, depressions and raised bottoms: subtract the volume removed.
  • Actual capacity = apparent capacity − volume of the bump.
  • Air in a building = volume of its whole inside shape, minus anything placed inside.
  • Same unit for every length; cm³ ÷ 1000 = litres; m³ × 1000 = litres; mass = volume × mass of 1 cm³.
