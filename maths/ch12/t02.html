Topic 2 of 5

Surface area of solids joined together

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain why the total surface area of a combination is not the sum of the parts' total surface areas, and name the faces hidden when two solids are joined.
  • Find the surface area of two solids joined face to face with equal faces, finding each part's height from a total height.
  • Find the surface area when the joined faces are unequal (a ring stays outside), and the area to be painted or covered and its cost.

Glue a paper cone onto a ball of clay and paint the toy. Where the cone's round base presses on the clay, no paint can reach — that circle is inside the toy now. And the clay's own circle under the cone is hidden too. So the paint you need is less than the paint for the cone and the ball painted separately. This whole topic is about finding exactly which faces disappear.

  1. Joined faces disappear

When two solids are stuck together face to face, the two faces that touch are pressed against each other. They are no longer on the outside, so they are not part of the surface area of the new solid.

TSA of the new solid ≠ TSA of part 1 + TSA of part 2.

Volumes are different: no space disappears when you join two solids, so volumes simply add (Topic 4).

  1. The hidden-faces routine

  1. Name the pieces (for example, "a cone on a hemisphere").
  2. List the hidden faces: the two faces that are joined.
  3. List the outside faces: every curved surface, and any flat face that still shows.
  4. Write the word equation: "surface area = CSA of cone + CSA of hemisphere".
  5. Put in the numbers, with the π the question gives.

  1. Read each part's height from the total

Draw a line beside the figure and split the total height (or length) into its parts.

A useful fact for capsules: the two hemispherical ends together have area 4πr2=2πr×2r4\pi r^2 = 2\pi r \times 2r4πr2=2πr×2r — the same as a cylinder of length 2r2r2r. So a capsule's surface is 2πr×2\pi r \times2πr× (total length).

  1. When the joined faces are not equal

If a cone of radius R sits on a narrower cylinder of radius r, only the middle circle of the cone's base (radius r) is pressed against the cylinder. The ring around it stays outside:

ring=πR2−πr2=π(R2−r2)\text{ring} = \pi R^2 - \pi r^2 = \pi(R^2 - r^2)ring=πR2−πr2=π(R2−r2)Swipe to see the whole equation →

Hidden faces: the top of the cylinder, and a circle of the same size on the cone's base — both of radius r.

  1. Painting, covering and cost

  • Paint, cloth or tin sheet covers only the outside — so use the surface area you found.
  • Cost === area × rate. Check the units: ₹ per m² needs the area in m².

Worked examples

Worked example 1

A tanker (cylinder with two hemispherical ends)

Question. The body of a water tanker is a cylinder with a hemisphere at each end. Its total length is 5.6 m and its diameter is 1.4 m. Find the area of its outer surface. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: total length 5.6 m; diameter 1.4 m, so r=0.7r = 0.7r=0.7 m. Asked: the outer surface area.

Concept used: surface = CSA of cylinder + CSA of the two hemispheres. Why it applies: the ends of the cylinder are covered by the hemispheres.

  1. Hidden faces: the two circular ends of the cylinder and the flat faces of the two hemispheres are joined in pairs — none of them is on the outside.

  2. 1

    Length of the cylinder =5.6−2×0.7=5.6−1.4=4.2= 5.6 - 2 \times 0.7 = 5.6 - 1.4 = 4.2=5.6−2×0.7=5.6−1.4=4.2 m.

  3. 2

    CSA of the cylinder =2×227×0.7×4.2=4.4×4.2=18.48= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 0.7 \times 4.2 = 4.4 \times 4.2 = 18.48=2×722​×0.7×4.2=4.4×4.2=18.48 m².

  4. 3

    CSA of two hemispheres =2×2πr2=4×227×0.7×0.7=6.16= 2 \times 2\pi r^2 = 4 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 0.7 \times 0.7 = 6.16=2×2πr2=4×722​×0.7×0.7=6.16 m².

  5. 4

    Outer surface =18.48+6.16=24.64= 18.48 + 6.16 = 24.64=18.48+6.16=24.64 m².

  6. Check: a capsule's surface is 2πr×2\pi r \times2πr× total length: 2×227×0.7×5.6=4.4×5.6=24.642 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 0.7 \times 5.6 = 4.4 \times 5.6 = 24.642×722​×0.7×5.6=4.4×5.6=24.64 m² ✔.

  7. Answer 24.64 m²

    Common trap: Using the whole length 5.6 m for the cylinder too — that counts the ends twice and gives 30.8 m².

Worked example 2

A toy: a cone on a hemisphere

Question. A wooden toy is a cone standing on a hemisphere of the same radius, 10 cm. The total height of the toy is 34 cm. Find the area to be painted (the whole outside). (Use π = 3.14.)

Given: r=10r = 10r=10 cm; total height 34 cm. Asked: the outer surface area.

Concept used: surface = CSA of cone + CSA of hemisphere; cone height = total height − r. Why it applies: the hemisphere's radius is part of the total height.

  1. Hidden faces: the base of the cone and the flat face of the hemisphere are glued together.

  2. 1

    Height of the cone =34−10=24= 34 - 10 = 24=34−10=24 cm.

  3. 2

    Slant height l=102+242=100+576=676=26l = \sqrt{10^2 + 24^2} = \sqrt{100 + 576} = \sqrt{676} = 26l=102+242​=100+576​=676​=26 cm.

  4. 3

    CSA of the cone =π×10×26=260π= \pi \times 10 \times 26 = 260\pi=π×10×26=260π cm².

  5. 4

    CSA of the hemisphere =2×π×10×10=200π= 2 \times \pi \times 10 \times 10 = 200\pi=2×π×10×10=200π cm².

  6. 5

    Surface =260π+200π=460π=460×3.14=1444.4= 260\pi + 200\pi = 460\pi = 460 \times 3.14 = 1444.4=260π+200π=460π=460×3.14=1444.4 cm².

  7. Check: start from the two TSAs and take away the two hidden circles: cone TSA =π×10×(26+10)=360π= \pi \times 10 \times (26 + 10) = 360\pi=π×10×(26+10)=360π, hemisphere TSA =3×100π=300π= 3 \times 100\pi = 300\pi=3×100π=300π; 360π+300π−2×100π=460π360\pi + 300\pi - 2 \times 100\pi = 460\pi360π+300π−2×100π=460π ✔.

  8. Answer 1444.4 cm²

    Common trap: Taking the cone's height as 34 cm (the whole toy), which makes the slant height about 35.44 cm.

Worked example 3

The canvas for a tent

Question. A tent is a cylinder of radius 3.5 m and height 2.5 m with a conical top of slant height 6 m. The floor is not covered. Find the area of canvas needed and its cost at ₹120 per m². (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=3.5r = 3.5r=3.5 m; wall height 2.5 m; l=6l = 6l=6 m; ₹120 per m². Asked: the canvas area and its cost.

Concept used: canvas = CSA of cylinder + CSA of cone. Why it applies: only the wall and the roof are made of canvas.

  1. Hidden faces: the base of the cone and the top of the cylinder are joined (open inside the tent, no canvas); the floor of the cylinder is not covered.

  2. 1

    Wall (CSA of cylinder) =2×227×3.5×2.5=22×2.5=55= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 2.5 = 22 \times 2.5 = 55=2×722​×3.5×2.5=22×2.5=55 m².

  3. 2

    Roof (CSA of cone) =227×3.5×6=11×6=66= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 6 = 11 \times 6 = 66=722​×3.5×6=11×6=66 m².

  4. 3

    Canvas =55+66=121= 55 + 66 = 121=55+66=121 m².

  5. 4

    Cost =121×120=14 520= 121 \times 120 = 14\,520=121×120=14520, so ₹14 520.

  6. Check: in one step, πr(2h+l)=227×3.5×(5+6)=11×11=121\pi r(2h + l) = \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times (5 + 6) = 11 \times 11 = 121πr(2h+l)=722​×3.5×(5+6)=11×11=121 m² ✔.

  7. Answer 121 m²; ₹14 520

    Common trap: Adding the floor πr2=38.5\pi r^2 = 38.5πr2=38.5 m² — the question says the floor has no canvas.

Worked example 4

A hemisphere on a cube

Question. A trophy base is a cube of edge 10 cm with a hemisphere of diameter 7 cm fixed on its top face. Find the total surface area of the trophy base. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: cube edge 10 cm; hemisphere r=3.5r = 3.5r=3.5 cm. Asked: the TSA.

Concept used: TSA of cube − circle covered + CSA of hemisphere. Why it applies: the hemisphere covers a circle of the top face and adds its curved surface.

  1. Hidden faces: the flat face of the hemisphere and the circle of the cube's top face under it.

  2. 1

    TSA of the cube =6×10×10=600= 6 \times 10 \times 10 = 600=6×10×10=600 cm².

  3. 2

    Circle covered =227×3.5×3.5=38.5= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 3.5 = 38.5=722​×3.5×3.5=38.5 cm².

  4. 3

    CSA of the hemisphere =2×38.5=77= 2 \times 38.5 = 77=2×38.5=77 cm².

  5. 4

    TSA =600−38.5+77=638.5= 600 - 38.5 + 77 = 638.5=600−38.5+77=638.5 cm².

  6. Check: the hemisphere takes away πr2\pi r^2πr2 and adds 2πr22\pi r^22πr2, so the net change is +πr2+\pi r^2+πr2: 600+38.5=638.5600 + 38.5 = 638.5600+38.5=638.5 cm² ✔.

  7. Answer 638.5 cm²

    Common trap: Adding the TSA of the hemisphere (3πr2=115.53\pi r^2 = 115.53πr2=115.5) to 600 — the flat face is hidden, and so is the circle under it.

Worked example 5

Three cubes in a row

Question. Three cubes, each of edge 5 cm, are joined end to end in a row. Find the total surface area of the cuboid formed. (π is not needed here.)

Given: three cubes, edge 5 cm. Asked: the TSA of the cuboid.

Concept used: TSA of a cuboid 2(lb+bh+hl)2(lb + bh + hl)2(lb+bh+hl). Why it applies: the row is a cuboid 15 cm × 5 cm × 5 cm.

  1. Hidden faces: two joins, and each join hides one face of each of the two cubes that meet — 4 faces in all.

  2. 1

    The cuboid is 15×5×515 \times 5 \times 515×5×5 (cm).

  3. 2

    TSA =2(15×5+5×5+5×15)=2(75+25+75)=2×175=350= 2(15 \times 5 + 5 \times 5 + 5 \times 15) = 2(75 + 25 + 75) = 2 \times 175 = 350=2(15×5+5×5+5×15)=2(75+25+75)=2×175=350 cm².

  4. Check: three separate cubes have 3×6×25=4503 \times 6 \times 25 = 4503×6×25=450 cm²; the 4 hidden faces are 4×25=1004 \times 25 = 1004×25=100 cm²; 450−100=350450 - 100 = 350450−100=350 cm² ✔.

  5. Answer 350 cm²

    Common trap: Taking away only 2 faces (one per join) gives 400 cm². Each join hides two faces.

Worked example 6

Unequal faces: a cone on a narrower cylinder

Question. A toy rocket is a cone of base radius 5 cm and height 12 cm placed on top of a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 16 cm, with their centres in line. The cone (with the part of its base that sticks out) is painted red; the cylinder (with its bottom) is painted white. Find each area. (Use π = 3.14.)

Given: cone R=5R = 5R=5 cm, h=12h = 12h=12 cm; cylinder r=3r = 3r=3 cm, height 16 cm. Asked: the red area and the white area.

Concept used: red = CSA of cone + ring π(R2−r2)\pi(R^2 - r^2)π(R2−r2); white = CSA of cylinder + bottom. Why it applies: the cone's base is wider than the cylinder.

  1. Hidden faces: the top of the cylinder (radius 3 cm) and the circle of radius 3 cm in the middle of the cone's base. The ring of the cone's base between radius 3 cm and 5 cm stays outside.

  2. 1

    Slant height l=52+122=13l = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = 13l=52+122​=13 cm; CSA of cone =π×5×13=65π= \pi \times 5 \times 13 = 65\pi=π×5×13=65π cm².

  3. 2

    Ring =π(52−32)=π(25−9)=16π= \pi(5^2 - 3^2) = \pi(25 - 9) = 16\pi=π(52−32)=π(25−9)=16π cm².

  4. 3

    Red =65π+16π=81π=81×3.14=254.34= 65\pi + 16\pi = 81\pi = 81 \times 3.14 = 254.34=65π+16π=81π=81×3.14=254.34 cm².

  5. 4

    CSA of cylinder =2×π×3×16=96π= 2 \times \pi \times 3 \times 16 = 96\pi=2×π×3×16=96π cm²; bottom =π×9=9π= \pi \times 9 = 9\pi=π×9=9π cm².

  6. 5

    White =96π+9π=105π=105×3.14=329.7= 96\pi + 9\pi = 105\pi = 105 \times 3.14 = 329.7=96π+9π=105π=105×3.14=329.7 cm².

  7. Check: the TSAs of the cone (π×5×18=90π\pi \times 5 \times 18 = 90\piπ×5×18=90π) and the cylinder (2π×3×19=114π2\pi \times 3 \times 19 = 114\pi2π×3×19=114π) add to 204π204\pi204π; take away the two hidden circles of radius 3 cm (2×9π2 \times 9\pi2×9π): 204π−18π=186π204\pi - 18\pi = 186\pi204π−18π=186π, and red + white =81π+105π=186π= 81\pi + 105\pi = 186\pi=81π+105π=186π ✔.

  8. Answer red 254.34 cm²; white 329.7 cm²

    Common trap: Leaving out the ring (red would be only 204.1 cm²) — the outer part of the cone's base can be seen and must be painted.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that counting the joined faces, reading the height of a part wrongly and mixing up the slant height and the height are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • TSA of the toy = TSA of cone + TSA of hemisphere

    The two joined circles are counted, but they are inside.

    List the hidden faces first; use CSAs for the joined pieces.

  • Two joined cubes: 2×6a2−a22 \times 6a^2 - a^22×6a2−a2

    Each join hides two faces, one of each cube.

    12a2−2a2=10a212a^2 - 2a^2 = 10a^212a2−2a2=10a2.

  • Capsule: cylinder length = total length

    The two hemispheres take up rrr at each end.

    Cylinder length =L−2r= L - 2r=L−2r.

  • Toy: cone height = total height

    The hemisphere's radius is part of the height.

    Cone height =H−r= H - r=H−r.

  • Unequal faces: the whole base of the wider solid removed

    Only a circle as big as the narrower face is pressed.

    Hidden: two circles of the smaller radius; the ring stays outside.

  • Roof or toy: πrh\pi r hπrh for the cone's curved surface

    The curved surface slopes; h is the upright height.

    Find l=r2+h2l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}l=r2+h2​ first; CSA =πrl= \pi r l=πrl.

Key points

  • Joined faces are hidden: the TSA of a combination is not the sum of the TSAs. Volumes, though, simply add.
  • Routine: name the pieces → list the hidden faces → list the outside faces → word equation → numbers.
  • Split a total height or length first: capsule L−2rL - 2rL−2r; toy H−rH - rH−r; tent H−hH - hH−h.
  • Equal faces: both joined faces disappear. Unequal faces: two circles of the smaller radius disappear, and the ring π(R2−r2)\pi(R^2 - r^2)π(R2−r2) stays outside.
  • Paint, canvas or sheet covers only the outside; cost = area × rate, with matching units.
