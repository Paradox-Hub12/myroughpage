Topic 3 of 5

Surface area of solids with a part cut out

About 50 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find the surface area of a solid with a hemisphere, cone or cylinder hollowed out of it (subtract the opening, add the inner surface).
  • Find the inner surface area of an open vessel made of two hollow solids, and the largest hemisphere, cone or cylinder that can be cut from a cube or cylinder.
  • Compare a solid with a part added and with the same part cut out, for surface area and for volume.

A soap bar with a round dip for your thumb, a wooden pen stand with holes, a bead with a hole for the thread — each is a solid with a piece taken out. You might expect the surface to get smaller. Often it gets bigger: the hole has walls of its own, and they are on the outside of the solid too (you can touch them).

  1. Cutting out: one face goes, a new one comes

When a hollow is cut into a solid:

  • the opening — the flat piece of the face that is cut away — is no longer there, so subtract it;
  • the inside of the hollow is now part of the surface, so add it (the curved surface of the hollow, and its flat bottom if it has one).

This is the same hidden-faces routine as Topic 2: name the pieces, list the faces that go, list the faces that are outside, write the word equation, then the numbers.

Take care: the hollow adds only its inner surfaces — never the TSA of the piece that was removed.

  1. Open vessels

A vessel (a bowl, a mug, a tank) is hollow. When a question asks for the inner surface, add only the inside faces you could wet:

  • a hollow hemisphere at the bottom: 2πr22\pi r^22πr2;
  • a hollow cylinder above it: 2πrh2\pi r h2πrh;
  • no top (it is open) and no circle where the two parts meet (water passes straight through).

  1. Added or cut out — what changes?

A hemisphere stuck on a face and the same hemisphere scooped out of that face change the surface area by the same amount, +πr2+\pi r^2+πr2. But the volumes go in opposite directions: added gives V+23πr3V + \dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3V+32​πr3; cut out gives V−23πr3V - \dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3V−32​πr3 (Topic 4).

Worked example 1

The largest hemisphere scooped from a cube

Question. The largest possible hemisphere is scooped out of one face of a wooden cube of edge 14 cm. Find the surface area of the remaining solid. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: cube edge 14 cm. Asked: the surface area of what is left.

Concept used: largest hemisphere: r=a2r = \dfrac{a}{2}r=2a​; surface = TSA of cube − opening + inner curved surface. Why it applies: the hollow's circle must fit inside a face of the cube.

  1. Hidden faces: the circle of radius 7 cm cut out of the top face is gone; the inside of the hollow (2πr22\pi r^22πr2) is new surface.

  2. 1

    Radius of the hemisphere =142=7= \dfrac{14}{2} = 7=214​=7 cm.

  3. 2

    TSA of the cube =6×14×14=1176= 6 \times 14 \times 14 = 1176=6×14×14=1176 cm².

  4. 3

    Opening =227×7×7=154= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = 154=722​×7×7=154 cm²; inner surface =2×154=308= 2 \times 154 = 308=2×154=308 cm².

  5. 4

    Surface =1176−154+308=1330= 1176 - 154 + 308 = 1330=1176−154+308=1330 cm².

  6. Check: the general result a24(π+24)\dfrac{a^2}{4}(\pi + 24)4a2​(π+24) (NCERT Exercise 12.1, Q5) with a=14a = 14a=14: 49×(227+24)=154+1176=133049 \times \left(\dfrac{22}{7} + 24\right) = 154 + 1176 = 133049×(722​+24)=154+1176=1330 cm² ✔.

  7. Answer 1330 cm²

    Common trap: Subtracting the hollow instead of adding it — 1176−154−308=7141176 - 154 - 308 = 7141176−154−308=714 cm². Cutting the hollow adds a curved surface.

Worked example 2

A cone hollowed out of a cylinder

Question. From a solid cylinder of radius 12 cm and height 5 cm, a cone of the same base radius and the same height is hollowed out (its base is the top face of the cylinder). Find the total surface area of the remaining solid. (Use π = 3.14.)

Given: r=12r = 12r=12 cm; h=5h = 5h=5 cm. Asked: the TSA of what is left.

Concept used: surface = CSA of cylinder + bottom + CSA of the conical hollow. Why it applies: the top circle is the cone's base, which is cut away.

  1. Hidden faces: the whole top circle of the cylinder (the cone's base) is cut away; the cone's curved surface is now the inside of the hollow.

  2. 1

    Slant height of the hollow: l=122+52=169=13l = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13l=122+52​=169​=13 cm.

  3. 2

    CSA of cylinder =2×π×12×5=120π= 2 \times \pi \times 12 \times 5 = 120\pi=2×π×12×5=120π cm².

  4. 3

    Bottom =π×12×12=144π= \pi \times 12 \times 12 = 144\pi=π×12×12=144π cm².

  5. 4

    Inner surface of the hollow =π×12×13=156π= \pi \times 12 \times 13 = 156\pi=π×12×13=156π cm².

  6. 5

    Total =120π+144π+156π=420π=420×3.14=1318.8= 120\pi + 144\pi + 156\pi = 420\pi = 420 \times 3.14 = 1318.8=120π+144π+156π=420π=420×3.14=1318.8 cm².

  7. Check: from the full cylinder: TSA =2π×12×(12+5)=408π= 2\pi \times 12 \times (12 + 5) = 408\pi=2π×12×(12+5)=408π; take away the top 144π144\pi144π and add the hollow 156π156\pi156π: 408π−144π+156π=420π408\pi - 144\pi + 156\pi = 420\pi408π−144π+156π=420π ✔.

  8. Answer 1318.8 cm²

    Common trap: Keeping the top circle (564π≈1770.96564\pi \approx 1770.96564π≈1770.96 cm²) — it has been cut away.

Worked example 3

A hemisphere scooped from each end

Question. A wooden spool is a solid cylinder of radius 5 cm and height 15 cm with a bowl-shaped (hemispherical) hollow carved into each of its two flat ends. Find its total surface area. (Use π = 3.14.)

Given: r=5r = 5r=5 cm; h=15h = 15h=15 cm. Asked: the TSA.

Concept used: surface = CSA of cylinder + inner surfaces of two hemispheres. Why it applies: both end circles are cut away.

  1. Hidden faces: both circular ends of the cylinder are cut away; the two hollows add 2×2πr22 \times 2\pi r^22×2πr2. (The hollows do not meet: 2×5=10<152 \times 5 = 10 < 152×5=10<15.)

  2. 1

    CSA of cylinder =2×π×5×15=150π= 2 \times \pi \times 5 \times 15 = 150\pi=2×π×5×15=150π cm².

  3. 2

    Two hollows =2×2×π×5×5=100π= 2 \times 2 \times \pi \times 5 \times 5 = 100\pi=2×2×π×5×5=100π cm².

  4. 3

    Total =150π+100π=250π=250×3.14=785= 150\pi + 100\pi = 250\pi = 250 \times 3.14 = 785=150π+100π=250π=250×3.14=785 cm².

  5. Check: from the full cylinder: 2π×5×20=200π2\pi \times 5 \times 20 = 200\pi2π×5×20=200π; take away 2 ends (50π50\pi50π) and add 2 hollows (100π100\pi100π): 200π−50π+100π=250π200\pi - 50\pi + 100\pi = 250\pi200π−50π+100π=250π ✔.

  6. Answer 785 cm²

    Common trap: Adding the TSA of two hemispheres (6πr26\pi r^26πr2) — only their curved surfaces are inside the article.

Worked example 4

The inner surface of an open vessel

Question. A vessel is a hollow hemisphere with a hollow cylinder on top of it, open at the top. Its inner diameter is 21 cm and its total inner height is 25.5 cm. Find its inner surface area. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=10.5r = 10.5r=10.5 cm; total height 25.5 cm. Asked: the inner surface area.

Concept used: inner surface = inner CSA of cylinder + inner CSA of hemisphere. Why it applies: the vessel is open at the top and the two parts open into each other.

  1. Hidden faces: there is no surface where the hemisphere meets the cylinder (the rim of the bowl and the bottom of the cylinder are joined and open into each other), and none at the open top.

  2. 1

    Height of the cylinder =25.5−10.5=15= 25.5 - 10.5 = 15=25.5−10.5=15 cm.

  3. 2

    Cylinder =2×227×10.5×15=66×15=990= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 15 = 66 \times 15 = 990=2×722​×10.5×15=66×15=990 cm².

  4. 3

    Hemisphere =2×227×10.5×10.5=66×10.5=693= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 = 66 \times 10.5 = 693=2×722​×10.5×10.5=66×10.5=693 cm².

  5. 4

    Inner surface =990+693=1683= 990 + 693 = 1683=990+693=1683 cm².

  6. Check: 2πr(h+r)=66×(15+10.5)=66×25.5=16832\pi r(h + r) = 66 \times (15 + 10.5) = 66 \times 25.5 = 16832πr(h+r)=66×(15+10.5)=66×25.5=1683 cm² ✔.

  7. Answer 1683 cm²

    Common trap: Using 25.5 cm as the cylinder's height — the bowl's radius is part of the total height.

Worked example 5

A hole right through a cylinder

Question. A wooden roller is a solid cylinder of radius 7 cm and length 20 cm. A hole of radius 3.5 cm is drilled right through it along its axis. Find the total surface area of the roller. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: outer radius 7 cm; hole radius 3.5 cm; length 20 cm. Asked: the TSA.

Concept used: surface = outer CSA + inner CSA (the hole) + two ring-shaped ends. Why it applies: the hole removes a circle from each end and adds its wall.

  1. Hidden faces: the two circles of radius 3.5 cm cut out of the ends are gone; the wall of the hole is new surface.

  2. 1

    Outer CSA =2×227×7×20=880= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 20 = 880=2×722​×7×20=880 cm².

  3. 2

    Inner CSA =2×227×3.5×20=440= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 20 = 440=2×722​×3.5×20=440 cm².

  4. 3

    One end (a ring) =227×(72−3.52)=227×36.75=115.5= \dfrac{22}{7} \times (7^2 - 3.5^2) = \dfrac{22}{7} \times 36.75 = 115.5=722​×(72−3.52)=722​×36.75=115.5 cm²; two ends =231= 231=231 cm².

  5. 4

    TSA =880+440+231=1551= 880 + 440 + 231 = 1551=880+440+231=1551 cm².

  6. Check: a solid roller has 2π×7×(7+20)=11882\pi \times 7 \times (7 + 20) = 11882π×7×(7+20)=1188 cm²; take away the two holes in the ends, 2×38.5=772 \times 38.5 = 772×38.5=77, and add the wall, 440: 1188−77+440=15511188 - 77 + 440 = 15511188−77+440=1551 cm² ✔.

  7. Answer 1551 cm²

    Common trap: Forgetting the wall of the hole (that gives 1111 cm²) — it is on the outside of the solid: you can touch it.

Worked example 6

Added or cut out?

Question. Two blocks start as cubes of edge 8 cm. On block A a hemisphere of radius 3.5 cm is stuck on the top face; from block B a hemisphere of radius 3.5 cm is scooped out of the top face. Find the surface area and the volume of each block. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: cube edge 8 cm; hemisphere radius 3.5 cm. Asked: the surface area and volume of A and of B.

Concept used: each change of surface is −πr2+2πr2-\pi r^2 + 2\pi r^2−πr2+2πr2; the volume of the hemisphere is added for A and subtracted for B. Why it applies: the same piece is joined on (A) or taken away (B).

  1. Hidden faces: A — the hemisphere's flat face and the circle under it are joined; B — the circle of the opening is gone and the hollow's inside is new.

  2. 1

    Cube: TSA =6×64=384= 6 \times 64 = 384=6×64=384 cm²; volume =512= 512=512 cm³.

  3. 2

    πr2=227×3.5×3.5=38.5\pi r^2 = \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 3.5 = 38.5πr2=722​×3.5×3.5=38.5 cm²; 2πr2=772\pi r^2 = 772πr2=77 cm².

  4. 3

    Surface of A === surface of B =384−38.5+77=422.5= 384 - 38.5 + 77 = 422.5=384−38.5+77=422.5 cm².

  5. 4

    Volume of the hemisphere =23×227×3.5×3.5×3.5=5396≈89.83= \dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 3.5 \times 3.5 = \dfrac{539}{6} \approx 89.83=32​×722​×3.5×3.5×3.5=6539​≈89.83 cm³.

  6. 5

    A: 512+5396=36116≈601.83512 + \dfrac{539}{6} = \dfrac{3611}{6} \approx 601.83512+6539​=63611​≈601.83 cm³. B: 512−5396=25336≈422.17512 - \dfrac{539}{6} = \dfrac{2533}{6} \approx 422.17512−6539​=62533​≈422.17 cm³.

  7. Check: the two volumes differ by two hemispheres — one sphere: 43×227×3.53=5393≈179.67\dfrac{4}{3} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5^3 = \dfrac{539}{3} \approx 179.6734​×722​×3.53=3539​≈179.67, and 36116−25336=10786=5393\dfrac{3611}{6} - \dfrac{2533}{6} = \dfrac{1078}{6} = \dfrac{539}{3}63611​−62533​=61078​=3539​ ✔.

  8. Answer A and B: 422.5 cm² each; volume of A ≈ 601.83 cm³, of B ≈ 422.17 cm³

    Common trap: Thinking a hollow makes the surface smaller. The opening goes, but a bigger curved surface comes in.

(π stays as a letter.) Scooping a hemisphere of radius r out of a flat face takes away a circle of area ……, adds a curved surface of area ……, and so changes the surface area by …… .

Answer: πr2\pi r^2πr2; 2πr22\pi r^22πr2; +πr2+\pi r^2+πr2 (an increase).

The hollow's curved surface is twice the circle it replaces, so the surface area grows by one circle — exactly as when the hemisphere is stuck on instead.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that getting the surfaces of a cut-out wrong and using the formulas of the hemisphere or sphere wrongly are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • Subtracting the hollow's surface

    The inside of a hollow is part of the surface.

    Take away the opening; add the hollow's inner surface.

  • Forgetting to take away the opening

    That piece of the face is no longer there.

    Opening: −πr2-\pi r^2−πr2 for each hole.

  • Adding the TSA of the removed piece

    Its flat face was never on the solid.

    Add only the inner surfaces (curved, and a flat bottom if the hole has one).

  • Inner surface of an open vessel with a lid or a joining circle

    The top is open; the two hollow parts open into each other.

    Add only the inner curved surfaces.

  • Largest hemisphere in a cube of edge a: radius a

    The circle must fit inside a face.

    r=a2r = \dfrac{a}{2}r=2a​.

Key points

  • Cutting out: take away the opening, add the inside of the hollow (its curved surface, and its bottom if it has one).
  • Hemisphere scooped from a face: +πr2+\pi r^2+πr2. Cone hollowed from a cylinder: −πr2+πrl-\pi r^2 + \pi r l−πr2+πrl. Hole of depth d: +2πrd+2\pi r d+2πrd.
  • Open vessel: inner curved surfaces only — no top, no circle where the parts meet.
  • Largest hemisphere, cone or cylinder in a cube of edge a: radius a2\dfrac{a}{2}2a​.
  • The same hemisphere added or cut out changes the surface by the same +πr2+\pi r^2+πr2, but the volume in opposite directions.
