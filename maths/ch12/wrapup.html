Wrap-up

The formula card, hidden faces, add or take away, and mixed problems

About 30 minutes

Every question in this chapter uses the same three moves: break the object into basic solids, decide which faces are hidden (for surface area), and decide whether to add or take away (for volume). This page puts the three cards on one sheet and then gives four mixed problems in the styles of the sample papers — a test tube case, a pen stand with holes, a tent with its canvas and air, and a toy dropped into a jar of water.

Mixed example 1

Case study: the test tube

Question. A test tube is a thin glass cylinder of inner radius 2.1 cm with a hemispherical bottom of the same radius; the cylindrical part is 10 cm long and the top is open. Take the thickness of the glass as negligible. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)
(i) Find the volume of liquid the test tube can hold. (1 mark)
(ii) Find the area of glass in the test tube. (1 mark)
(iii) A rack holds 25 such test tubes, each filled to the top. How many litres of solution are in the rack? (2 marks)

Given: r=2.1r = 2.1r=2.1 cm; cylinder 10 cm; open top. Asked: the volume, the glass area, the litres in 25 tubes.

Concept used: volume = cylinder + hemisphere; glass = CSA of cylinder + CSA of hemisphere; 25 tubes = 25 × one tube; litres = cm³ ÷ 1000. Why it applies: the tube is two hollow solids joined.

  1. Hidden faces: the cylinder's bottom circle and the hemisphere's rim circle are joined (they open into each other — no glass there); the top is open.

  2. 1

    (i) Cylinder =227×2.1×2.1×10=138.6= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 2.1 \times 2.1 \times 10 = 138.6=722​×2.1×2.1×10=138.6 cm³; hemisphere =23×227×2.1×2.1×2.1=19.404= \dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 2.1 \times 2.1 \times 2.1 = 19.404=32​×722​×2.1×2.1×2.1=19.404 cm³; total =158.004= 158.004=158.004 cm³.

  3. 2

    (ii) Cylinder =2×227×2.1×10=132= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 2.1 \times 10 = 132=2×722​×2.1×10=132 cm²; hemisphere =2×227×2.1×2.1=27.72= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 2.1 \times 2.1 = 27.72=2×722​×2.1×2.1=27.72 cm²; glass =159.72= 159.72=159.72 cm².

  4. 3

    (iii) 25×158.004=3950.125 \times 158.004 = 3950.125×158.004=3950.1 cm³ =3950.1÷1000=3.9501= 3950.1 \div 1000 = 3.9501=3950.1÷1000=3.9501 litres, about 3.95 litres.

  5. Check: (iii) 158.004×100=15 800.4158.004 \times 100 = 15\,800.4158.004×100=15800.4, and a quarter of that is 3950.13950.13950.1 cm³ ✔ (25 is a quarter of 100).

  6. Answer (i) 158.004 cm³ (ii) 159.72 cm² (iii) about 3.95 litres

    Common trap: In (iii), dividing by 100 instead of 1000 (39.5 litres).

Mixed example 2

A pen stand with two holes

Question. A wooden pen stand is a cuboid 15 cm × 12 cm × 6 cm. Two cylindrical holes, each of radius 1.4 cm and depth 4.2 cm, are drilled into its top face. Find (a) the volume of wood and (b) the total surface area of the stand. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: cuboid 15 × 12 × 6 (cm); two holes, r=1.4r = 1.4r=1.4 cm, depth 4.2 cm. Asked: the volume of wood; the TSA.

Concept used: (a) cuboid − 2 cylinders; (b) TSA of cuboid − 2 openings + 2 × (wall + bottom of a hole). Why it applies: each hole removes wood, removes a circle from the top and adds its own wall and bottom.

  1. Hidden faces: the two circular openings in the top face are gone; each hole adds a wall and a flat bottom.

  2. 1

    (a) Cuboid =15×12×6=1080= 15 \times 12 \times 6 = 1080=15×12×6=1080 cm³. One hole =227×1.4×1.4×4.2=25.872= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 1.4 \times 1.4 \times 4.2 = 25.872=722​×1.4×1.4×4.2=25.872 cm³; two holes =51.744= 51.744=51.744 cm³.

  3. 2

    Wood =1080−51.744=1028.256= 1080 - 51.744 = 1028.256=1080−51.744=1028.256 cm³.

  4. 3

    (b) TSA of cuboid =2(15×12+12×6+6×15)=2(180+72+90)=684= 2(15 \times 12 + 12 \times 6 + 6 \times 15) = 2(180 + 72 + 90) = 684=2(15×12+12×6+6×15)=2(180+72+90)=684 cm².

  5. 4

    For one hole: opening 227×1.4×1.4=6.16\dfrac{22}{7} \times 1.4 \times 1.4 = 6.16722​×1.4×1.4=6.16 cm² (gone); wall 2×227×1.4×4.2=36.962 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 1.4 \times 4.2 = 36.962×722​×1.4×4.2=36.96 cm²; bottom 6.16 cm².

  6. 5

    TSA =684+2×(−6.16+36.96+6.16)=684+2×36.96=757.92= 684 + 2 \times (-6.16 + 36.96 + 6.16) = 684 + 2 \times 36.96 = 757.92=684+2×(−6.16+36.96+6.16)=684+2×36.96=757.92 cm².

  7. Check: a hole that does not go through changes the surface by its wall only, 2πrd2\pi r d2πrd, because its bottom replaces its opening: 684+2×36.96=757.92684 + 2 \times 36.96 = 757.92684+2×36.96=757.92 cm² ✔.

  8. Answer (a) 1028.256 cm³ (b) 757.92 cm²

    Common trap: In (b), subtracting the holes' walls — a hole adds surface you can touch.

Mixed example 3

A tent: canvas and air

Question. A tent is a cylinder of radius 2.8 m and height 2.5 m with a conical top of height 9.6 m. Find (a) the canvas needed for the wall and the roof, and (b) the volume of air inside. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=2.8r = 2.8r=2.8 m; wall 2.5 m; cone height 9.6 m. Asked: the canvas; the air.

Concept used: (a) CSA of cylinder + CSA of cone; (b) volume of cylinder + volume of cone. Why it applies: the canvas covers the wall and the roof; the air fills the whole inside.

  1. Hidden faces: the cone's base and the cylinder's top are joined (no canvas there, but the air spaces join and their volumes add); the floor has no canvas.

  2. 1

    Slant height l=2.82+9.62=7.84+92.16=100=10l = \sqrt{2.8^2 + 9.6^2} = \sqrt{7.84 + 92.16} = \sqrt{100} = 10l=2.82+9.62​=7.84+92.16​=100​=10 m.

  3. 2

    (a) Wall =2×227×2.8×2.5=44= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 2.8 \times 2.5 = 44=2×722​×2.8×2.5=44 m²; roof =227×2.8×10=88= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 2.8 \times 10 = 88=722​×2.8×10=88 m²; canvas =132= 132=132 m².

  4. 3

    (b) Cylinder =227×2.8×2.8×2.5=61.6= \dfrac{22}{7} \times 2.8 \times 2.8 \times 2.5 = 61.6=722​×2.8×2.8×2.5=61.6 m³; cone =13×24.64×9.6=78.848= \dfrac{1}{3} \times 24.64 \times 9.6 = 78.848=31​×24.64×9.6=78.848 m³; air =140.448= 140.448=140.448 m³.

  5. Check: (a) πr(2h+l)=8.8×(5+10)=132\pi r(2h + l) = 8.8 \times (5 + 10) = 132πr(2h+l)=8.8×(5+10)=132 ✔; (b) πr2(h+9.63)=24.64×5.7=140.448\pi r^2\left(h + \dfrac{9.6}{3}\right) = 24.64 \times 5.7 = 140.448πr2(h+39.6​)=24.64×5.7=140.448 ✔.

  6. Answer (a) 132 m² (b) 140.448 m³

    Common trap: Using 9.6 m (the height) in πrl\pi r lπrl for the roof — use l=10l = 10l=10 m.

Mixed example 4

How far does the water rise?

Question. A solid toy is a cone of height 12 cm on a hemisphere of radius 6 cm (the cone has the same radius). It is dropped into a cylindrical jar of radius 10 cm that holds enough water to cover it; no water flows out. By how much does the water level rise? Work in terms of π.

Given: toy: r=6r = 6r=6 cm, cone height 12 cm; jar radius 10 cm. Asked: the rise h.

Concept used: volume of the toy = volume of water pushed up =π×102×h= \pi \times 10^2 \times h=π×102×h. Why it applies: the toy takes the place of its own volume of water.

  1. Hidden faces: the cone's base and the hemisphere's flat face are joined; the toy's volume is cone + hemisphere. The jar is the container.

  2. 1

    Toy =13π×36×12+23π×216=144π+144π=288π= \dfrac{1}{3}\pi \times 36 \times 12 + \dfrac{2}{3}\pi \times 216 = 144\pi + 144\pi = 288\pi=31​π×36×12+32​π×216=144π+144π=288π cm³.

  3. 2

    100π×h=288π100\pi \times h = 288\pi100π×h=288π, so h=288100=2.88h = \dfrac{288}{100} = 2.88h=100288​=2.88 cm.

  4. Check: π×10×10×2.88=288π\pi \times 10 \times 10 \times 2.88 = 288\piπ×10×10×2.88=288π cm³ — the toy's volume ✔.

  5. Answer 2.88 cm

    Common trap: Dividing by the jar's circumference (20π20\pi20π) instead of its base area (100π100\pi100π) — that gives 14.4 cm.
