Toolkit: the basic solids

About 30 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find the volume, curved surface area and total surface area of a cuboid, cube, cylinder, cone, sphere and hemisphere (the slant height of a cone by Pythagoras), with π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​, π = 3.14 or in terms of π.
  • Work backwards from a volume or surface area to a radius or height, and from a diameter to a radius.
  • Use units correctly — cm, cm², cm³; 1 litre = 1000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1000 litres; mass from volume.
Why this lesson exists

Every object in this chapter — a capsule, a tent, a toy top — is two basic solids put together. If you know the formulas for the basic solids from Class 9 well, the rest of the chapter is about deciding what to add and what to leave out. If you already have these formulas ready, prove it below and go straight to Topic 2.

Work through this toolkit — it takes about 30 minutes. Then try the questions again.

Look at a medicine capsule, a pencil sharpened to a point, or a toy top (lattu). None of them is a cylinder, a cone or a sphere — but each is two of them stuck together. To find how much paint covers the toy or how much medicine fills the capsule, you will need the formula for each piece. This lesson puts those formulas in one place.

  1. Surface area and volume — two different questions

  • Surface area is how much material covers the outside (paint, cloth, tin sheet). It is measured in square units: cm², m².
  • Volume is how much space the solid fills (wood, metal, water inside). It is measured in cubic units: cm³, m³.
  • Curved surface area (CSA) is only the curved part. Total surface area (TSA) adds the flat faces too.

  1. The slant height of a cone

The height h, the radius r and the slant height l form a right triangle (the height meets the base at its centre at 90°). By Pythagoras:

l=r2+h2l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}l=r2+h2​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  • Use l for the curved surface (πrl\pi r lπrl).
  • Use h for the volume (13πr2h\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h31​πr2h).

  1. Which π?

The question says Use Typical radii
"Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​" (CBSE papers also say: take 227\dfrac{22}{7}722​ if nothing is stated) 227\dfrac{22}{7}722​ 7, 14, 21, 3.5, 10.5…
"Use π = 3.14" 3.14 5, 6, 10…
"in terms of π" keep π as a letter: 144π144\pi144π cm³ any

Use one value of π in one answer, and round only at the very end.

  1. Units, litres and mass

  • 1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m² = 10 000 cm² and 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³.
  • 1 litre = 1000 cm³, and 1 m³ = 1000 litres.
  • Put every length in the same unit before you use a formula.
  • Mass = volume × mass of 1 cm³ (for example 8 g per cm³); then 1000 g = 1 kg.

  1. Working backwards

If you know a volume or an area, put it into the formula and solve for the unknown. For example, a cylinder of radius 7 cm with volume 1540 cm³: 227×49×h=1540\dfrac{22}{7} \times 49 \times h = 1540722​×49×h=1540, so 154h=1540154h = 1540154h=1540 and h=10h = 10h=10 cm.

Worked example 1

Everything about one cone

Question. A cone has base radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. Find its slant height, curved surface area, total surface area and volume. (Use π = 3.14.)

Given: r=5r = 5r=5 cm; h=12h = 12h=12 cm; π = 3.14. Asked: l, CSA, TSA and V.

Concept used: l=r2+h2l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}l=r2+h2​; CSA =πrl= \pi r l=πrl; TSA =πr(l+r)= \pi r(l + r)=πr(l+r); V=13πr2hV = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 hV=31​πr2h. Why it applies: the height, radius and slant height make a right triangle.

  3. 3

    TSA =3.14×5×(13+5)=3.14×90=282.6= 3.14 \times 5 \times (13 + 5) = 3.14 \times 90 = 282.6=3.14×5×(13+5)=3.14×90=282.6 cm².

  4. 4

    Volume =13×3.14×5×5×12=3.14×100=314= \dfrac{1}{3} \times 3.14 \times 5 \times 5 \times 12 = 3.14 \times 100 = 314=31​×3.14×5×5×12=3.14×100=314 cm³.

  5. Check: 132=169=52+12213^2 = 169 = 5^2 + 12^2132=169=52+122 ✔ (so l is right); TSA − CSA must be the base circle: 282.6−204.1=78.5=3.14×25282.6 - 204.1 = 78.5 = 3.14 \times 25282.6−204.1=78.5=3.14×25 ✔.

Worked example 2

A sphere and a hemisphere, in terms of π

Question. A sphere has radius 9 cm. Find (a) its surface area and volume, and (b) the curved surface area, total surface area and volume of a hemisphere of the same radius. Give the answers in terms of π.

Given: r=9r = 9r=9 cm; answers in terms of π. Asked: five values.

Concept used: sphere 4πr24\pi r^24πr2 and 43πr3\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^334​πr3; hemisphere CSA 2πr22\pi r^22πr2, TSA 3πr23\pi r^23πr2, volume 23πr3\dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^332​πr3. Why it applies: a hemisphere is half a sphere plus, for the TSA, its flat circle.

  1. 1

    r2=81r^2 = 81r2=81 and r3=729r^3 = 729r3=729.

  3. 3

    (b) CSA =2×π×81=162π= 2 \times \pi \times 81 = 162\pi=2×π×81=162π cm² (half of 324π324\pi324π).

  4. 4

    TSA =162π+81π=243π= 162\pi + 81\pi = 243\pi=162π+81π=243π cm² (the flat circle πr2=81π\pi r^2 = 81\piπr2=81π added).

Worked example 3

Working backwards from litres

Question. A cylindrical can of radius 7 cm holds 2.31 litres when full. Find its height. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=7r = 7r=7 cm; capacity 2.31 litres. Asked: the height h.

Concept used: V=πr2hV = \pi r^2 hV=πr2h, worked backwards; 1 litre = 1000 cm³. Why it applies: the can is a cylinder, and its capacity is its volume.

  1. 1

    Change litres to cm³: 2.31×1000=23102.31 \times 1000 = 23102.31×1000=2310 cm³.

  2. 2

    Base area: 227×7×7=154\dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = 154722​×7×7=154 cm².

  3. 3

    154×h=2310154 \times h = 2310154×h=2310, so h=2310154=15h = \dfrac{2310}{154} = 15h=1542310​=15 cm.

  4. Check: 154×15=2310154 \times 15 = 2310154×15=2310 cm³ =2.31= 2.31=2.31 litres ✔.

  5. Answer 15 cm

    Common trap: Leaving the capacity as 2.31 — the radius is in cm, so the volume must be in cm³ too. 2.31154\dfrac{2.31}{154}1542.31​ gives a height of 0.015 cm, which no can has.

Worked example 4

From volume to mass

Question. A solid metal rod is a cylinder of radius 3.5 cm and length 20 cm. One cm³ of the metal has a mass of 8.5 g. Find the mass of the rod in kilograms. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=3.5r = 3.5r=3.5 cm; h=20h = 20h=20 cm; 8.5 g per cm³. Asked: the mass in kg.

Concept used: V=πr2hV = \pi r^2 hV=πr2h; mass = volume × mass of 1 cm³; 1000 g = 1 kg. Why it applies: the rod is a solid cylinder.

  1. 1

    V=227×3.5×3.5×20=38.5×20=770V = \dfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 3.5 \times 20 = 38.5 \times 20 = 770V=722​×3.5×3.5×20=38.5×20=770 cm³.

  2. 2

    Mass =770×8.5=6545= 770 \times 8.5 = 6545=770×8.5=6545 g.

  3. 3

    In kilograms: 65451000=6.545\dfrac{6545}{1000} = 6.54510006545​=6.545 kg.

  4. Check: group it another way — base area × (length × mass of 1 cm³): 38.5×(20×8.5)=38.5×170=654538.5 \times (20 \times 8.5) = 38.5 \times 170 = 654538.5×(20×8.5)=38.5×170=6545 g ✔ (a slip in the volume or in the mass would change this product); 6545 g ÷ 1000 = 6.545 kg.

  5. Answer 6.545 kg

    Common trap: Stopping at 6545 and writing "kg" — that is grams.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that mixing up the hemisphere and sphere formulas, mixing up the slant height and the height, and mixing units, litres and values of π are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • "Diameter 14 cm", then 43π×143\dfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 14^334​π×143

    Every formula uses the radius.

    Halve first: r=7r = 7r=7 cm.

  • TSA of a solid hemisphere =2πr2= 2\pi r^2=2πr2 (or 4πr24\pi r^24πr2)

    2πr22\pi r^22πr2 is only the curved part; 4πr24\pi r^24πr2 is a whole sphere.

    Solid hemisphere: 3πr23\pi r^23πr2. Bowl (open): 2πr22\pi r^22πr2.

  • Volume of a hemisphere =43πr3= \dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3=34​πr3

    That is the whole sphere.

    Hemisphere: 23πr3\dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^332​πr3.

  • h in πrl\pi r lπrl, or l in 13πr2h\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h31​πr2h

    The slant side and the height are different lengths.

    Find both first: l=r2+h2l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}l=r2+h2​.

  • An area in cm³, a volume in cm²

    Area is square units, volume cubic units.

    Surfaces: cm², m². Volumes: cm³, m³, litres.

  • cm³ to litres: ÷ 100, or m³ to litres: ÷ 1000

    1 litre = 1000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1000 litres.

    cm³ → litres: ÷ 1000. m³ → litres: × 1000.

  • Surface area (cm²) covers the outside; volume (cm³) fills the inside. CSA is the curved part; TSA adds the flat faces.
  • Cylinder πr2h\pi r^2 hπr2h, 2πrh2\pi rh2πrh; cone 13πr2h\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h31​πr2h, πrl\pi r lπrl with l=r2+h2l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}l=r2+h2​; sphere 43πr3\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^334​πr3, 4πr24\pi r^24πr2; hemisphere 23πr3\dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^332​πr3, CSA 2πr22\pi r^22πr2, TSA 3πr23\pi r^23πr2.
