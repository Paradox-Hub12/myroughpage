Topic 2 of 5

Sectors and arcs

About 50 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Name minor and major sectors and segments and the angle of a sector; derive the arc length and the sector area by the unitary method.
  • Find arc lengths, sector areas, major sectors and perimeters of sectors, and work backwards to r or θ (including area = ½ × l × r).
  • Find the angle of a sector from a situation (n equal sectors, clock hands, quadrants and semicircles).

A round pizza is cut into 8 equal slices. Each slice is 18\dfrac{1}{8}81​ of the pizza — so its area is 18\dfrac{1}{8}81​ of the whole area, and its curved crust is 18\dfrac{1}{8}81​ of the whole crust. That one idea — a slice is a fraction of the circle — is all you need for this topic. The only new step is to read the fraction from the angle at the centre.

  1. Sector, segment and their names

  • A sector is the part of a circle between two radii and the arc joining their ends. The angle between the radii, ∠AOB=θ\angle AOB = \theta∠AOB=θ, is the angle of the sector.
  • Two radii OA and OB cut the circle into two sectors: the smaller is the minor sector (angle θ); the larger is the major sector (angle 360°−θ360° - \theta360°−θ).
  • A segment is the part of a circle between a chord and its arc. The smaller piece is the minor segment, the larger the major segment (Topic 3).
  • When a question just says "sector" or "segment", it means the minor one.

  1. The fraction of the circle (unitary method)

A whole circle is a "sector" of 360°. Its area is πr2\pi r^2πr2 and its arc is the whole circumference 2πr2\pi r2πr.

  • For an angle of 360°: area πr2\pi r^2πr2.
  • For an angle of 1°: area πr2360\dfrac{\pi r^2}{360}360πr2​.
  • For an angle of θ: area θ360×πr2\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2360θ​×πr2.

The arc is found in exactly the same way. So, with θ in degrees:

  1. Reading the angle from a situation

Situation Angle of the sector
Semicircle; quarter circle (quadrant) 180°; 90°
n equal sectors (n spokes, n ribs, n equal slices) 360°n\dfrac{360°}{n}n360°​

Worked example 1

Area and arc of a sector

Question. Find the area and the arc length of a sector of radius 7 cm and angle 72°. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=7r = 7r=7 cm; θ=72°\theta = 72°θ=72°. Asked: the area and the arc length.

Concept used: the sector is θ360\dfrac{\theta}{360}360θ​ of the circle. Why it applies: the sector is bounded by two radii and an arc.

  1. 1

    Fraction of the circle: 72360=15\dfrac{72}{360} = \dfrac{1}{5}36072​=51​.

  2. 2

    Area of the whole circle: 227×7×7=154\dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = 154722​×7×7=154 cm².

  3. 3

    Area of the sector =15×154=30.8= \dfrac{1}{5} \times 154 = 30.8=51​×154=30.8 cm².

  4. 4

    Circumference: 2×227×7=442 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 = 442×722​×7=44 cm.

  5. 5

    Arc length =15×44=8.8= \dfrac{1}{5} \times 44 = 8.8=51​×44=8.8 cm.

  6. Check: area =12×l×r=12×8.8×7=30.8= \dfrac{1}{2} \times l \times r = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 8.8 \times 7 = 30.8=21​×l×r=21​×8.8×7=30.8 cm² ✔.

  7. Answer area 30.8 cm²; arc 8.8 cm

    Common trap: Mixing the two formulas — 15×2πr\dfrac{1}{5} \times 2\pi r51​×2πr is the arc (a length), 15×πr2\dfrac{1}{5} \times \pi r^251​×πr2 the area.

Worked example 2

The perimeter of a sector

Question. A sector of a circle of radius 10.5 cm has angle 120°. Find its perimeter. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=10.5r = 10.5r=10.5 cm; θ=120°\theta = 120°θ=120°. Asked: the perimeter.

Concept used: perimeter of a sector =2r+l= 2r + l=2r+l, with l=θ360×2πrl = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi rl=360θ​×2πr. Why it applies: the boundary is two radii and the arc.

  1. 1

    Arc: l=120360×2×227×10.5l = \dfrac{120}{360} \times 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 10.5l=360120​×2×722​×10.5.

  2. 2

    2×227×10.5=662 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 10.5 = 662×722​×10.5=66, and 13×66=22\dfrac{1}{3} \times 66 = 2231​×66=22 cm.

  3. 3

    Perimeter =2×10.5+22=21+22=43= 2 \times 10.5 + 22 = 21 + 22 = 43=2×10.5+22=21+22=43 cm.

  4. Check: the arc (22 cm) is a third of the circumference (66 cm) ✔, and the perimeter is longer than the arc ✔.

  5. Answer 43 cm

    Common trap: Giving 22 cm — that is the arc only. The two radii are part of the boundary.

  1. 1

    Angle of the major sector: 360°−54°=306°360° - 54° = 306°360°−54°=306°.

  2. 2

    Area of the whole circle: 3.14×20×20=12563.14 \times 20 \times 20 = 12563.14×20×20=1256 cm².

  3. 3

    Area of the major sector =306360×1256=0.85×1256=1067.6= \dfrac{306}{360} \times 1256 = 0.85 \times 1256 = 1067.6=360306​×1256=0.85×1256=1067.6 cm².

  4. Check: minor sector =54360×1256=188.4= \dfrac{54}{360} \times 1256 = 188.4=36054​×1256=188.4 cm², and 188.4+1067.6=1256188.4 + 1067.6 = 1256188.4+1067.6=1256 ✔.

  5. Answer 1067.6 cm²

Worked example 4

Working backwards from the arc

Question. The arc of a sector of a circle of radius 14 cm is 11 cm long. Find the angle of the sector and its area. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=14r = 14r=14 cm; l=11l = 11l=11 cm. Asked: θ and the area.

Concept used: l=θ360×2πrl = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi rl=360θ​×2πr worked backwards; area =12×l×r= \dfrac{1}{2} \times l \times r=21​×l×r. Why it applies: the arc and the radius are known.

  1. 1

    Circumference: 2×227×14=882 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 = 882×722​×14=88 cm.

  2. 2

    θ360×88=11\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 88 = 11360θ​×88=11, so θ=11×36088=45°\theta = \dfrac{11 \times 360}{88} = 45°θ=8811×360​=45°.

  3. 3

    Area =12×l×r=12×11×14=77= \dfrac{1}{2} \times l \times r = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 11 \times 14 = 77=21​×l×r=21​×11×14=77 cm².

  4. Check: with θ = 45°: 45360×227×14×14=18×616=77\dfrac{45}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 14 = \dfrac{1}{8} \times 616 = 7736045​×722​×14×14=81​×616=77 cm² ✔.

  5. Answer θ = 45°; area 77 cm²

Worked example 5

The area swept by a minute hand

Question. The minute hand of a clock is 10.5 cm long. Find the area it sweeps in 20 minutes. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=10.5r = 10.5r=10.5 cm; 20 minutes. Asked: the area swept.

Concept used: the minute hand turns 360° in 60 minutes, so 6° per minute; the area swept is a sector. Why it applies: the hand is a radius turning about the centre.

  1. 1

    Angle: 20×6°=120°20 \times 6° = 120°20×6°=120°.

  2. 2

    Area =120360×227×10.5×10.5= \dfrac{120}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5=360120​×722​×10.5×10.5.

  3. 3

    227×10.5×10.5=346.5\dfrac{22}{7} \times 10.5 \times 10.5 = 346.5722​×10.5×10.5=346.5, and 13×346.5=115.5\dfrac{1}{3} \times 346.5 = 115.531​×346.5=115.5 cm².

  4. Check: 20 minutes is a third of an hour, so the area is a third of the clock face (346.5 cm²) ✔.

  5. Answer 115.5 cm²

    Common trap: Taking the angle as 20° (one degree per minute). The minute hand moves 6° each minute.

Worked example 6

Twelve equal sectors

Question. A circular rangoli of radius 14 cm is divided into 12 equal sectors by 6 diameters made of coloured thread, and the edge is also outlined in thread. Find (a) the area of each sector and (b) the total length of thread. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=14r = 14r=14 cm; 12 equal sectors; 6 diameters. Asked: the area of one sector; the thread length.

Concept used: n equal sectors have angle 360°n\dfrac{360°}{n}n360°​; the thread is the circumference plus the diameters. Why it applies: the 6 diameters pass through the centre and cut the circle into 12 equal parts.

  1. 1

    Angle of each sector: 360°12=30°\dfrac{360°}{12} = 30°12360°​=30°.

  2. 2

    Area of the circle: 227×14×14=616\dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 14 = 616722​×14×14=616 cm².

  3. 3

    (a) Area of each sector =30360×616=61612=1543≈51.33= \dfrac{30}{360} \times 616 = \dfrac{616}{12} = \dfrac{154}{3} \approx 51.33=36030​×616=12616​=3154​≈51.33 cm².

  4. 4

    (b) Circumference =2×227×14=88= 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 = 88=2×722​×14=88 cm; 6 diameters =6×28=168= 6 \times 28 = 168=6×28=168 cm.

  5. 5

    Thread =88+168=256= 88 + 168 = 256=88+168=256 cm.

  6. Check: 12×1543=61612 \times \dfrac{154}{3} = 61612×3154​=616 cm², the whole circle ✔.

  7. Answer (a) 1543≈51.33\dfrac{154}{3} \approx 51.333154​≈51.33 cm² (b) 256 cm

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that mixing up the arc and area formulas, leaving a part out of the perimeter, using θ for a major sector and reading the angle wrongly from a situation are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter.

  • Arc =θ360×πr2= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2=360θ​×πr2, or area =θ360×2πr= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r=360θ​×2πr

    2πr2\pi r2πr is a length; πr2\pi r^2πr2 is an area.

    Arc ↔ circumference 2πr2\pi r2πr; area ↔ πr2\pi r^2πr2.

  • θ180\dfrac{\theta}{180}180θ​ in place of θ360\dfrac{\theta}{360}360θ​

    A full turn is 360°.

    Always θ360\dfrac{\theta}{360}360θ​.

  • Perimeter of a sector = the arc only

    The boundary also has two radii.

    Perimeter =2r+l= 2r + l=2r+l.

  • Major sector with the angle θ

    θ is the minor angle.

    Use 360°−θ360° - \theta360°−θ, or circle − minor sector.

  • Minute hand: 5 minutes = 5°

    The minute hand goes 360° in 60 minutes.

    6° per minute (hour hand 0.5° per minute).

  • n equal sectors: angle 360°n−1\dfrac{360°}{n - 1}n−1360°​ or "n°"

    n equal parts share 360° equally.

    Each angle is 360°n\dfrac{360°}{n}n360°​.
