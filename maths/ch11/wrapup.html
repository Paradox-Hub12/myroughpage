Wrap-up

The formula card, the 60–90–120 triangles, shaded regions and mixed problems

About 30 minutes

Every question in this chapter uses the same few moves: the fraction θ360\dfrac{\theta}{360}360θ​ of the circle (for an arc or for an area), segment = sector − triangle, and build the region from pieces you can name. This page puts the three cards you need on one sheet and then gives four mixed problems in the styles of the sample papers — a sprinkler case that works backwards, a pendant cut off by a chord, a fan-shaped sheet and a running track.

Mixed example 1

Case study: the sprinkler

Question. A lawn sprinkler throws water to a distance of 18 m and turns through an angle of 100°. (Use π=3.14\pi = 3.14π=3.14.)
(i) Find the area of the lawn it waters. (1 mark)
(ii) Find the length of the curved edge of the watered region. (1 mark)
(iii) The sprinkler is moved to a corner where it can reach only 12 m. Through what angle must it turn to water the same area as before? (2 marks)

Given: r=18r = 18r=18 m; θ=100°\theta = 100°θ=100°; new radius 12 m. Asked: the area; the arc; the new angle.

Concept used: area of a sector; arc length; the area formula worked backwards. Why it applies: the watered region is a sector with its centre at the sprinkler.

  1. 1

    (i) Area =100360×3.14×18×18=518×1017.36=282.6= \dfrac{100}{360} \times 3.14 \times 18 \times 18 = \dfrac{5}{18} \times 1017.36 = 282.6=360100​×3.14×18×18=185​×1017.36=282.6 m².

  2. 2

    (ii) Arc =100360×2×3.14×18=518×113.04=31.4= \dfrac{100}{360} \times 2 \times 3.14 \times 18 = \dfrac{5}{18} \times 113.04 = 31.4=360100​×2×3.14×18=185​×113.04=31.4 m.

  3. 3

    (iii) Same area with r=12r = 12r=12: θ360×3.14×144=100360×3.14×324\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 3.14 \times 144 = \dfrac{100}{360} \times 3.14 \times 324360θ​×3.14×144=360100​×3.14×324.

  4. 4

    π and 360 cancel: 144θ=100×324=32 400144\theta = 100 \times 324 = 32\,400144θ=100×324=32400, so θ=225°\theta = 225°θ=225°.

  5. Check: (iii) 225360×3.14×144=0.625×452.16=282.6\dfrac{225}{360} \times 3.14 \times 144 = 0.625 \times 452.16 = 282.6360225​×3.14×144=0.625×452.16=282.6 m² ✔; and area =12×31.4×18=282.6= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 31.4 \times 18 = 282.6=21​×31.4×18=282.6 ✔.

  6. Answer (i) 282.6 m² (ii) 31.4 m (iii) 225°

    Common trap: In (iii), scaling the angle by the radii, 100×1812=150°100 \times \dfrac{18}{12} = 150°100×1218​=150°. Area depends on r2r^2r2, so the angle scales by 182122=94\dfrac{18^2}{12^2} = \dfrac{9}{4}122182​=49​.

Mixed example 2

A pendant cut off by a chord

Question. A silver pendant has the shape of the minor segment cut off from a circle of radius 3 cm by a chord that subtends 120° at the centre. Find the area of the pendant and the length of its edge. (Use π=3.14\pi = 3.14π=3.14 and 3=1.73\sqrt{3} = 1.733​=1.73.)

Given: r=3r = 3r=3 cm; θ=120°\theta = 120°θ=120°. Asked: the area and the perimeter of the segment.

Concept used: segment = sector − triangle; perimeter = chord + arc; for 120°, OM=r2OM = \dfrac{r}{2}OM=2r​ and AB=r3AB = r\sqrt{3}AB=r3​. Why it applies: the pendant is a minor segment.

  1. 1

    Sector =120360×3.14×3×3=13×28.26=9.42= \dfrac{120}{360} \times 3.14 \times 3 \times 3 = \dfrac{1}{3} \times 28.26 = 9.42=360120​×3.14×3×3=31​×28.26=9.42 cm².

  2. 2

    OM=3cos⁡60°=1.5OM = 3 \cos 60° = 1.5OM=3cos60°=1.5 cm; AB=2×3sin⁡60°=33AB = 2 \times 3 \sin 60° = 3\sqrt{3}AB=2×3sin60°=33​ cm.

  3. 3

    Triangle =12×33×1.5=934=9×1.734=3.8925= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 3\sqrt{3} \times 1.5 = \dfrac{9\sqrt{3}}{4} = \dfrac{9 \times 1.73}{4} = 3.8925=21​×33​×1.5=493​​=49×1.73​=3.8925 cm².

  4. 4

    Area =9.42−3.8925=5.5275≈5.53= 9.42 - 3.8925 = 5.5275 \approx 5.53=9.42−3.8925=5.5275≈5.53 cm².

  5. 5

    Arc =13×2×3.14×3=6.28= \dfrac{1}{3} \times 2 \times 3.14 \times 3 = 6.28=31​×2×3.14×3=6.28 cm; chord =3×1.73=5.19= 3 \times 1.73 = 5.19=3×1.73=5.19 cm.

  6. 6

    Perimeter =5.19+6.28=11.47= 5.19 + 6.28 = 11.47=5.19+6.28=11.47 cm.

  7. Check: the arc (6.28 cm) is longer than the chord (5.19 cm) ✔; the segment is less than the sector ✔.

  8. Answer area about 5.53 cm²; edge 11.47 cm

    Common trap: Adding two radii to the edge (as for a sector). The pendant's edge is only the chord and the arc.

Mixed example 3

Case study: a decorative fan

Question. The coloured sheet of a decorative fan lies between two arcs with the same centre, of radii 6 cm and 16 cm, and the fan opens to an angle of 135°. (Use π=3.14\pi = 3.14π=3.14.)
(i) Find the area of the coloured sheet on one face. (2 marks)
(ii) A ribbon is stitched along the whole boundary of the sheet. Find its length. (2 marks)

Given: r=6r = 6r=6 cm; R=16R = 16R=16 cm; θ=135°\theta = 135°θ=135°. Asked: the area of the ring-sector and its boundary.

Concept used: ring-sector area =θ360π(R2−r2)= \dfrac{\theta}{360}\pi(R^2 - r^2)=360θ​π(R2−r2); boundary = two arcs + 2(R−r)2(R - r)2(R−r). Why it applies: the sheet is the part of a sector outside a smaller sector with the same centre and angle.

  1. 1

    (i) 135360=38\dfrac{135}{360} = \dfrac{3}{8}360135​=83​. Area =38×3.14×(162−62)=38×3.14×220=38×690.8=259.05= \dfrac{3}{8} \times 3.14 \times (16^2 - 6^2) = \dfrac{3}{8} \times 3.14 \times 220 = \dfrac{3}{8} \times 690.8 = 259.05=83​×3.14×(162−62)=83​×3.14×220=83​×690.8=259.05 cm².

  2. 2

    (ii) Arcs: 38×2×3.14×(16+6)=38×138.16=51.81\dfrac{3}{8} \times 2 \times 3.14 \times (16 + 6) = \dfrac{3}{8} \times 138.16 = 51.8183​×2×3.14×(16+6)=83​×138.16=51.81 cm.

  3. 3

    Straight edges: 2×(16−6)=202 \times (16 - 6) = 202×(16−6)=20 cm.

  4. 4

    Ribbon =51.81+20=71.81= 51.81 + 20 = 71.81=51.81+20=71.81 cm.

  5. Check: big sector 38×3.14×256=301.44\dfrac{3}{8} \times 3.14 \times 256 = 301.4483​×3.14×256=301.44, small sector 38×3.14×36=42.39\dfrac{3}{8} \times 3.14 \times 36 = 42.3983​×3.14×36=42.39; 301.44−42.39=259.05301.44 - 42.39 = 259.05301.44−42.39=259.05 ✔.

  6. Answer (i) 259.05 cm² (ii) 71.81 cm

    Common trap: In (ii), taking the straight edges as 16 cm each (the outer radius). The sheet starts 6 cm from the centre, so each straight edge is 16−6=1016 - 6 = 1016−6=10 cm.

Mixed example 4

A running track

Question. A running track has two straight parts, each 100 m long, joined by two semicircular ends. The inner edge of each end is a semicircle of radius 21 m, and the track is 7 m wide all the way round. Find (a) the area of the track and (b) the length of its inner edge. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: straights 100 m; inner radius 21 m; width 7 m. Asked: the area of the track; the inner edge.

Concept used: the track = two rectangles (100 m × 7 m) + two half-rings, which together make one whole ring between radii 21 m and 28 m. Why it applies: the two semicircular ends have the same centre line and the same radii.

  1. 1

    Two straight parts: 2×100×7=14002 \times 100 \times 7 = 14002×100×7=1400 m².

  2. 2

    Two half-rings = one ring: 227×(282−212)=227×343=1078\dfrac{22}{7} \times (28^2 - 21^2) = \dfrac{22}{7} \times 343 = 1078722​×(282−212)=722​×343=1078 m².

  3. 3

    (a) Track =1400+1078=2478= 1400 + 1078 = 2478=1400+1078=2478 m².

  4. 4

    (b) Inner edge =2×100+2×227×21=200+132=332= 2 \times 100 + 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 21 = 200 + 132 = 332=2×100+2×722​×21=200+132=332 m.

  5. Check: 282−212=(28−21)(28+21)=7×49=34328^2 - 21^2 = (28 - 21)(28 + 21) = 7 \times 49 = 343282−212=(28−21)(28+21)=7×49=343 ✔.

  6. Answer (a) 2478 m² (b) 332 m

    Common trap: Counting each semicircular end as a whole ring (2156 m²). Two half-rings make one ring.
