Topic 4 of 5

Sectors and segments in real life

About 50 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Model grazing, wiper, lighthouse, sprinkler and clock situations as sectors and find the areas (including the increase when the radius changes).
  • Solve design problems made of equal sectors or segments, including cost at a given rate.
  • Solve multi-part case-study questions on sectors and segments.

A goat tied to a peg can eat every blade of grass within one rope's length — a circle of grass. Put the peg in the corner of a fenced field and the fence cuts the circle down to a quarter. A car wiper, a lighthouse beam, a garden sprinkler and a clock hand all sweep out sectors too. In each case, two questions find the answer: what is the radius? and what is the angle?

  1. The modelling routine

  1. Draw the situation from above and shade the region that is swept, grazed or lit.
  2. Radius: the rope, the blade, the reach of the beam or the jet, the length of the hand.
  3. Angle: read it from the situation — the corner of the field, the angle of the sweep, the time on the clock (6° per minute for the minute hand), or 360°n\dfrac{360°}{n}n360°​ for n equal parts.
  4. Region: sector, segment, or a sum or difference of them.
  5. Calculate with the π the question gives; answer with units.

  1. Grazing at a corner

A peg at a corner of a field lets the animal graze a sector whose angle is the corner's angle, as long as the rope does not reach any other side of the field. For a square or rectangle that means shorter than both sides at that corner; for a triangle it must also be shorter than the height from that corner:

Peg at the corner of Angle Grazed area
a square or rectangle 90° 14πr2\dfrac{1}{4}\pi r^241​πr2 (a quadrant)
an equilateral triangle 60° 16πr2\dfrac{1}{6}\pi r^261​πr2
any triangle, at angle A A A360×πr2\dfrac{A}{360} \times \pi r^2360A​×πr2

The area it cannot graze is the field's area minus the grazed sector. If the rope gets longer, the increase is the difference of two sectors with the same angle: θ360π(R2−r2)\dfrac{\theta}{360}\pi(R^2 - r^2)360θ​π(R2−r2).

  1. Sweeping: wipers, beams, sprinklers

A blade or beam of length r turning through θ sweeps a sector: area θ360πr2\dfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2360θ​πr2. The tip moves along the arc: distance θ360×2πr\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r360θ​×2πr. Two wipers that do not overlap clean twice the area of one.

  1. Designs and cost

Designs are often made of equal sectors or segments: find one, then multiply by how many there are. When the corners of a square or a regular hexagon touch the circle, the pieces outside it are equal segments of 90° or 60°. For a cost: cost = area × rate (₹ per cm²), with the area in the same unit as the rate.

Worked example 1

The goat in the corner

Question. A goat is tied to a peg at one corner of a rectangular field 30 m long and 20 m wide, with a rope 7 m long. Find (a) the area it can graze and (b) the area of the field it cannot graze. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: field 30 m × 20 m; rope 7 m at a corner. Asked: the grazed and ungrazed areas.

Concept used: a peg at the corner of a rectangle gives a sector of 90° (a quadrant). Why it applies: the rope (7 m) is shorter than both sides, so the fences stop the goat only along the two sides at that corner.

  1. 1

    Angle of the sector = the angle of the corner = 90°.

  2. 2

    (a) Grazed area =90360×227×7×7=14×154=38.5= \dfrac{90}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = \dfrac{1}{4} \times 154 = 38.5=36090​×722​×7×7=41​×154=38.5 m².

  3. 3

    Area of the field =30×20=600= 30 \times 20 = 600=30×20=600 m².

  4. 4

    (b) Not grazed =600−38.5=561.5= 600 - 38.5 = 561.5=600−38.5=561.5 m².

  5. Check: 38.5+561.5=60038.5 + 561.5 = 60038.5+561.5=600 ✔; a quarter-circle of radius 7 m easily fits in the corner of a 30 m × 20 m field ✔.

  6. Answer (a) 38.5 m² (b) 561.5 m²

    Common trap: Taking the whole circle, 154154154 m². The fences meet at the corner at 90°, so the goat reaches only a quarter of the circle.

Worked example 2

A longer rope

Question. A calf is tied at a corner of a square field of side 25 m with a rope 7 m long. The rope is then replaced by one 14 m long. Find the increase in the area the calf can graze. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: square of side 25 m; rope 7 m, then 14 m. Asked: the increase in the grazing area.

Concept used: both grazed regions are quadrants; the increase is the difference: 90360π(R2−r2)\dfrac{90}{360}\pi(R^2 - r^2)36090​π(R2−r2). Why it applies: both ropes are shorter than the side, 25 m.

  1. 1

    Increase =14×227×(142−72)= \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times (14^2 - 7^2)=41​×722​×(142−72).

  2. 2

    142−72=196−49=14714^2 - 7^2 = 196 - 49 = 147142−72=196−49=147.

  3. 3

    14×227×147=14×462=115.5\dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 147 = \dfrac{1}{4} \times 462 = 115.541​×722​×147=41​×462=115.5 m².

  4. Check: new quadrant 14×616=154\dfrac{1}{4} \times 616 = 15441​×616=154 m², old 38.538.538.5 m²; 154−38.5=115.5154 - 38.5 = 115.5154−38.5=115.5 ✔.

  5. Answer 115.5 m²

    Common trap: 14π(14−7)2=38.5\dfrac{1}{4}\pi (14 - 7)^2 = 38.541​π(14−7)2=38.5 m². Square each radius first, then subtract.

Worked example 3

A long wiper

Question. The single wiper of a bus windscreen has a blade 42 cm long and sweeps through 150°. Find the area it cleans in one sweep. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=42r = 42r=42 cm; θ=150°\theta = 150°θ=150°. Asked: the area cleaned.

Concept used: a blade turning about one end sweeps a sector. Why it applies: the blade is a radius turning through 150°.

  1. 1

    Area =150360×227×42×42= \dfrac{150}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 42 \times 42=360150​×722​×42×42.

  2. 2

    227×42×42=5544\dfrac{22}{7} \times 42 \times 42 = 5544722​×42×42=5544; 150360=512\dfrac{150}{360} = \dfrac{5}{12}360150​=125​.

  3. 3

    512×5544=2310\dfrac{5}{12} \times 5544 = 2310125​×5544=2310 cm².

  4. Check: 150° is a little less than half a turn, and 2310 is a little less than half of 5544 (2772) ✔.

  5. Answer 2310 cm²

    Common trap: Using 150180\dfrac{150}{180}180150​ — a sector's fraction always has 360 below.

Worked example 4

A garden sprinkler

Question. A sprinkler throws water up to 14 m and turns to and fro through 135°. Find the area of lawn it waters. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=14r = 14r=14 m; θ=135°\theta = 135°θ=135°. Asked: the watered area.

Concept used: the watered region is a sector. Why it applies: every point it waters is within 14 m and within the 135° turn.

  1. 1

    Fraction: 135360=38\dfrac{135}{360} = \dfrac{3}{8}360135​=83​.

  2. 2

    Circle: 227×14×14=616\dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 14 = 616722​×14×14=616 m².

  3. 3

    Watered area =38×616=231= \dfrac{3}{8} \times 616 = 231=83​×616=231 m².

  4. Check: area =12×l×r= \dfrac{1}{2} \times l \times r=21​×l×r: l=38×88=33l = \dfrac{3}{8} \times 88 = 33l=83​×88=33 m, and 12×33×14=231\dfrac{1}{2} \times 33 \times 14 = 23121​×33×14=231 m² ✔.

  5. Answer 231 m²

    Common trap: Using the arc (33 m) as the answer — the question asks for an area (m²).

Worked example 5

The clock between two times

Question. The minute hand of a clock is 7 cm long. Find the area it sweeps between 8:15 a.m. and 8:40 a.m., and the distance moved by its tip. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=7r = 7r=7 cm; from 8:15 to 8:40. Asked: the area swept and the distance moved by the tip.

Concept used: 6° per minute; area of a sector and length of an arc. Why it applies: the hand turns about the centre of the clock.

  1. 1

    Time: 40−15=2540 - 15 = 2540−15=25 minutes. Angle =25×6°=150°= 25 \times 6° = 150°=25×6°=150°.

  2. 2

    Area =150360×227×7×7=512×154=3856≈64.17= \dfrac{150}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = \dfrac{5}{12} \times 154 = \dfrac{385}{6} \approx 64.17=360150​×722​×7×7=125​×154=6385​≈64.17 cm².

  3. 3

    Tip: arc =512×2×227×7=512×44=553≈18.33= \dfrac{5}{12} \times 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 = \dfrac{5}{12} \times 44 = \dfrac{55}{3} \approx 18.33=125​×2×722​×7=125​×44=355​≈18.33 cm.

  4. Check: 12×l×r=12×553×7=3856\dfrac{1}{2} \times l \times r = \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{55}{3} \times 7 = \dfrac{385}{6}21​×l×r=21​×355​×7=6385​ ✔.

  5. Answer area 3856≈64.17\dfrac{385}{6} \approx 64.176385​≈64.17 cm²; tip 553≈18.33\dfrac{55}{3} \approx 18.33355​≈18.33 cm

    Common trap: Using the clock times as an angle (40° − 15° = 25°). Turn minutes into degrees: × 6.

Worked example 6

A badge design and its cost

Question. A circular badge of radius 14 cm has a square drawn inside it with all four corners on the edge. The four pieces of the badge outside the square are painted gold at ₹2 per cm². Find the cost of painting. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=14r = 14r=14 cm; a square with its corners on the circle; rate ₹2 per cm². Asked: the cost.

Concept used: each side of the square is a chord that subtends 360°4=90°\dfrac{360°}{4} = 90°4360°​=90° at the centre, so each painted piece is a 90° segment. Why it applies: the four sides are equal chords, so they subtend equal angles, which together make 360°.

  1. 1

    One segment: sector 14×227×14×14=154\dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 14 = 15441​×722​×14×14=154 cm²; triangle 12×14×14=98\dfrac{1}{2} \times 14 \times 14 = 9821​×14×14=98 cm²; segment =154−98=56= 154 - 98 = 56=154−98=56 cm².

  2. 2

    Four segments: 4×56=2244 \times 56 = 2244×56=224 cm².

  3. 3

    Cost =224×2=448= 224 \times 2 = 448=224×2=448, that is ₹448.

  4. Check: circle − square: 616−4×98=616−392=224616 - 4 \times 98 = 616 - 392 = 224616−4×98=616−392=224 cm² ✔ (the square is made of four triangles OAB).

  5. Answer ₹448

    Common trap: Painting only one segment, or multiplying the circle's area (616 cm²) by the rate. Find the painted area first, then multiply.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that reading the angle wrongly from the situation and mixing values of π or units are among the most common reasons for lost marks in these word problems.

  • Whole circle (or a semicircle) for a peg at the corner of a field

    The fences meet at the corner's angle.

    Angle of the sector = angle of the corner (90° for a square or rectangle).

  • Increase in grazing area =14π(R−r)2= \dfrac{1}{4}\pi(R - r)^2=41​π(R−r)2

    Areas grow with the square of the radius.

    14π(R2−r2)\dfrac{1}{4}\pi(R^2 - r^2)41​π(R2−r2).

  • 25 minutes = 25°

    The minute hand moves 6° per minute.

    25×6°=150°25 \times 6° = 150°25×6°=150°.

  • n ribs give 360°n−1\dfrac{360°}{n - 1}n−1360°​

    Round a circle, n ribs make n gaps.

    360°n\dfrac{360°}{n}n360°​.

  • Two wipers: the area of one

    Two blades that do not overlap clean two sectors.

    Multiply by 2.

  • Cost = circle area × rate

    Only the painted or covered part is paid for.

    Painted area first, then × rate.

Key points

  • Model first: radius = rope, blade, beam, jet or hand; angle = corner, sweep, time (6° per minute) or 360°n\dfrac{360°}{n}n360°​.
  • Peg at the corner of a square or rectangle → quadrant, 14πr2\dfrac{1}{4}\pi r^241​πr2; a longer rope → increase 14π(R2−r2)\dfrac{1}{4}\pi(R^2 - r^2)41​π(R2−r2).
  • Area swept: θ360πr2\dfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2360θ​πr2; distance moved by the tip: θ360×2πr\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r360θ​×2πr.
  • Equal pieces: find one, then multiply. A square in a circle → four 90° segments; a regular hexagon → six 60° segments.
  • Cost = area × rate.
