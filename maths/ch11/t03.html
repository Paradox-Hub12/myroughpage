Topic 3 of 5

Segments (central angle 60°, 90°, 120°)

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find the area of a minor segment as sector − triangle, with the triangle OAB for 60°, 90° and 120°.
  • Find the area of a major segment, the length of the chord and the perimeter of a segment.
  • Give answers exactly (in terms of π and √3) or with the stated values of π and √3, and choose minor or major correctly.

Slice the top off a round melon with one straight cut. The piece that comes away is a segment — a chord and an arc, with no point at the centre. You cannot find its area with one formula, but you can build it: take the sector that reaches the centre, then remove the triangle that you did not want. That is the whole method — and the syllabus keeps the triangle easy: the angle at the centre is always 60°, 90° or 120°.

  1. Segment = sector − triangle

Let the chord AB of a circle with centre O and radius r make ∠AOB=θ\angle AOB = \theta∠AOB=θ. The sector OAB is made of two pieces: the triangle OAB and the minor segment. So

The half-angles are 30°, 45° and 60°. At 60°, △OAB is equilateral (two equal sides with 60° between them). At 90°, OA ⟂ OB, so the two radii are the base and the height: 12r2\dfrac{1}{2}r^221​r2. At 120°: 12×r3×r2=34r2\dfrac{1}{2} \times r\sqrt{3} \times \dfrac{r}{2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}r^221​×r3​×2r​=43​​r2.

Notice: the 60° and 120° triangles have the same area, 34r2\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}r^243​​r2, but different shapes. You do not need to learn the table — draw OM and use sin and cos of the half-angle each time.

  1. Major segment

The chord cuts the whole circle into the minor and the major segment:

major segment=πr2−minor segment\text{major segment} = \pi r^2 - \text{minor segment}major segment=πr2−minor segmentSwipe to see the whole equation →

(Another way: major sector + triangle OAB — the triangle sits inside the major segment.)

  1. Perimeter of a segment

The boundary of a segment is the chord and the arc: perimeter =AB+l= AB + l=AB+l. The chord comes from the table: r, r2r\sqrt{2}r2​ or r3r\sqrt{3}r3​.

  1. Exact or numerical?

  • "In terms of π" (and 3\sqrt{3}3​): keep both as symbols, e.g. (12π−93)(12\pi - 9\sqrt{3})(12π−93​) cm².
  • "Use π = 3.14 and 3=1.73\sqrt{3} = 1.733​=1.73": work exactly as far as you can and put the values in at the end. Round only the final answer.
Optional — not for the exam

The same method works for any angle at the centre. In the 2026-27 syllabus, segment questions use only 60°, 90° and 120°, so the triangle is always one of the three above.

Worked examples

Worked example 1

A 90° segment

Question. A chord of a circle of radius 14 cm subtends a right angle at the centre. Find the area of the minor segment. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=14r = 14r=14 cm; θ=90°\theta = 90°θ=90°. Asked: the area of the minor segment.

Concept used: segment = sector − triangle; at 90° the triangle's base and height are the two radii. Why it applies: OA ⟂ OB.

  1. 1

    Sector: 90360×227×14×14=14×616=154\dfrac{90}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 14 = \dfrac{1}{4} \times 616 = 15436090​×722​×14×14=41​×616=154 cm².

  2. 2

    Triangle OAB: 12×14×14=98\dfrac{1}{2} \times 14 \times 14 = 9821​×14×14=98 cm².

  3. 3

    Segment =154−98=56= 154 - 98 = 56=154−98=56 cm².

  4. Check: the segment is smaller than the sector and positive ✔; 56+98=15456 + 98 = 15456+98=154 ✔.

  5. Answer 56 cm²

    Common trap: Giving 154 cm² — that is the sector. The triangle must be taken away.

Worked example 2

A 60° segment

Question. A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends an angle of 60° at the centre. Find the area of the minor segment. (Use π=3.14\pi = 3.14π=3.14 and 3=1.73\sqrt{3} = 1.733​=1.73.)

Given: r=10r = 10r=10 cm; θ=60°\theta = 60°θ=60°. Asked: the area of the minor segment.

Concept used: segment = sector − triangle; at 60° the triangle is equilateral. Why it applies: OA = OB and the angle between them is 60°, so the other two angles are also 60°.

  1. 1

    Sector: 60360×3.14×10×10=3146=1573≈52.33\dfrac{60}{360} \times 3.14 \times 10 \times 10 = \dfrac{314}{6} = \dfrac{157}{3} \approx 52.3336060​×3.14×10×10=6314​=3157​≈52.33 cm².

  2. 2

    △OAB is equilateral with side 10 cm: area =34×102=253=25×1.73=43.25= \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 10^2 = 25\sqrt{3} = 25 \times 1.73 = 43.25=43​​×102=253​=25×1.73=43.25 cm².

  3. 3

    Segment =1573−43.25≈9.08= \dfrac{157}{3} - 43.25 \approx 9.08=3157​−43.25≈9.08 cm².

  4. Check: sector ≈ 52.33 and 52.33−43.25=9.0852.33 - 43.25 = 9.0852.33−43.25=9.08 ✔; a 60° segment is a thin sliver, so a small answer is sensible ✔.

  5. Answer about 9.08 cm²

    Common trap: Using 12×10×10=50\dfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 10 = 5021​×10×10=50 cm² for the triangle. That is the 90° triangle; at 60° the height is less than the radius.

Worked example 3

A 120° segment (draw OM)

Question. A chord AB of a circle with centre O and radius 18 cm subtends an angle of 120° at the centre. Find the area of the minor segment. (Use π=3.14\pi = 3.14π=3.14 and 3=1.73\sqrt{3} = 1.733​=1.73.)

Given: r=18r = 18r=18 cm; ∠AOB=120°\angle AOB = 120°∠AOB=120°. Asked: the area of the minor segment.

Concept used: segment = sector − triangle; for the triangle, draw OM⊥ABOM \perp ABOM⊥AB, which halves the angle and the chord. Why it applies: △OAB is isosceles (OA = OB).

  1. 1

    Sector: 120360×3.14×18×18=13×1017.36=339.12\dfrac{120}{360} \times 3.14 \times 18 \times 18 = \dfrac{1}{3} \times 1017.36 = 339.12360120​×3.14×18×18=31​×1017.36=339.12 cm².

  2. 2

    Draw OM⊥ABOM \perp ABOM⊥AB. Then ∠AOM=60°\angle AOM = 60°∠AOM=60° and AM=MBAM = MBAM=MB.

  3. 3

    In right △OMA: OM=18cos⁡60°=18×12=9OM = 18 \cos 60° = 18 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = 9OM=18cos60°=18×21​=9 cm.

  4. 4

    AM=18sin⁡60°=18×32=93AM = 18 \sin 60° = 18 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 9\sqrt{3}AM=18sin60°=18×23​​=93​ cm, so AB=2×93=183AB = 2 \times 9\sqrt{3} = 18\sqrt{3}AB=2×93​=183​ cm.

  5. 5

    Triangle: 12×183×9=813=81×1.73=140.13\dfrac{1}{2} \times 18\sqrt{3} \times 9 = 81\sqrt{3} = 81 \times 1.73 = 140.1321​×183​×9=813​=81×1.73=140.13 cm².

  6. 6

    Segment =339.12−140.13=198.99= 339.12 - 140.13 = 198.99=339.12−140.13=198.99 cm².

  7. Check: 34×182=813\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 18^2 = 81\sqrt{3}43​​×182=813​ — the same triangle area ✔.

  8. Answer 198.99 cm²

    Common trap: Taking OM as the radius (18 cm). OM is the height of the triangle, rcos⁡60°=9r \cos 60° = 9rcos60°=9 cm.

Worked example 4

The major segment

Question. A chord of a circle of radius 7 cm subtends an angle of 90° at the centre. Find the areas of the minor and the major segments. (Use π=227\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}π=722​.)

Given: r=7r = 7r=7 cm; θ=90°\theta = 90°θ=90°. Asked: both segments.

Concept used: minor segment = sector − triangle; major segment = circle − minor segment. Why it applies: the chord cuts the circle into exactly these two pieces.

  1. 1

    Circle: 227×7×7=154\dfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = 154722​×7×7=154 cm².

  2. 2

    Sector: 14×154=38.5\dfrac{1}{4} \times 154 = 38.541​×154=38.5 cm²; triangle: 12×7×7=24.5\dfrac{1}{2} \times 7 \times 7 = 24.521​×7×7=24.5 cm².

  3. 3

    Minor segment =38.5−24.5=14= 38.5 - 24.5 = 14=38.5−24.5=14 cm².

  4. 4

    Major segment =154−14=140= 154 - 14 = 140=154−14=140 cm².

  5. Check (another way): major sector =270360×154=115.5= \dfrac{270}{360} \times 154 = 115.5=360270​×154=115.5 cm², and major sector + triangle =115.5+24.5=140= 115.5 + 24.5 = 140=115.5+24.5=140 cm² ✔.

  6. Answer minor segment 14 cm²; major segment 140 cm²

    Common trap: "Major segment = major sector − triangle" =115.5−24.5=91= 115.5 - 24.5 = 91=115.5−24.5=91 cm². The triangle is inside the major segment, so it is added, not taken away.

Worked example 5

The perimeter of a segment

Question. Find the perimeter of the minor segment cut off by a chord that subtends 60° at the centre of a circle of radius 9 cm. (Use π=3.14\pi = 3.14π=3.14.)

Given: r=9r = 9r=9 cm; θ=60°\theta = 60°θ=60°. Asked: the perimeter of the minor segment.

Concept used: perimeter of a segment = chord + arc. Why it applies: the segment is bounded by the chord AB and the arc AB.

  1. 1

    △OAB is equilateral (OA = OB and the angle between them is 60°), so the chord AB=9AB = 9AB=9 cm.

  2. 2

    Arc: 60360×2×3.14×9=16×56.52=9.42\dfrac{60}{360} \times 2 \times 3.14 \times 9 = \dfrac{1}{6} \times 56.52 = 9.4236060​×2×3.14×9=61​×56.52=9.42 cm.

  3. 3

    Perimeter =9+9.42=18.42= 9 + 9.42 = 18.42=9+9.42=18.42 cm.

  4. Check: the arc (9.42 cm) is a little longer than the chord (9 cm) — a curve is longer than the straight line between its ends ✔.

  5. Answer 18.42 cm

    Common trap: Adding the two radii (as for a sector): 9.42+18=27.429.42 + 18 = 27.429.42+18=27.42 cm. A segment has no radii in its boundary — only the chord.

Worked example 6

An exact answer

Question. Find, in terms of π, the area of the minor segment of a circle of radius 6 cm whose chord subtends 120° at the centre.

Given: r=6r = 6r=6 cm; θ=120°\theta = 120°θ=120°; exact answer. Asked: the area of the minor segment.

Concept used: segment = sector − triangle, keeping π and 3\sqrt{3}3​ as symbols. Why it applies: the question asks "in terms of π".

  1. 1

    Sector: 120360×π×6×6=13×36π=12π\dfrac{120}{360} \times \pi \times 6 \times 6 = \dfrac{1}{3} \times 36\pi = 12\pi360120​×π×6×6=31​×36π=12π cm².

  2. 2

    Triangle (OM ⟂ AB): OM=6cos⁡60°=3OM = 6 \cos 60° = 3OM=6cos60°=3 cm; AB=2×6sin⁡60°=63AB = 2 \times 6 \sin 60° = 6\sqrt{3}AB=2×6sin60°=63​ cm; area =12×63×3=93= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 6\sqrt{3} \times 3 = 9\sqrt{3}=21​×63​×3=93​ cm².

  3. 3

    Segment =(12π−93)= (12\pi - 9\sqrt{3})=(12π−93​) cm².

  4. Check: with π ≈ 3.14 and 3≈1.73\sqrt{3} \approx 1.733​≈1.73: 37.68−15.57=22.1137.68 - 15.57 = 22.1137.68−15.57=22.11 cm², a positive area smaller than the sector (37.68 cm²) ✔.

  5. Answer (12π−93)(12\pi - 9\sqrt{3})(12π−93​) cm²

    Common trap: Writing 3π33\pi\sqrt{3}3π3​ or "3π3\pi3π". 12π12\pi12π and 939\sqrt{3}93​ are unlike terms: they cannot be combined.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that segment slips (no subtraction, or the wrong triangle) and confusing major and minor are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter, together with leaving the chord out of a perimeter.

  • Segment area = sector area

    The sector also contains the triangle OAB.

    Segment = sector − triangle.

  • 12r2\dfrac{1}{2}r^221​r2 for the triangle at 60° or 120°

    12r2\dfrac{1}{2}r^221​r2 is right only when OA ⟂ OB (90°).

    Draw OM ⟂ AB: OM =rcos⁡θ2= r \cos \dfrac{\theta}{2}=rcos2θ​, AB =2rsin⁡θ2= 2r \sin \dfrac{\theta}{2}=2rsin2θ​.

  • Major segment = major sector − triangle

    The triangle is inside the major segment.

    Major segment = circle − minor segment (or major sector + triangle).

  • Perimeter of a segment = arc + 2r

    A segment's boundary is the chord and the arc.

    Perimeter = AB + arc.

  • 12π−9312\pi - 9\sqrt{3}12π−93​ "simplified" to 3π33\pi\sqrt{3}3π3​

    Terms with π and with 3\sqrt{3}3​ are unlike terms.

    Leave the answer as (12π−93)(12\pi - 9\sqrt{3})(12π−93​) cm².

  • 3\sqrt{3}3​ replaced by 1.73 in the first line

    Errors grow when you round early.

    Keep 3\sqrt{3}3​ until the last line.

  • Minor segment =θ360πr2−= \dfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2 -=360θ​πr2− area of △OAB.
