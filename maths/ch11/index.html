Chapter 11 · Unit VI

Areas Related to Circles

5 topicsAbout 8 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

A slice of pizza, the strip of glass a wiper cleans, the patch of grass a tethered goat can reach, the sea lit by a lighthouse beam — each is a sector of a circle. Cut a roti with one straight line and the small piece is a segment. This chapter gives you two formulas — for the arc and the area of a sector — and one subtraction, segment = sector − triangle. With them, and the circle and triangle areas you already know, you can find the area and the edge of almost any curved shape in real life and in the exam.

In the board exam this chapter is part of Unit VI, Mensuration (10 marks together with Chapter 12, Surface Areas and Volumes), with the same content in Mathematics Standard and Mathematics Basic. In the four sample papers for 2025-26 and 2026-27 it carries 4 to 5 marks: 1-mark MCQs (a sector's area from its arc, a wheel's revolutions, ratios of areas), a 2-mark sector or segment question, and in both Basic papers a 4-mark case study (a paper fan, a water sprinkler). The syllabus limits segment questions to central angles of 60°, 90° and 120° — every segment in this chapter uses one of these.

Not in the syllabus: NCERT's former section on long "areas of combinations of plane figures" (removed from the book). Topic 5 teaches only the regions the sample papers ask: shapes built from circles, sectors and segments together with a triangle, square or rectangle.
