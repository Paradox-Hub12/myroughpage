13 questions from official CBSE sources. Each answer has two views: Understand it (every step) and Write it in the exam (what earns the marks).

This section currently holds the Probability items from the CBSE Mathematics Sample Question Papers 2026-27 and 2025-26 — Mathematics Standard (041) and Mathematics Basic (241) — answered with their official marking schemes (newest first). Past year board questions, and the "Exam focus" analysis, will be added once the papers are sourced.

Both 2026-27 sample papers state that there is no change in the question paper design and assessment pattern for 2026-27, so the 2025-26 items remain good practice. Standard and Basic share the same syllabus, so every student should try all thirteen items. This chapter is part of Unit VII, Statistics and Probability (11 marks, shared with Statistics). It carries 5 marks in the 2026-27 Standard paper (three MCQs and a 2-mark question), 6 marks in the 2026-27 Basic paper (an MCQ, an assertion–reason item and a 4-mark case study), 5 marks in the 2025-26 Standard paper (two MCQs and a 3-mark question) and 6 marks in the 2025-26 Basic paper (an MCQ, an assertion–reason item and a 4-mark case study). None of these items has an alternative for visually impaired candidates. In every card question, the face cards are the kings, queens and jacks; aces are not face cards.

Each item has two parts:

  • Understand it — the full step-by-step explanation: Given / Asked → Concept used (and why) → steps → Check → Common trap.
  • Write it in the exam — the answer as you should write it, following the official marking scheme, with marks shown.

Question: T-shirts marked with numbers 4 to 99 are placed in a box. Gunika is fond of numbers. She randomly takes out a T-shirt from this box. The probability that she gets a T-shirt marked with a number that is either a perfect square or a perfect cube is:

896\dfrac{8}{96}968​ — the squares only.
396\dfrac{3}{96}963​ — the cubes only.
1196\dfrac{11}{96}9611​ — 64 counted twice.
1096=548\dfrac{10}{96} = \dfrac{5}{48}9610​=485​.

Given: numbers 4 to 99. Asked: P(square or cube).

Concept used: total =99−4+1= 99 - 4 + 1=99−4+1; A or B = A + B − both (Topics 1, 4). Why it applies: 64 is both a square and a cube.

  1. 1

    All outcomes: 99−4+1=9699 - 4 + 1 = 9699−4+1=96.

  2. 2

    Squares: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81 → 8. Cubes: 8, 27, 64 → 3. Both: 64 → 1.

  3. 3

    Favourable: 8+3−1=108 + 3 - 1 = 108+3−1=10. P=1096=548P = \dfrac{10}{96} = \dfrac{5}{48}P=9610​=485​.

    Reasoning for each option:

    • (A) ✘ 896\dfrac{8}{96}968​ — the squares only.
    • (B) ✘ 396\dfrac{3}{96}963​ — the cubes only.
    • (C) ✘ 1196\dfrac{11}{96}9611​ — 64 counted twice.
    • (D) ✔ 1096=548\dfrac{10}{96} = \dfrac{5}{48}9610​=485​.

  4. Check: list them: 4, 8, 9, 16, 25, 27, 36, 49, 64, 81 — 10 numbers ✔.

    Common trap: Including 1 (a square and a cube) — the T-shirts start at 4.
Total outcomes = 96. Perfect squares: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81; perfect cubes: 8, 27, 64; common: 64. Favourable outcomes = 10. P =1096=548= \dfrac{10}{96} = \dfrac{5}{48}=9610​=485​. Answer: (D)

REASON (R): The probability of an impossible event is zero (0)
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A). (B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A). (C) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false. (D) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
(The paper prints stray labels "Q1." and "Q2." in front of the assertion and the reason; they are left out here.)

Both true; R explains A.
R is the reason for A.
R is true.
A is true.

Given: a die; the number 8. Asked: whether A and R are true, and whether R explains A.

Concept used: an impossible event has probability 0 (Topic 3). Why it applies: no face of a die shows 8.

  1. 1

    A: the faces are 1 to 6, so 8 cannot come up: P=06=0P = \dfrac{0}{6} = 0P=60​=0 — true.

  2. 2

    R: true, and it is exactly the reason for A (getting 8 is an impossible event).

    Reasoning for each option:

    • (A) ✔ Both true; R explains A.
    • (B) ✘ R is the reason for A.
    • (C) ✘ R is true.
    • (D) ✘ A is true.

  3. Check: the sure event "a number less than 7" has probability 66=1\dfrac{6}{6} = 166​=1; "8" has none of the 6 outcomes ✔.

    Common trap: Choosing (B) because R "sounds general" — a general rule can still be the explanation.
P(getting 8 on rolling a die) =06=0= \dfrac{0}{6} = 0=60​=0; this is an impossible event, as the numbers on the faces of a die are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. Answer: (A)
