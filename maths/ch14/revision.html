The whole chapter on one page

The idea — for an experiment whose outcomes are equally likely (fair coin, fair die, well-shuffled cards, "at random"), the theoretical (classical) probability of an event E is P(E)=number of outcomes favourable to Enumber of all possible outcomesP(E) = \dfrac{\text{number of outcomes favourable to } E}{\text{number of all possible outcomes}}P(E)=number of all possible outcomesnumber of outcomes favourable to E​. Count objects (balls, cards, faces, letters), not kinds (colours). This chapter uses theoretical probability only; experimental probability is not in the 2026-27 syllabus, and past results never change the probability.

The rules — 0≤P(E)≤10 \le P(E) \le 10≤P(E)≤1; impossible event 0; sure event 1. The probabilities of all the elementary events (single outcomes) add to 1. P(Eˉ)=1−P(E)P(\bar{E}) = 1 - P(E)P(Eˉ)=1−P(E). Favourable = P(E)×P(E) \timesP(E)× all; for an unknown, write PPP with the unknown and solve. Answers in lowest terms.

The words — at least k / at most k include k; more than / less than / between leave the ends out; not E = all − E; A or B = A + B − both; neither A nor B = all − (A or B); at least one = 1 − none.

The sample spaces — coin 2; two coins 4 (HH, HT, TH, TT); three coins 8 (1 + 3 + 3 + 1 by heads; HHT, HTH, THH are different); die 6; two dice or one die twice 36 ordered pairs ((1, 4) ≠ (4, 1); doubles count once; sums 2–12 are not equally likely); deck 52 (4 suits × 13; 26 red, 26 black; face cards J, Q, K = 12; aces are not face cards); numbers a to b: b−a+1b - a + 1b−a+1; 1 is not a prime; a year: 52 weeks + 1 day (ordinary, P(53 Sundays)=17P(53 \text{ Sundays}) = \dfrac{1}{7}P(53 Sundays)=71​) or + 2 days (leap, 27\dfrac{2}{7}72​). Cards removed or an object not put back: new total, new counts.
