5 questions, about 20 minutes

Focus: mixed questions in exam style.

M1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

Two dice are thrown. Find the probability that the product of the numbers is a multiple of 3.

Answer: The product is a multiple of 3 when at least one die shows 3 or 6. Neither: 4×4=164 \times 4 = 164×4=16 pairs. P=1−1636=2036=59P = 1 - \dfrac{16}{36} = \dfrac{20}{36} = \dfrac{5}{9}P=1−3616​=3620​=95​. (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

M1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

All the 10s, jacks and queens are removed from a deck of 52 cards (face cards are kings, queens and jacks; aces are not face cards). A card is drawn at random from the rest. Find the probability that it is a red ace.

Answer: Removed 4×3=124 \times 3 = 124×3=12; left 40. Red aces: 2. P=240=120P = \dfrac{2}{40} = \dfrac{1}{20}P=402​=201​. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

M1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

A number is chosen at random from 1 to 63. Find the probability that it is a multiple of 7 or of 9.

Answer: Multiples of 7: 9; of 9: 7; of 63 (both): 1. 9+7−1=159 + 7 - 1 = 159+7−1=15; 1563=521\dfrac{15}{63} = \dfrac{5}{21}6315​=215​. (Topic 4)

Answer: 0.24×50=120.24 \times 50 = 120.24×50=12. (Topic 3)

Answer: "Between 1 and 4" leaves out 1 and 4: only 2 and 3 are favourable. P=26=13P = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}P=62​=31​. Ravi counted 1, 2, 3, 4 ("from 1 to 4"). (Topics 1 and 2)
