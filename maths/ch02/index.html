Chapter 2 · Unit II

Polynomials

5 topicsAbout 6–7 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Throw a ball, aim a fountain jet or look at the arch of a bridge: each traces a curve called a parabola, the graph of a quadratic polynomial. The questions engineers ask about such curves — where does it meet the ground? how wide is it? how high? — come down to finding the zeroes of a polynomial. This chapter shows you how to see zeroes on a graph, how to find them by factorising, and a surprising shortcut: the zeroes of ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c always add up to −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​ and multiply to ca\dfrac{c}{a}ac​, so you can learn a lot about them without ever finding them.

In the board exam this chapter is part of Unit II, Algebra (20 marks, shared with Pair of Linear Equations, Quadratic Equations and Arithmetic Progressions), in both Mathematics Standard and Mathematics Basic. In the 2026-27 sample papers it supplies two 1-mark items and a 3-mark question in Standard (about 5 marks), and two 1-mark items and a 2-mark question in Basic.

What you'll learn

  • Substitute values (including negatives and fractions), factorise a quadratic by splitting the middle term (including fraction and surd coefficients), and use (a±b)2(a \pm b)^2(a±b)2 and a2−b2a^2 - b^2a2−b2. Take the 5-question skip-check first — score 5 out of 5 and go straight to Topic 2.
  • Name the degree and type of a polynomial; find p(k)p(k)p(k) and decide whether k is a zero; find the zero of a linear polynomial, −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​.
  • Read the zeroes where a graph meets the x-axis; count them for linear, quadratic and cubic graphs (a touch counts once); link the shape of a parabola to the sign of a and to two, one or no zeroes. Includes an interactive graph explorer.
  • Find the zeroes of any factorisable quadratic (including x2−6x^2 - 6x2−6, no constant term, surd and fraction coefficients) and verify sum =−ba= -\dfrac{b}{a}=−ab​ and product =ca= \dfrac{c}{a}=ac​.
  • Build a quadratic from a sum and product or from its zeroes; find expressions such as α2+β2\alpha^2 + \beta^2α2+β2, (α−β)2(\alpha - \beta)^2(α−β)2 and 1α+1β\dfrac{1}{\alpha} + \dfrac{1}{\beta}α1​+β1​ without finding α\alphaα and β\betaβ; find a missing coefficient from a condition on the zeroes. (Includes a short, not-for-exam section on cubic polynomials.)
