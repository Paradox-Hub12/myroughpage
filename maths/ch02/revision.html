Polynomials — sums of terms (number) × xnx^nxn with whole-number powers n. Coefficients can be any real numbers (3 x2\sqrt{3}\,x^23​x2 is fine); x\sqrt{x}x​ and 1x\dfrac{1}{x}x1​ are not allowed. Degree = highest power after simplifying: 1 linear (ax+bax + bax+b), 2 quadratic (ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c), 3 cubic, always with a≠0a \neq 0a=0.

Zeroes — k is a zero of p(x)p(x)p(x) if p(k)=0p(k) = 0p(k)=0. p(0)p(0)p(0) is just the constant term — not "the zero". The zero of ax+bax + bax+b is −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​.

On a graph — the zeroes are the x-coordinates of the points where the graph of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) meets the x-axis; the y-axis point is p(0)p(0)p(0). Count points: a cross counts once, a touch counts once; no meeting point means no zeroes. A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes.

Parabolas — y=ax2+bx+cy = ax^2 + bx + cy=ax2+bx+c opens up if a>0a > 0a>0, down if a<0a < 0a<0. It cuts the x-axis twice (two zeroes), touches it once (two equal zeroes) or misses it (no zeroes).

Finding zeroes — standard form → read a, b, c with signs → factorise (split the middle term, common factor, or a2−b2a^2 - b^2a2−b2 with surds) → set each factor to 0.

The relationship — for zeroes α\alphaα, β\betaβ of ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c: α+β=−ba\alpha + \beta = -\dfrac{b}{a}α+β=−ab​ (the minus belongs to the sum) and αβ=ca\alpha\beta = \dfrac{c}{a}αβ=ac​. To verify, work out both sides and show they agree.
