Exercise 2.1

The question (for all six parts): 1. The graphs of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) are given in Fig. 2.10 below, for some polynomials p(x)p(x)p(x). Find the number of zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x), in each case.

The method for every part (Topic 3): the number of zeroes is the number of points where the graph meets the x-axis — a crossing counts once and a touch counts once. Side check: follow the curve from left to right. Each time it crosses the x-axis it changes side (above ↔ below); a touch does not change side. So if the curve starts and ends on different sides, it must cross an odd number of times; if it starts and ends on the same side, it crosses an even number of times.

Exercise 2.1, Q1 (i)A line parallel to the x-axis1 mark

The graphs of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) are given in Fig. 2.10 below, for some polynomials p(x)p(x)p(x). Find the number of zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x), in each case.
(i) Graph (i), described: a straight line parallel to the x-axis, drawn above it (it cuts the y-axis above the origin O, with arrows at both ends). It never meets the x-axis.

Given: a horizontal line above the x-axis. Asked: the number of zeroes.

Concept used: zeroes = points where the graph meets the x-axis (Topic 3). Why it applies: we only need to count those points.

  1. 1

    The line is parallel to the x-axis, so it never meets it, however far it is extended.

  2. 2

    There is no point where p(x)=0p(x) = 0p(x)=0.

  3. Check: a horizontal line is the graph of a constant polynomial y=cy = cy=c; here c>0c > 0c>0 (the line is above the axis), and a non-zero constant is never 0 ✔.

  4. Answer p(x)p(x)p(x) has no zeroes.

    Common trap: Answering "1" because the line crosses the y-axis. Zeroes are read on the x-axis only.
Exercise 2.1, Q1 (ii)One crossing1 mark

The graphs of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) are given in Fig. 2.10 below, for some polynomials p(x)p(x)p(x). Find the number of zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x), in each case.
(ii) Graph (ii), described: the curve starts at the lower left, below the x-axis, rises to a small hump that stays below the x-axis (to the left of the y-axis), comes down to a low point below the x-axis (to the right of the y-axis), then rises steeply and crosses the x-axis once, on the positive side, continuing upwards.

Given: the curve described above. Asked: the number of zeroes.

Concept used: count the points on the x-axis. Why it applies: each such point is one zero.

  1. 1

    The hump and the low point are both below the x-axis, so neither of them meets it.

  2. 2

    The only meeting point is where the curve crosses the x-axis on the right.

  3. 3

    Number of points on the x-axis: 1.

  4. Check (side check): the curve starts below the axis and ends above it, so it crosses an odd number of times; 1 is odd ✔.

  5. Answer 1 zero

    Common trap: Counting the hump as a zero because it "turns". A turning point counts only if it lies on the x-axis.
Exercise 2.1, Q1 (iii)Three crossings1 mark

The graphs of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) are given in Fig. 2.10 below, for some polynomials p(x)p(x)p(x). Find the number of zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x), in each case.
(iii) Graph (iii), described: the curve starts at the lower left, rises and crosses the x-axis on the negative side, reaches a peak on the y-axis above O, comes down and crosses the x-axis on the positive side, dips a little below the axis, then rises, crosses the x-axis again and continues upwards.

Given: the curve described above. Asked: the number of zeroes.

Concept used: count the points on the x-axis. Why it applies: each crossing is one zero.

  1. 1

    First crossing: on the negative side of the x-axis, on the way up to the peak.

  2. 2

    Second crossing: on the positive side, on the way down from the peak.

  3. 3

    Third crossing: after the dip, on the way up.

  4. 4

    Number of points on the x-axis: 3.

  5. Check (side check): it starts below and ends above, so the number of crossings is odd; 3 is odd ✔. Also, the shape (up, down, up) is that of a cubic, which can have at most 3 zeroes ✔.

  6. Answer 3 zeroes

    Common trap: Missing the third crossing because the dip below the axis is shallow. Look closely at both sides of the dip.
Exercise 2.1, Q1 (iv)A parabola on the left1 mark

The graphs of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) are given in Fig. 2.10 below, for some polynomials p(x)p(x)p(x). Find the number of zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x), in each case.
(iv) Graph (iv), described: a U-shaped curve (a parabola opening upwards) drawn entirely to the left of the y-axis. Its lowest point is a little below the x-axis, so it crosses the x-axis at two points, both on the negative side.

Given: the parabola described above. Asked: the number of zeroes.

Concept used: count the points on the x-axis. Why it applies: each crossing is one zero.

  1. 1

    Coming down on the left, the curve crosses the x-axis once.

  2. 2

    Going up after the lowest point, it crosses the x-axis again.

  3. 3

    Number of points on the x-axis: 2.

  4. Check (side check): it starts above and ends above, so the number of crossings is even; 2 is even ✔. A parabola has at most 2 zeroes ✔.

  5. Answer 2 zeroes (both negative)

    Common trap: Answering "no zeroes" because the curve is away from the origin. The zeroes do not have to be near O; they are wherever the curve meets the x-axis.

The graphs of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) are given in Fig. 2.10 below, for some polynomials p(x)p(x)p(x). Find the number of zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x), in each case.
(v) Graph (v), described: the curve starts below the x-axis on the far left, rises and crosses the x-axis, reaches a hump, comes down and crosses the x-axis just to the left of O, dips to a low point below the axis near O, rises and crosses the x-axis just to the right of O, reaches a larger hump, then comes down and crosses the x-axis on the right, ending below it.

Given: the curve described above. Asked: the number of zeroes.

Concept used: count the points on the x-axis. Why it applies: each crossing is one zero.

  1. 1

    First crossing: on the far left, on the way up to the first hump.

  2. 2

    Second crossing: just left of O, on the way down.

  3. 3

    Third crossing: just right of O, on the way up.

  4. 4

    Fourth crossing: on the right, on the way down from the larger hump.

  5. 5

    Number of points on the x-axis: 4.

    Common trap: Counting the humps and the dip (three turning points) instead of the crossings.

    One crossing point, on the far left.

  2. 2

The method for every part (Topic 5): a quadratic whose zeroes have sum S and product P is k{x2−Sx+P}k\{x^2 - Sx + P\}k{x2−Sx+P} for any k≠0k \neq 0k=0. Choose k to clear fractions. Check each answer by working out its −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​ and ca\dfrac{c}{a}ac​.

A note on parts (iii), (iv) and (v)

The polynomials you get there have no real zeroes — their graphs do not meet the x-axis (for (iii), x2+5x^2 + \sqrt{5}x2+5​ is always at least 5\sqrt{5}5​). Chapter 4 explains how to tell. The question asks only for the polynomial, and the method gives it correctly.
