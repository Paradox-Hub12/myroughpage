Finding zeroes and verifying the relationship

About 55 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find the zeroes of a quadratic polynomial by factorising, including cases such as x² − 6, c = 0, and surd or fraction coefficients.
  • Verify that the sum of the zeroes is −b/a and the product is c/a.

Here are three quadratics and their zeroes: x2−5x+6x^2 - 5x + 6x2−5x+6 has zeroes 2 and 3; x2+x−12x^2 + x - 12x2+x−12 has zeroes 3 and −4-4−4; 2x2−7x+32x^2 - 7x + 32x2−7x+3 has zeroes 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ and 3. Add the zeroes, multiply them, and compare with the coefficients. Do you see a pattern? There is one, it holds for every quadratic that has zeroes, and it lets you check every answer in this topic in two lines.

Finding the zeroes by factorising

To find the zeroes of p(x)=ax2+bx+cp(x) = ax^2 + bx + cp(x)=ax2+bx+c, factorise it (Topic 1) and use the fact that a product is 0 only when one of its factors is 0.

For example, x2−5x+6=(x−2)(x−3)x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)x2−5x+6=(x−2)(x−3). This is 0 when x−2=0x - 2 = 0x−2=0 or x−3=0x - 3 = 0x−3=0, so the zeroes are 2 and 3.

Quick reminder (surds)

6×6=6\sqrt{6} \times \sqrt{6} = 66​×6​=6, so x2−6=(x−6)(x+6)x^2 - 6 = (x - \sqrt{6})(x + \sqrt{6})x2−6=(x−6​)(x+6​) and the zeroes are ±6\pm\sqrt{6}±6​. To add a fraction and a surd, write them over one denominator: 13+5=1+353\dfrac{1}{3} + \sqrt{5} = \dfrac{1 + 3\sqrt{5}}{3}31​+5​=31+35​​.

Standard form first

Before reading a, b and c, write the polynomial in the order ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c and keep each sign with its number:

  • 7x+4−2x27x + 4 - 2x^27x+4−2x2 becomes −2x2+7x+4-2x^2 + 7x + 4−2x2+7x+4: a=−2a = -2a=−2, b=7b = 7b=7, c=4c = 4c=4.
  • 3x2−183x^2 - 183x2−18 has no x-term: b=0b = 0b=0.
  • 6t2−9t6t^2 - 9t6t2−9t has no constant term: c=0c = 0c=0.

Fraction coefficients

If the coefficients are fractions, take out a fraction first (Topic 1). For x2−16x−13=16(3x−2)(2x+1)x^2 - \dfrac{1}{6}x - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{6}(3x - 2)(2x + 1)x2−61​x−31​=61​(3x−2)(2x+1) the zeroes are 23\dfrac{2}{3}32​ and −12-\dfrac{1}{2}−21​. The verification still uses the original coefficients a=1a = 1a=1, b=−16b = -\dfrac{1}{6}b=−61​, c=−13c = -\dfrac{1}{3}c=−31​: the sum 23−12=16=−b\dfrac{2}{3} - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{6} = -b32​−21​=61​=−b ✔ and the product −13=c-\dfrac{1}{3} = c−31​=c ✔. Using the whole-number version 6x2−x−26x^2 - x - 26x2−x−2 gives the same ratios, because multiplying a, b and c by 6 does not change ba\dfrac{b}{a}ab​ or ca\dfrac{c}{a}ac​.

When it will not factorise neatly

Some quadratics, such as 2x2−6x+32x^2 - 6x + 32x2−6x+3, have zeroes that are not whole numbers, fractions or simple surds you can spot. You will learn a formula for them in Chapter 4. The relationship still holds for them: the sum of the zeroes is still −ba=3-\dfrac{b}{a} = 3−ab​=3 and the product still ca=32\dfrac{c}{a} = \dfrac{3}{2}ac​=23​. Topic 5 uses exactly this — it works with the sum and product without finding the zeroes.

The method

  1. Write p(x)p(x)p(x) in standard form and note a, b, c with signs.
  2. Factorise (split the middle term, take out a common factor, or use a2−b2a^2 - b^2a2−b2).
  3. Set each factor equal to 0 to get the two zeroes.
  4. Verify: work out the sum of the zeroes and −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​ separately, and show they are equal. Do the same for the product and ca\dfrac{c}{a}ac​.

Setting out your answer

A typical 3-mark question says "Find the zeroes … and verify the relationship between the zeroes and the coefficients". The marking scheme gives 1 mark for splitting the middle term, 1 for the factors and the zeroes, and 1 for the verification (CBSE Sample Paper 2025-26, Standard, Question 28). In the verification, write both sides in full: "Sum of zeroes =13+5=1+353= \dfrac{1}{3} + \sqrt{5} = \dfrac{1 + 3\sqrt{5}}{3}=31​+5​=31+35​​ and −ba=1+353-\dfrac{b}{a} = \dfrac{1 + 3\sqrt{5}}{3}−ab​=31+35​​. Verified." Writing only "sum =−ba= -\dfrac{b}{a}=−ab​" is stating the rule, not verifying it.

Common mistakes!

  • The sign of the sum is the single most common slip in this chapter. It turns a correct factorisation into a "failed" verification, and costs the mark every time.

  • Sum =ba= \dfrac{b}{a}=ab​

    From a(x−α)(x−β)a(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)a(x−α)(x−β), b=−a(α+β)b = -a(\alpha + \beta)b=−a(α+β).

    Sum =−ba= -\dfrac{b}{a}=−ab​. Write the minus first, then substitute b in brackets.

  • A minus sign on the product

    c=aαβc = a\alpha\betac=aαβ has no minus.

    Product =ca= \dfrac{c}{a}=ac​.

  • Reading a, b, c before rearranging (7x+4−2x27x + 4 - 2x^27x+4−2x2)

    The letters belong to fixed powers of x.

Key points

  • Factorise; a product is 0 only when a factor is 0. That gives the zeroes.
  • For ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c with zeroes α\alphaα and β\betaβ: α+β=−ba\alpha + \beta = -\dfrac{b}{a}α+β=−ab​ and αβ=ca\alpha\beta = \dfrac{c}{a}αβ=ac​.
  • The minus belongs to the sum only.
  • Standard form first; read a, b, c with their signs (b=0b = 0b=0 or c=0c = 0c=0 is allowed).
  • To verify, calculate each side separately and show that they agree. Equal zeroes count twice.
