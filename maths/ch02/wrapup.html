Wrap-up

The relationship card and mixed problems

About 35 minutes

Almost every question in this chapter rests on two facts: the zeroes are where the graph meets the x-axis, and for ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c the zeroes have sum −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​ and product ca\dfrac{c}{a}ac​. This page puts the cards you need on one sheet, then gives five mixed problems that join the graph and the algebra.

Mixed example 1

From a graph to the polynomial

Question. The graph of a quadratic polynomial p(x)p(x)p(x) meets the x-axis at (−1,0)(-1, 0)(−1,0) and (5,0)(5, 0)(5,0), and the y-axis at (0,−10)(0, -10)(0,−10). Find p(x)p(x)p(x).

Given: zeroes −1-1−1 and 555; p(0)=−10p(0) = -10p(0)=−10. Asked: p(x)p(x)p(x).

Concept used: every quadratic with zeroes α\alphaα and β\betaβ is k(x−α)(x−β)k(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)k(x−α)(x−β); the y-intercept fixes k. Why it applies: the zeroes alone allow many polynomials; one more point picks the right one.

  1. 1

    Write the family with zeroes −1-1−1 and 5.

    p(x)=k(x+1)(x−5)p(x) = k(x + 1)(x - 5)p(x)=k(x+1)(x−5)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Use p(0)=−10p(0) = -10p(0)=−10.

    k(0+1)(0−5)=−10k(0 + 1)(0 - 5) = -10k(0+1)(0−5)=−10Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Simplify.

    −5k=−10-5k = -10−5k=−10Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Divide by −5-5−5.

    k=2k = 2k=2Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Multiply out.

    p(x)=2(x2−4x−5)=2x2−8x−10p(x) = 2(x^2 - 4x - 5) = 2x^2 - 8x - 10p(x)=2(x2−4x−5)=2x2−8x−10Swipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: p(−1)=2+8−10=0p(-1) = 2 + 8 - 10 = 0p(−1)=2+8−10=0 ✔, p(5)=50−40−10=0p(5) = 50 - 40 - 10 = 0p(5)=50−40−10=0 ✔, p(0)=−10p(0) = -10p(0)=−10 ✔. Also a=2>0a = 2 > 0a=2>0, so the parabola opens upwards, as a curve through these three points must.

    Common trap: Stopping at x2−4x−5x^2 - 4x - 5x2−4x−5. It has the right zeroes, but p(0)=−5p(0) = -5p(0)=−5, not −10-10−10.

Mixed example 2

A polynomial with reciprocal zeroes

Question. α\alphaα and β\betaβ are the zeroes of x2−5x+3x^2 - 5x + 3x2−5x+3. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are 1α\dfrac{1}{\alpha}α1​ and 1β\dfrac{1}{\beta}β1​.

Given: α+β=5\alpha + \beta = 5α+β=5, αβ=3\alpha\beta = 3αβ=3. Asked: a polynomial with zeroes 1α\dfrac{1}{\alpha}α1​, 1β\dfrac{1}{\beta}β1​.

Concept used: find the new sum and product with the symmetric-expression card, then build. Why it applies: both are expressions in α\alphaα and β\betaβ.

  1. 1

    New sum.

    1α+1β=α+βαβ=53\dfrac{1}{\alpha} + \dfrac{1}{\beta} = \dfrac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta} = \dfrac{5}{3}α1​+β1​=αβα+β​=35​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    New product.

    1α×1β=1αβ=13\dfrac{1}{\alpha} \times \dfrac{1}{\beta} = \dfrac{1}{\alpha\beta} = \dfrac{1}{3}α1​×β1​=αβ1​=31​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Build: k{x2−53x+13}k\left\{x^2 - \dfrac{5}{3}x + \dfrac{1}{3}\right\}k{x2−35​x+31​}. Take k=3k = 3k=3.

    3x2−5x+13x^2 - 5x + 13x2−5x+1Swipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: for 3x2−5x+13x^2 - 5x + 13x2−5x+1, −ba=53-\dfrac{b}{a} = \dfrac{5}{3}−ab​=35​ ✔ and ca=13\dfrac{c}{a} = \dfrac{1}{3}ac​=31​ ✔. Notice the coefficients of x2−5x+3x^2 - 5x + 3x2−5x+3 appear in reverse order — that always happens with reciprocal zeroes.

  5. Answer 3x2−5x+13x^2 - 5x + 13x2−5x+1

    Common trap: Writing the new sum as 1α+β=15\dfrac{1}{\alpha + \beta} = \dfrac{1}{5}α+β1​=51​. Add the fractions properly: α+βαβ\dfrac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta}αβα+β​.

Mixed example 3

When a + b + c = 0

Question. Show that 1 is a zero of ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c whenever a+b+c=0a + b + c = 0a+b+c=0. Hence find both zeroes of 4x2−7x+34x^2 - 7x + 34x2−7x+3 without factorising.

Given: a+b+c=0a + b + c = 0a+b+c=0. Asked: show 1 is a zero; zeroes of 4x2−7x+34x^2 - 7x + 34x2−7x+3.

Concept used: k is a zero when p(k)=0p(k) = 0p(k)=0; then the product of zeroes. Why it applies: p(1)p(1)p(1) is exactly a+b+ca + b + ca+b+c.

  1. 1

    Substitute x=1x = 1x=1.

    p(1)=a(1)2+b(1)+c=a+b+cp(1) = a(1)^2 + b(1) + c = a + b + cp(1)=a(1)2+b(1)+c=a+b+cSwipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    We are told a+b+c=0a + b + c = 0a+b+c=0, so p(1)=0p(1) = 0p(1)=0: 1 is a zero.

  3. 3

    For 4x2−7x+34x^2 - 7x + 34x2−7x+3: 4+(−7)+3=04 + (-7) + 3 = 04+(−7)+3=0, so 1 is a zero.

  4. 4

    Product of zeroes =ca=34= \dfrac{c}{a} = \dfrac{3}{4}=ac​=43​. With one zero 1, the other is 34\dfrac{3}{4}43​.

  5. Check: sum 1+34=74=−−741 + \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{7}{4} = -\dfrac{-7}{4}1+43​=47​=−4−7​ ✔; and 4x2−7x+3=(x−1)(4x−3)4x^2 - 7x + 3 = (x - 1)(4x - 3)4x2−7x+3=(x−1)(4x−3) ✔.

  6. Answer The zeroes are 111 and 34\dfrac{3}{4}43​.

    Common trap: Using the sum instead and forgetting the sign: "1+β=−741 + \beta = -\dfrac{7}{4}1+β=−47​, so β=−114\beta = -\dfrac{11}{4}β=−411​". The sum is −ba=+74-\dfrac{b}{a} = +\dfrac{7}{4}−ab​=+47​.

Mixed example 4

Signs of the zeroes from the coefficients

Question. The polynomial 3x2+17x+113x^2 + 17x + 113x2+17x+11 has two real zeroes. Without finding them, decide whether they are positive or negative.

Given: a=3a = 3a=3, b=17b = 17b=17, c=11c = 11c=11; the zeroes are real. Asked: their signs.

Concept used: the product tells you whether the signs are the same; the sum tells you which sign. Why it applies: two real numbers with a positive product have the same sign.

  1. 1

    Product of zeroes.

    αβ=113>0\alpha\beta = \dfrac{11}{3} > 0αβ=311​>0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    A positive product means α\alphaα and β\betaβ have the same sign.

  3. 3

    Sum of zeroes.

    α+β=−173<0\alpha + \beta = -\dfrac{17}{3} < 0α+β=−317​<0Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Two numbers of the same sign with a negative sum are both negative.

  5. Check: a quick test of signs: for any x≥0x \geq 0x≥0, every term of 3x2+17x+113x^2 + 17x + 113x2+17x+11 is ≥0\geq 0≥0 and the constant is 11, so p(x)≥11p(x) \geq 11p(x)≥11 — it cannot be 0 for a positive x (or for 0). So the zeroes must be negative ✔.

  6. Answer Both zeroes are negative.

    Common trap: Deciding from the product alone ("positive product, so both positive"). The product only says "same sign"; the sum decides which.

Mixed example 5

A graph that touches

Question. The graph of y=ax2+bx+cy = ax^2 + bx + cy=ax2+bx+c touches the x-axis at x=3x = 3x=3 and passes through (0,18)(0, 18)(0,18). Find a, b and c.

Concept used: equal zeroes α\alphaα = β\betaβ = 3, so p(x)=k(x−3)2p(x) = k(x - 3)^2p(x)=k(x−3)2; the y-intercept fixes k. Why it applies: a parabola touching the x-axis has two equal zeroes (Topic 3).

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Simplify.

  4. 4
