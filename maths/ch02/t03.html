Topic 3 of 5

Zeroes on a graph

About 50 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain that the zeroes of p(x) are the x-coordinates of the points where the graph of y = p(x) meets the x-axis.
  • Read the number of zeroes from the graphs of linear, quadratic and cubic (and other) polynomials.
  • Relate the shape of a parabola (opens up or down) to the sign of a, and recognise the three cases — two zeroes, one zero, no zero.

Look at the water from a garden hose or a fountain jet: it rises, curves over and comes back down. The shape it traces is a parabola — the graph of a quadratic polynomial. The two points where the water meets the ground are the two places where the "height" is zero. In this topic you will see every zero of a polynomial as a point where its graph meets a line: the x-axis.

Quick reminder

A point (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) is on the x-axis exactly when y=0y = 0y=0, and on the y-axis exactly when x=0x = 0x=0. To draw the graph of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x), choose some x-values, work out y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) for each, plot the points (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) and join them with a smooth curve.

The key idea

Take p(x)=x2−x−6p(x) = x^2 - x - 6p(x)=x2−x−6 and make a table of values:

x −3 −2 −1 0 1 2 3 4
y=x2−x−6y = x^2 - x - 6y=x2−x−6 6 0 −4 −6 −6 −4 0 6

Plotted and joined, these points make a U-shaped curve. Two of the points, (−2,0)(-2, 0)(−2,0) and (3,0)(3, 0)(3,0), have y=0y = 0y=0: they lie on the x-axis. And y=0y = 0y=0 means p(x)=0p(x) = 0p(x)=0 — so −2-2−2 and 333 are exactly the zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x).

The zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x) are the x-coordinates of the points where the graph of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) meets the x-axis.

The graph also crosses the y-axis, at (0,−6)(0, -6)(0,−6). That point gives p(0)=−6p(0) = -6p(0)=−6, the constant term. It is not a zero: zeroes are read on the x-axis only.

Linear polynomials

The graph of y=ax+by = ax + by=ax+b (a≠0a \neq 0a=0) is a straight line that is not horizontal, so it meets the x-axis at exactly one point, (−ba,0)\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}, 0\right)(−ab​,0). That is why a linear polynomial has exactly one zero. (A horizontal line y=5y = 5y=5 never meets the x-axis: the constant polynomial 5 has no zeroes.)

Quadratic polynomials: the parabola

The graph of y=ax2+bx+cy = ax^2 + bx + cy=ax2+bx+c (a≠0a \neq 0a=0) is a parabola:

  • if a>0a > 0a>0, it opens upwards (U shape);
  • if a<0a < 0a<0, it opens downwards (∩ shape).

A parabola can meet the x-axis in three ways:

Case What the graph does Number of zeroes
(i) cuts the x-axis at two different points 2 different zeroes
(ii) touches the x-axis at one point and turns back 1 zero (algebra calls it two equal zeroes)
(iii) stays completely above or completely below the x-axis no zeroes

So a quadratic has at most 2 zeroes. In Chapter 4 you will learn a quick test, the discriminant, that tells you which case you are in without drawing anything.

Cubic and higher polynomials

The graph of a cubic can meet the x-axis at 1, 2 or 3 points, never more. In general, the graph of a polynomial of degree n meets the x-axis at most n times, so it has at most n zeroes. Turned around: if a graph meets the x-axis at 4 points, the polynomial must have degree 4 or more.

How to count zeroes from a graph

  1. Look only at the x-axis.
  2. Count every point where the curve meets it — a cross counts once, a touch counts once.
  3. If the curve never meets the x-axis, there are no zeroes. ("No zeroes" does not mean "0 is a zero".)
  4. To read the zeroes themselves, read the x-coordinates of those points.

Reading a graph accurately

On graph paper, first find the scale: how much each small square stands for on each axis (it may differ between the axes). Read a zero by following the point straight up or down to the x-axis numbers. If a curve meets the axis between two grid lines, the zero is not a whole number — say so ("between 1 and 2"), or use the polynomial to find it exactly. And check each zero you read: substituting it into p(x)p(x)p(x) should give 0. A reading that gives p(x)=5p(x) = 5p(x)=5 is not a zero, however close the curve looks.

You will not be asked to plot graphs of quadratic or cubic polynomials in the board exam; you will be asked to read them. That is what this topic practises.

How many zeroes? Six graphs

Question. Each graph below is the graph of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) for some polynomial p(x)p(x)p(x). Find the number of zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x) in each case.
(a) A rising straight line that crosses the x-axis once, at x=1.5x = 1.5x=1.5.
(b) A parabola opening downwards, with its highest point at (1,4)(1, 4)(1,4), crossing the x-axis at x=−1x = -1x=−1 and x=3x = 3x=3.
(c) A parabola opening upwards whose lowest point (3,0)(3, 0)(3,0) is on the x-axis; it touches the axis there and turns back up.
(d) A parabola opening upwards whose lowest point is (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1), above the x-axis.
(e) A curve that crosses the x-axis at the origin, rises a little, comes down to touch the x-axis at x=2x = 2x=2, and rises again.
(f) A curve that crosses the x-axis at x=−2x = -2x=−2, x=−1x = -1x=−1 and x=3x = 3x=3.

    (a) One crossing point → 1 zero (it is 1.5=321.5 = \dfrac{3}{2}1.5=23​).

  2. 2

    (b) Two crossing points → 2 zeroes (−1-1−1 and 333).

  3. 3

    (c) One touching point → 1 zero (x=3x = 3x=3).

  4. 4

    (d) The curve never meets the x-axis → no zeroes.

  5. 5

    (e) One crossing point (at 0) and one touching point (at 2) → 2 zeroes.

  6. 6

    (f) Three crossing points → 3 zeroes.

  7. Check: the figure is drawn from 2x−32x - 32x−3, −x2+2x+3-x^2 + 2x + 3−x2+2x+3, x2−6x+9x^2 - 6x + 9x2−6x+9, x2+1x^2 + 1x2+1, x3−4x2+4xx^3 - 4x^2 + 4xx3−4x2+4x and x3−7x−6x^3 - 7x - 6x3−7x−6. Common mistakes!

  • Reading zeroes from graphs is one of the four most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter. Each slip below gives a confident wrong answer.

  • Reading the y-intercept as a zero

    The y-intercept is p(0)p(0)p(0), a value of p, not a value of x where p=0p = 0p=0.

    Look only at the x-axis.

  • Counting a touch as two zeroes

    One touching point is one point where p(x)=0p(x) = 0p(x)=0.

    Count points; a touch counts once.

  • Counting turning points (peaks and dips)

    A peak or dip is not on the x-axis unless the curve touches there.

    Count only where the curve meets the x-axis.

  • "No zeroes, so 0 is a zero"

    "No zeroes" means no x makes p(x)=0p(x) = 0p(x)=0.

    0 is a zero only if the graph passes through the origin.

  • Choosing a graph by its zeroes alone

    Many polynomials share the same zeroes.

    Also check the direction (sign of a) and p(0)p(0)p(0).

Key points

  • Zeroes of p(x)p(x)p(x) = x-coordinates of the points where the graph of y=p(x)y = p(x)y=p(x) meets the x-axis. The y-axis point gives p(0)p(0)p(0).
  • Linear: a straight line, exactly 1 zero.
  • Quadratic: a parabola; a>0a > 0a>0 opens up, a<0a < 0a<0 opens down; it has 2, 1 (two equal) or no zeroes.
  • Degree n: at most n zeroes (cubic at most 3).
  • Count points on the x-axis — a cross or a touch counts once; no meeting point means no zeroes.
