Topic 5 of 5

Using the relationship

About 70 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Form a quadratic polynomial from a given sum and product of zeroes, or from given zeroes.
  • Evaluate symmetric expressions of α and β, such as α² + β², (α − β)² and 1/α + 1/β, without finding α and β.
  • Find unknown coefficients from conditions on the zeroes (one zero given; reciprocal zeroes; equal zeroes; zeroes with a given difference, sum or product).

A detective who knows only that two suspects' ages add up to 30 and multiply to 216 can still answer many questions — the difference of their ages, the sum of their squares — without ever learning the ages themselves. Sum and product are powerful clues. In this topic you will use −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​ and ca\dfrac{c}{a}ac​ backwards (to build a polynomial) and sideways (to evaluate expressions and find missing coefficients).

  1. Building a polynomial from its zeroes

If a quadratic has zeroes α\alphaα and β\betaβ, it can be written as k(x−α)(x−β)k(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)k(x−α)(x−β) for some number k≠0k \neq 0k=0. Multiplying out:

k(x−α)(x−β)=k{x2−(α+β)x+αβ}k(x - \alpha)(x - \beta) = k\{x^2 - (\alpha + \beta)x + \alpha\beta\}k(x−α)(x−β)=k{x2−(α+β)x+αβ}Swipe to see the whole equation →

So if you know the sum S=α+βS = \alpha + \betaS=α+β and the product P=αβP = \alpha\betaP=αβ, every quadratic with those zeroes is

k{x2−Sx+P},k≠0k\{x^2 - Sx + P\}, \quad k \neq 0k{x2−Sx+P},k=0Swipe to see the whole equation →

Two things to notice:

  • The sum goes in with a minus sign; the product with a plus sign.
  • There is not just one answer. Any non-zero k gives a polynomial with the same zeroes. "Find a quadratic polynomial" means any one of them; choose k to clear fractions — unless the question gives one more fact, such as the y-intercept or a point on the graph. Then that fact decides k (see the wrap-up, Mixed example 1). "Find all quadratic polynomials" means you must write the kkk.

Zeroes such as 3+23 + \sqrt{2}3+2​ and 3−23 - \sqrt{2}3−2​. When the two zeroes differ only in the sign of the root, the roots cancel in the sum (666) and multiply out in the product (9−2=79 - 2 = 79−2=7, using a2−b2a^2 - b^2a2−b2). So the polynomial, x2−6x+7x^2 - 6x + 7x2−6x+7, has whole-number coefficients even though its zeroes are surds. Expect this pattern in exam questions.

Two checks for any polynomial you build. Work out its −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​ and ca\dfrac{c}{a}ac​ and compare them with S and P; or substitute one of the given zeroes and confirm that you get 0. Either check takes under a minute and catches the commonest slip, a wrong sign on the x-term.

Choosing k. If S or P is a fraction, take k equal to the common denominator: for S=25S = \dfrac{2}{5}S=52​ and P=−3P = -3P=−3, k{x2−25x−3}k\left\{x^2 - \dfrac{2}{5}x - 3\right\}k{x2−52​x−3} with k=5k = 5k=5 gives 5x2−2x−155x^2 - 2x - 155x2−2x−15. If S or P contains a root, k=1k = 1k=1 is usually simplest. Whatever you choose, check your answer: its −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​ must be S and its ca\dfrac{c}{a}ac​ must be P. If the question also gives a point, such as p(0)=8p(0) = 8p(0)=8, substitute it to find k instead of choosing it.

  1. Expressions in α\alphaα and β\betaβ — without finding α\alphaα and β\betaβ

Setting out your answer. In a 3-mark question, the marking scheme gives about one mark for writing α+β\alpha + \betaα+β and αβ\alpha\betaαβ correctly, then marks for rewriting the expression in terms of them and for the final value (CBSE Sample Paper 2026-27, Standard, Question 27: 1 + ½ + 1 + ½). So always write the line "α+β=…\alpha + \beta = \ldotsα+β=… and αβ=…\alpha\beta = \ldotsαβ=…" first, even if you can do the rest in your head — and write the rewritten form, such as "α2+β2=(α+β)2−2αβ\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\betaα2+β2=(α+β)2−2αβ", before substituting.

  1. Finding an unknown coefficient

When a coefficient is unknown (call it k), a fact about the zeroes gives you an equation for k.

You are told… Write this equation
a number r is a zero p(r)=0p(r) = 0p(r)=0 (then the other zero from the sum or product)
the zeroes are reciprocals (α\alphaα and 1α\dfrac{1}{\alpha}α1​) product =1= 1=1: ca=1\dfrac{c}{a} = 1ac​=1, so a=ca = ca=c
the zeroes are equal (α=β\alpha = \betaα=β) sum =2α= 2\alpha=2α and product =α2= \alpha^2=α2

the zeroes are negatives of each other sum =0= 0=0, so b=0b = 0b=0

After solving, substitute k back and check the condition — it catches sign slips.
