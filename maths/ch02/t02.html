Polynomials and their zeroes

About 40 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Identify the degree and type (linear, quadratic, cubic) of a polynomial.
  • Find the value p(k) and decide whether k is a zero of p(x).
  • Find the zero of a linear polynomial ax + b as −b/a.

A cricket ball hit high into the air rises, slows, and falls back to the ground. If you write its height after t seconds as an expression in t, the moment it lands is the value of t that makes the height zero. Engineers ask the same question about bridges, profits and rocket paths: "When does this expression become 0?" Those special values are called the zeroes of a polynomial, and they are the heart of this chapter.

What is a polynomial?

A polynomial in x is a sum of terms of the form (number) × xnx^nxn, where every power n is a whole number (0, 1, 2, 3, …). The numbers in front are the coefficients; they can be any real numbers, including fractions and roots.

  • 4x2−3 x+124x^2 - \sqrt{3}\,x + \dfrac{1}{2}4x2−3​x+21​ is a polynomial (the coefficient −3-\sqrt{3}−3​ is fine; the power of x is what matters).
  • x+3\sqrt{x} + 3x​+3 is not a polynomial, because x\sqrt{x}x​ is x to the power 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​, a fraction.
  • x+2xx + \dfrac{2}{x}x+x2​ is not a polynomial, because 2x=2x−1\dfrac{2}{x} = 2x^{-1}x2​=2x−1 has a negative power.

We name polynomials with letters such as p(x)p(x)p(x), q(t)q(t)q(t) or f(y)f(y)f(y). The letter in brackets is the variable.

The degree is the highest power of the variable that appears (with a non-zero coefficient). Polynomials are named by their degree:

Zeroes of a polynomial

A real number k is a zero of p(x)p(x)p(x) if p(k)=0p(k) = 0p(k)=0. For p(x)=x2+x−12p(x) = x^2 + x - 12p(x)=x2+x−12, try 3: 9+3−12=09 + 3 - 12 = 09+3−12=0, so 3 is a zero. Try −4-4−4: 16−4−12=016 - 4 - 12 = 016−4−12=0, so −4-4−4 is a zero too.

Two ideas students often mix up:

  • "Find p(0)" asks for the value of p at x=0x = 0x=0 (the constant term).
  • "Find the zeroes of p" asks for the values of x that make p(x)=0p(x) = 0p(x)=0.

They are different questions. For x2+x−12x^2 + x - 12x2+x−12, p(0)=−12p(0) = -12p(0)=−12, but the zeroes are 3 and −4-4−4. Zero is a zero of p only when the constant term is 0 (for example x2−5xx^2 - 5xx2−5x).

Setting out "is k a zero?" in the exam

Write three short lines: the substitution with every value in brackets, the arithmetic, and a sentence that answers the question. For example: "p(−3)=(−3)2+(−3)−12=9−3−12=−6p(-3) = (-3)^2 + (-3) - 12 = 9 - 3 - 12 = -6p(−3)=(−3)2+(−3)−12=9−3−12=−6. Since p(−3)≠0p(-3) \neq 0p(−3)=0, −3-3−3 is not a zero of p(x)p(x)p(x)." The last sentence earns the mark — a bare number does not say whether k is a zero. If the answer is 0, say so in words: "p(3)=0p(3) = 0p(3)=0, so 3 is a zero."

Finding zeroes of a quadratic: a preview

For a linear polynomial there is a formula. For a quadratic you usually factorise: x2+x−12=(x−3)(x+4)x^2 + x - 12 = (x - 3)(x + 4)x2+x−12=(x−3)(x+4), and a product is 0 only when one of its factors is 0, so the zeroes are 3 and −4-4−4. Topic 4 does this carefully for every kind of quadratic, and Topic 3 first shows you what the zeroes look like on a graph.

How many zeroes can a polynomial have?

A linear polynomial has exactly 1 zero. A quadratic has at most 2, and a cubic has at most 3. In general, a polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes. Topic 3 shows why, using graphs.

Worked example 2

Is k a zero?

Question. (a) For p(x)=x2+x−12p(x) = x^2 + x - 12p(x)=x2+x−12, check whether 3 and −3-3−3 are zeroes. (b) Is 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ a zero of q(x)=2x2−7x+3q(x) = 2x^2 - 7x + 3q(x)=2x2−7x+3?

Common mistakes!

  • Calling x+1\sqrt{x} + 1x​+1 or x+1xx + \dfrac{1}{x}x+x1​ a polynomial

    The powers 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ and −1-1−1 are not whole numbers.

    Check the powers of the variable, not the coefficients.

  • Naming the type before simplifying

    (x+1)2−x2(x + 1)^2 - x^2(x+1)2−x2 is 2x+12x + 12x+1, linear.

    Simplify, then read the highest power.

  • "The zero of p is p(0)p(0)p(0)"

    p(0)p(0)p(0) is the value at x=0x = 0x=0 (the constant term).

    A zero is a value of x that makes p(x)=0p(x) = 0p(x)=0.

  • Zero of 6−4x6 - 4x6−4x =−−46= -\dfrac{-4}{6}=−6−4​

    a and b were read in the wrong order.

    Write −4x+6-4x + 6−4x+6: a=−4a = -4a=−4, b=6b = 6b=6, zero =−ba=32= -\dfrac{b}{a} = \dfrac{3}{2}=−ab​=23​.

  • Degree of 9 is 1

    9 has no x at all.

    A non-zero constant has degree 0.

Key points

  • A polynomial has only whole-number powers of the variable; coefficients can be any real numbers.
  • Degree 1 linear, 2 quadratic, 3 cubic; in the general form the leading coefficient a≠0a \neq 0a=0.
  • p(k)p(k)p(k) = value at x=kx = kx=k; p(0)p(0)p(0) = constant term.
  • k is a zero of p if p(k)=0p(k) = 0p(k)=0.
  • Zero of ax+bax + bax+b: −ba-\dfrac{b}{a}−ab​ (read a and b with their signs). A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes.
