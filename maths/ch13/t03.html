Topic 3 of 6

Mean: the step-deviation method and missing frequencies

About 50 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find the mean by the step-deviation method, including classes of unequal size (h a common divisor of the deviations) and decimal classes.
  • Choose a suitable method for finding the mean of a given table.
  • Find one or two missing frequencies when the mean (and the total frequency) is given.

Weekly wages of ₹3250, ₹3750, ₹4250 … are awkward to multiply by 6, 11 and 17 without a calculator. But look: they all differ by ₹500. Shift them so the middle one is 0, then divide by 500, and they become −2, −1, 0, 1, 2. Multiplying by those is easy. That is the step-deviation method — and the same idea lets you work backwards to a missing frequency.

  1. The step-deviation method

When all the deviations di=xi−ad_i = x_i - adi​=xi​−a have a common factor h (usually the class size), divide by it:

ui=xi−ah,xˉ=a+h×ΣfiuiΣfiu_i = \frac{x_i - a}{h}, \qquad \bar{x} = a + h \times \frac{\Sigma f_i u_i}{\Sigma f_i}ui​=hxi​−a​,xˉ=a+h×Σfi​Σfi​ui​​Swipe to see the whole equation →

Why it works. Dividing every deviation by h divides their mean by h; so ΣfiuiΣfi=xˉ−ah\dfrac{\Sigma f_i u_i}{\Sigma f_i} = \dfrac{\bar{x} - a}{h}Σfi​Σfi​ui​​=hxˉ−a​. Multiply by h and add a.

The table: Class | fif_ifi​ | xix_ixi​ | uiu_iui​ | fiuif_i u_ifi​ui​. With equal classes and a in the middle, the uiu_iui​ are …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, … — you can often write them down without working out did_idi​.

Undo both steps at the end: multiply by h, then add a. Forgetting either is the commonest slip.

  1. Classes of unequal size

If the classes are not all the same size, the uiu_iui​ are not neat integers when you divide by one class size. Choose h as a common divisor of all the did_idi​ instead. The formula is the same. (NCERT notes that the formula xˉ=a+huˉ\bar{x} = a + h\bar{u}xˉ=a+huˉ holds for any non-zero h and any a with ui=xi−ahu_i = \dfrac{x_i - a}{h}ui​=hxi​−a​ — h is chosen only to keep the numbers whole.)

  1. Decimal classes

Classes such as 1.5 – 2.0 kg work in the same way: with h=0.5h = 0.5h=0.5, the uiu_iui​ are again whole numbers.

  1. Choosing a method

Situation Best method
Small class marks and small frequencies Direct
Large class marks, deviations with no common factor Assumed mean
Large class marks; equal classes (or deviations with a common factor) Step deviation

All three give the same mean; the choice only changes the amount of arithmetic. If a question asks "which method did you use, and why?", name the method and give the reason from this table.

  1. Missing frequencies

If the mean is given and one frequency is unknown (say p), write Σfi\Sigma f_iΣfi​ and Σfiui\Sigma f_i u_iΣfi​ui​ (or Σfixi\Sigma f_i x_iΣfi​xi​) with p in them, put them into the formula, and solve the equation for p. If two frequencies x and y are unknown, the question also gives the total Σfi\Sigma f_iΣfi​; that gives a second equation, and you solve the pair (Chapter 3).

The step-deviation method

Worked example 2

Classes of unequal size

Worked example 3

Decimal classes

Question. The weights (in kg) of 50 babies born in a hospital in one month are: 1.5 – 2.0: 3; 2.0 – 2.5: 8; 2.5 – 3.0: 17; 3.0 – 3.5: 15; 3.5 – 4.0: 7. Find the mean weight.

Worked example 4

One missing frequency

Question. The numbers of books read in a year by some students are: 0 – 10: 5 students; 10 – 20: 8; 20 – 30: p; 30 – 40: 9; 40 – 50: 4. The mean is 24.75. Find p.

Given: one unknown frequency p; mean 24.75. Asked: p.

Concept used: step-deviation formula with p kept in Σfi\Sigma f_iΣfi​. Why it applies: the mean is known, so the formula becomes an equation in p.

  1. 1

    Class marks 5, 15, 25, 35, 45; take a=25a = 25a=25, h=10h = 10h=10: ui=−2,−1,0,1,2u_i = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2ui​=−2,−1,0,1,2.

  2. 2

    Σfi=5+8+p+9+4=26+p\Sigma f_i = 5 + 8 + p + 9 + 4 = 26 + pΣfi​=5+8+p+9+4=26+p

Worked example 5

Two missing frequencies

Question. The distribution below has total frequency 50 and mean 50.8. Find x and y. Classes: 0 – 20: 6; 20 – 40: x; 40 – 60: 15; 60 – 80: y; 80 – 100: 5.

Given: Σfi=50\Sigma f_i = 50Σfi​=50; mean 50.8; unknowns x and y. Asked: x and y.

Concept used: the total gives one equation; the mean formula gives a second. Why it applies: two unknowns need two equations.

  1. 1

    Total: 6+x+15+y+5=506 + x + 15 + y + 5 = 506+x+15+y+5=50, so x+y=24x + y = 24x+y=24 … (1)

  2. 2

    Class marks 10, 30, 50, 70, 90; take a=50a = 50a=50, h=20h = 20h=20: ui=−2,−1,0,1,2u_i = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2ui​=−2,−1,0,1,2.

  3. 3

    Σfiui=−12−x+0+y+10=y−x−2\Sigma f_i u_i = -12 - x + 0 + y + 10 = y - x - 2Σfi​ui​=−12−x+0+y+10=y−x−2

  4. 4

    50.8=50+20×y−x−25050.8 = 50 + 20 \times \dfrac{y - x - 2}{50}50.8=50+20×50y−x−2​, so 2(y−x−2)5=0.8\dfrac{2(y - x - 2)}{5} = 0.852(y−x−2)​=0.8 and y−x−2=2y - x - 2 = 2y−x−2=2, that is, y−x=4y - x = 4y−x=4 … (2)

  5. 5

    Add (1) and (2): 2y=282y = 282y=28, so y=14y = 14y=14; then x=24−14=10x = 24 - 14 = 10x=24−14=10.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that forgetting to multiply by h or to add a back, wrong signs below a, and a wrong h for unequal classes are among the most common reasons for lost marks with the step-deviation method.

  • xˉ=a+ΣfiuiΣfi\bar{x} = a + \dfrac{\Sigma f_i u_i}{\Sigma f_i}xˉ=a+Σfi​Σfi​ui​​ (no h)

    The uiu_iui​ were divided by h; the result must be multiplied back.

    xˉ=a+h×ΣfiuiΣfi\bar{x} = a + h \times \dfrac{\Sigma f_i u_i}{\Sigma f_i}xˉ=a+h×Σfi​Σfi​ui​​.

  • xˉ=h×ΣfiuiΣfi\bar{x} = h \times \dfrac{\Sigma f_i u_i}{\Sigma f_i}xˉ=h×Σfi​Σfi​ui​​ (no a)

    That is only how far the mean is from a.

    Add a.

  • uiu_iui​ below a written as positive

    xi<ax_i < axi​<a gives a negative uiu_iui​.

    Write −1, −2, … above the 0.

  • Unequal classes: h = size of the first class

    The uiu_iui​ are then fractions, and errors creep in.

    h = a common divisor of all the did_idi​.

  • Missing frequency left out of Σfi\Sigma f_iΣfi​

    The unknown class has observations too.

    Σfi=(known total)+p\Sigma f_i = (\text{known total}) + pΣfi​=(known total)+p.

Key points

  • Step deviation: ui=xi−ahu_i = \dfrac{x_i - a}{h}ui​=hxi​−a​ and xˉ=a+h×ΣfiuiΣfi\bar{x} = a + h \times \dfrac{\Sigma f_i u_i}{\Sigma f_i}xˉ=a+h×Σfi​Σfi​ui​​ — multiply by h, then add a.
  • Equal classes with a in the middle: ui=…,−2,−1,0,1,2,…u_i = \ldots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \ldotsui​=…,−2,−1,0,1,2,…
  • Unequal classes: choose h to divide every did_idi​; decimal classes: h can be a decimal such as 0.5.
  • All three methods give the same mean; choose the one with the least arithmetic, and say why.
  • Missing frequency: keep the unknown in Σfi\Sigma f_iΣfi​ and Σfiui\Sigma f_i u_iΣfi​ui​; use the total as a second equation when there are two unknowns.
