Toolkit: data, classes and the three averages

About 30 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Find the mean, median and mode of ungrouped data and of an ungrouped frequency table.
  • Read a grouped frequency table — class limits, class size and class mark; put a value on a boundary in the right class; make inclusive classes continuous.
  • Use the Σ sign for sums such as Σfi\Sigma f_iΣfi​ and Σfixi\Sigma f_i x_iΣfi​xi​.
Why this lesson exists

This chapter takes the mean, median and mode you met in Class IX and finds them for grouped data — data given in classes such as 10 – 20, 20 – 30. Every method in the chapter uses three things from Class IX: the three averages, the parts of a class (limits, size, mark) and the Σ sign. Forty students write a test. You could read out all forty marks — or you could say "the class scored about 32 on average, and most students scored between 30 and 40". The second answer is shorter and more useful. Finding one number that represents a whole set of data is what this chapter is about. This lesson brings back the tools from Class IX.

  1. The three averages (measures of central tendency)

  • Mean = sum of all the values ÷ number of values.
  • Median = the middle value after the values are arranged in order. With n values: if n is odd, the median is the (n+12)\left(\dfrac{n + 1}{2}\right)(2n+1​)th value; if n is even, it is the average of the (n2)\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)(2n​)th and the (n2+1)\left(\dfrac{n}{2} + 1\right)(2n​+1)th values.
  • Mode = the value that occurs most often.

They answer different questions: the mean shares the total equally; the median splits the data into two equal halves; the mode is the most common value.

  1. The Σ sign

The Greek capital letter Σ (sigma) means "add up all of these". If the values are x1,x2,…,xnx_1, x_2, \ldots, x_nx1​,x2​,…,xn​ and they occur f1,f2,…,fnf_1, f_2, \ldots, f_nf1​,f2​,…,fn​ times, then

  • Σfi=f1+f2+…+fn\Sigma f_i = f_1 + f_2 + \ldots + f_nΣfi​=f1​+f2​+…+fn​ = the number of observations, and
  • Σfixi=f1x1+f2x2+…+fnxn\Sigma f_i x_i = f_1x_1 + f_2x_2 + \ldots + f_nx_nΣfi​xi​=f1​x1​+f2​x2​+…+fn​xn​ = the sum of all the observations.

So for a frequency table, mean=ΣfixiΣfi\text{mean} = \dfrac{\Sigma f_i x_i}{\Sigma f_i}mean=Σfi​Σfi​xi​​. You will use this line in every mean question of the chapter.

  1. Grouped data: the parts of a class

When there are many values, we put them in classes (class intervals) such as 10 – 20, 20 – 30, 30 – 40 and count how many fall in each. For the class 20 – 30:

Word Meaning For 20 – 30
lower class limit the smaller end 20
upper class limit the larger end 30
class size (h) upper limit − lower limit 10
class mark (xix_ixi​) the middle: lower+upper2\dfrac{\text{lower} + \text{upper}}{2}2lower+upper​ 25
frequency (fif_ifi​) how many values lie in the class —

A value on a boundary goes into the higher class. A mark of exactly 30 is counted in 30 – 40, not in 20 – 30. (This is the convention the NCERT book uses.)

  1. Inclusive classes: closing the gap

Classes are sometimes written as 21 – 30, 31 – 40, 41 – 50 (often for whole-number data such as marks). There is a gap of 1 between 30 and 31. To make the classes continuous, take half the gap (0.5) away from every lower limit and add it to every upper limit:

Written as Continuous class Class mark
21 – 30 20.5 – 30.5 25.5
31 – 40 30.5 – 40.5 35.5
41 – 50 40.5 – 50.5 45.5

The class marks do not change, so for the mean you may use either form. For the median and the mode you must use the continuous form (Topics 4 and 5), because their formulas use the lower limit l and the class size h.

Worked example 1

Mean, median and mode of a short list

Question. A football team scored these goals in 11 matches: 1, 3, 0, 2, 4, 1, 1, 5, 2, 1, 3. Find the mean, the median and the mode.

Given: 11 values. Asked: the mean, the median and the mode.

Concept used: mean = sum ÷ number; median = middle value in order; mode = most frequent value (Class IX). Why it applies: the data are a plain list (ungrouped).

  1. 1

    Sum =1+3+0+2+4+1+1+5+2+1+3=23= 1 + 3 + 0 + 2 + 4 + 1 + 1 + 5 + 2 + 1 + 3 = 23=1+3+0+2+4+1+1+5+2+1+3=23; number of values =11= 11=11.

  2. 2

    Mean =2311≈2.09= \dfrac{23}{11} \approx 2.09=1123​≈2.09 goals (to two decimal places).

  3. 3

    In order: 0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5. With n=11n = 11n=11 (odd), the median is the (11+12)\left(\dfrac{11 + 1}{2}\right)(211+1​)th =6= 6=6th value, which is 2.

  4. 4

    The value 1 occurs 4 times — more often than any other — so the mode is 1.

  5. Check: five values lie before the 6th and five after it (0, 1, 1, 1, 1 | 2 | 2, 3, 3, 4, 5) ✔; and 11×2.09=22.9911 \times 2.09 = 22.9911×2.09=22.99, close to the sum 23 ✔.

  6. Answer Mean =2311≈2.09= \dfrac{23}{11} \approx 2.09=1123​≈2.09 goals; median =2= 2=2 goals; mode =1= 1=1 goal

    Common trap: Taking the middle of the list as written (the 6th value written is 1) — the list must be put in order first.

Worked example 2

The three averages from a frequency table

Question. A survey of 40 families recorded the number of children in each family: 0 children — 4 families; 1 child — 11; 2 children — 15; 3 children — 7; 4 children — 3. Find the mean, the median and the mode.

Given: values xi=0,1,2,3,4x_i = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4xi​=0,1,2,3,4 with frequencies fi=4,11,15,7,3f_i = 4, 11, 15, 7, 3fi​=4,11,15,7,3. Asked: the three averages.

Concept used: mean =ΣfixiΣfi= \dfrac{\Sigma f_i x_i}{\Sigma f_i}=Σfi​Σfi​xi​​; the median from running totals (cumulative frequencies); the mode is the value with the largest frequency. Why it applies: each value is repeated, so a frequency table is quicker than a list.

  1. 1

    Make the table with fixif_i x_ifi​xi​ and the running total:

    xix_ixi​ fif_ifi​ fixif_i x_ifi​xi​ running total
    0 4 0 4
    1 11 11 15
    2 15 30 30
    3 7 21 37
    4 3 12 40
    Total Σfi=40\Sigma f_i = 40Σfi​=40 Σfixi=74\Sigma f_i x_i = 74Σfi​xi​=74
  2. 2

    Mean =7440=1.85= \dfrac{74}{40} = 1.85=4074​=1.85 children.

  3. 3

    n=40n = 40n=40 is even, so the median is the average of the 20th and 21st values. The running total passes 15 at x=1x = 1x=1 and reaches 30 at x=2x = 2x=2, so the 16th to the 30th values are all 2. The 20th and 21st values are 2 and 2: median =2= 2=2.

  4. 4

    The largest frequency is 15, for x=2x = 2x=2: mode =2= 2=2.

  5. Check: count from the other end — the 4-child and 3-child families are 3+7=103 + 7 = 103+7=10 families, so values 11 to 25 from the top are 2s; the 20th and 21st from the top are also 2 ✔. A mean below 2 makes sense: more families have fewer than 2 children (15) than more than 2 (10).

  6. Answer Mean =1.85= 1.85=1.85; median =2= 2=2; mode =2= 2=2 (children per family)

    Common trap: Dividing Σfixi=74\Sigma f_i x_i = 74Σfi​xi​=74 by 5 (the number of different values) instead of by 40 (the number of families).

Worked example 3

Class limits, sizes and marks; inclusive classes

Question. The marks of a class are grouped as 1 – 10, 11 – 20, 21 – 30, 31 – 40. (a) Write the class size and the class mark of each class. (b) Write the classes as continuous classes.

Given: four inclusive classes. Asked: sizes, marks and the continuous classes.

Concept used: class mark =lower limit+upper limit2= \dfrac{\text{lower limit} + \text{upper limit}}{2}=2lower limit+upper limit​; to make inclusive classes continuous, move each end by half the gap. Why it applies: there is a gap of 1 between 10 and 11, 20 and 21, 30 and 31.

  1. 1

    Gap =11−10=1= 11 - 10 = 1=11−10=1, so half the gap =0.5= 0.5=0.5.

  2. 2

    Continuous classes: 0.5 – 10.5, 10.5 – 20.5, 20.5 – 30.5, 30.5 – 40.5.

  3. 3

    Class size =10.5−0.5=10= 10.5 - 0.5 = 10=10.5−0.5=10 (the same for every class). Note: 10−1=910 - 1 = 910−1=9 is not the size — it forgets the gap.

  4. 4

    Class marks: 1+102=5.5\dfrac{1 + 10}{2} = 5.521+10​=5.5, 11+202=15.5\dfrac{11 + 20}{2} = 15.5211+20​=15.5, 21+302=25.5\dfrac{21 + 30}{2} = 25.5221+30​=25.5, 31+402=35.5\dfrac{31 + 40}{2} = 35.5231+40​=35.5.

  5. Check: the class marks from the continuous classes are the same — 0.5+10.52=5.5\dfrac{0.5 + 10.5}{2} = 5.520.5+10.5​=5.5 ✔ — and consecutive class marks differ by the class size: 15.5−5.5=1015.5 - 5.5 = 1015.5−5.5=10 ✔.

  6. Answer (a) Class size 10 for each class; class marks 5.5, 15.5, 25.5, 35.5 (b) 0.5 – 10.5, 10.5 – 20.5, 20.5 – 30.5, 30.5 – 40.5

    Common trap: Taking the class size as 10−1=910 - 1 = 910−1=9. With whole-number marks, 1 – 10 holds ten possible marks (1, 2, …, 10).

Worked example 4

Putting raw data into classes

Question. Twenty students scored these marks (out of 50) in a test: 12, 25, 31, 18, 40, 27, 33, 20, 45, 30, 22, 38, 29, 35, 10, 41, 26, 30, 17, 36. Make a grouped frequency table with the classes 10 – 20, 20 – 30, 30 – 40 and 40 – 50.

Given: 20 marks. Asked: the frequency of each class.

Concept used: count the values in each class; a value on a boundary goes into the higher class. Why it applies: the marks 10, 20, 30 and 40 lie on boundaries.

  1. 1

    10 – 20: 12, 18, 10, 17 → 4 (10 is included; 20 is not).

  2. 2

    20 – 30: 25, 27, 20, 22, 29, 26 → 6 (20 is included).

  3. 3

    30 – 40: 31, 33, 30, 38, 35, 30, 36 → 7 (both 30s are included).

  4. 4

    40 – 50: 40, 45, 41 → 3.

    Marks Number of students
    10 – 20 4
    20 – 30 6
    30 – 40 7
    40 – 50 3

  5. Check: 4+6+7+3=204 + 6 + 7 + 3 = 204+6+7+3=20 ✔ — every student has been counted once.

  6. Answer 10 – 20: 4; 20 – 30: 6; 30 – 40: 7; 40 – 50: 3

    Common trap: Putting 30 in 20 – 30 and also in 30 – 40 — then the total comes to more than 20.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that getting the class mark wrong, dividing by the wrong number and forgetting to make inclusive classes continuous are among the most common reasons for lost marks in this chapter. All three start here.

  • Class mark of 20 – 30 written as 20, 30 or 30−202=5\dfrac{30 - 20}{2} = 5230−20​=5

    The class mark is the middle of the class, not an end or half the size.

    xi=20+302=25x_i = \dfrac{20 + 30}{2} = 25xi​=220+30​=25.

  • Σfixi\Sigma f_i x_iΣfi​xi​ divided by the number of classes (or of different values)

    The mean shares the total among all the observations.

    Divide by Σfi\Sigma f_iΣfi​, the total frequency.

  • Median taken from the list as written

    The median is the middle of the ordered list.

    Arrange in order first, then count.

  • 1 – 10, 11 – 20 used as they are for a median or mode; class size taken as 9

    The classes have gaps; the formulas need touching classes.

    0.5 – 10.5, 10.5 – 20.5 …; size 10.

  • A boundary value counted in both classes

    Each observation must be counted once.

    It goes into the higher class.

Key points

  • Mean = sum ÷ number; for a frequency table, xˉ=ΣfixiΣfi\bar{x} = \dfrac{\Sigma f_i x_i}{\Sigma f_i}xˉ=Σfi​Σfi​xi​​.
  • Median = middle value of the ordered data; mode = most frequent value.
  • Class size = upper limit − lower limit; class mark = lower+upper2\dfrac{\text{lower} + \text{upper}}{2}2lower+upper​.
  • A value on a boundary goes into the higher class.
  • Inclusive classes (21 – 30, 31 – 40): move each end by half the gap to make them continuous; class marks stay the same.
