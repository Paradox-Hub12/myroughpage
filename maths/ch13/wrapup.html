Wrap-up

The method card, median and mode, which measure, and mixed problems

About 30 minutes

Every question in this chapter follows the same three moves: build the table with the columns the method needs, find the class (modal class or median class) before any formula, and say what the number means for the people or things in the question. This page puts the three cards on one sheet and then gives four mixed problems in the styles of the sample papers — a sports-day case, a missing frequency followed by the mode, a less-than table, and two sections compared.

Case study: sports day

(1 mark) (ii) Write the median class. (1 mark) (iii) (A) Find the mean jump. (2 marks) OR (iii) (B) Find the modal jump. (2 marks)

Mixed example 2

A missing frequency, then the mode

Question. The scores of some students in a quiz are: 0 – 20: 5; 20 – 40: 9; 40 – 60: 16; 60 – 80: k; 80 – 100: 6. The mean score is 52.8. (a) Find k. (b) Find the modal score.

Given: mean 52.8; unknown k. Asked: k, then the mode.

Concept used: the step-deviation formula as an equation in k; then the mode formula (Topics 3 and 4). Why it applies: k is needed before the modal class's neighbour is known.

Mixed example 3

A less-than table: the median and the mean

Question. A fitness app recorded the number of steps (in thousands) walked one day by 50 users: less than 2: 4; less than 4: 12; less than 6: 27; less than 8: 39; less than 10: 50. Find the median and the mean number of steps.

Given: a less-than table; n=50n = 50n=50. Asked: the median and the mean.

Concept used: frequencies from the cumulative table; the median formula; the direct mean (Topics 2 and 5). Why it applies: both measures need the class frequencies.

Mixed example 4

Two sections compared

Question. The marks (out of 50) of two sections of 30 students each are:

  1. Marks Section A Section B
    0 – 10 2 3
    10 – 20 6 5
    20 – 30 12 8
    30 – 40 8 10
    40 – 50 2 4

    Find the mean and the median of each section. Which section did better?

    Given: two tables, n=30n = 30n=30 each. Asked: the means, the medians and a comparison.

    Concept used: the direct mean; the median formula; the mean compares groups (Topics 2, 5 and 6). Why it applies: the same classes make a fair comparison.

  2. 1

    Section A: Σfixi=10+90+300+280+90=770\Sigma f_i x_i = 10 + 90 + 300 + 280 + 90 = 770Σfi​xi​=10+90+300+280+90=770; mean =77030≈25.67= \dfrac{770}{30} \approx 25.67=30770​≈25.67. cf 2, 8, 20 → median class 20 – 30: 20+15−812×10=20+356≈25.8320 + \dfrac{15 - 8}{12} \times 10 = 20 + \dfrac{35}{6} \approx 25.8320+1215−8​×10=20+635​≈25.83.

  3. 2

    Section B: Σfixi=15+75+200+350+180=820\Sigma f_i x_i = 15 + 75 + 200 + 350 + 180 = 820Σfi​xi​=15+75+200+350+180=820; mean =82030≈27.33= \dfrac{820}{30} \approx 27.33=30820​≈27.33. cf 3, 8, 16 → median class 20 – 30: 20+15−88×10=20+8.75=28.7520 + \dfrac{15 - 8}{8} \times 10 = 20 + 8.75 = 28.7520+815−8​×10=20+8.75=28.75.

  4. 3

    Section B has the higher mean (27.33 against 25.67) and the higher median (28.75 against 25.83): Section B did better.

  5. Check: step deviation for B with a=25a = 25a=25, h=10h = 10h=10: Σfiui=−6−5+0+10+8=7\Sigma f_i u_i = -6 - 5 + 0 + 10 + 8 = 7Σfi​ui​=−6−5+0+10+8=7; 25+10×730≈27.3325 + 10 \times \dfrac{7}{30} \approx 27.3325+10×307​≈27.33 ✔.

  6. Answer A: mean ≈ 25.67, median ≈ 25.83; B: mean ≈ 27.33, median = 28.75; Section B did better.

    Common trap: Comparing only the modal classes (20 – 30 for A, 30 – 40 for B) — a modal class shows the busiest class, not how well the whole section did; compare groups by their means.
