Topic 5 of 6

Median of grouped data

About 60 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Make cumulative frequency tables of the less-than and more-than type, and change a cumulative table back into frequencies.
  • Find the median class and the median of grouped data by the formula, including inclusive classes and tables given as less-than or more-than type.
  • Find missing frequencies when the median and the total frequency are given.

A mobile company says that half its customers use less than about 14 GB of data a month. That "half below, half above" number is the median. In a list you find it by counting to the middle. In a grouped table you count to the middle class with running totals — and then a formula tells you how far into that class to go.

  1. Cumulative frequency

The cumulative frequency (cf) of a class is the number of observations up to the end of that class — the running total of the frequencies.

Type Each row says How to make it
less-than "less than 10: 5", "less than 15: 13" … (upper limits) add the frequencies from the top
more-than "10 or more: 45", "15 or more: 37" … (lower limits) start from the total and take away the frequencies from the top

Going back to frequencies: in a less-than table, frequency of a class = its cf − the cf of the class before. In a more-than table, frequency of a class = the "or more" number at its lower limit − the "or more" number at its upper limit. The last class of a more-than table keeps its own number.

  1. The median class

Let n=Σfin = \Sigma f_in=Σfi​. Work out n2\dfrac{n}{2}2n​. The median class is the first class whose cumulative frequency is greater than n2\dfrac{n}{2}2n​ (NCERT: "greater than and nearest to"). Compare n2\dfrac{n}{2}2n​ with the cf column — never with the f column.

  1. The formula

Median=l+(n2−cff)×h\text{Median} = l + \left(\frac{\frac{n}{2} - cf}{f}\right) \times hMedian=l+(f2n​−cf​)×hSwipe to see the whole equation →
Letter Meaning
l lower limit of the median class
n number of observations, Σfi\Sigma f_iΣfi​
cf cumulative frequency of the class before the median class
f frequency of the median class (not its cf)
h class size (classes of equal size, and touching)

What it says. Before the median class there are cf observations. We need n2−cf\dfrac{n}{2} - cf2n​−cf more of them. The median class holds f observations spread evenly across its width h, so we walk n2−cff\dfrac{\frac{n}{2} - cf}{f}f2n​−cf​ of the way across it.

For grouped data we use n2\dfrac{n}{2}2n​ — not n+12\dfrac{n + 1}{2}2n+1​ — whether n is odd or even.

  1. A check from the other end

Count down from the top instead: if m observations lie above the median class, then

Median=(l+h)−(n2−mf)×h.\text{Median} = (l + h) - \left(\frac{\frac{n}{2} - m}{f}\right) \times h.Median=(l+h)−(f2n​−m​)×h.Swipe to see the whole equation →

Both ways must give the same number. This uses different numbers (m and the upper limit), so it catches a slip in the first route.

  1. Inclusive classes

As for the mode, first make the classes continuous: for 11 – 15, l=10.5l = 10.5l=10.5 and h=5h = 5h=5.

Worked example 1

Cumulative frequencies and the median

Worked example 2

From a less-than table

Question. The heights of 60 plants in a nursery are given as: less than 10 cm: 3; less than 20 cm: 10; less than 30 cm: 24; less than 40 cm: 41; less than 50 cm: 52; less than 60 cm: 60. Find the median height.

Concept used: frequency = cf − cf of the class before; then the median formula. Why it applies: the formula needs the frequency f of the median class.

Worked example 3

From a more-than table

Question. The marks of 80 students are given as: 0 or more: 80; 10 or more: 74; 20 or more: 62; 30 or more: 45; 40 or more: 22; 50 or more: 9 (the highest mark is below 60). Find the median mark.

Given: a more-than table; n=80n = 80n=80. Asked: the median.

Concept used: frequency of a class = (number at its lower limit) − (number at its upper limit); then the median formula. Why it applies: "10 or more: 74" and "20 or more: 62" mean that 74−62=1274 - 62 = 1274−62=12 students scored from 10 up to (not including) 20.

  1. 1

    Frequencies: 0 – 10: 80−74=680 - 74 = 680−74=6; 10 – 20: 74−62=1274 - 62 = 1274−62=12; 20 – 30: 62−45=1762 - 45 = 1762−45=17; 30 – 40: 45−22=2345 - 22 = 2345−22=23; 40 – 50: 22−9=1322 - 9 = 1322−9=13; 50 – 60: 9.

    Marks fif_ifi​ cfcfcf
    0 – 10 6 6
    10 – 20 12 18
    20 – 30 17 35
    30 – 40 23 58 ← median
    40 – 50 13 71
    50 – 60 9 80
  2. 2

    n2=40\dfrac{n}{2} = 402n​=40; the first cf greater than 40 is 58 → median class 30 – 40.

  3. 3

    l=30l = 30l=30, cf=35cf = 35cf=35, f=23f = 23f=23, h=10h = 10h=10: Median=30+(40−3523)×10=30+5023≈32.17\text{Median} = 30 + \left(\dfrac{40 - 35}{23}\right) \times 10 = 30 + \dfrac{50}{23} \approx 32.17Median=30+(2340−35​)×10=30+2350​≈32.17.

  4. Check: the more-than table itself gives the count from the top: 22 students scored 40 or more, so m=22m = 22m=22 and 40−(40−2223)×10=40−18023≈32.1740 - \left(\dfrac{40 - 22}{23}\right) \times 10 = 40 - \dfrac{180}{23} \approx 32.1740−(2340−22​)×10=40−23180​≈32.17 ✔.

    Common trap: Treating the more-than numbers as frequencies (80, 74, 62, …) — they are running totals counted from the top.

Worked example 4

Inclusive classes

Question. The weights of 50 parcels at a post office were (in kg): 1 – 5: 6; 6 – 10: 13; 11 – 15: 18; 16 – 20: 9; 21 – 25: 4. Find the median weight.

Given: inclusive classes with a gap of 1. Asked: the median.

Concept used: make the classes continuous, then use the median formula. Why it applies: l and h must come from touching classes.

  1. 1

Worked example 5

Missing frequencies from the median

Question. The median of the distribution 0 – 10: 4; 10 – 20: a; 20 – 30: 15; 30 – 40: b; 40 – 50: 7; 50 – 60: 2 is 28, and the total frequency is 50. Find a and b.

Common mistakes!

  • Teachers say that choosing the median class from the frequencies, using the wrong cf and misreading cumulative tables are among the most common reasons for lost marks with the median.

  • Median class = class with the largest frequency

    That is the modal class.

    Compare n2\dfrac{n}{2}2n​ with the cf column.

  • n+12\dfrac{n + 1}{2}2n+1​ used for grouped data

    The formula is built on n2\dfrac{n}{2}2n​.

    Use n2\dfrac{n}{2}2n​ for grouped data, odd or even n.

  • cf of the median class used in the formula

    cf must count only the observations before the median class.

    Take the cf of the class just above it in the table.

  • A cumulative frequency used as f

    f is the median class's own frequency.

    Read f from the f column.

  • Upper limit used as l

    The walk starts at the lower limit.

    l = lower limit of the median class.

  • Less-than or more-than numbers used as frequencies

    They are running totals.

    Subtract neighbouring numbers to get frequencies.

  • Inclusive classes used as they are

    The formula needs touching classes.

    11 – 15 → 10.5 – 15.5; h = 5.

Key points

  • cf = running total; less-than tables use upper limits, more-than tables use lower limits; subtract neighbouring numbers to get back the frequencies.
  • Median class = the first class whose cf is greater than n2\dfrac{n}{2}2n​.
  • Median=l+(n2−cff)×h\text{Median} = l + \left(\dfrac{\frac{n}{2} - cf}{f}\right) \times hMedian=l+(f2n​−cf​)×h, with cf of the class before and f of the median class.
  • Check by counting down from the top: (l+h)−(n2−mf)×h(l + h) - \left(\dfrac{\frac{n}{2} - m}{f}\right) \times h(l+h)−(f2n​−m​)×h.
  • Inclusive classes: continuous limits first. Missing frequencies: the total gives one equation, the median gives the other.
