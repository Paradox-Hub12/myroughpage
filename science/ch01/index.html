Chapter 1 · Unit I

Chemical Reactions and Equations

7 topicsAbout 6–7 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Every morning, something in your kitchen is quietly changing into something new. The gas in the burner turns into carbon dioxide and water. Dosa batter rises because tiny microbes produce gas. A cut apple turns brown on the plate. Each of these is a chemical reaction — and chemists have a short, exact "language" for writing them down.

This chapter teaches you that language. Once you can read and write chemical equations, the rest of Class 10 chemistry (acids, metals, carbon compounds) becomes much easier, because every chapter after this one is written in equations.

What you'll learn

  • Spot the four signs of a chemical reaction — a change in state, a change in colour, a gas given off, a change in temperature — and use them to decide whether an everyday change is a chemical reaction.
  • Write correct formulae using valency and common ions; count atoms in formulae with brackets; remember which gases exist as pairs of atoms (HX2\ce{H2}HX2​, OX2\ce{O2}OX2​, NX2\ce{N2}NX2​, ClX2\ce{Cl2}ClX2​).
  • Turn a sentence into a word equation and then a chemical equation; explain why equations must be balanced; balance equations step by step; add state symbols and conditions.
  • Recognise reactions where substances join to make one product, and reactions that give out heat.
  • Recognise reactions where one substance breaks down (by heat, light or electricity); explain the 2 : 1 gas ratio when water is split; recognise reactions that take in energy.
  • Tell apart reactions where one element pushes another out, and reactions where two compounds swap partners; recognise a precipitate.
  • Say which substance is oxidised and which is reduced; explain rusting and why old oil smells bad.
  • Use a "Which type is it?" flowchart to classify any reaction.
