Combination reactions and exothermic reactions

About 30 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Identify a combination reaction and write its equation.
  • Define exothermic reactions and give examples (burning, respiration, quicklime + water).

Watch painters preparing chuna (lime) for whitewashing. They tip white lumps into a drum of water — and the water hisses, bubbles and becomes too hot to touch. Nobody lit a fire. Where did all that heat come from?

Combination reactions: "many in, one out"

In a combination reaction, two or more reactants join together to form a single product.

A+B⟶AB\text{A} + \text{B} \longrightarrow \text{AB}A+B⟶ABSwipe to see the whole equation →

The reactants can be elements or compounds. The key test is simple: count the products. If there is exactly one, it is a combination reaction.

The lab reaction: quicklime and water

Calcium oxide (CaO\ce{CaO}CaO) is commonly called quicklime. Calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)X2\ce{Ca(OH)2}Ca(OH)X2​) is called slaked lime.

Lab notebook · do → see → shows
Do
Put a small amount of quicklime in a beaker. Add water slowly. Carefully touch the outside of the beaker.
See
The mixture hisses and may bubble; the beaker becomes very hot.
Shows
Calcium oxide and water have combined to make one product, calcium hydroxide, and a large amount of heat has been released.
CaO(s)+HX2O(l)→Ca(OH)X2(aq)+heat\ce{CaO(s) + H2O(l) -> Ca(OH)2(aq)} + \text{heat}CaO(s)+HX2​O(l)​Ca(OH)X2​(aq)+heatSwipe to see the whole equation →

Two reactants, one product → combination. Heat given out → exothermic (see below).

Safety

quicklime and its hot mixture can burn skin and eyes. Add water slowly, in small amounts, and never hold your face over the beaker.

Exothermic reactions: heat comes out

A reaction that gives out heat to its surroundings as the products form is called an exothermic reaction ("exo" = out, "thermic" = heat). The surroundings get warmer.

Where does the heat come from? Every substance stores energy in its chemical bonds. If the products store less energy than the reactants, the extra energy is released, mostly as heat (sometimes also as light).

Examples you know:

  1. Burning fuels. LPG, CNG, kerosene, wood and coal all release heat when they burn. CNG is mainly methane:
CHX4(g)+2 OX2(g)→COX2(g)+2 HX2O(g)+heat\ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O(g)} + \text{heat}CHX4​(g)+2OX2​(g)​COX2​(g)+2HX2​O(g)+heatSwipe to see the whole equation →
  1. Respiration. Your body breaks down carbohydrates (from rice, roti, potatoes) into glucose. In your cells, glucose combines with oxygen and releases energy — that is why your body stays warm at about 37 °C.
CX6HX12OX6(aq)+6 OX2(aq)→6 COX2(aq)+6 HX2O(l)+energy\ce{C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(aq) -> 6CO2(aq) + 6H2O(l)} + \text{energy}CX6​HX12​OX6​(aq)+6OX2​(aq)​6COX2​(aq)+6HX2​O(l)+energySwipe to see the whole equation →
  1. Composting. When vegetable peels rot into compost, the heap becomes warm inside. The decomposition of vegetable matter is exothermic.
  2. Quicklime + water, as above.

Two ideas, not one

"Combination" and "exothermic" answer different questions:

  • Combination is about what happens to the substances (many → one).
  • Exothermic is about what happens to the energy (heat out).

So one reaction can be both — like quicklime + water, or burning magnesium. But:

  • Burning methane is exothermic but not a combination reaction — it gives two products.
  • Most combination reactions are exothermic, but you should still check each one.

"If it releases heat, why do I need to light it?"

A matchstick, a gas stove and a magnesium ribbon all need a spark or flame to start. That small starting push is used to begin breaking bonds. Once the reaction is going, it gives out far more heat than was put in, so overall it is exothermic.

Worked example 1

Identify and justify

Question. Classify the reaction 2 HX2(g)+OX2(g)→2 HX2O(l)\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)}2HX2​(g)+OX2​(g)​2HX2​O(l). Is it a combination reaction? Is it exothermic? (Hydrogen is used as a rocket fuel.)

Given: the balanced equation; the hint that hydrogen is a fuel. Asked: reaction type(s), with reasons.

Concept used: combination = a single product; exothermic = heat released. Why it applies: we can count the products from the equation, and the fuel hint tells us about heat.

  1. 1

    Count the reactants: hydrogen and oxygen → two reactants.

  2. 2

    Count the products: only water → one product.

  3. 3

    Two or more reactants forming one product → combination reaction.

  4. 4

    Hydrogen is used as a fuel, so its reaction with oxygen releases a lot of heat → exothermic.

  5. Check: the equation is balanced (H 4 | 4, O 2 | 2), and "2H₂O" is one substance with a coefficient, so there really is only one product ✔.

  6. Answer A combination reaction, and exothermic.

    Common trap: Thinking "2 HX2O\ce{2H2O}2HX2​O" means two products. The 2 is just a coefficient — it is still one substance, water.

Worked example 2

Exothermic but not combination

Question. Natural gas burns: CHX4(g)+2 OX2(g)→COX2(g)+2 HX2O(g)\ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O(g)}CHX4​(g)+2OX2​(g)​COX2​(g)+2HX2​O(g). A student says, "It releases heat, so it must be a combination reaction." Is the student right?

Given: the equation for burning methane and the student's claim. Asked: evaluate the claim.

Concept used: combination and exothermic are separate ideas. Why it applies: the student has mixed up a statement about energy with a statement about substances.

  1. 1

    Count the products: COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ and HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O → two different products.

  2. 2

    A combination reaction must give one product. So this is not combination.

  3. 3

    It does release heat → it is exothermic.

  4. Check: compare C+OX2→COX2\ce{C + O2 -> CO2}C+OX2​​COX2​ — one product, so that one is combination. Methane gives two products, so it is not ✔.

  5. Answer The student is wrong. The reaction is exothermic, but not a combination reaction because it forms two products. (It is the burning of a fuel — see the wrap-up.)

    Common trap: Linking "releases heat" with "combination". The reaction type depends on the substances; exothermic depends on the energy.

Worked example 3

From a description to an equation

Question. When coal (carbon) burns completely in air, it forms carbon dioxide and gives out heat. Write the balanced equation with state symbols and classify the reaction in two ways.

Given: the reaction in words, and that heat is given out. Asked: balanced equation with states; two classifications.

Concept used: writing equations (Topic 3); combination; exothermic. Why it applies: the sentence names the reactants and the single product, and tells us heat is released.

  1. 1

    Word equation: carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide.

  2. 2

    Formulae: carbon is written C (not diatomic); oxygen gas is OX2\ce{O2}OX2​; carbon dioxide is COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​.

  3. 3

    Count: C 1 | 1; O 2 | 2 → already balanced.

  4. 4

    States: coal is solid; oxygen and carbon dioxide are gases.

    C(s)+OX2(g)→COX2(g)+heat\ce{C(s) + O2(g) -> CO2(g)} + \text{heat}C(s)+OX2​(g)​COX2​(g)+heatSwipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Two reactants → one product → combination.

  6. 6

    Heat given out → exothermic.

  7. Check: C 1 | 1, O 2 | 2 ✔; only one product, so "combination" fits ✔.

  8. Answer C(s)+OX2(g)→COX2(g)\ce{C(s) + O2(g) -> CO2(g)}C(s)+OX2​(g)​COX2​(g) + heat — combination and exothermic.

    Common trap: Writing C+O→COX2\ce{C + O -> CO2}C+O​COX2​. Oxygen gas is diatomic, and that equation is unbalanced.

Common mistakes!

  • "Any reaction that releases heat is a combination reaction."

    Exothermic is about energy; combination is about the number of products.

    Ask two separate questions: how many products? and heat in or out?

  • "Exothermic reactions need no energy to start."

    Magnesium, LPG and matches all need a flame or spark to start.

    Exothermic means overall more heat comes out than goes in.

  • Counting "2 HX2O\ce{2H2O}2HX2​O" as two products

    A coefficient does not make a new substance.

    Count different substances, not coefficients.

  • Calling quicklime "slaked lime"

    Quicklime = CaO\ce{CaO}CaO; slaked lime = Ca(OH)X2\ce{Ca(OH)2}Ca(OH)X2​ (after water is added).

    Remember: water slakes (satisfies) the lime.

Key points

  • Combination: two or more reactants → one product, e.g. CaO+HX2O→Ca(OH)X2\ce{CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2}CaO+HX2​O​Ca(OH)X2​.
  • Exothermic: heat is released; the surroundings get warmer.
  • Examples of exothermic reactions: burning fuels, respiration, composting, quicklime + water.
  • A reaction can be both combination and exothermic — but the two words answer different questions.
  • Many exothermic reactions need a small push (a flame) to start.
