Wrap-up

Which type is it?

About 25 minutes

Board questions rarely say "here is a displacement reaction". They give you an equation or an observation and ask you to identify the type — and many reactions carry more than one label. This flowchart gives you a fixed routine.

The key idea: a main type, plus extra labels

Most reactions in this chapter get one main type — combination, decomposition, displacement or double displacement. Some reactions fit none of the four. The most important example is the burning of fuels (methane, LPG, kerosene, wood): it is labelled exothermic and redox, but it is not one of the four main types. (You will study burning of fuels as combustion in Chapter 4.)

Then add every extra label that fits:

Main type (at most one) Extra labels (add every one that fits)
Combination · Decomposition · Displacement · Double displacement · or none of these Precipitation · Neutralisation (Chapter 2) · Redox · Exothermic · Endothermic

Text version of the flowchart

Stage 1 — find the main type (ask in this order and stop at the first "yes")

  1. Write the balanced equation and count the different substances on each side.
  2. Only one product? → Combination.
  3. Only one reactant? → Decomposition. Name it by the energy used: heat → thermal; electricity → electrolytic; light → photolytic.
  4. Is a fuel (a compound of carbon and hydrogen, or a material made of such compounds — methane, LPG, wax, wood, or glucose in respiration) reacting with oxygen? → None of the four main types. Label it exothermic and redox. (Chapter 4 calls this combustion.)
  5. Is a reactant a free element, and does a different element come out free? → Displacement.
  6. Are both reactants compounds that exchange partners, with no free element formed? → Double displacement. (Sometimes one swapped product then splits into water and a gas — for example, a carbonate with an acid gives carbon dioxide and water. It is still double displacement.) If an insoluble solid forms → also precipitation. If it is acid + base → salt + water → also neutralisation.
  7. None of the above? → None of the four main types. Go on to Stage 2.

Stage 2 — add every extra label that fits

  1. Redox?
    - Not redox at Class 10 level: a decomposition (only one reactant), a double displacement (partners swap; oxygen and hydrogen stay inside their groups such as SOX4\ce{SO4}SOX4​, COX3\ce{CO3}COX3​, OH), or two compounds simply joining (such as CaO+HX2O\ce{CaO + H2O}CaO+HX2​O).
    - Otherwise, it is redox if any of these is true:
    • (a) Oxygen gas (OX2\ce{O2}OX2​) is a reactant. The substance it joins is oxidised; oxygen is reduced.
    • (b) One reactant loses oxygen to a different reactant. The one losing oxygen is reduced; the one gaining it is oxidised.
    • (c) One reactant loses hydrogen to a different reactant. The one losing hydrogen is oxidised; the one gaining it is reduced.
    • If none is true, do not label it redox.
  2. Energy? Heat given out → exothermic. Energy taken in (heat, light or electricity) → endothermic. If the question gives no information, don't guess.

Beyond the syllabus: in Class 11 you will learn a wider, electron-based definition of redox. Under it, some reactions that we do not label redox here (for example a metal displacing another metal from its salt, or the electrolysis of water) are also redox. For Class 10, use the rule above.

Six mixed worked examples — identify and justify

Mixed example 1

Question. Classify: MgCOX3(s)→heatMgO(s)+COX2(g)\ce{MgCO3(s) ->[\text{heat}] MgO(s) + CO2(g)}MgCOX3​(s)heat​MgO(s)+COX2​(g)

Given: the balanced equation, with heat over the arrow. Asked: every label that fits.

Concept used: the two-stage routine. Why it applies: it gives the main type first, then extra labels.

  1. 1

    One product? No — two products.

  2. 2

    One reactant? Yes (MgCOX3\ce{MgCO3}MgCOX3​) → decomposition.

  3. 3

    Energy used: heat → thermal decomposition.

  4. 4

    Redox? It is a decomposition (only one reactant) → not redox at Class 10 level.

  5. 5

    Energy: heat has to be supplied → endothermic.

  6. Check: Mg 1 | 1, C 1 | 1, O 3 | 3 ✔ — and the result matches the very similar CaCOX3\ce{CaCO3}CaCOX3​ example in Topic 5.

  7. Answer Thermal decomposition; endothermic.

    Common trap: Calling it redox because "MgCOX3\ce{MgCO3}MgCOX3​ loses oxygen". With only one reactant there is no other substance to take the oxygen.

Mixed example 2

Question. Classify: Mg(s)+2 AgNOX3(aq)→Mg(NOX3)X2(aq)+2 Ag(s)\ce{Mg(s) + 2AgNO3(aq) -> Mg(NO3)2(aq) + 2Ag(s)}Mg(s)+2AgNOX3​(aq)​Mg(NOX3​)X2​(aq)+2Ag(s)

Given: the balanced equation. Asked: every label that fits.

Concept used: the two-stage routine; the reactivity ladder (Topic 6). Why it applies: a free metal meets a salt solution.

  1. 1

    One product? No. One reactant? No.

  2. 2

    A fuel burning? No.

  3. 3

    Free element among the reactants (Mg), and a different element out free (Ag)? Yes → displacement. (Magnesium is far above silver on the ladder, so the reaction happens.)

  4. 4

    Redox? No oxygen gas; no reactant loses oxygen or hydrogen to another (the nitrate group stays whole) → not labelled redox at Class 10 level.

  5. Check: Mg 1 | 1, Ag 2 | 2, N 2 | 2, O 6 | 6 ✔.

  6. Answer Displacement reaction. (You would see shiny grey-white silver deposit on the magnesium.)

    Common trap: Calling it double displacement because the nitrate "moves". The reactant Mg is a free element, so it is displacement.

Mixed example 3

Question. Classify: 2 SOX2(g)+OX2(g)→2 SOX3(g)\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) -> 2SO3(g)}2SOX2​(g)+OX2​(g)​2SOX3​(g) (a step in making sulphuric acid)

Given: the balanced equation. Asked: every label that fits.

Concept used: the two-stage routine. Why it applies: oxygen gas reacts with a compound.

  1. 1

    One product? Yes (SOX3\ce{SO3}SOX3​) → combination. (Stage 1 stops here; SOX2\ce{SO2}SOX2​ is not a fuel anyway.)

  2. 2

    Redox? Test (a): oxygen gas is a reactant → SOX2\ce{SO2}SOX2​, which joins with it, is oxidised; oxygen is reduced → redox.

  3. Check: S 2 | 2, O 4 + 2 = 6 | 6 ✔.

  4. Answer Combination reaction; also redox — sulphur dioxide is oxidised.

    Common trap: Treating every reaction with OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ as "burning of a fuel". Only compounds of carbon and hydrogen are fuels here.

Mixed example 4

Question. Blue copper sulphate solution is mixed with sodium hydroxide solution and a pale blue solid forms. Classify: CuSOX4(aq)+2 NaOH(aq)→Cu(OH)X2(s)+NaX2SOX4(aq)\ce{CuSO4(aq) + 2NaOH(aq) -> Cu(OH)2(s) + Na2SO4(aq)}CuSOX4​(aq)+2NaOH(aq)​Cu(OH)X2​(s)+NaX2​SOX4​(aq)

Given: the balanced equation and the observation of a solid. Asked: every label that fits.

Concept used: the two-stage routine. Why it applies: two solutions of compounds are mixed.

  1. 1

    One product? No. One reactant? No. A fuel? No.

  2. 2

    Free element among the reactants? No.

  3. 3

    Both reactants are compounds, and the products are partner swaps (Cu with OH; Na with SOX4\ce{SO4}SOX4​) → double displacement.

  4. 4

    Cu(OH)X2\ce{Cu(OH)2}Cu(OH)X2​ is an insoluble solid → also precipitation.

  5. 5

    Redox? It is a double displacement — the SOX4\ce{SO4}SOX4​ and OH groups only change partners → not redox.

  6. Check: Cu 1 | 1, S 1 | 1, O 4 + 2 = 6 | 2 + 4 = 6, Na 2 | 2, H 2 | 2 ✔.

  7. Answer Double displacement and precipitation reaction.

    Common trap: Saying "CuSOX4\ce{CuSO4}CuSOX4​ gains hydrogen, so it is reduced". The hydrogen arrives inside the OH group, which just swaps partners.

Mixed example 5

Question. Classify: CHX4(g)+2 OX2(g)→COX2(g)+2 HX2O(g)\ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O(g)}CHX4​(g)+2OX2​(g)​COX2​(g)+2HX2​O(g)

Given: the balanced equation for burning methane (CNG, biogas). Asked: every label that fits.

Concept used: the two-stage routine. Why it applies: a fuel reacts with oxygen.

  1. 1

    One product? No (two). One reactant? No.

  2. 2

    Is a fuel (a compound of carbon and hydrogen) reacting with oxygen? Yes → none of the four main types — it is the burning of a fuel.

  3. 3

    Redox? Test (a): oxygen gas is a reactant → methane is oxidised, oxygen is reduced → redox.

  4. 4

    Energy: heat is given out → exothermic.

  5. Check: C 1 | 1, H 4 | 4, O 4 | 2 + 2 = 4 ✔.

  6. Answer Not one of the four main types — burning of a fuel; redox and exothermic.

    Common trap: Calling it displacement just because a free element (OX2\ce{O2}OX2​) reacts. Stage 1 asks about fuels before displacement — and no element comes out free.

Mixed example 6

Question. Classify: 3 Fe(s)+4 HX2O(g)→FeX3OX4(s)+4 HX2(g)\ce{3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) -> Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g)}3Fe(s)+4HX2​O(g)​FeX3​OX4​(s)+4HX2​(g)

Given: the balanced equation (iron with steam). Asked: every label that fits.

Concept used: the two-stage routine. Why it applies: a free metal reacts with a compound.

  1. 1

    One product? No. One reactant? No. A fuel? No.

  2. 2

    Free element among the reactants (Fe), and a different element out free (HX2\ce{H2}HX2​)? Yes → displacement.

  3. 3

    Redox? Test (b): water loses oxygen to iron → water is reduced, iron is oxidised → redox. (Water is the oxidising agent; iron is the reducing agent.)

  4. Check: Fe 3 | 3, H 8 | 8, O 4 | 4 ✔.

  5. Answer Displacement reaction — and also a redox reaction.

    Lesson: one reaction can carry two correct labels. In the exam, give every label the question asks for, each with its reason.
