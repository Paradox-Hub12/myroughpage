The whole chapter on one page

Chemical reaction — new substances (products) form from reactants. Signs: change in state, change in colour, gas evolved, change in temperature.

Writing equations — sentence → word equation → skeletal equation (correct formulae) → balanced (same number of each atom on both sides, because of the law of conservation of mass) → state symbols (s), (l), (g), (aq) and conditions over the arrow.

Balancing (hit-and-trial) — never change a formula (box rule); count → start with the biggest formula → one element at a time → recount → smallest whole numbers. Treat unchanged polyatomic ions as one block. For fuels: C → H → O last.

Energy — exothermic: heat released (burning, respiration, quicklime + water). Endothermic: energy absorbed (most decompositions, photosynthesis).

Oxidation = gain of O / loss of H. Reduction = loss of O / gain of H. Oxidising agent gets reduced; reducing agent gets oxidised. Always name the reactant (lead oxide is reduced, not lead).

Effects of oxidation — Corrosion: metals attacked by air, moisture, acids (rust on iron = reddish-brown; silver → black AgX2S\ce{Ag2S}AgX2​S; copper → green). Prevent by paint, oil, galvanising. Rancidity: oxidation of fats and oils → bad smell/taste. Prevent by antioxidants, airtight containers, nitrogen flushing, refrigeration.

Lab facts to remember — Mg burns with a dazzling white flame → white MgO\ce{MgO}MgO. Electrolysis of water: HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ at cathode, OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ at anode, 2 : 1. FeSOX4\ce{FeSO4}FeSOX4​ crystals: green → reddish-brown residue, smell of burning sulphur. Iron nail in CuSOX4\ce{CuSO4}CuSOX4​: brown copper coat, blue → pale green. Lead nitrate on heating: brown NOX2\ce{NO2}NOX2​ fumes.

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Balanced equation: equal number of atoms of each element on both sides.
  • Combination: two or more substances → a single product.
  • Decomposition: a single substance → two or more simpler substances.
  • Displacement: a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.
  • Double displacement: exchange of ions between two compounds.
  • Precipitate: insoluble solid formed in a reaction between solutions.
  • Exothermic / endothermic: heat released / energy absorbed.
  • Oxidation / reduction: gain of O or loss of H / loss of O or gain of H.
  • Corrosion: the slow damage of a metal's surface by things in its surroundings — oxygen, moisture, acids.
  • Rancidity: oxidation of fats and oils, changing smell and taste.
