Topic 5 of 7

Decomposition reactions and endothermic reactions

About 40 minutes · 4 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Identify decomposition reactions and classify them as thermal, electrolytic or photolytic (by heat, electricity, light).
  • Explain why the gas volumes in the electrolysis of water are in a 2:1 ratio.
  • Define endothermic reactions and explain why decomposition usually needs energy.
  • Explain why decomposition is the opposite of combination.

Why does the chemist sell hydrogen peroxide and some medicines in dark brown bottles? Because light can break certain substances apart. In Topic 4, substances joined. In this topic, they break up — and to break up, they need energy from somewhere: heat, light or electricity.

Decomposition: "one in, many out"

In a decomposition reaction, a single reactant breaks down into two or more simpler products.

AB⟶A+B\text{AB} \longrightarrow \text{A} + \text{B}AB⟶A+BSwipe to see the whole equation →

The key test: count the reactants. If there is exactly one, and it gives two or more products, it is decomposition.

Breaking bonds needs energy. Depending on what kind of energy is supplied, we name three kinds of decomposition:

Kind Energy used Name of the process Example
Thermal decomposition heat thermolysis heating limestone, ferrous sulphate, lead nitrate
Electrolytic decomposition electricity electrolysis splitting water
Photolytic decomposition light photolysis silver chloride in sunlight

  1. Thermal decomposition — breaking with heat

Heating ferrous sulphate crystals (a CBSE practical)

Do
Put about 2 g of green ferrous sulphate crystals in a dry boiling tube. Hold it with a test-tube holder, tilt it so the mouth points away from you and everyone else, and heat it over a burner. To smell the gas, do not sniff the tube directly — wave (waft) the air from the tube towards your nose with your hand.
See
First, droplets of water appear on the cooler upper part of the tube and the green crystals turn whitish. On stronger heating, a smell like burning sulphur is noticed and a reddish-brown solid is left.

Ferric oxide (FeX2OX3\ce{Fe2O3}FeX2​OX3​) is the reddish-brown solid; sulphur dioxide and sulphur trioxide are the gases with the sharp smell. One reactant → three products → thermal decomposition.

Limestone (calcium carbonate) gives quicklime (calcium oxide), used to make cement.

  1. Electrolytic decomposition — breaking with electricity

Electrolysis of water

Lab notebook · do → see → shows
Do
Fit two carbon rods (electrodes) through the bottom of a plastic container and connect them to a 6 V battery. Fill the container with water and add a few drops of dilute sulphuric acid. Fill two test tubes with water and turn them upside down over the electrodes. Switch on.
See
Bubbles rise at both electrodes and push water out of the test tubes. The tube over the negative electrode (cathode) collects twice as much gas as the tube over the positive electrode (anode).
Test
Testing the gases (done by the teacher): the gas from the cathode burns with a "pop" sound → hydrogen. The gas from the anode makes a glowing or burning splint burn more brightly → oxygen.
Shows
Electricity has split water into hydrogen and oxygen:
2 HX2O(l)→electric current2 HX2(g)+OX2(g)\ce{2H2O(l) ->[\text{electric current}] 2H2(g) + O2(g)}2HX2​O(l)electric current​2HX2​(g)+OX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →

Why add acid? Pure water conducts electricity very poorly. A few drops of acid provide ions that carry the current, so the reaction can happen.

Why is the volume ratio 2 : 1? Look at the formula HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O: every water molecule has 2 hydrogen atoms for every 1 oxygen atom. Both gases are made of two-atom molecules (HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ and OX2\ce{O2}OX2​), so the balanced equation gives 2 molecules of hydrogen for every 1 molecule of oxygen. At the same temperature and pressure, equal volumes of gases contain equal numbers of molecules, so twice as many molecules take up twice the volume.

It is not because hydrogen is lighter. The ratio comes from the formula of water.

(The idea that equal volumes of gases hold equal numbers of molecules is Avogadro's law — beyond the syllabus; you only need the "2 H for every 1 O in water" reason.)

These reactions were the basis of black-and-white photography: light falling on film coated with silver salts formed dark silver where the light was brightest. It is also why silver chloride is stored in dark-coloured bottles.

Endothermic reactions: energy goes in

A reaction in which energy is absorbed (taken in) from the surroundings is called an endothermic reaction ("endo" = in). The energy may be heat, light or electricity. If heat is absorbed from the surroundings, the reaction mixture or container feels cold.

Why are most decomposition reactions endothermic? To break a compound apart, its bonds must be broken, and breaking bonds needs energy. That is exactly why we have to keep heating limestone, keep the current flowing for water, or keep the silver chloride in sunlight. Take away the energy supply and the reaction stops.

Other endothermic examples:

  • Photosynthesis — plants absorb sunlight to make glucose.
  • Mixing solid barium hydroxide with ammonium chloride — the test tube becomes noticeably cold (you will try this in the NCERT in-text activity).

Decomposition is the opposite of combination

Compare these pairs:

Combination (many → one) Decomposition (one → many)
CaO(s)+COX2(g)→CaCOX3(s)\ce{CaO(s) + CO2(g) -> CaCO3(s)}CaO(s)+COX2​(g)​CaCOX3​(s) CaCOX3(s)→heatCaO(s)+COX2(g)\ce{CaCO3(s) ->[\text{heat}] CaO(s) + CO2(g)}CaCOX3​(s)heat​CaO(s)+COX2​(g)
2 HX2(g)+OX2(g)→2 HX2O(l)\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)}2HX2​(g)+OX2​(g)​2HX2​O(l) 2 HX2O(l)→electric current2 HX2(g)+OX2(g)\ce{2H2O(l) ->[\text{electric current}] 2H2(g) + O2(g)}2HX2​O(l)electric current​2HX2​(g)+OX2​(g)

In each row, the products of one reaction are the reactants of the other. Combination usually gives out energy; decomposition usually takes in energy. They are mirror images.

Worked example 1

Classify fully

Question. Classify CaCOX3(s)→heatCaO(s)+COX2(g)\ce{CaCO3(s) ->[\text{heat}] CaO(s) + CO2(g)}CaCOX3​(s)heat​CaO(s)+COX2​(g) as completely as you can.

Given: the equation, with heat over the arrow. Asked: every type that fits.

Concept used: decomposition (one reactant → several products); the energy source names the kind; continuous energy input means endothermic. Why it applies: there is one reactant, and heat is written over the arrow.

  1. 1

    Count reactants: one (CaCOX3\ce{CaCO3}CaCOX3​). Count products: two (CaO\ce{CaO}CaO, COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​) → decomposition.

  2. 2

    The energy supplied is heat → thermal decomposition.

  3. 3

    Heat must be supplied all the time for the reaction to continue → endothermic.

  4. Check: Ca 1 | 1, C 1 | 1, O 3 | 3 ✔. It is the reverse of CaO+COX2→CaCOX3\ce{CaO + CO2 -> CaCO3}CaO+COX2​​CaCOX3​ (combination), as expected for a decomposition ✔.

  5. Answer Thermal decomposition reaction; endothermic.

    Common trap: Calling it a combination reaction because "two things are formed". Combination is many → one; this is one → many.

Worked example 2

Using the 2 : 1 ratio

Question. In the electrolysis of water, 12 mL of gas is collected at the anode. (a) Which gas is it? (b) How much gas is collected at the cathode in the same time, and which gas is it? (c) Explain the ratio.

Given: 12 mL of gas at the anode. Asked: (a) the gas; (b) the cathode gas and its volume; (c) the reason for the ratio.

Concept used: in the electrolysis of water, oxygen forms at the anode (+) and hydrogen at the cathode (−), in a 2 : 1 volume ratio. Why it applies: the question gives one volume and asks for the other.

  1. 1

    (a) The anode is the positive electrode; oxygen collects there.

  2. 2

    (b) Hydrogen (at the cathode) is twice the volume of oxygen.

    volume of hydrogen=2×12 mL\text{volume of hydrogen} = 2 \times 12\ \text{mL}volume of hydrogen=2×12 mLSwipe to see the whole equation →
    volume of hydrogen=24 mL\text{volume of hydrogen} = 24\ \text{mL}volume of hydrogen=24 mLSwipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    (c) The balanced equation is 2 HX2O→2 HX2+OX2\ce{2H2O -> 2H2 + O2}2HX2​O​2HX2​+OX2​: water has 2 H atoms for every 1 O atom, so 2 molecules of HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ form for each molecule of OX2\ce{O2}OX2​. Twice the molecules → twice the volume.

  4. Check: 24 : 12 = 2 : 1 ✔, and the larger volume is at the cathode, as it should be ✔.

  5. Answer (a) Oxygen. (b) 24 mL of hydrogen. (c) Water has 2 H atoms for every 1 O atom, so twice as much hydrogen gas is formed.

    Common trap: Putting hydrogen at the anode. Hydrogen forms at the negative electrode (cathode).

Worked example 3

Light as the energy source

Question. Silver bromide on a black-and-white photographic film darkens where light falls on it. Write the balanced equation and name the type of reaction.

Given: silver bromide decomposes in light. Asked: the balanced equation and the reaction type.

Concept used: photolytic decomposition; bromine is diatomic. Why it applies: one compound breaks down, and the energy is light.

  1. 1

    Reactant: silver bromide, AgBr (Ag = 1, Br = 1). Products: silver, Ag, and bromine, BrX2\ce{Br2}BrX2​ (diatomic).

  2. 2

    Skeletal: AgBr→Ag+BrX2\ce{AgBr -> Ag + Br2}AgBr​Ag+BrX2​. Count: Br 1 | 2.

  3. 3

    Put 2 in front of AgBr: Br 2 | 2 ✔; now Ag 2 | 1.

  4. 4

    Put 2 in front of Ag: Ag 2 | 2 ✔.

    2 AgBr(s)→light2 Ag(s)+BrX2(g)\ce{2AgBr(s) ->[\text{light}] 2Ag(s) + Br2(g)}2AgBr(s)light​2Ag(s)+BrX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    One reactant → two products, energy supplied as light → photolytic decomposition (and endothermic).

  6. Check: Ag 2 | 2; Br 2 | 2 ✔. The dark specks on the film are silver, a product of this reaction ✔.

  7. Answer 2 AgBr(s)→light2 Ag(s)+BrX2(g)\ce{2AgBr(s) ->[\text{light}] 2Ag(s) + Br2(g)}2AgBr(s)light​2Ag(s)+BrX2​(g) — photolytic decomposition.

    In the lab…

    bromine is actually a red-brown liquid at room temperature, so BrX2(l)\ce{Br2(l)}BrX2​(l) would be more accurate. NCERT writes BrX2(g)\ce{Br2(g)}BrX2​(g); use the NCERT form in the exam.

    Common trap: Writing AgBr→Ag+Br\ce{AgBr -> Ag + Br}AgBr​Ag+Br. Bromine, like chlorine, is diatomic.

Worked example 4

Opposite reactions

Question. Show, with one pair of equations, why decomposition is called the opposite of combination.

Given: — Asked: a pair of equations showing the two types are opposites.

Concept used: combination (many → one) and decomposition (one → many). Why it applies: choosing one compound that can be both made and broken shows the reversal clearly.

  1. 1

    Choose a compound that can be both made and broken: water.

  2. 2

    Combination: 2 HX2(g)+OX2(g)→2 HX2O(l)\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)}2HX2​(g)+OX2​(g)​2HX2​O(l) — two reactants → one product; energy released.

  3. 3

    Decomposition: 2 HX2O(l)→electric current2 HX2(g)+OX2(g)\ce{2H2O(l) ->[\text{electric current}] 2H2(g) + O2(g)}2HX2​O(l)electric current​2HX2​(g)+OX2​(g) — one reactant → two products; energy absorbed.

  4. 4

    The reactants of the first are the products of the second, and vice versa.

  5. Check: both equations are balanced (H 4 | 4, O 2 | 2), and they contain exactly the same substances on opposite sides ✔.

  6. Answer In combination, substances join into one product; in decomposition, one substance breaks into those same simpler substances. One is the reverse of the other.

    Common trap: Using two unrelated reactions. Only a pair with the same substances shows the "opposite" clearly.

Common mistakes!

  • "All decomposition needs heat."

    Light (AgCl\ce{AgCl}AgCl) and electricity (water) also decompose substances.

    Name the energy: heat → thermal; electricity → electrolytic; light → photolytic.

  • "2 : 1 because hydrogen is lighter."

    The ratio comes from the formula HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O.

    2 H atoms for every 1 O atom → 2 HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ : 1 OX2\ce{O2}OX2​.

  • Hydrogen at the anode

    Hydrogen forms at the negative electrode.

    Cathode (−) → hydrogen; anode (+) → oxygen.

  • Heating a boiling tube pointed at a classmate

    Hot gases and spray can escape.

    Point the mouth away from everyone; waft to smell.

  • Writing AgCl→Ag+Cl\ce{AgCl -> Ag + Cl}AgCl​Ag+Cl

    Chlorine gas is diatomic and the equation is unbalanced.

    2 AgCl→2 Ag+ClX2\ce{2AgCl -> 2Ag + Cl2}2AgCl​2Ag+ClX2​.

Key points

  • Decomposition: one reactant → two or more products. It is the opposite of combination.
  • Thermal (heat): CaCOX3\ce{CaCO3}CaCOX3​, FeSOX4\ce{FeSO4}FeSOX4​, Pb(NOX3)X2\ce{Pb(NO3)2}Pb(NOX3​)X2​. Electrolytic (electricity): water. Photolytic (light): AgCl\ce{AgCl}AgCl, AgBr\ce{AgBr}AgBr.
  • Electrolysis of water gives hydrogen at the cathode and oxygen at the anode, 2 : 1 by volume, because water has 2 H for every 1 O.
  • Endothermic: energy is absorbed. Most decompositions are endothermic because breaking bonds needs energy.
