Chapter 5 · Unit II

Life Processes

7 topicsAbout 9–10 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Right now, while you read this sentence, your heart is pushing blood through about a lakh kilometres of tubes, your lungs are swapping carbon dioxide for oxygen, your small intestine is still working on breakfast, and your kidneys are cleaning your blood. A neem tree outside is lifting water from its roots to leaves many metres up — with no heart at all. None of this stops when you sleep. What keeps a living thing alive?

This chapter answers with four life processes: nutrition (getting energy and raw materials), respiration (releasing energy from food), transport (carrying materials round the body) and excretion (removing harmful wastes). For each one you will compare how plants and animals solve the same problem. It is the first chapter of Unit II, World of Living, and its labelled diagrams (stomata, digestive system, respiratory system, heart, nephron), its "explain why" questions and its comparisons (aerobic vs anaerobic, xylem vs phloem, arteries vs veins) appear in both CBSE sample papers.

What you'll learn

  • Name the parts of a cell and their jobs; explain diffusion and osmosis; say what enzymes do.
  • Explain why living things need life processes; describe photosynthesis, its equation and its three events; explain how stomata open and close; read the starch-test experiments.
  • Describe how fungi, parasites, Amoeba and Paramoecium get food; follow food through the human alimentary canal, naming each enzyme and where it works; label the digestive system.
  • Describe the three pathways that break down glucose; compare aerobic and anaerobic respiration; explain how the lungs and alveoli work; compare breathing in water and on land.
  • Describe blood and lymph; follow blood through the four chambers of the heart; explain double circulation; compare arteries, veins and capillaries; label the heart.
  • Explain how water rises in xylem (root pressure, transpiration pull) and how food moves in phloem (translocation); compare the two.
  • Describe the human excretory system and how a nephron makes urine; explain how urine volume is controlled; describe how plants get rid of wastes; label the nephron.
  • Compare all four processes in plants and animals on one card; use the "draw it in the exam" checklist and the label-the-diagram trainer.

This is the first biology chapter. Most of its marks come from three skills, and every topic practises them:

  1. Explain a process in order — food, air, blood or urine, one step at a time.
  2. Draw and label — every key diagram has a "Draw it in the exam" checklist and a plain line version to copy.
  3. Compare — tables with matched rows, the way marking schemes award marks.

The spaced-review sets come back to you after 1 day, 1 week and 1 month. Don't skip them — remembering something after a gap is what makes it stick.
