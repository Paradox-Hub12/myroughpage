P17●● StandardShort answer · 2 marksOriginal

Two identical leafy celery stalks are stood in beakers of water coloured with red ink. From stalk B all the leaves are removed. After an hour, the red colour has risen much higher in stalk A. Explain.

Full solution

Given: A with leaves, B without; dye rises faster in A. Asked: explanation.

Concept used: transpiration pull (Topic 6). Why it applies: the only difference is the leaves, which carry the stomata.

  1. 1

    The coloured water rises in the xylem of both stalks.

  2. 2

    Stalk A has leaves → water evaporates from the leaf cells through the stomata (transpiration).

  3. 3

    This creates a suction (transpiration pull) that pulls water up the xylem faster.

  4. 4

    Stalk B has no leaves → almost no transpiration → no pull, so the water rises only slowly.

  5. Check: the experiment changes only the leaves, so the faster rise must be due to transpiration from them ✔.

  6. Answer Leaves lose water by transpiration, creating a suction (transpiration pull) that draws water up the xylem; without leaves, there is little pull, so the dye rises slowly.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Leaves lose water by transpiration through stomata. (1)
    • This creates transpiration pull/suction, drawing water up the xylem faster. (1)
    Common trap: "Stalk A has more xylem." Both stalks are identical; only the leaves differ.
