CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q11 mark

Question: During photosynthesis, what happens to carbon dioxide and water as glucose is formed?
A. Carbon dioxide gains hydrogen, while water loses electrons.
B. Carbon dioxide loses oxygen, while water gains oxygen.
C. Carbon dioxide is oxidised, while water is reduced.
D. Both carbon dioxide and water undergo reduction.

Given: COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ and water → glucose. Asked: what happens to each, in terms of oxidation and reduction.

Concept used: the events of photosynthesis — water is split; carbon dioxide is reduced to carbohydrate (Topic 2); gain of hydrogen = reduction, loss of hydrogen/electrons = oxidation (Chapter 1). Why it applies: photosynthesis is a redox process.

  1. 1

    In the second event, water is split into hydrogen and oxygen → water loses hydrogen (with its electrons) and its oxygen is released as gas → water is oxidised.

  2. 2

    In the third event, carbon dioxide is reduced to carbohydrate → it gains hydrogen (from water).

  3. 3

    So A is correct: COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ gains hydrogen; water loses electrons.

  4. 4

    B: water does not gain oxygen — it loses hydrogen and gives off oxygen. C has the roles reversed. D: water is oxidised, not reduced.

  5. Check: a redox reaction needs one substance oxidised and one reduced — A gives exactly that pair ✔.

    Common trap: Choosing C because "photosynthesis makes oxygen, so something is oxidised" — it is water that is oxidised; carbon dioxide is reduced.
A. Carbon dioxide gains hydrogen, while water loses electrons.

CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q21 mark

Question: Lime water turns cloudy in the presence of a gas, which is a by-product of respiration. Shown below are four setups kept in sunlight for 24 hours. In which setup is lime water expected to be the cloudiest?
[Figure described: four closed tanks, each with a dish of lime water. Set P — a glass tank with a pot of soil but no plant. Set Q — a glass tank with a potted green plant. Sets R and S — tanks coated with black paint: R with a potted green plant, S with a pot but no plant.]

Given: four sealed tanks in sunlight for 24 hours; lime water in each. Asked: where the most carbon dioxide collects.

Concept used: a plant respires all the time; in light it also photosynthesises and uses COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​; in the dark it only respires (Topics 2 and 4). Why it applies: the lime water measures the COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ left in each tank.

  1. 1

    P and S have no plant, so there is no plant respiration to add COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ → little change.

  2. 2

    Q has a plant in light: it respires, but it also photosynthesises, using up COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ → the lime water stays nearly clear.

  3. 3

    R has a plant in a tank coated with black paint → no light → no photosynthesis; the plant only respires, giving out COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ for 24 hours → lime water cloudiest. (R is the only dark tank with a plant; S is dark but has no plant.)

  4. Check: R is the only set-up where COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ is produced and not used ✔.

    Common trap: Choosing Q because "a plant gives out gases". In light, photosynthesis uses up the COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ from respiration.
C. R

Question: Which of the following statement is incorrect concerning the experiment to show that sunlight is necessary for photosynthesis?
A. The leaf should be destarched before conducting the experiment.
B. The leaf kept in darkness will show the presence of starch.
C. The leaf exposed to sunlight will show the presence of starch.
D. Starch is the end product of photosynthesis.

Given: four statements about the sunlight experiment. Asked: the incorrect one.

Concept used: destarching and the starch test (Topic 2). Why it applies: only a leaf that has photosynthesised contains new starch.

  1. 1

    A is correct: destarching removes old starch so the result is fair.

  2. 2

    B is incorrect: a destarched leaf kept in darkness cannot photosynthesise, so it has no starch.

  3. 3

    C is correct: a leaf in sunlight photosynthesises and stores starch.

  4. 4

    D is treated as correct here: strictly, glucose is made first and the extra is stored as starch, which is what the iodine test detects.

  5. Check: B contradicts the conclusion of the experiment itself — that light is needed ✔.

    Common trap: Missing the word incorrect and choosing A or C, which are true.
B. The leaf kept in darkness will show the presence of starch.

CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q31 mark

Question: During urine formation, tubular reabsorption and secretion take place in the kidneys. Which of the following sets correctly indicates both the substances that are reabsorbed in the kidneys?
A. Glucose and salts
B. Glucose and starch
C. Glycogen and salts
D. Glycogen and starch

Given: substances in the filtrate. Asked: which pair is reabsorbed.

Concept used: selective reabsorption in the tubule — glucose, amino acids, salts and a major amount of water (Topic 7). Why it applies: only small, dissolved, useful substances are in the filtrate and taken back.

  1. 1

    Glucose and salts are both in the textbook's list of reabsorbed substances → A.

  2. 2

    Starch and glycogen are large storage molecules; they are not in blood plasma as such, so they are never in the filtrate → B, C and D are wrong.

  3. Check: every option except A contains starch or glycogen ✔.

    Common trap: Picking C because it contains "salts". Both substances in the pair must be correct.

    (The stem also mentions tubular "secretion"; your textbook describes only filtration and reabsorption, which is all you need for this question.)

A. Glucose and salts

CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q81 mark

Question: Choose: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. B. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A. C. A is true but R is false. D. A is false but R is true.

Assertion (A): The rate of breathing in aquatic organisms is much lower than in the terrestrial organisms.

Reason (R): The amount of oxygen dissolved in water is less as compared to the amount of oxygen in air.

Given: an assertion about breathing rates; a reason about dissolved oxygen. Asked: the correct option.

Concept used: aquatic organisms breathe faster because dissolved oxygen is low (Topic 4). Why it applies: the assertion reverses this fact.

  1. 1

    A is false: the rate of breathing in aquatic organisms is much faster, not lower.

  2. 2

    R is true: water holds far less dissolved oxygen than the same volume of air holds.

  3. 3

    A false, R true → D.

  4. Check: R, if anything, explains the opposite of A — low oxygen forces faster breathing ✔.

    Common trap: Reading "lower" as "faster" in a hurry. Read assertions word by word.
D. A is false but R is true.

CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q102 marks

Question:
A. Due to narrowing of the bile duct, the bile secretion from the liver to the small intestine was obstructed. How will the process of digestion be affected?
B. How does the length of the intestine depend on the nature of food habit in different animals?

Given: (A) bile cannot reach the small intestine; (B) different diets. Asked: effect on digestion; link between diet and intestine length.

Concept used: the two roles of bile; intestine length and diet (Topic 3). Why it applies: both are stated in the textbook's section on the small intestine.

  1. 1

    A

    Bile normally makes the acidic food from the stomach alkaline, so that the pancreatic enzymes can act → without it, these enzymes work poorly.

  2. 2

    Bile salts normally break fats into smaller globules (emulsification), increasing the efficiency of lipase → without them, fats stay as large globules.

  3. 3

    So digestion — especially of fats — will be improper/incomplete.

  4. 4

    B

    Herbivores eating grass need a longer small intestine to allow the cellulose to be digested.

  5. 5

    Meat is easier to digest, so carnivores such as tigers have a shorter small intestine.

  6. Check: A uses both roles of bile named in the textbook; B gives one example from each diet ✔.

    Common trap: (A) "Fats will not be digested because bile digests them." Bile only emulsifies fats and makes the medium alkaline; lipase digests them.
A. Bile juice makes the food alkaline for the pancreatic enzymes to act (½). Bile salts break down fats into smaller globules, increasing the efficiency of enzyme action; if the bile duct is obstructed, digestion of fats will be improper (½).
B. Herbivores eating grass need a longer small intestine to allow the cellulose to be digested. Meat is easier to digest, hence carnivores like tigers have a shorter small intestine (1).

CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q16 option A5 marks

[Figure described: three hearts. A — a heart with two chambers in a line, all blue (deoxygenated blood only). B — a heart with three chambers: two atria (one blue, one red) above a single ventricle in which blue and red blood mix. C — a heart with four chambers, the right side blue and the left side red, separated by a wall.]

Question: Observe the structure of hearts A, B and C shown above and answer the following questions
I. Which of the hearts depicted above is present in both birds and Mammals and how are they able to maintain their body temperature? Give reason for your answer.
II. Which of the hearts depicted above is present in fishes, how does circulation of blood take place in them?

Given: two-, three- and four-chambered hearts. Asked: I — the birds' and mammals' heart and the link to body temperature; II — the fish heart and its circulation.

Concept used: separation of the two sides of the heart; single circulation in fish (Topic 5). Why it applies: chamber number and mixing decide how efficiently oxygen is supplied.

  1. 1

    I

    C — the four-chambered heart.

  2. 2

    The right and left sides are separated, so oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix.

  3. 3

    This gives a highly efficient supply of oxygen to the body.

  4. 4

    Birds and mammals have high energy needs: they constantly use energy to maintain their body temperature. Efficient oxygen supply → plenty of aerobic respiration → enough energy to keep warm.

  5. 5

    II

    A — the two-chambered heart.

  6. 6

    Blood is pumped to the gills, is oxygenated there, and passes directly to the rest of the body → it goes through the heart only once in each cycle (single circulation).

  7. Check: B (three chambers, with mixing) fits amphibians and many reptiles, whose body temperature depends on the surroundings — so it is not the answer to I or II ✔.

    Common trap: Choosing B for birds because it "has two kinds of blood". In B they mix — exactly what birds and mammals avoid.
I. C — four-chambered heart (1). The separation of the right side and the left side of the heart keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood from mixing; this allows a highly efficient supply of oxygen to the body, which is useful in animals such as birds and mammals that have high energy needs and constantly use energy to maintain their body temperature (2).
II. A — two-chambered heart (1). Blood is pumped to the gills, is oxygenated there, and passes directly to the rest of the body; thus blood goes only once through the heart in the fish during one cycle of passage through the body (1).

Question:

Given: two chest diagrams; a statement about diffusion. Asked: I — which shows inhalation and how it happens; II — how oxygen is actually delivered.

Concept used: breathing in (Topic 4); haemoglobin and the circulation (Topics 4 and 5). Why it applies: I is about the chest cavity; II about transport.

  1. 1

    I

  2. 2

II. If diffusion were to move oxygen in our body, it is estimated that it would take 3 years for a molecule of oxygen to get to our toes from our lungs then, how is oxygen delivered to all parts of our body?

CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section A, Q102 marks

Question: Unlike animals, plants do not have any excretory products as they do not eat food. Comment upon the statement with justification.

Given: a claim that plants have no excretory products. Asked: a comment with justification.

Concept used: excretion in plants (Topic 7). Why it applies: plants make wastes in photosynthesis, respiration and other metabolism.

  1. 1

    The statement is wrong: plants do produce excretory products.

  2. 2

    They simply use different strategies from animals. Any two:

    • oxygen, a waste of photosynthesis, is removed through the stomata;
    • excess water is removed by transpiration through the stomata;
    • other wastes are stored in dead cells, resins and gums, or removed with falling old leaves;
    • many wastes are stored in cellular vacuoles.

  3. Check: the scheme gives 1 mark for rejecting the statement and 1 for any two strategies ✔ (split inferred from the layout).

    Common trap: Agreeing with the statement because plants "make their own food". Making food still produces wastes — oxygen itself is one.
It is completely wrong to say that plants do not produce any excretory products (1, inferred). Plants use completely different strategies for excretion than animals (any two): (i) oxygen, a photosynthetic waste, is removed through stomata; (ii) excess water is removed by transpiration through stomata; (iii) other metabolic wastes are stored in dead cells, resins and gums, or removed through falling of old leaves; (iv) many waste products are stored in cellular vacuoles (1, inferred).

Question: How many chambers are there in the heart of the following organisms? How is mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood prevented in their body?
(i) Fishes
(ii) Humans

Given: fish and humans. Asked: number of chambers; how mixing is prevented.

    (i) Fish — two chambers. Blood is pumped to the gills, oxygenated there, and goes directly to the body; the heart only ever holds deoxygenated blood, so no mixing occurs.

  2. 2

  3. Check: fish avoid mixing by never returning oxygenated blood to the heart; humans by a wall between the two sides ✔.

CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section A, Q11 option B2 marks

Question: Explain the mechanism by which the water is transported in plants?

Given: water transport in plants. Asked: the mechanism.

Concept used: xylem; root pressure; transpiration pull (Topic 6). Why it applies: these are the forces that move water up.

  1. 1

    Xylem moves water and minerals from the soil, through the roots, to all other parts of the plant, in one direction (upwards).

  2. 2

    Transpiration from the leaves creates a pull in the tracheids and vessels of xylem, helping water move up.

  3. 3

    Root cells actively take up ions from the soil; the concentration difference makes water move into the roots, creating a steady movement of water into the root xylem.

  4. Check: the scheme separates its points with "//" — any of them answers the question; give at least two for 2 marks ✔.

    Common trap: Describing phloem or translocation — that is food transport.
Xylem moves water and minerals obtained from the soil through roots to all other parts of the plant in a unidirectional manner // Transpiration from the leaf causes a transpirational pull in the tracheids and vessels of xylem, facilitating upward movement of water // Roots actively take up ions from the soil, creating a concentration difference, so water moves into the roots — a steady movement of water into root xylem. (Any two points, 1 mark each — split inferred.)
