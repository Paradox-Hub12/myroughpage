Week 1

Set 2 — Week 1

5 questions, about 8 minutes

Focus: applying the ideas — slightly harder.

W1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

In the starch test on a leaf, why is the leaf (a) dipped in boiling water and then (b) boiled in alcohol in a water bath?

Answer: (a) To soften the leaf (and kill its cells) so the chemicals can enter; (b) to remove chlorophyll so that the iodine colour can be seen clearly — in a water bath because alcohol catches fire. (Topic 2)

Did you get it?

W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

Name the final products of digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats, and the juice that completes their digestion.

Answer: Glucose; amino acids; fatty acids and glycerol — completed by the intestinal juice. (Topic 3)

Did you get it?

W1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

A green plant gives out oxygen by day and carbon dioxide at night. Does it respire during the day? Explain.

Answer: Yes — all its cells respire all the time. By day, the COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ from respiration is used up in photosynthesis, which is faster, so oxygen release is the main exchange; at night only respiration happens. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Explain why the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood although it is an artery.

Answer: An artery is any vessel carrying blood away from the heart. The pulmonary artery carries blood from the right ventricle to the lungs — blood that has come from the body and has not yet picked up oxygen. (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

W1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

Which force moves water up the xylem mainly by day, and which matters more at night? Why the difference?

Answer: Transpiration pull by day — the stomata are open and water evaporates fast; root pressure at night, when transpiration is low. (Topic 6)

Did you get it?

Month 1

Set 3 — Month 1

5 questions, about 12 minutes

Focus: linking topics — the kind of thinking the board exam rewards.

M1.1 · 1 of 5●●● Advanced

Give two similarities and one difference between the way alveoli and nephrons work.

Answer: Similarities: both are thin-walled and richly supplied with blood capillaries; both occur in very large numbers, giving a large surface; both remove a waste from blood. Difference: alveoli exchange gases by diffusion; nephrons filter blood and selectively reabsorb useful substances. (Topics 4 and 7)

Did you get it?

M1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

Digested fat is absorbed in the small intestine. Which fluid carries it away, and how does that fluid reach the blood?

Answer: Lymph carries absorbed fat; lymph flows through lymphatic capillaries into larger lymph vessels, which open into large veins. (Topics 3 and 5)

Did you get it?

M1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

In Practical 7, what would happen to the water level if the flask were not air-tight? Explain.

Answer: The water would not rise (or rise very little): air leaking in would replace the carbon dioxide absorbed by KOH, so the pressure inside would not fall. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

M1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Without looking, list the labels you would put on (a) a sectional view of the heart and (b) a nephron. Then check against the wrap-up's "Draw it in the exam" card.

Answer: (a) Right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle, septum, aorta, pulmonary artery, pulmonary veins, vena cava. (b) Glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, tubular part, collecting duct, branch of renal artery, branch of renal vein, capillaries. (Topics 5 and 7)

Did you get it?

M1.5 · 5 of 5●●● Advanced

"Respiration is just photosynthesis run backwards." Give one way this is a useful idea and two ways it is misleading.

Answer: Useful: the raw materials of one are the products of the other (COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ + water ↔ glucose + OX2\ce{O2}OX2​). Misleading: respiration happens in all cells all the time, photosynthesis only in green cells in light; respiration releases energy (as ATP) in mitochondria, photosynthesis stores light energy in chloroplasts; anaerobic respiration gives other products (ethanol + COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​, or lactic acid). (Topics 2 and 4)
