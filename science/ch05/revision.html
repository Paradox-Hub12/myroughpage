The whole chapter on one page

Life processes — visible movement is not enough to show life; living things show molecular movement to repair and maintain their organised structure. The maintenance processes — nutrition, respiration, transport, excretion — are life processes. In multicellular organisms most cells are far from the surface, so diffusion is not enough: specialised organs and a transport system are needed.

Nutrition in plants — autotrophs (green plants, some bacteria) make food from COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ and water. Photosynthesis: events — (i) light absorbed by chlorophyll, (ii) light → chemical energy + water split into hydrogen and oxygen, (iii) COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ reduced to carbohydrate; extra carbohydrate stored as starch. Desert plants take in COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ at night. Stomata: guard cells swell (water in) → open; shrink (water out) → close. Minerals from soil; nitrogen as nitrates/nitrites or from bacteria. Experiments: variegated leaf → chlorophyll needed; KOH → COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ needed; black paper → light needed (always destarch first).

Nutrition in animals — heterotrophs: fungi digest food outside and absorb; Amoeba — pseudopodia → food vacuole; Paramoecium — cilia, fixed spot; parasites — Cuscuta, ticks, lice, leeches, tapeworms. Humans: mouth (salivary amylase: starch → sugar) → oesophagus (peristalsis) → stomach (gastric glands: HCl — acidic medium; pepsin — proteins; mucus — protects lining) → sphincter → small intestine (bile from liver — alkaline + emulsifies fats, no enzyme; pancreatic juice — trypsin (proteins), lipase (fats); intestinal juice → amino acids, glucose, fatty acids + glycerol; villi absorb) → large intestine (absorbs water) → anus (anal sphincter).

Respiration — glucose (6C) → pyruvate (3C) in the cytoplasm; then: OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ present, mitochondria → COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ + water + most energy; no OX2\ce{O2}OX2​, yeast → ethanol + COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​; lack of OX2\ce{O2}OX2​, muscles → lactic acid (cramps). Energy stored as ATP. Plants exchange gases by diffusion (stomata, intercellular spaces): night → COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ out, day → OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ out. Aquatic animals breathe faster (little dissolved OX2\ce{O2}OX2​). Air path: nostrils → pharynx → larynx → trachea (rings of cartilage) → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli. Breathing in: ribs lifted, diaphragm flattened, chest cavity larger, air sucked in. Haemoglobin (RBCs) carries OX2\ce{O2}OX2​; COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ mostly dissolved.

Transport in humans — plasma (food, COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​, nitrogenous wastes), RBCs (OX2\ce{O2}OX2​), platelets (clotting). Heart: 4 chambers; atria receive, ventricles pump (thicker walls); valves stop backflow. Right: vena cava → RA → RV → pulmonary artery → lungs. Left: pulmonary vein → LA → LV → aorta → body. Double circulation — blood passes through the heart twice per cycle; no mixing → efficient OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ supply (birds, mammals keep body temperature). Amphibians/many reptiles: 3 chambers; fish: 2, single circulation. Arteries (away; thick, elastic; high pressure), veins (towards; thin; valves), capillaries (one cell thick; exchange). Lymph: colourless, less protein; absorbed fat; returns tissue fluid.

Transport in plants — xylem (vessels, tracheids): water + minerals upwards; root pressure (ions taken up actively → water follows; night) + transpiration pull (day). Transpiration = water vapour lost from aerial parts → helps absorption/upward movement + temperature regulation. Phloem (sieve tubes + companion cells): translocation of food both ways, using ATP.

Excretion — kidneys → ureters → bladder → urethra. Nephron: glomerulus in Bowman's capsule → filtration; tubule → selective reabsorption (glucose, amino acids, salts, much water); rest = urine → collecting duct. Urine volume depends on excess water and dissolved waste. Plants: OX2\ce{O2}OX2​/COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ via stomata; water by transpiration; wastes in vacuoles, falling leaves, resins and gums in old xylem, into soil.

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Life processes: processes that together perform the maintenance job of living organisms — nutrition, respiration, transport, excretion.
  • Autotrophic nutrition: making food from simple inorganic materials (COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​, water) using sunlight and chlorophyll. Heterotrophic nutrition: taking in complex food made by other organisms.
  • Photosynthesis: the process by which autotrophs convert COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ and water into carbohydrates in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, giving out oxygen.
  • Enzymes: biological catalysts that speed up reactions, e.g. breaking complex food into simple molecules.
  • Emulsification: breaking large fat globules into small droplets (by bile salts).
  • Peristalsis: rhythmic contraction of the muscles of the alimentary canal that pushes food forward.
  • Respiration: breakdown of food in cells to release energy. Aerobic — with oxygen; anaerobic — without oxygen.
  • Double circulation: blood passes through the heart twice in each complete cycle of the body.
  • Transpiration: loss of water in the form of vapour from the aerial parts of a plant.
  • Translocation: transport of soluble products of photosynthesis in the phloem.
  • Excretion: removal of harmful metabolic wastes from the body.
  • Nephron: the filtration unit of the kidney.
