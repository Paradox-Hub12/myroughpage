Many questions on this chapter link two or three topics — for example "Compare the functioning of alveoli and nephrons" (NCERT Exercise Q13), "How does oxygen reach a muscle cell?" or "Do plants respire at night only?" This page puts the chapter on three cards — the four processes in plants and animals, photosynthesis against respiration, and a "draw it in the exam" checklist — and then works through six mixed questions.

Card 1 — The four life processes in plants and animals

Process In plants In humans
Nutrition Autotrophic — photosynthesis in chloroplasts: COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ + water → glucose (stored as starch) + OX2\ce{O2}OX2​, using sunlight and chlorophyll; minerals from the soil Heterotrophic — digestion by enzymes in the alimentary canal; absorption by villi in the small intestine; extra stored as glycogen
Respiration In every cell, day and night; gases exchanged by diffusion through stomata and intercellular spaces In every cell (mitochondria); lungs and alveoli exchange gases; haemoglobin carries OX2\ce{O2}OX2​
Transport Xylem — water and minerals, upwards (root pressure, transpiration pull); phloem — food, both ways, uses ATP Heart, blood vessels, blood (double circulation); lymph carries absorbed fat
Excretion OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ and COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ via stomata; water by transpiration; wastes in vacuoles, falling leaves, resins and gums, or into soil Kidneys (nephrons: filtration + selective reabsorption) → ureters → bladder → urethra; COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ via lungs

The one-line rule: a green plant always respires; in light it also photosynthesises, faster than it respires, so overall it gives out oxygen by day and carbon dioxide at night.

Card 3 — Draw it in the exam

Rules for every biology diagram

  1. Use a sharp pencil; draw neat outlines, no shading, about a third to half a page in size.
  2. Draw every label line with a ruler; lines must touch the part and must not cross.
  3. Write labels horizontally, lined up on one or both sides, in neat print.
  4. Write a title under the diagram (e.g. "Sectional view of the human heart").
  5. Learn the label set — most questions name the labels they want, but you should know all of them.

Six mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

The sealed glass box

Question. A potted plant is kept in a sealed glass box with a sensor that reads the amount of carbon dioxide in the air. Over one sunny day and the following night, the reading falls from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. and rises from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. Explain both changes. Does the plant respire during the day?

Given: COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ falls by day, rises by night. Asked: explanations; whether the plant respires by day.

Concept used: photosynthesis (Topic 2) and gas exchange in plants (Topic 4). Why it applies: both processes change the COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ in the box, in opposite directions.

  1. 1

    By day: the plant photosynthesises, taking in COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​; it also respires, giving out COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​.

  2. 2

    Photosynthesis is faster than respiration in bright light, so the plant takes in more COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ than it gives out → the reading falls.

  3. 3

    At night: there is no light, so no photosynthesis; respiration continues, giving out COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ → the reading rises.

  4. 4

    Yes, the plant respires by day — its living cells need energy all the time. The COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ it makes is simply used up in photosynthesis, so none is released.

  5. Check: if the box were covered with black cloth during the day, the reading would rise then too — the change follows light, not the clock ✔.

  6. Answer By day photosynthesis uses more COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ than respiration makes, so COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ falls; at night only respiration happens, so COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ rises. The plant respires all the time.

    Common trap: "Plants respire only at night." They respire day and night; by day the COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ released is used for photosynthesis.

Mixed example 2

One oxygen molecule's journey

Question. Trace a molecule of oxygen from the air outside your nose to a mitochondrion in a muscle cell of your leg, and say what it is used for there.

Given: start — air; finish — a leg-muscle mitochondrion. Asked: the path, and its use.

Concept used: respiratory system (Topic 4), double circulation (Topic 5), aerobic respiration (Topic 4). Why it applies: the journey crosses three systems.

  1. 1

    Nostrils → nasal passage (filtered by hairs and mucus) → pharynx → larynx → trachea (held open by rings of cartilage) → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.

  2. 2

    It diffuses across the thin alveolar wall into a lung capillary and is picked up by haemoglobin in a red blood corpuscle.

  3. 3

    Blood: pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → arteries → capillaries in the leg muscle.

  4. 4

    Haemoglobin releases the oxygen in the oxygen-poor muscle tissue; it diffuses through the one-cell-thick capillary wall into a muscle cell.

  5. 5

    In a mitochondrion it is used to break down pyruvate into carbon dioxide and water, releasing a lot of energy, stored as ATP.

  6. Check: the oxygen travelled in oxygenated blood all the way (pulmonary vein → left side → aorta) ✔; and its final use is the aerobic pathway, which happens only in mitochondria ✔.

  7. Answer Nose → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli → (diffusion) haemoglobin in RBC → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → leg capillaries → (diffusion) muscle cell → mitochondrion, where it breaks down pyruvate to COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ + water, releasing energy (ATP).

    Common trap: Sending the oxygenated blood to the right side of the heart. Blood from the lungs enters the left atrium.

Mixed example 3

From leaf to root

Question. A sugar made in a mango leaf is used, a day later, by a cell at the tip of a root to release energy. Name the process that made it, the tissue and process that carried it, how the carrying is powered, and the process that released its energy.

Given: sugar made in a leaf; used in a root-tip cell. Asked: four processes or tissues.

Concept used: photosynthesis (Topic 2), translocation (Topic 6), respiration (Topic 4). Why it applies: making, moving and using food are three different life processes.

  1. 1

    Made by photosynthesis in the leaf's chloroplasts (COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ + water, using sunlight and chlorophyll).

  2. 2

    Carried in phloem — translocation, through sieve tubes with companion cells; here the movement is downwards.

  3. 3

    Powered by ATP: sucrose is loaded into the phloem using energy from ATP; water follows by osmosis, building pressure that moves the sugar towards the root, where pressure is lower.

  4. 4

    Used in respiration in the root cell: glucose → pyruvate in the cytoplasm → (with oxygen from the air spaces in the soil and root) COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ + water in the mitochondria, releasing energy.

  5. Check: the root cell has no chloroplasts, so it cannot make its own food — it must receive it through phloem ✔.

  6. Answer Photosynthesis (leaf) → translocation in phloem (powered by ATP) → respiration in the root cell.

    Common trap: Sending the sugar down in xylem. Xylem carries water and minerals upwards only.

Mixed example 4

Villi and alveoli: one design, two jobs

Question. State three features shared by the villi of the small intestine and the alveoli of the lungs, and say what each organ absorbs.

Given: villi; alveoli. Asked: three shared features; what each absorbs.

Concept used: absorption in the small intestine (Topic 3); gas exchange in the alveoli (Topic 4). Why it applies: both are surfaces built for absorbing materials into the blood.

  1. 1

    Large surface area: villi are thousands of finger-like projections; alveoli are millions of balloon-like sacs.

  2. 2

    Thin walls: materials need to cross only a short distance.

  3. 3

    Rich blood supply: blood vessels carry absorbed materials away at once, keeping the difference in concentration high.

  4. 4

    What each absorbs: villi — digested food (glucose, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol); alveoli — oxygen (and they release carbon dioxide).

  5. Check: each shared feature speeds up the same process — diffusion into the blood (Topic 1): more area, shorter distance, steeper difference ✔.

  6. Answer Both have a large surface area, thin walls and a rich blood supply. Villi absorb digested food; alveoli absorb oxygen and release carbon dioxide.

    Common trap: Listing "both have enzymes". Alveoli have no digestive enzymes; enzymes digest food before the villi absorb it.

Mixed example 5

"Plants photosynthesise; animals respire"

Question. Correct this student's statement and explain using three points of comparison: "Plants carry out photosynthesis and animals carry out respiration."

Given: a wrong statement. Asked: correction; three comparisons.

Concept used: Card 2 (this page). Why it applies: the statement confuses two different processes.

  1. 1

    Correction: plants carry out both photosynthesis and respiration; animals carry out only respiration.

  2. 2

    Where: photosynthesis happens only in green cells (chloroplasts); respiration happens in all living cells, of plants and animals.

  3. 3

    When: photosynthesis only in light; respiration all the time.

  4. 4

    Energy: photosynthesis stores energy in glucose; respiration releases it (as ATP).

  5. Check: the correction is consistent with Mixed example 1 — the plant in the box gave out COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ at night, which only respiration can explain ✔.

  6. Answer Plants carry out both processes; animals only respiration. Photosynthesis: green cells, light only, stores energy. Respiration: all cells, all the time, releases energy.

    Common trap: Calling photosynthesis "the plant's respiration". They are opposite in raw materials, products and energy.

Mixed example 6

Which organ? Read the clues

Question. Name the organ or structure in each case: (a) secretes juice containing trypsin and lipase; (b) has walls one cell thick, where materials are exchanged with body cells; (c) a cluster of thin-walled capillaries where blood is filtered; (d) pores bounded by two cells that swell to open them; (e) prevents the air passage from collapsing; (f) carries food in a plant both upwards and downwards.

Given: six clues. Asked: the organ or structure.

Concept used: the structure–function links of the whole chapter. Why it applies: each clue names a job unique to one structure.

  1. 1

    (a) Trypsin and lipase are in pancreatic juice → pancreas (Topic 3).

  2. 2

    (b) One-cell-thick walls, exchange → capillaries (Topic 5).

  3. 3

    (c) Filtering capillary cluster → glomerulus (Topic 7).

  4. 4

    (d) Pores with swelling guard cells → stomata (Topic 2).

  5. 5

    (e) Keeps the air passage open → rings of cartilage (Topic 4).

  6. 6

    (f) Food both ways → phloem (Topic 6).

  7. Check: each answer comes from a different system — digestion, circulation, excretion, plant nutrition, breathing, plant transport ✔.

  8. Answer (a) pancreas (b) capillaries (c) glomerulus (d) stomata (e) rings of cartilage (f) phloem

    Common trap: (c) "Bowman's capsule". The capsule is the cup that collects the filtrate; the capillary cluster inside it is the glomerulus.
