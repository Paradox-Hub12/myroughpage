Case: The pumpkin patch

Ravi grows pumpkins on his terrace. Each plant has two kinds of flowers: some with only stamens, and others with only a pistil and a small swollen ovary below the petals. One season, very few bees visited, and most flowers with a pistil withered without forming pumpkins. His neighbour suggested he pick a flower with stamens each morning and gently touch its anthers onto the stigmas of the other flowers. After he did this, many small pumpkins began to grow.

(a) Are pumpkin flowers unisexual or bisexual? Give a reason. (1)
(b) Why did most pistil-bearing flowers fail to form fruit when bees were few? (1)
(c) What did Ravi's method achieve, and which process had to follow it inside the flower before fruit could form? (1)
(d) Name two changes in the flower after this process. (1)

Full solution

Given: separate flowers with stamens and with pistils; few pollinators; hand transfer of pollen. Asked: flower type; reason for failure; what the method did and what followed; two changes.

Concept used: unisexual flowers, cross-pollination, fertilisation and changes after it (Topic 3). Why it applies: a pistil-only flower depends on pollen from another flower.

  1. 1

    (a) Unisexual — each flower has either stamens or a pistil.

  2. 2
