CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q41 mark

Question: In a controlled experiment, the anthers of a mustard plant's flowers were carefully removed in the bud stage before they matured. The bud was covered with a paper bag.
What will be the most likely result for these flowers?
A. They will still produce seeds and fruit through self-pollination.
B. They will attract more insects and produce larger flowers but no fruit.
C. They will not produce any seeds or fruits.
D. They will produce seeds and fruits that are genetically identical to the parent.

Given: a bisexual mustard flower; anthers removed before they matured; bud bagged. Asked: the most likely result.

Concept used: pollination must come before fertilisation; seeds and fruit form only after fertilisation (Topic 3). Why it applies: both sources of pollen have been removed.

  1. 1

    Removing the anthers removes the flower's own pollen → no self-pollination.

  2. 2

    The paper bag keeps out insects and wind → no cross-pollination.

  3. 3

    No pollination → no pollen tube → no fertilisation → no zygote.

  4. 4

    Without fertilisation, the ovules cannot become seeds and the ovary cannot become a fruit → C.

  5. 5

    A ✘ — there are no anthers, so no self-pollination. B ✘ — inside a bag no insects can visit, and nothing makes the flower larger. D ✘ — seeds come from sexual reproduction, which needs fertilisation.

  6. Check: compare Topic 3, Worked example 1 — the same set-up in Hibiscus ✔.

    Common trap: choosing A because mustard is bisexual. A bisexual flower can self-pollinate only if it has its anthers.
C. They will not produce any seeds or fruits.
1 mark
CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q51 mark

Question: The Vas deferens and the Oviduct are both targets for surgical contraception (Vasectomy and Tubectomy respectively). Analysing the process of reproduction, what common biological event is prevented by blocking both these structures?
A. The production of gametes (sperm and egg).
B. The implantation of the fertilized egg into the uterine wall.
C. The successful meeting and fusion of the male and the female gametes.
D. The release of hormones - testosterone and estrogen.

Given: the vas deferens (male) or oviduct (female) blocked. Asked: the common event prevented.

Concept used: surgical methods of contraception (Topic 5); the path of gametes (Topic 4). Why it applies: each block cuts the route a gamete must travel.

  1. 1

    Vas deferens blocked → sperms cannot be transferred.

  2. 2

    Oviduct blocked → the egg cannot travel along the tube to meet sperms (and reach the uterus).

  3. 3

    In both cases sperm and egg cannot meet, so fertilisation cannot take place → C.

  4. 4

    A ✘ — the testes and ovaries still make gametes; only the tube is blocked. B ✘ — implantation is prevented only because there is no fertilised egg; the event blocked first is the meeting of gametes. D ✘ — hormones from the testes and ovaries go into the blood, not along these tubes.

  5. Check: Topic 5, Worked example 2 — "both prevent the meeting of sperm and egg" ✔.

    Common trap: choosing A. Surgery blocks a tube; it does not stop gametes being made.
C. The successful meeting and fusion of the male and the female gametes.
1 mark
CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q61 mark

Question: The male gamete of an apple plant has seventeen chromosomes. What is the number of chromosomes in the female gamete and in the cells of the adult plant respectively?
A. 17,17
B. 17,34
C. 34,34
D. 34,51

Given: male gamete = 17 chromosomes. Asked: female gamete; adult plant's cells.

Concept used: gametes have half the chromosomes; fusion restores the full number (Topic 3). Why it applies: both gametes of a species carry the same half number.

  1. 1

    Female gamete → also half the full number → 17.

  2. 2

    Zygote → 17 + 17 = 34; all the cells of the adult plant come from the zygote → 34.

  3. 3

    So the answer is 17, 34 → B.

  4. Check: 34 is double 17, so the next generation's gametes again carry 17 — the number stays constant ✔.

    Common trap: choosing C (34, 34) — the female gamete is a gamete, so it has half, like the male gamete.
B. 17, 34
1 mark
CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q122 marks

Question: What changes are observed in the uterus subsequent to implantation of the young embryo in a human female?

Given: an embryo has implanted in the uterus. Asked: the changes in the uterus.

Concept used: implantation and the placenta (Topic 4). Why it applies: the uterus must now nourish the embryo for about nine months.

  1. 1

    Lining → the uterine lining thickens and is richly supplied with blood (blood vessels) to nourish the embryo.

  2. 2

    Placenta → a placental connection — the placenta, a disc in the uterine wall — develops between the uterus and the embryo; through it the embryo gets glucose and oxygen and gives out wastes.

  3. Check: both points are in the human sequence card, branch A (Topic 4) ✔.

    Common trap: describing menstruation. That happens when the egg is not fertilised; here an embryo has implanted.
  • Uterine lining thickens and is richly supplied with blood vessels to nourish the embryo. (1)
  • Placental connection is developed between the uterus and embryo. (1) — 2 marks (split inferred: one mark per point; the scheme gives only the total)
CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section A, Q16 option A5 marks

Question: Puneet wanted to grow banana plants.
(i) Based on your knowledge on plant reproduction should he opt for seeds or any alternate method of reproduction. Justify your answer.
(ii) Offsprings of a banana plant usually show very little variation. What causes variation and are variations good or bad? Justify.

Given: banana plants; little variation in their offspring. Asked: (i) seeds or another method, with reason; (ii) the cause of variation, and whether it is good or bad.

Concept used: vegetative propagation (Topic 2); DNA copying and variation (Topic 1). Why it applies: the banana is one of the textbook's seedless plants.

  1. 1

    (i) Not seeds → banana plants have lost the capacity to produce seeds. He should use vegetative propagation (growing new plants from parts of the parent plant).

  2. 2

    (ii) Cause → errors (inaccuracies) in DNA copying produce variation. (Banana offspring show little variation because vegetative propagation uses one parent — no combining of DNA from two individuals.)

  3. 3

    Good → variation can help a population survive unfavourable conditions: a few variants may survive a change in the niche.

  4. 4

    Bad → the parent's desirable characters may be lost; some variants cannot survive in new conditions, or cannot use the cellular apparatus properly.

  5. Check: both sides of the "good or bad" question are needed — the scheme gives one point for each ✔.

    Common trap: (ii) answering only "good". The scheme expects both why it can be good and why it can be bad.
(i) Puneet should not choose seeds as banana plants have lost the capacity to produce seeds. He should go for vegetative propagation of banana. (1 + 1)
(ii) Errors and variations in DNA copying cause variation. (1) Variation is good as it can help a population tide over unfavourable conditions by survival of some variants. (1) It is bad as parents' desirable characters are lost / sometimes variants are not able to survive in the new conditions / the variant is not able to use the cellular apparatus efficiently. (1) — 5 marks (split inferred; the scheme gives only the total)

(The scheme adds "(by stem cutting)" after "vegetative propagation of banana". Your textbook does not name the method used for bananas; "vegetative propagation" is enough.)

CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section A, Q16 option B5 marks

Question: Annie was conducting research on the number of fruits produced by watermelon under different conditions. She grew 25 watermelon plants each in both glass house A and B. She introduced pollinators in glass house A only.
(i) What difference will she observe in the number of fruits produced in the two glass houses? Explain with reason.
(ii) List 3 changes that will occur in a flower once it gets fertilized.

Given: watermelon plants with and without pollinators. Asked: (i) the difference in fruits, with reason; (ii) three changes after fertilisation.

Concept used: unisexual flowers and cross-pollination; changes after fertilisation (Topic 3). Why it applies: watermelon is one of the textbook's unisexual flowers.

  1. 1

    (i) Unisexual flowers → watermelon flowers are unisexual: male and female flowers are separate, so pollen must be carried from one flower to another.

  2. 2

    Glass house A → pollinators carry pollen between flowers → cross-pollination → more fertilisation → more fruits.

  3. 3

    Glass house B → without pollinators, little pollen reaches the stigmas of female flowers (especially if they are far apart) → fewer fruits.

  4. 4

    (ii) Three changes: the ovule develops a tough coat and becomes the seed; the ovary grows and ripens to form the fruit; the petals, sepals, stamens, style and stigma may shrivel and fall off.

  5. Check: the changes in (ii) are the "after fertilisation" table in Topic 3 ✔.

    Common trap: (i) "the same number, because the plants are the same". Fruits need fertilisation, which needs pollination — and unisexual flowers depend on agents.
(i) Watermelon has unisexual flowers; the male and female flowers are separate. (1) The presence of pollinators will facilitate cross pollination between the flowers, increasing the chance of fertilisation and the number of fruits produced; without pollinators the probability of pollen falling on the stigma reduces, so the number of fruits produced will be less. (1)
(ii) - Ovule develops a tough coat and becomes seed. (1)
  • Ovary grows and ripens to form fruit. (1)
  • Petals, sepals, stamen, style and stigma may shrivel and fall off. (1) — 5 marks (split inferred; the scheme gives only the total)
