The whole chapter on one page

Why reproduce — reproduction is not needed to keep an individual alive, but it makes new individuals like the parents, so the species continues. Its basic event is a copy of the DNA (the blueprint for proteins and body design) plus extra cellular apparatus; then the cell divides. Copying is never perfect → variation. Consistent copying keeps the body design that suits the niche → stable populations. Variation lets a few individuals survive if the niche changes (heat-resistant bacteria) → good for the species, not necessarily the individual; it is the basis of evolution.

Asexual reproduction (one parent; method depends on body design) — binary fission: two cells (Amoeba, any plane; Leishmania, definite orientation, whip-like structure); multiple fission: many cells at once (Plasmodium); budding: outgrowth at one site that detaches (Hydra, yeast); fragmentation: a simple body breaks into pieces (Spirogyra); regeneration: a cut piece grows a whole body, by specialised cells (Planaria, Hydra) — not the same as reproduction, and complex organisms cannot do it; vegetative propagation: root, stem or leaf → new plant (potato eyes, Bryophyllum leaf buds, money-plant; layering and grafting for sugarcane, rose, grapes) — earlier flowers and fruits, seedless plants (banana, orange, rose, jasmine), all the parent's characteristics; spore formation: sporangia release thick-walled spores that grow on moist surfaces (Rhizopus; hyphae are not reproductive).

Sexual reproduction in flowering plants — combining DNA from two individuals → more variation. Gametes have half the chromosomes (meiosis); fusion restores the full number (pea 7 + 7 = 14). Female gamete large with food stores; male gamete small, motile. Flower: stamen (anther + filament; pollen) — male; pistil (stigma, style, ovary with ovules, each with an egg cell) — female. Unisexual: papaya, watermelon; bisexual: Hibiscus, mustard. Pollination: pollen from anther to stigma (self or cross; wind, water, animals). Pollen tube grows through the style to the ovary. Fertilisation: gametes fuse in the ovule → zygote → embryo. Ovule → seed; ovary → fruit; petals, sepals, stamens, style, stigma may fall off. Germination: plumule (future shoot), radicle (future root), cotyledon (food store).

Human reproduction — puberty: reproductive tissues mature as general growth slows; common changes (hair in armpits and genital area, oily skin, pimples); girls (breasts grow, menstruation begins); boys (facial hair, voice cracks). Male: testes in the scrotum (lower temperature) make sperms and testosterone → vas deferens → urethra (common passage for sperms and urine); seminal vesicles and prostate add fluid for transport and nutrition. Female: ovaries (eggs and hormones; one egg a month) → oviduct (fallopian tube) → uterus → cervix → vagina. Fertilisation in the oviduct → zygote → embryo → implantation in the uterine lining (thick, blood-rich) → placenta (disc in the uterine wall; villi on the embryo's side, blood spaces on the mother's side; glucose and oxygen in, wastes out) → about nine months → birth by uterine contractions. No fertilisation → egg lives about a day → lining breaks down → menstruation (about 2–8 days).

Reproductive health — maturity ≠ readiness; pressures from friends, families, government agencies. STIs: bacterial — gonorrhoea, syphilis; viral — warts, HIV-AIDS; a condom helps prevent many, to some extent. Pregnancy makes major demands on the woman's body and mind → contraception: barrier (sperm cannot reach egg; only one that also helps against STIs), pills (eggs not released; side-effects), loop / copper-T in the uterus (irritation), surgical (vas deferens or fallopian tube blocked; safe long-term but surgery can cause infections). Female foeticide is illegal; the sex ratio must be maintained; prenatal sex determination is prohibited by law.

Definition sheet

This chapter has no equations. Learn the two sequences and the definitions below instead.

Sequences to copy

  • Flower: pollination (anther → stigma) → pollen tube through the style → fertilisation in the ovule → zygote → embryo → ovule becomes seed, ovary becomes fruit → germination → seedling.
  • Human: egg released from the ovary → fertilisation in the oviduct → zygote → embryo → implantation in the uterine lining → placenta → foetus → birth (about nine months). If not fertilised: lining breaks down → menstruation.

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Reproduction: the process by which organisms produce new individuals like themselves.
  • Variation: small differences between individuals, arising because DNA copying is not perfectly accurate (and, in sexual reproduction, from combining DNA of two individuals).
  • Asexual reproduction: producing new individuals from a single parent, without gametes.
  • Binary fission: division of one cell into two daughter cells.
  • Multiple fission: division of one cell into many daughter cells at the same time.
  • Budding: a new individual grows as an outgrowth (bud) from one site of the parent and then separates.
  • Fragmentation: a simple multicellular organism breaks into pieces, each growing into a new individual.
  • Regeneration: a whole organism grows from a cut piece of its body, by specialised cells.
  • Vegetative propagation: a new plant grows from a root, stem or leaf of the parent.
  • Gamete (germ-cell): a reproductive cell with half the number of chromosomes.
  • Pollination: transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma.
  • Fertilisation: fusion of male and female gametes, forming a zygote.
  • Zygote: the cell formed by fertilisation.
  • Germination: growth of the embryo in a seed into a seedling.
  • Puberty: the period during adolescence when the reproductive tissues mature.
  • Implantation: the embryo becoming fixed in the lining of the uterus.
  • Placenta: a disc-shaped tissue in the uterine wall through which the embryo gets glucose and oxygen and gives out wastes.
  • Menstruation: the monthly breakdown and discharge of the uterine lining when the egg is not fertilised.
  • Contraception: methods used to avoid pregnancy.
