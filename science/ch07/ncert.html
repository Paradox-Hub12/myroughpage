Intext p.114, Q1Importance of DNA copying2 marks

What is the importance of DNA copying in reproduction?

Given: DNA copying in reproduction. Asked: why it is important.

Concept used: DNA as the blueprint of body design; DNA copying with variation (Topic 1). Why it applies: reproduction must pass the body design on to the new individual.

  1. 1

    DNA is the blueprint → the DNA in the nucleus is the information source for making proteins, and proteins decide the body design.

  2. 2

    Copy passed on → by copying its DNA, a parent passes this information to the offspring, so the offspring has the same body design and looks like the parent.

  3. 3

    Fits the niche → consistent copying keeps the body-design features that let the organism use its niche, so populations stay stable.

  4. 4

    Variation → copying is not perfectly accurate, so small variations arise; these help the species survive if conditions change, and are the basis of evolution.

  5. Check: these are the two sides of DNA copying in Topic 1's table — consistency (stability) and variation (survival) ✔.

  6. Answer DNA copying passes the information for the body design from parent to offspring, so offspring resemble their parents and fit the same niche (stability of the species). Because copying is not perfect, it also produces variations, which help the species survive changes and are the basis of evolution.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • DNA carries the information (blueprint) for body design; copying passes it to the offspring, so they resemble the parent and can use the same niche. (1)
    • Slight inaccuracies in copying produce variations, which help the species survive changing conditions (basis of evolution). (1)
    Common trap: writing only "to make a new cell". Say what the DNA carries (body design) and why copying matters (resemblance + variation).
Intext p.114, Q2Variation: good for the species, not always for the individual3 marks

Why is variation beneficial to the species but not necessarily for the individual?

Given: variation. Asked: why it helps the species but not necessarily the individual.

Concept used: the importance of variation (Topic 1). Why it applies: niches can change drastically.

  1. 1

    Niches change → a population is suited to its niche, but the niche can change for reasons beyond its control — rising or falling temperature, changing water levels, a meteorite hit.

  2. 2

    Without variation → if all individuals were alike, a drastic change could wipe out the whole population.

  3. 3

    With variation → a few individuals may have variations that suit the new conditions; they survive and reproduce, so the species continues. Example: when water warms, most bacteria die, but a few heat-resistant variants survive and grow.

  4. 4

    The individual → for most individuals the variation gives no advantage (it may not suit the new conditions, and a drastic variation can even make the new cell die). So variation is useful for the survival of the species over time, not necessarily for each individual.

  5. Check: the bacteria example — the species survived, but most individual bacteria still died ✔.

  6. Answer When the niche changes drastically, a few individuals with suitable variations survive and keep the species going, so variation is useful for the species over time. But a variation does not necessarily help a particular individual — it may not suit the conditions it meets, and some variations even prevent survival.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Niches may change drastically (temperature, water level, etc.), and a population without variation could be wiped out. (1)
    • Some variants may suit the new conditions, survive and reproduce (e.g. heat-resistant bacteria when water warms), so the species survives. (1)
    • A variation does not necessarily help the individual having it; it is useful only for the survival of the species over time. (1)
    Common trap: "Variation helps every individual to adapt." Only a few variants survive a change.

Intext p.119, Q1Binary vs multiple fission2 marks

How does binary fission differ from multiple fission?

Given: two kinds of fission. Asked: the difference.

Concept used: fission in unicellular organisms (Topic 2). Why it applies: both are cell divisions that differ in the number of daughter cells.

  1. 1

    Number of daughter cells → binary fission: the cell splits into two; multiple fission: the cell divides into many daughter cells.

  2. 2

    How → in binary fission the two halves are usually equal; in multiple fission many daughter cells form at the same time (simultaneously).

  3. 3

    Examples → binary: Amoeba, Leishmania (many bacteria and protozoa); multiple: Plasmodium (the malarial parasite).

  4. Check: compare with the binary vs multiple fission table in Topic 2 ✔.

  5. Answer In binary fission one cell divides into two daughter cells (e.g. Amoeba, Leishmania); in multiple fission one cell divides into many daughter cells at the same time (e.g. Plasmodium).

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Binary fission Multiple fission
    Parent cell divides into two daughter cells. (½) Parent cell divides into many daughter cells simultaneously. (½)
    Example: Amoeba / Leishmania. (½) Example: Plasmodium. (½)
    Common trap: giving Amoeba as the example of multiple fission. Amoeba — binary; Plasmodium — multiple.
Intext p.119, Q2Benefits of spores2 marks

How will an organism be benefited if it reproduces through spores?

Given: reproduction by spores. Asked: the benefits.

Concept used: spore formation in Rhizopus (Topic 2). Why it applies: spores are tiny cells that can each grow into a new individual.

  1. 1

    Many offspring → each sporangium contains many spores, so a large number of new individuals can form.

  2. 2

    Protection → spores have thick walls that protect them in unfavourable conditions until they reach a moist surface, where they begin to grow.

  3. 3

    Spread → spores are tiny and light, so they spread easily (for example, through air) to new places.

  4. Check: this explains why bread mould appears so quickly on any damp bread (Topic 2, Worked example 4) ✔.

  5. Answer The organism produces a large number of spores in its sporangia; the spores spread easily, and their thick walls protect them until they reach a moist surface where they can grow — so many new individuals can form, even after unfavourable conditions.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Large numbers of spores are produced in sporangia and spread easily. (1)
    • Thick walls protect the spores until they find a moist surface to grow. (1)
    Common trap: "Spores grow in dry places." They need a moist surface to begin growing.
Intext p.119, Q3Why complex organisms cannot regenerate2 marks

Can you think of reasons why more complex organisms cannot give rise to new individuals through regeneration?

Given: complex organisms. Asked: reasons they cannot reproduce by regeneration.

Concept used: body organisation and regeneration (Topic 2). Why it applies: regeneration needs cells that can make every other cell type.

  1. 1

    Highly organised body → in complex organisms, specialised cells form tissues, tissues form organs and organ systems, and these must be placed at definite positions in the body.

  2. 2

    Specialised cells → most of their cells are specialised for one job and cannot multiply and change into all the other cell types needed to rebuild a whole body.

  3. 3

    Simple organisms differ → Planaria and Hydra have relatively simple bodies with specialised regenerative cells that can do this; complex organisms cannot rebuild their complicated organ systems from a piece.

  4. Check: in your textbook, regeneration is shown only in simple animals (Hydra, Planaria) ✔.

  5. Answer Complex organisms have highly specialised cells organised into tissues, organs and organ systems at definite positions. A cut piece does not have cells that can multiply and form all these cell types in the right places, so a whole new individual cannot regenerate from it.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Complex organisms have a highly organised body: cells → tissues → organs and organ systems at definite positions. (1)
    • Their cells are specialised and cannot form all the other cell types, so a piece cannot rebuild the whole organism. (1)
    Common trap: "Because they are too big." Size is not the reason — organisation and specialisation are.
Intext p.119, Q4Why vegetative propagation is practised3 marks

Why is vegetative propagation practised for growing some types of plants?

Given: vegetative propagation. Asked: why it is used.

Concept used: uses of vegetative propagation (Topic 2). Why it applies: farmers choose it for definite advantages.

  1. 1

    Seedless plants → plants such as banana, orange, rose and jasmine have lost the capacity to produce seeds; vegetative propagation is the way to grow them.

  2. 2

    Earlier flowers and fruits → plants raised this way bear flowers and fruits earlier than plants grown from seeds.

  3. 3

    Same characteristics → all the new plants are genetically similar enough to the parent to have all its characteristics — useful for keeping a good variety.

  4. 4

    Methods → layering and grafting are used to grow sugarcane, roses and grapes.

  5. Check: these are the advantages listed for vegetative propagation in Topic 2 ✔.

  6. Answer It is used because (i) plants such as banana, orange, rose and jasmine have lost the ability to make seeds; (ii) the plants flower and fruit earlier than those from seeds; (iii) the new plants have all the characteristics of the parent.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Plants that have lost the capacity to produce seeds (banana, orange, rose, jasmine) can be grown. (1)
    • Such plants bear flowers and fruits earlier than those grown from seeds. (1)
    • All new plants are genetically similar to the parent and keep all its characteristics. (1)
    Common trap: writing only "it is easy". Give the three textbook reasons.
Intext p.119, Q5DNA copying as an essential part of reproduction2 marks

Why is DNA copying an essential part of the process of reproduction?

Given: DNA copying. Asked: why it is essential.

Concept used: DNA as the blueprint; DNA copy with cellular apparatus (Topic 1). Why it applies: a new individual needs its own set of instructions.

  1. 1

    Blueprint → the DNA carries the information for making proteins and so for the body design; organisms of a species look alike because their DNA blueprints are alike.

  2. 2

    Each new cell needs a copy → reproduction creates new cells or individuals; each must receive a copy of the DNA, together with its own cellular apparatus, to carry out life processes and build the same body design.

  3. 3

    Without copying → the new individual would have no instructions and could not survive or resemble its parent.

  4. Check: this is why "a basic event in reproduction is the creation of a DNA copy" (Topic 1) ✔.

  5. Answer DNA carries the information for the body design. Every new individual needs its own copy of this information (with its own cellular apparatus) to live and to have the same body design as its parent, so DNA copying is the basic event of reproduction.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • DNA is the information source (blueprint) for proteins and body design. (1)
    • Each new individual must receive a copy of the DNA (with cellular apparatus) to survive and resemble the parent. (1)
    Common trap: confusing this with p.114 Q1. Here, stress that each offspring needs its own copy to have the body design at all.

Intext p.126, Q1Pollination vs fertilisation2 marks

How is the process of pollination different from fertilisation?

Given: two processes in a flower. Asked: how they differ.

Concept used: the pollination vs fertilisation card (Topic 3). Why it applies: both are steps of sexual reproduction in flowers but are different events.

  1. 1

    What happens → pollination is the transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma; fertilisation is the fusion of the male and female germ-cells (gametes).

  2. 2

    Where → pollination ends on the stigma; fertilisation takes place inside the ovule in the ovary.

  3. 3

    Result → pollination places pollen on the stigma; fertilisation forms the zygote.

  4. 4

    Order → pollination comes first; then a pollen tube grows through the style, and then fertilisation takes place.

  5. Check: compare with the matched card in Topic 3 — what happens, where, result, order ✔.

  6. Answer Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma (by wind, water or animals). Fertilisation is the fusion of the male germ-cell with the female germ-cell inside the ovule, forming a zygote. Pollination comes first; fertilisation follows.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Pollination Fertilisation
    Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma. (½) Fusion of male and female gametes. (½)
    Takes place on the stigma; no zygote is formed. (½) Takes place in the ovule; forms the zygote. (½)
    Common trap: "Fertilisation is when pollen reaches the stigma." That is pollination.
Intext p.126, Q2Seminal vesicles and prostate gland2 marks

What is the role of the seminal vesicles and the prostate gland?

Given: two glands of the male system. Asked: their role.

Concept used: the male reproductive system (Topic 4). Why it applies: these glands lie along the path of the vas deferens.

  1. 1

    Where → along the path of the vas deferens.

  2. 2

    What they do → they add their secretions, so the sperms are now in a fluid.

  3. 3

    Why → this fluid makes the transport of sperms easier and also provides nutrition to the sperms.

  4. Check: neither gland makes sperms — that is the job of the testes ✔.

  5. Answer The seminal vesicles and the prostate gland add their secretions to the sperms, forming a fluid that makes the transport of sperms easier and provides them with nutrition.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • They add their secretions to the sperms along the vas deferens, forming a fluid. (1)
    • The fluid makes the transport of sperms easier and provides nutrition. (1)
    Common trap: "They produce sperms" or "testosterone". Both are made by the testes.

What are the changes seen in girls at the time of puberty?

Given: puberty in girls. Asked: the changes.

Concept used: changes at puberty (Topic 4). Why it applies: the textbook lists changes common to boys and girls and changes specific to girls.

  1. 1

    Breasts → breast size begins to increase, and the skin of the nipples at the tips of the breasts becomes darker.

  2. 2

    Menstruation → girls begin to menstruate.

Intext p.126, Q4How the embryo gets nourishment3 marks

How does the embryo get nourishment inside the mother’s body?

Given: an embryo in the uterus. Asked: how it is nourished.

Concept used: implantation and the placenta (Topic 4). Why it applies: the embryo gets everything it needs through the placenta.

  1. 1

    Implantation → the embryo is implanted in the lining of the uterus, which has become thick and richly supplied with blood.

  2. 2

    Placenta → the embryo gets nutrition from the mother's blood through a special tissue, the placenta — a disc embedded in the uterine wall.

  3. 3

    Large surface → the placenta has villi on the embryo's side, surrounded by blood spaces on the mother's side; this gives a large surface area.

  4. 4

    Exchange → glucose and oxygen pass from the mother to the embryo; wastes from the embryo pass into the mother's blood.

  5. Check: follows the human sequence card — implantation → placenta (Topic 4) ✔.

  6. Answer The embryo is implanted in the thick, blood-rich lining of the uterus and gets its nourishment from the mother's blood through the placenta. The villi of the placenta (embryo's side) are surrounded by blood spaces (mother's side), giving a large surface area through which glucose and oxygen pass to the embryo and wastes pass to the mother's blood.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • The embryo is implanted in the thickened, blood-rich lining of the uterus. (1)
    • A special tissue, the placenta (a disc in the uterine wall), has villi on the embryo's side surrounded by blood spaces on the mother's side — a large surface area. (1)
    • Glucose and oxygen pass from mother to embryo; wastes pass from embryo to mother's blood. (1)
    Common trap: "The mother's blood flows into the embryo." Substances pass across the placenta.
Intext p.126, Q5Copper-T and sexually transmitted diseases2 marks

If a woman is using a copper-T, will it help in protecting her from sexually transmitted diseases?

Given: a woman using a copper-T. Asked: will it protect her from STDs?

Concept used: methods of contraception and STIs (Topic 5). Why it applies: only barrier methods help prevent STIs.

  1. 1

    How a copper-T works → it is placed inside the uterus to prevent pregnancy.

  2. 2

    Not a barrier → it does not stop contact between the partners during the sexual act, so microbes can still pass from one person to the other.

  3. 3

    What does help → a barrier such as a condom helps prevent the transmission of many of these infections, to some extent.

  4. Check: contraception card (Topic 5): copper-T — "Protects against STIs? no" ✔.

  5. Answer No. A copper-T only prevents pregnancy; it is placed in the uterus and is not a barrier, so it does not protect her from sexually transmitted diseases. A condom (a barrier method) is needed for that.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • No; the copper-T is placed in the uterus to prevent pregnancy only. (1)
    • It is not a barrier, so infections can still pass during the sexual act; a condom helps prevent them. (1)
    Common trap: "Yes, because it is a contraceptive." Preventing pregnancy is not the same as preventing infection.

Exercise Q4Advantages of sexual reproduction3 marks

What are the advantages of sexual reproduction over asexual reproduction?

Given: two modes of reproduction. Asked: the advantages of the sexual mode.

Concept used: why the sexual mode (Topic 3); importance of variation (Topic 1). Why it applies: the key difference is how much variation is produced.

  1. 1

    Combines DNA from two individuals → each parent carries different variations built up over generations; sexual reproduction combines them, so each offspring gets a new combination.

  2. 2

    More variation, faster → asexual reproduction gets variation only from small errors in DNA copying, which is slow; sexual reproduction produces more variation, more quickly.

  3. 3

    Survival of the species → more variation gives a better chance that some individuals survive if the niche changes, so the species survives.

  4. 4

    Evolution → variation is the basis of evolution.

  5. Check: compare with Card 1 of the wrap-up — variation row: asexual "little", sexual "more" ✔.

  6. Answer Sexual reproduction combines DNA from two individuals, giving new combinations of variations in every offspring — more variation, made faster than by copying errors alone. This increases the chance that some individuals survive changes in the environment, helping the species survive, and provides the variation on which evolution depends.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • DNA from two different individuals is combined, giving new combinations in the offspring. (1)
    • This generates more variation (and faster) than asexual reproduction, where variation comes only from DNA-copying errors. (1)
    • Greater variation improves the chance of survival of the species in changing conditions and is the basis of evolution. (1)
    Common trap: "Sexual reproduction produces more offspring." The advantage is variation, not numbers.

Exercise Q6Why menstruation occurs3 marks

Why does menstruation occur?

Given: menstruation. Asked: why it happens.

Concept used: what happens when the egg is not fertilised (Topic 4). Why it applies: menstruation is the result of a prepared lining not being used.

  1. 1

    Monthly preparation → the ovary releases one egg every month, so the uterus prepares every month to receive a fertilised egg: its lining becomes thick and spongy (and rich in blood) to nourish an embryo.

  2. 2

    No fertilisation → if the egg is not fertilised, it lives for about one day, and the thick lining is no longer needed.

  3. 3

    Lining shed → the lining slowly breaks down and comes out through the vagina as blood and mucus.

  4. 4

    Cycle → this happens roughly every month and is called menstruation; it usually lasts about two to eight days.

  5. Check: branch B of the human sequence card (Topic 4) ✔.

  6. Answer Every month the uterus prepares to receive a fertilised egg, and its lining becomes thick and spongy. If the egg is not fertilised, this lining is not needed; it breaks down and comes out through the vagina as blood and mucus. This monthly event is menstruation (lasting about 2–8 days).

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • One egg is released each month, and the uterine lining becomes thick and spongy to nourish an embryo. (1)
    • If the egg is not fertilised (it lives about one day), the lining is not needed. (1)
    • The lining breaks down and is discharged through the vagina as blood and mucus — menstruation, about every month, lasting 2–8 days. (1)
    Common trap: "Menstruation occurs because an egg is fertilised." It occurs when the egg is not fertilised.

Exercise Q8Methods of contraception5 marks

What are the different methods of contraception?

Given: contraception. Asked: the different methods.

Concept used: methods of contraception (Topic 5). Why it applies: your textbook groups them into categories by how they work.

  1. 1

    Mechanical barrier → a condom on the penis, or a similar covering worn in the vagina, stops sperm from reaching the egg. It also helps prevent the transmission of many sexually transmitted infections, to some extent.

  2. 2

    Hormonal (chemical) method → oral pills change the hormonal balance of the body so that eggs are not released, so fertilisation cannot occur. Because they change hormonal balance, they can cause side-effects.

  3. 3

    Devices in the uterus → the loop or copper-T is placed in the uterus to prevent pregnancy. It can cause side-effects due to irritation of the uterus.

  4. 4

    Surgical methods → blocking the vas deferens in the male prevents sperm transfer; blocking the fallopian tube in the female stops the egg from reaching the uterus. In both cases fertilisation does not take place. They are safe in the long run, but surgery can cause infections and other problems if not done properly.

  5. Check: four categories, as on the contraception card (Topic 5) ✔.

  6. Answer (i) Barrier methods — condoms or similar coverings stop sperm reaching the egg (and the condom helps prevent STIs, to some extent). (ii) Hormonal methods — oral pills prevent the release of eggs (may have side-effects). (iii) Loop or copper-T placed in the uterus (may irritate the uterus). (iv) Surgical methods — blocking the vas deferens (male) or fallopian tubes (female) prevents the gametes from meeting.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    • Barrier: condom / vaginal covering — prevents sperm reaching the egg; the condom also helps prevent sexually transmitted infections, to some extent. (1¼)
    • Hormonal: oral pills — change hormonal balance so eggs are not released; side-effects. (1¼)
    • Loop / copper-T placed in the uterus — prevents pregnancy; side-effects from irritation of the uterus. (1¼)
    • Surgical: vas deferens blocked (no sperm transfer) or fallopian tube blocked (egg cannot reach uterus); safe long-term, but surgery may cause infections if not done properly. (1¼)
    Common trap: listing methods without saying how each works. Each category has a different way of preventing pregnancy.
Exercise Q9Unicellular vs multicellular reproduction3 marks

How are the modes for reproduction different in unicellular and multicellular organisms?

Given: unicellular and multicellular organisms. Asked: how their modes of reproduction differ.

Concept used: asexual methods and body design (Topic 2); sexual reproduction (Topic 3). Why it applies: the mode depends on body design.

  1. 1

    Unicellular → the whole single cell divides to form new individuals — fission: binary (Amoeba, Leishmania) or multiple (Plasmodium); or budding (yeast).

  2. 2

    Multicellular — simple → a very simple body can just break into pieces — fragmentation (Spirogyra).

  3. 3

    Multicellular — organised → most multicellular organisms have tissues and organs at definite positions, so they cannot divide cell by cell. They use specialised cells or parts: regeneration (Planaria, Hydra — though your textbook notes it is not the same as normal reproduction), budding (Hydra), vegetative propagation (potato, Bryophyllum), spores in sporangia (Rhizopus).

  4. 4

    Sexual reproduction → many multicellular organisms reproduce sexually, with gametes made in specialised reproductive organs (flowers; testes and ovaries).

  5. Check: compare with the "unicellular vs multicellular" line under Card 1 in the wrap-up ✔.

  6. Answer In unicellular organisms, the single cell itself divides (binary or multiple fission) or buds (yeast). Multicellular organisms cannot simply divide cell by cell, because their cells form tissues and organs at definite positions; they use fragmentation (simple forms like Spirogyra), regeneration by specialised cells (Planaria, Hydra), budding (Hydra), vegetative propagation, spore formation, or sexual reproduction with gametes made in special reproductive organs.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Unicellular: the whole cell divides — binary fission (Amoeba), multiple fission (Plasmodium), budding (yeast). (1)
    • Multicellular: cannot divide cell by cell (organised tissues and organs); use specialised cells/parts — fragmentation, regeneration, budding (Hydra), vegetative propagation, spores. (1)
    • Multicellular organisms also reproduce sexually, with gametes from specialised reproductive organs. (1)
    Common trap: "Unicellular organisms reproduce by fragmentation." Fragmentation needs a body of many cells (Spirogyra).
Exercise Q10Reproduction and stability of populations3 marks

How does reproduction help in providing stability to populations of species?

Given: reproduction and populations. Asked: how reproduction gives stability.

Concept used: niches and consistent DNA copying (Topic 1). Why it applies: the textbook links reproduction to the stability of populations.

  1. 1

    Populations fill niches → populations of organisms fill well-defined places (niches) in the ecosystem, using their ability to reproduce.

  2. 2

    Consistent copying → reproduction copies the DNA consistently, so offspring keep the body design features that let them use that particular niche.

  3. 3

    Replacing losses → new individuals produced by reproduction replace those that die, keeping the number of the species steady.

  4. 4

    Variation as a safeguard → the small variations produced also let some individuals survive if the niche changes, so the species continues.

  5. Check: the textbook's statement: "Reproduction is therefore linked to the stability of populations of species" ✔.

  6. Answer Through reproduction, organisms produce new individuals that keep the same body design (consistent DNA copying), so they can use the same niche and replace those that die; this keeps the population of the species stable. The small variations produced also help the species survive changes in the niche.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Populations fill their niches in the ecosystem by reproducing. (1)
    • Consistent DNA copying keeps the body design suited to the niche, and new individuals replace those that die. (1)
    • Variations allow some individuals to survive changes, so the species continues. (1)
    Common trap: writing only "reproduction increases numbers". Stability needs the same body design (consistent copying) as well as new individuals.
Exercise Q11Reasons for contraception3 marks

What could be the reasons for adopting contraceptive methods?

Given: contraception. Asked: reasons for using it.

Concept used: reproductive health (Topic 5). Why it applies: your textbook gives health, personal and social reasons.

  1. 1

    Health of the woman → pregnancy makes major demands on the body and mind of the woman; if she is not ready, her health is harmed. Contraception lets her avoid pregnancy until she is ready.

  2. 2

    Readiness → sexual maturity does not mean the body or mind is ready to have and bring up children; contraception avoids unwanted pregnancy.

  3. 3

    Preventing infections → barrier methods (condoms) also help prevent the spread of sexually transmitted infections such as gonorrhoea, syphilis, warts and HIV-AIDS.

  4. 4

    Population → contraception helps control the birth rate, and so the size of the population, which many people link to improving standards of living.

  5. Check: each reason comes from the reproductive-health section of Topic 5 ✔.

  6. Answer To protect the woman's health, since pregnancy makes great demands on her body and mind; to avoid pregnancy when a person is not ready to have and bring up children; to help prevent sexually transmitted infections (barrier methods); and to help control population growth.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • To avoid unwanted pregnancy and protect the woman's health (pregnancy makes major demands on body and mind). (1)
    • Barrier methods help prevent sexually transmitted infections (e.g. HIV-AIDS, gonorrhoea). (1)
    • To control the birth rate and so the population size. (1)
    Common trap: writing "to find out the sex of the baby". Prenatal sex determination is prohibited by law — it has nothing to do with contraception.
