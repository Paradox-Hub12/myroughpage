Topic 3 of 7

How acids and bases react

About 50 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Write word and balanced equations for acid + metal, base + metal (Zn, Al with NaOH), acid + metal carbonate/hydrogencarbonate, acid + base (neutralisation), metal oxide + acid, and non-metal oxide + base.
  • Describe and draw the test for hydrogen and for carbon dioxide.
  • Conclude that metal oxides are basic and non-metal oxides acidic.

Squeeze a lemon into a glass of water, add half a spoon of baking soda — and it foams over the rim. Drop a piece of marble chip into dilute acid and it fizzes the same way. Two very different solids, the same bubbles. Is it the same gas? And can you predict it before you try?

Acids and bases react with only a handful of types of substances, and each type follows one pattern. Learn the six patterns below and you can write equations for substances you have never seen.

Pattern 1 — Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen gas

The metal takes the place of the hydrogen in the acid; the hydrogen is set free as HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ gas.

Mg(s)+HX2SOX4(aq)→MgSOX4(aq)+HX2(g)\ce{Mg(s) + H2SO4(aq) -> MgSO4(aq) + H2(g)}Mg(s)+HX2​SOX4​(aq)​MgSOX4​(aq)+HX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →

Lab: zinc and dilute sulphuric acid (NCERT Activity 2.3)

Lab notebook · do → see → shows
Do
Drop a few zinc granules into a test tube holding about 5 mL of dilute sulphuric acid. Lead the gas through a delivery tube into soap solution, then hold a burning candle next to one of the bubbles.
See
Bubbles form on the zinc; soap bubbles fill with the gas; the bubble burns with a "pop" sound.
Shows
The gas is hydrogen — the pop test. Dilute hydrochloric acid gives the same result.
Note
Safety: do this only with your teacher. Hydrogen catches fire easily — bring the flame near a soap bubble, never to the mouth of the test tube.

Watch out: copper (and silver, gold) do not give hydrogen with dilute hydrochloric or sulphuric acid — they are less reactive than hydrogen. Nitric acid is a special case you will meet in Chapter 3.

Pattern 2 — Base + some metals → salt + hydrogen gas

Only a few metals, such as zinc and aluminium, react with sodium hydroxide solution (on warming). The salt formed has a negative ion made of the metal and oxygen.

2 NaOH(aq)+Zn(s)→NaX2ZnOX2(s)+HX2(g)\ce{2NaOH(aq) + Zn(s) -> Na2ZnO2(s) + H2(g)}2NaOH(aq)+Zn(s)​NaX2​ZnOX2​(s)+HX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →

The salt NaX2ZnOX2\ce{Na2ZnO2}NaX2​ZnOX2​ is sodium zincate. The gas again gives the pop test. Magnesium, iron and copper do not react with sodium hydroxide like this.

In the lab…

sodium zincate actually stays dissolved, so NaX2ZnOX2(aq)\ce{Na2ZnO2(aq)}NaX2​ZnOX2​(aq) is more accurate. The textbook writes (s); write it the textbook's way in the exam.

Pattern 3 — Acid + metal carbonate or hydrogencarbonate → salt + carbon dioxide + water

MgCOX3(s)+2 HNOX3(aq)→Mg(NOX3)X2(aq)+HX2O(l)+COX2(g)\ce{MgCO3(s) + 2HNO3(aq) -> Mg(NO3)2(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g)}MgCOX3​(s)+2HNOX3​(aq)​Mg(NOX3​)X2​(aq)+HX2​O(l)+COX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →

Every carbonate (COX3X2−\ce{CO3^2-}COX3​X2−) and hydrogencarbonate (HCOX3X−\ce{HCO3-}HCOX3​X−) does this. Limestone, chalk and marble are all forms of calcium carbonate, so they all fizz with acids.

The two gas tests side by side

Gas Test Result
Hydrogen, HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ bring a burning splint or candle near it burns with a pop
Carbon dioxide, COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ pass it through lime water lime water turns milky; with excess COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ it turns clear again. COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ also puts out a burning splint.

Pattern 4 — Acid + base → salt + water (neutralisation)

KOH(aq)+HCl(aq)→KCl(aq)+HX2O(l)\ce{KOH(aq) + HCl(aq) -> KCl(aq) + H2O(l)}KOH(aq)+HCl(aq)​KCl(aq)+HX2​O(l)Swipe to see the whole equation →

A reaction between an acid and a base that gives a salt and water is called neutralisation: each cancels the other's effect.

Lab: watching a neutralisation (NCERT Activity 2.6)

Lab notebook · do → see → shows
Do
Add two drops of phenolphthalein to 2 mL of dilute NaOH. Add dilute HCl drop by drop. Then add a few drops of NaOH again.
See
Pink → colourless when enough acid is added → pink again when more base is added.
Shows
The acid has used up the base, so the solution is no longer basic; adding base brings the basic nature back.

Pattern 5 — Metal oxide + acid → salt + water

MgO(s)+2 HCl(aq)→MgClX2(aq)+HX2O(l)\ce{MgO(s) + 2HCl(aq) -> MgCl2(aq) + H2O(l)}MgO(s)+2HCl(aq)​MgClX2​(aq)+HX2​O(l)Swipe to see the whole equation →

In NCERT Activity 2.7, black copper oxide dissolves in dilute hydrochloric acid and the solution turns blue-green — the colour of copper(II) chloride. Because metal oxides react with acids just as bases do, metal oxides are basic oxides.

Pattern 6 — Non-metal oxide + base → salt + water

Ca(OH)X2(aq)+SOX2(g)→CaSOX3(s)+HX2O(l)\ce{Ca(OH)2(aq) + SO2(g) -> CaSO3(s) + H2O(l)}Ca(OH)X2​(aq)+SOX2​(g)​CaSOX3​(s)+HX2​O(l)Swipe to see the whole equation →

The lime-water test is itself an example: carbon dioxide (a non-metal oxide) reacts with the base calcium hydroxide to give a salt and water. Because non-metal oxides react with bases just as acids do, non-metal oxides are acidic oxides.

The patterns at a glance

Pattern Word template
1 acid + metal → salt + hydrogen
2 base (NaOH) + zinc or aluminium → salt + hydrogen
3 acid + carbonate / hydrogencarbonate → salt + carbon dioxide + water
4 acid + base → salt + water
5 acid + metal oxide → salt + water
6 base + non-metal oxide → salt + water

Virtual lab — CBSE Practical 1(B): properties of HCl and NaOH

What you do: Test dilute hydrochloric acid and dilute sodium hydroxide with (i) red and blue litmus, (ii) zinc granules, (iii) solid sodium carbonate.

Test Dilute HCl Dilute NaOH
Blue litmus turns red no change
Red litmus no change turns blue
Zinc granules bubbles at once; colourless gas burns with a pop (hydrogen) no visible change in the cold; on warming, slow bubbles of hydrogen
Solid sodium carbonate brisk effervescence; gas turns lime water milky (COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​) no reaction

What it shows: HCl is an acid; NaOH is a base. A base does not react with a carbonate.

Safety

Wear goggles; handle acids and alkalis with a dropper; hold test tubes with a holder; never smell a gas directly — waft it towards you.

Worked example 1

Acid + metal from the template

Question. Write the word equation and the balanced equation for aluminium foil reacting with dilute hydrochloric acid. How would you test the gas?

Given: aluminium + dilute HCl. Asked: word equation, balanced equation, gas test.

Concept used: Pattern 1 — acid + metal → salt + hydrogen. Why it applies: aluminium is a metal that is more reactive than hydrogen.

  1. 1

    Word equation: aluminium + hydrochloric acid → aluminium chloride + hydrogen.

  2. 2

    Formula of the salt: AlX3+\ce{Al^3+}AlX3+ and ClX−\ce{Cl-}ClX− → AlClX3\ce{AlCl3}AlClX3​.

  3. 3

    Skeletal equation: Al+HCl→AlClX3+HX2\ce{Al + HCl -> AlCl3 + H2}Al+HCl​AlClX3​+HX2​.

  4. 4

    Chlorine: left 1, right 3 → 3 HCl\ce{3HCl}3HCl.

  5. 5

    Hydrogen on the left is now 3, an odd number, but HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ always gives an even number — so double: 6 HCl\ce{6HCl}6HCl.

  6. 6

    Chlorine on the right must now be 6 → 2 AlClX3\ce{2AlCl3}2AlClX3​.

  7. 7

    Aluminium on the right is 2 → 2 Al\ce{2Al}2Al.

  8. 8

    Hydrogen: left 6 → 3 HX2\ce{3H2}3HX2​.

    2 Al(s)+6 HCl(aq)→2 AlClX3(aq)+3 HX2(g)\ce{2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) -> 2AlCl3(aq) + 3H2(g)}2Al(s)+6HCl(aq)​2AlClX3​(aq)+3HX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  9. 9

    Test: bring a burning splint near the gas → it burns with a pop.

  10. Check: Al 2 | 2; H 6 | 6; Cl 6 | 6 ✔.

  11. Answer 2 Al(s)+6 HCl(aq)→2 AlClX3(aq)+3 HX2(g)\ce{2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) -> 2AlCl3(aq) + 3H2(g)}2Al(s)+6HCl(aq)​2AlClX3​(aq)+3HX2​(g); hydrogen burns with a pop.

    Common trap: Writing AlClX2\ce{AlCl2}AlClX2​ (wrong valency) or forgetting that hydrogen gas is HX2\ce{H2}HX2​.

Worked example 2

A hydrogencarbonate and an acid

Question. Potassium hydrogencarbonate reacts with dilute sulphuric acid. Write the balanced equation and name the test for the gas.

Given: KHCOX3\ce{KHCO3}KHCOX3​ + dilute HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​. Asked: balanced equation; gas test.

Concept used: Pattern 3 — acid + hydrogencarbonate → salt + carbon dioxide + water. Why it applies: HCOX3X−\ce{HCO3-}HCOX3​X− is in the reactant.

  1. 1

    Salt: KX+\ce{K+}KX+ and SOX4X2−\ce{SO4^2-}SOX4​X2− → KX2SOX4\ce{K2SO4}KX2​SOX4​.

  2. 2

    Skeletal: KHCOX3+HX2SOX4→KX2SOX4+HX2O+COX2\ce{KHCO3 + H2SO4 -> K2SO4 + H2O + CO2}KHCOX3​+HX2​SOX4​​KX2​SOX4​+HX2​O+COX2​.

  3. 3

    Potassium: 1 | 2 → 2 KHCOX3\ce{2KHCO3}2KHCOX3​.

  4. 4

    Carbon: left 2 → 2 COX2\ce{2CO2}2COX2​.

  5. 5

    Hydrogen: left 2 + 2 = 4 → 2 HX2O\ce{2H2O}2HX2​O.

  6. 6

    Oxygen: left 6 + 4 = 10; right 4 + 2 + 4 = 10 ✔.

    2 KHCOX3(s)+HX2SOX4(aq)→KX2SOX4(aq)+2 HX2O(l)+2 COX2(g)\ce{2KHCO3(s) + H2SO4(aq) -> K2SO4(aq) + 2H2O(l) + 2CO2(g)}2KHCOX3​(s)+HX2​SOX4​(aq)​KX2​SOX4​(aq)+2HX2​O(l)+2COX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    Test: pass the gas through lime water → it turns milky.

  8. Check: K 2 | 2; H 4 | 4; C 2 | 2; S 1 | 1; O 10 | 10 ✔.

  9. Answer 2 KHCOX3(s)+HX2SOX4(aq)→KX2SOX4(aq)+2 HX2O(l)+2 COX2(g)\ce{2KHCO3(s) + H2SO4(aq) -> K2SO4(aq) + 2H2O(l) + 2CO2(g)}2KHCOX3​(s)+HX2​SOX4​(aq)​KX2​SOX4​(aq)+2HX2​O(l)+2COX2​(g); lime water turns milky.

    Common trap: Giving hydrogen as the gas. Carbonates and hydrogencarbonates give carbon dioxide, never hydrogen.

Worked example 3

Neutralisation and a metal oxide

Question. Write balanced equations for (a) calcium hydroxide with dilute nitric acid, (b) zinc oxide with dilute sulphuric acid. What does (b) tell you about zinc oxide?

Given: two reactions of acids. Asked: equations; nature of ZnO.

Concept used: Pattern 4 (acid + base → salt + water) and Pattern 5 (acid + metal oxide → salt + water). Why it applies: Ca(OH)X2\ce{Ca(OH)2}Ca(OH)X2​ is a base; ZnO is a metal oxide.

  1. 1

    (a)

    Salt: CaX2+\ce{Ca^2+}CaX2+ and NOX3X−\ce{NO3-}NOX3​X− → Ca(NOX3)X2\ce{Ca(NO3)2}Ca(NOX3​)X2​.

  2. 2

    Nitrate: 1 | 2 → 2 HNOX3\ce{2HNO3}2HNOX3​.

  3. 3

    Hydrogen: left 2 + 2 = 4 → 2 HX2O\ce{2H2O}2HX2​O.

    Ca(OH)X2(aq)+2 HNOX3(aq)→Ca(NOX3)X2(aq)+2 HX2O(l)\ce{Ca(OH)2(aq) + 2HNO3(aq) -> Ca(NO3)2(aq) + 2H2O(l)}Ca(OH)X2​(aq)+2HNOX3​(aq)​Ca(NOX3​)X2​(aq)+2HX2​O(l)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    (b)

    Salt: ZnX2+\ce{Zn^2+}ZnX2+ and SOX4X2−\ce{SO4^2-}SOX4​X2− → ZnSOX4\ce{ZnSO4}ZnSOX4​.

  5. 5

    Zn 1 | 1; S 1 | 1; H 2 | 2; O 1 + 4 = 5 | 4 + 1 = 5 → already balanced.

    ZnO(s)+HX2SOX4(aq)→ZnSOX4(aq)+HX2O(l)\ce{ZnO(s) + H2SO4(aq) -> ZnSO4(aq) + H2O(l)}ZnO(s)+HX2​SOX4​(aq)​ZnSOX4​(aq)+HX2​O(l)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    A metal oxide that reacts with an acid to give salt and water behaves like a base.

  7. Check: (a) Ca 1 | 1; N 2 | 2; H 4 | 4; O 8 | 8 ✔.

  8. Answer (a) Ca(OH)X2+2 HNOX3→Ca(NOX3)X2+2 HX2O\ce{Ca(OH)2 + 2HNO3 -> Ca(NO3)2 + 2H2O}Ca(OH)X2​+2HNOX3​​Ca(NOX3​)X2​+2HX2​O (b) ZnO+HX2SOX4→ZnSOX4+HX2O\ce{ZnO + H2SO4 -> ZnSO4 + H2O}ZnO+HX2​SOX4​​ZnSOX4​+HX2​O — zinc oxide behaves as a basic oxide here.

    Common trap: Writing hydrogen as a product in (b). An oxide has no free metal to push out hydrogen — the product is water. (In Chapter 3 you will see that zinc oxide also reacts with bases — it is amphoteric. For this question, the reaction with acid is what counts.)

Worked example 4

Non-metal oxide + base

Question. Sulphur dioxide is bubbled through sodium hydroxide solution. Predict the products and write the balanced equation. What does it show about sulphur dioxide?

Given: SOX2\ce{SO2}SOX2​ + NaOH solution. Asked: products, equation, nature of SOX2\ce{SO2}SOX2​.

Concept used: Pattern 6 — base + non-metal oxide → salt + water. Why it applies: sulphur is a non-metal; its oxide reacts with a base.

  1. 1

    The salt contains NaX+\ce{Na+}NaX+ and the sulphite ion SOX3X2−\ce{SO3^2-}SOX3​X2− (from SOX2\ce{SO2}SOX2​ + O) → NaX2SOX3\ce{Na2SO3}NaX2​SOX3​ (sodium sulphite).

  2. 2

    Skeletal: NaOH+SOX2→NaX2SOX3+HX2O\ce{NaOH + SO2 -> Na2SO3 + H2O}NaOH+SOX2​​NaX2​SOX3​+HX2​O.

  3. 3

    Sodium: 1 | 2 → 2 NaOH\ce{2NaOH}2NaOH.

  4. 4

    Hydrogen: 2 | 2 ✔. Oxygen: 2 + 2 = 4 | 3 + 1 = 4 ✔.

    2 NaOH(aq)+SOX2(g)→NaX2SOX3(aq)+HX2O(l)\ce{2NaOH(aq) + SO2(g) -> Na2SO3(aq) + H2O(l)}2NaOH(aq)+SOX2​(g)​NaX2​SOX3​(aq)+HX2​O(l)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    It reacts with a base like an acid does → SOX2\ce{SO2}SOX2​ is an acidic oxide.

  6. Check: Na 2 | 2; S 1 | 1; O 4 | 4; H 2 | 2 ✔.

  7. Answer 2 NaOH(aq)+SOX2(g)→NaX2SOX3(aq)+HX2O(l)\ce{2NaOH(aq) + SO2(g) -> Na2SO3(aq) + H2O(l)}2NaOH(aq)+SOX2​(g)​NaX2​SOX3​(aq)+HX2​O(l); sulphur dioxide is an acidic (non-metal) oxide.

    Common trap: Writing sodium sulphate (NaX2SOX4\ce{Na2SO4}NaX2​SOX4​). SOX2\ce{SO2}SOX2​ gives sulphite, SOX3X2−\ce{SO3^2-}SOX3​X2−.

Worked example 5

Identify the metal and the gas

Question. A silvery metal M reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid and also with hot sodium hydroxide solution. Both reactions give the same gas G, which burns with a pop. The chloride of M has the formula MClX3\ce{MCl3}MClX3​. Identify M and G, and write the equation for M with hydrochloric acid.

Given: M reacts with an acid and a base; gas G pops; chloride MClX3\ce{MCl3}MClX3​. Asked: M, G and one equation.

Concept used: Patterns 1 and 2, the pop test, and valency from a formula. Why it applies: only a few metals react with sodium hydroxide, and the chloride formula gives M's valency.

  1. 1

    G burns with a pop → G is hydrogen.

  2. 2

    M reacts with NaOH to give hydrogen → M is zinc or aluminium.

  3. 3

    MClX3\ce{MCl3}MClX3​ means M has valency 3. Zinc has valency 2; aluminium has valency 3 → M is aluminium.

  4. 4

    Equation (Pattern 1, as in Worked example 1):

    2 Al(s)+6 HCl(aq)→2 AlClX3(aq)+3 HX2(g)\ce{2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) -> 2AlCl3(aq) + 3H2(g)}2Al(s)+6HCl(aq)​2AlClX3​(aq)+3HX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →

  5. Check: zinc would give ZnClX2\ce{ZnCl2}ZnClX2​, not MClX3\ce{MCl3}MClX3​, so aluminium is the only fit ✔.

  6. Answer M = aluminium, G = hydrogen; 2 Al+6 HCl→2 AlClX3+3 HX2\ce{2Al + 6HCl -> 2AlCl3 + 3H2}2Al+6HCl​2AlClX3​+3HX2​

    Common trap: Choosing magnesium. Magnesium does not react with sodium hydroxide, and its chloride is MgClX2\ce{MgCl2}MgClX2​.

Common mistakes!

  • Carbonate + acid → salt + hydrogen

    Carbonates give carbon dioxide and water.

    Use Pattern 3: three products — salt, COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​, HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O.

  • NaOH+HX2SOX4→NaX2SOX4+HX2O\ce{NaOH + H2SO4 -> Na2SO4 + H2O}NaOH+HX2​SOX4​​NaX2​SOX4​+HX2​O

    Not balanced: sodium is 1 on the left but 2 on the right.

    2 NaOH+HX2SOX4→NaX2SOX4+2 HX2O\ce{2NaOH + H2SO4 -> Na2SO4 + 2H2O}2NaOH+HX2​SOX4​​NaX2​SOX4​+2HX2​O — count every element.

  • Wrong salt formula (AlClX2\ce{AlCl2}AlClX2​, MgCl\ce{MgCl}MgCl)

    The salt follows the valencies.

    Criss-cross the ions first (Topic 1).

  • "Carbon dioxide burns with a pop."

    That is hydrogen.

    HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ → pop; COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ → lime water milky, flame out.

  • "Lime water stays milky however much COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ is passed."

    Excess COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ makes soluble Ca(HCOX3)X2\ce{Ca(HCO3)2}Ca(HCOX3​)X2​.

    Milky first, clear with excess.

  • "Every metal reacts with sodium hydroxide."

    Only some (zinc, aluminium).

    Learn the two examples.

Key points

  • Six patterns: acid + metal → salt + HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ · NaOH + Zn/Al → salt + HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ · acid + carbonate/hydrogencarbonate → salt + COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ + HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O · acid + base → salt + water · acid + metal oxide → salt + water · base + non-metal oxide → salt + water.
  • Hydrogen burns with a pop; carbon dioxide turns lime water milky (clear again with excess) and puts out a flame.
  • Acid + base → salt + water is neutralisation.
  • Metal oxides are basic; non-metal oxides are acidic.
