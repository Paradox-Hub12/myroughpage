Salts, common salt and sodium hydroxide

About 40 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Identify the parent acid and base of a salt and predict whether its solution is acidic, basic or neutral.
  • Describe sources and uses of common salt.
  • Describe the chlor-alkali process (products at each electrode, equation) and uses of NaOH, Cl₂ and H₂.

Dissolve a spoon of table salt in water and test it with pH paper: green, pH 7. Now try a spoon of washing soda: violet-blue — the solution is basic. Both are "salts", both are made from an acid and a base. So why is one neutral and the other not? The answer lies in its parents.

A salt has two parents

You have seen that an acid and a base neutralise each other to make a salt and water. A salt is made of the positive ion of the base and the negative ion of the acid:

base+acid⟶salt+water\text{base} + \text{acid} \longrightarrow \text{salt} + \text{water}base+acid⟶salt+waterSwipe to see the whole equation →

So every salt has a parent base and a parent acid. To find them, split the salt into its two ions and add OHX−\ce{OH-}OHX− to the positive one and HX+\ce{H+}HX+ to the negative one:

Salt Positive ion → parent base Negative ion → parent acid
NaCl\ce{NaCl}NaCl NaX+\ce{Na+}NaX+ → NaOH\ce{NaOH}NaOH ClX−\ce{Cl-}ClX− → HCl\ce{HCl}HCl
CuSOX4\ce{CuSO4}CuSOX4​ CuX2+\ce{Cu^2+}CuX2+ → Cu(OH)X2\ce{Cu(OH)2}Cu(OH)X2​ SOX4X2−\ce{SO4^2-}SOX4​X2− → HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​
NaX2COX3\ce{Na2CO3}NaX2​COX3​ NaX+\ce{Na+}NaX+ → NaOH\ce{NaOH}NaOH COX3X2−\ce{CO3^2-}COX3​X2− → HX2COX3\ce{H2CO3}HX2​COX3​ (carbonic acid)
NHX4Cl\ce{NH4Cl}NHX4​Cl NHX4X+\ce{NH4+}NHX4​X+ → NHX4OH\ce{NH4OH}NHX4​OH ClX−\ce{Cl-}ClX− → HCl\ce{HCl}HCl

Families of salts (NCERT Activity 2.13)

Salts with the same positive ion or the same negative ion belong to one family. NaCl\ce{NaCl}NaCl, NaX2SOX4\ce{Na2SO4}NaX2​SOX4​, NaNOX3\ce{NaNO3}NaNOX3​ and NaX2COX3\ce{Na2CO3}NaX2​COX3​ are all sodium salts; NaCl\ce{NaCl}NaCl, KCl\ce{KCl}KCl and NHX4Cl\ce{NH4Cl}NHX4​Cl are all chloride salts. One salt can belong to two families at once.

Is a salt solution acidic, basic or neutral? (NCERT Activity 2.14)

The answer depends on whether each parent is strong or weak (Topic 5). The stronger parent "wins".

Parents (acid + base) Salt solution Examples
strong + strong neutral, pH 7 NaCl\ce{NaCl}NaCl, KNOX3\ce{KNO3}KNOX3​, NaX2SOX4\ce{Na2SO4}NaX2​SOX4​
strong + weak acidic, pH below 7 NHX4Cl\ce{NH4Cl}NHX4​Cl, CuSOX4\ce{CuSO4}CuSOX4​, ZnSOX4\ce{ZnSO4}ZnSOX4​, AlClX3\ce{AlCl3}AlClX3​
weak + strong basic, pH above 7 NaX2COX3\ce{Na2CO3}NaX2​COX3​, NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​, CHX3COONa\ce{CH3COONa}CHX3​COONa (sodium acetate)

For this chapter, remember:

  • Strong acids: HCl, HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​, HNOX3\ce{HNO3}HNOX3​. Weak acids: carbonic acid (HX2COX3\ce{H2CO3}HX2​COX3​), acetic acid (CHX3COOH\ce{CH3COOH}CHX3​COOH).
  • Strong bases: NaOH, KOH. Weak bases: NHX4OH\ce{NH4OH}NHX4​OH, and the hydroxides of copper, zinc, aluminium and iron.
Memory tip

"Strong parent decides." Strong acid parent → acidic salt; strong base parent → basic salt; both strong → neutral.

Common salt — where it comes from

The salt on your dining table is sodium chloride, the salt of hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide — a neutral salt.

  • Sea water contains many salts; sodium chloride is separated from them, mostly by letting sea water evaporate in shallow pans.
  • Rock salt is found as large crystals in underground beds, formed when seas of long ago dried up. The crystals are often brown because of impurities. Rock salt is mined, like coal.

Common salt is also a piece of Indian history: Mahatma Gandhi's Dandi March (1930) made salt a symbol of the freedom struggle.

Beyond the kitchen, common salt is a raw material for sodium hydroxide, baking soda, washing soda and bleaching powder (Topic 7). The first step is the chlor-alkali process.

The chlor-alkali process — making sodium hydroxide

Electrolysis means passing an electric current through a liquid so that it breaks down into new substances; the current enters and leaves through two rods called electrodes — the positive anode and the negative cathode. When electricity is passed through brine (a concentrated solution of sodium chloride in water), the brine decomposes:

2 NaCl(aq)+2 HX2O(l)→2 NaOH(aq)+ClX2(g)+HX2(g)\ce{2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) -> 2NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) + H2(g)}2NaCl(aq)+2HX2​O(l)​2NaOH(aq)+ClX2​(g)+HX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  • The anode (+) gives off chlorine gas.
  • Hydrogen gas is given off at the cathode (−).
  • Sodium hydroxide collects in the solution around the cathode.

The name says what it makes: chlor for chlorine, alkali for sodium hydroxide. All three products are useful:

Product Uses
Sodium hydroxide de-greasing metals; making soaps and detergents; paper making; artificial fibres
Chlorine water treatment; swimming pools; PVC; disinfectants; CFCs; pesticides; making bleaching powder
Hydrogen fuels; margarine (hardening of oils); ammonia for fertilisers
Memory tip

negative chloride ions are pulled to the positive anode — so chlorine at the anode. The cathode gives the clean fuel (hydrogen) and caustic soda (sodium hydroxide's common name).

Worked example 1

Classifying six salts

Question. For each salt, name the parent acid and parent base, and say whether its solution is acidic, basic or neutral: potassium chloride, ammonium nitrate, potassium carbonate, ammonium sulphate, potassium acetate, iron(III) chloride.

Given: six salts. Asked: parents and the nature of each solution.

Concept used: split the salt into ions → parents → compare strengths; the strong parent decides. Why it applies: every salt comes from one acid and one base.

  1. Salt Parent base Parent acid Nature
    KCl\ce{KCl}KCl KOH (strong) HCl (strong) neutral
    NHX4NOX3\ce{NH4NO3}NHX4​NOX3​ NHX4OH\ce{NH4OH}NHX4​OH (weak) HNOX3\ce{HNO3}HNOX3​ (strong) acidic
    KX2COX3\ce{K2CO3}KX2​COX3​ KOH (strong) HX2COX3\ce{H2CO3}HX2​COX3​ (weak) basic
    (NHX4)X2SOX4\ce{(NH4)2SO4}(NHX4​)X2​SOX4​ NHX4OH\ce{NH4OH}NHX4​OH (weak) HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​ (strong) acidic
    CHX3COOK\ce{CH3COOK}CHX3​COOK KOH (strong) CHX3COOH\ce{CH3COOH}CHX3​COOH (weak) basic
    FeClX3\ce{FeCl3}FeClX3​ Fe(OH)X3\ce{Fe(OH)3}Fe(OH)X3​ (weak) HCl (strong) acidic

    Steps for one row, potassium carbonate:

  2. 1

    Ions: KX+\ce{K+}KX+ and COX3X2−\ce{CO3^2-}COX3​X2−.

  3. 2

    Add OHX−\ce{OH-}OHX− to KX+\ce{K+}KX+ → KOH, a strong base.

  4. 3

    Add HX+\ce{H+}HX+ to COX3X2−\ce{CO3^2-}COX3​X2− → HX2COX3\ce{H2CO3}HX2​COX3​, a weak acid.

  5. 4

    The strong parent is the base → the solution is basic (pH above 7).

  6. Check: the two ammonium salts both come out acidic, and the three potassium salts are neutral or basic — never acidic, because KOH is strong ✔.

  7. Answer Neutral: KCl. Acidic: NHX4NOX3\ce{NH4NO3}NHX4​NOX3​, (NHX4)X2SOX4\ce{(NH4)2SO4}(NHX4​)X2​SOX4​, FeClX3\ce{FeCl3}FeClX3​. Basic: KX2COX3\ce{K2CO3}KX2​COX3​, CHX3COOK\ce{CH3COOK}CHX3​COOK.

    Common trap: Saying "every salt is neutral". Only salts of a strong acid and a strong base are neutral.

Worked example 2

The chlor-alkali plant and a town's needs

Question. A town has a chlor-alkali plant. (a) Write the balanced equation for the process. (b) Which product, and from which electrode, would supply: (i) the water-treatment works; (ii) a soap factory; (iii) a fertiliser factory making ammonia?

Given: a chlor-alkali plant; three users. Asked: equation; product and electrode for each user.

Concept used: electrolysis of brine gives ClX2\ce{Cl2}ClX2​ at the anode, HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ at the cathode and NaOH near the cathode. Why it applies: each user needs one of these three products.

  1. 1

    (a)

    Reactants: sodium chloride and water (brine). Products: sodium hydroxide, chlorine, hydrogen.

  2. 2

    Skeletal: NaCl+HX2O→NaOH+ClX2+HX2\ce{NaCl + H2O -> NaOH + Cl2 + H2}NaCl+HX2​O​NaOH+ClX2​+HX2​.

  3. 3

    Chlorine: 1 | 2 → 2 NaCl\ce{2NaCl}2NaCl; then sodium: 2 | 1 → 2 NaOH\ce{2NaOH}2NaOH.

  4. 4

    Hydrogen: left 2 (one HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O), right 2 + 2 = 4 → 2 HX2O\ce{2H2O}2HX2​O. Oxygen: 2 | 2 ✔.

    2 NaCl(aq)+2 HX2O(l)→2 NaOH(aq)+ClX2(g)+HX2(g)\ce{2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) -> 2NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) + H2(g)}2NaCl(aq)+2HX2​O(l)​2NaOH(aq)+ClX2​(g)+HX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    (b)

    (i) Water treatment needs a disinfectant → chlorine, from the anode.

  6. 6

    (ii) Soap making needs an alkali → sodium hydroxide, formed near the cathode.

  7. 7

    (iii) Ammonia is made from hydrogen → hydrogen, from the cathode.

  8. Check: Na 2 | 2; Cl 2 | 2; H 4 | 4; O 2 | 2 ✔. Each product matches its listed use ✔.

The aqueous solution of which salt will turn blue litmus red?

Strong acid + strong base → neutral; litmus does not change.
Weak acid + strong base → basic; it would turn red litmus blue. Do not assume all salts are neutral or that "carbonate" means acidic.

Common mistakes!

  • "All salts are neutral."

    Only salts of a strong acid and a strong base are neutral.

    Find both parents; the strong one decides.

  • "Sodium carbonate solution is acidic — it comes from an acid."

    Its acid (carbonic) is weak and its base (NaOH) is strong.

    NaX2COX3\ce{Na2CO3}NaX2​COX3​, NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​ solutions are basic.

  • "Ammonium chloride is basic — ammonia is a base."

    Its base is weak and its acid (HCl) strong.

    NHX4Cl\ce{NH4Cl}NHX4​Cl solution is acidic.

  • "Chlorine is formed at the cathode."

    Chloride ions are negative and go to the positive anode.

    Anode: ClX2\ce{Cl2}ClX2​; cathode: HX2\ce{H2}HX2​; NaOH near the cathode.

  • "The chlor-alkali process makes common salt."

    It uses salt (brine) to make NaOH, ClX2\ce{Cl2}ClX2​, HX2\ce{H2}HX2​.

    Brine in; three products out.

Key points

  • A salt = positive ion of a base + negative ion of an acid; salts with a common ion form a family.
  • Strong acid + strong base → neutral salt (NaCl); strong acid + weak base → acidic (NHX4Cl\ce{NH4Cl}NHX4​Cl, CuSOX4\ce{CuSO4}CuSOX4​); weak acid + strong base → basic (NaX2COX3\ce{Na2CO3}NaX2​COX3​, NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​, CHX3COONa\ce{CH3COONa}CHX3​COONa).
  • Common salt comes from sea water and rock salt; it is the raw material for NaOH, bleaching powder, baking soda and washing soda.
  • Chlor-alkali: electrolysis of brine gives ClX2\ce{Cl2}ClX2​ at the anode, HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ at the cathode and NaOH near the cathode:
