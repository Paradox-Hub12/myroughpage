Chapter 2 · Unit I

Acids, Bases and Salts

7 topicsAbout 7 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Lemon on your chaat, an antacid after a heavy meal, a spoon of baking soda in pakora batter, soap on a curry stain, chlorine in the town's water tank, a plaster cast on a broken arm — every one of these is acid–base chemistry. Your body, your teeth, the soil on a farm and the fish in a river all depend on keeping the right level of acidity.

This chapter shows you how to recognise acids and bases, how they react (only six patterns!), how to measure how strong they are on the pH scale, and how one kitchen substance — common salt — is the starting point for four useful chemicals (sodium hydroxide, bleaching powder, baking soda and washing soda). You will also meet a fifth, Plaster of Paris, which is made from the mineral gypsum. It is part of Unit I, Chemical Substances – Nature and Behaviour, and its equations and named chemicals come up in the board exam every year.

What you'll learn

  • Write the ions and formulae of common acids, bases and salts; balance equations by counting atoms (and charges); use state symbols.
  • Name natural, synthetic and smell-based indicators; give their colours in acids and bases; identify an unknown solution.
  • Write equations for six reaction patterns (with metals, carbonates, bases and oxides); test for hydrogen and carbon dioxide; say why metal oxides are basic and non-metal oxides acidic.
  • Explain acids and bases using HX+\ce{H+}HX+/HX3OX+\ce{H3O+}HX3​OX+ and OHX−\ce{OH-}OHX− ions; explain why acid solutions conduct, why dry HCl gas is not acidic, and how to dilute an acid safely.
  • Use universal indicator and the pH scale; tell strong/weak from concentrated/dilute; predict pH changes; explain why pH matters in the body, teeth, soil, stings and rain.
  • Find the parent acid and base of a salt and predict whether its solution is acidic, basic or neutral; describe common salt and the chlor-alkali process.
  • Give the formula, preparation and uses of each; explain water of crystallisation.
  • Use three summary cards and a clue table to identify unknown substances and gases.
