Chemistry toolkit — ions, formulae and balancing

About 20–25 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Write the ions and formulae of common acids, bases and salts from valencies (H⁺, OH⁻, Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, HCO₃⁻, Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Zn²⁺, Al³⁺, NH₄⁺).
  • Balance an equation by counting atoms.
  • Use state symbols (s), (l), (g), (aq).
Why this lesson exists

Almost every mark in this chapter needs a correct formula — of an acid, a base, a salt or a gas — inside a balanced equation. If you learnt this in Chapter 1, prove it below and skip ahead to Topic 2. If not, this short lesson is your toolkit.

Look at the label on a bottle of drain cleaner: contains sodium hydroxide, NaOH. On a packet of baking soda: sodium hydrogencarbonate, NaHCO₃. On the lab shelf: H₂SO₄. Chemistry labels are written in formulae. In this chapter you will meet about forty of them — but they are all built from the same small set of ions.

  1. Ions — the building blocks of acids, bases and salts

An ion is an atom, or a group of atoms, that carries an electric charge. The size of the charge is its valency — the number of "hands" it has for joining.

  • Positive ions (cations) come from metals, from hydrogen and from ammonium.
  • Negative ions (anions) come from non-metals and from acid groups.

A group such as SOX4X2−\ce{SO4^2-}SOX4​X2− or NHX4X+\ce{NH4+}NHX4​X+ that stays together and carries one charge is a polyatomic ion. It never splits up in the reactions of this chapter, so you can treat it as one block.

  1. Three families built from these ions

You can now see the whole chapter in one line:

  • Acids contain hydrogen that is released as HX+\ce{H+}HX+ in water: HCl\ce{HCl}HCl, HNOX3\ce{HNO3}HNOX3​, HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​, HX2COX3\ce{H2CO3}HX2​COX3​ (carbonic acid), CHX3COOH\ce{CH3COOH}CHX3​COOH (acetic acid).
  • Bases are metal oxides or hydroxides — the hydroxides give OHX−\ce{OH-}OHX− in water: NaOH\ce{NaOH}NaOH, KOH\ce{KOH}KOH, Ca(OH)X2\ce{Ca(OH)2}Ca(OH)X2​, Mg(OH)X2\ce{Mg(OH)2}Mg(OH)X2​, NHX4OH\ce{NH4OH}NHX4​OH.
  • Salts are made of the positive ion of a base and the negative ion of an acid: NaCl\ce{NaCl}NaCl, CuSOX4\ce{CuSO4}CuSOX4​, NHX4Cl\ce{NH4Cl}NHX4​Cl, CaCOX3\ce{CaCO3}CaCOX3​.
Memory tip

for a salt, take the first name from the base and the second name from the acid. Sodium hydroxide + sulphuric acid → sodium sulphate.

  1. Writing a formula: the criss-cross method

  1. Write the positive ion first, the negative ion second.
  2. Write each charge (valency) above it.
  3. Cross the numbers over to become subscripts.
  4. Simplify if both share a factor; never write a subscript 1.
  5. Put a bracket round a polyatomic ion if you need more than one of it.

Check every time: the total positive charge must equal the total negative charge.

  1. Balancing by counting atoms

A balanced equation has the same number of atoms of each element on both sides, because atoms are not created or destroyed in a reaction (law of conservation of mass). The method you used in Chapter 1:

  1. Write correct formulae first — never change a formula to balance; change only the big number (coefficient) in front.
  2. Count atoms on each side in a small table.
  3. Balance one element at a time; treat an unchanged polyatomic ion (SOX4\ce{SO4}SOX4​, NOX3\ce{NO3}NOX3​, COX3\ce{CO3}COX3​) as one block.
  4. Leave hydrogen and oxygen in water until last.
  5. Recount everything.

In this chapter you will also balance equations with ions. Then one more check is needed: the total charge must be the same on both sides. For example,

HX+(aq)+OHX−(aq)→HX2O(l)\ce{H+(aq) + OH-(aq) -> H2O(l)}HX+(aq)+OHX−(aq)​HX2​O(l)Swipe to see the whole equation →

has H 2 | 2, O 1 | 1 and charge (+1) + (−1) = 0 on the left, 0 on the right ✔.

Two shortcuts you may also see: an upward arrow ↑ after a gas, and a downward arrow ↓ after a precipitate.

  1. Water inside a crystal

Some crystals carry a fixed number of water molecules. The dot means "together with": CuSOX4 ⋅ 5 HX2O\ce{CuSO4.5H2O}CuSOX4​⋅5HX2​O is one CuSOX4\ce{CuSO4}CuSOX4​ unit with five water molecules. You will need this in Topic 7 for washing soda (NaX2COX3 ⋅ 10 HX2O\ce{Na2CO3.10H2O}NaX2​COX3​⋅10HX2​O) and gypsum (CaSOX4 ⋅ 2 HX2O\ce{CaSO4.2H2O}CaSOX4​⋅2HX2​O). Count the atoms of the water part separately and add them.

Worked example 1

Formulae of an acid, a base and two salts

Question. Write the formulae of (a) phosphoric acid (phosphate ion POX4X3−\ce{PO4^3-}POX4​X3−), (b) aluminium hydroxide, (c) ammonium sulphate, (d) calcium hydrogencarbonate.

Given: four names. Asked: their formulae.

Concept used: criss-cross with ion charges; brackets for polyatomic ions used more than once. Why it applies: each substance is made of one positive and one negative ion whose charges are on the ion card (phosphate is given).

  1. 1

    (a) Phosphoric acid

    Ions: HX+\ce{H+}HX+ (+1) and POX4X3−\ce{PO4^3-}POX4​X3− (−3).

  2. 2

    Cross over: H gets 3, POX4\ce{PO4}POX4​ gets 1 → HX3POX4\ce{H3PO4}HX3​POX4​.

  3. 3

    (b) Aluminium hydroxide

    Ions: AlX3+\ce{Al^3+}AlX3+ (+3) and OHX−\ce{OH-}OHX− (−1).

  4. 4

    Cross over: Al gets 1, OH gets 3. Three OH groups need a bracket → Al(OH)X3\ce{Al(OH)3}Al(OH)X3​.

  5. 5

    (c) Ammonium sulphate

    Ions: NHX4X+\ce{NH4+}NHX4​X+ (+1) and SOX4X2−\ce{SO4^2-}SOX4​X2− (−2).

  6. 6

    Cross over: NHX4\ce{NH4}NHX4​ gets 2, SOX4\ce{SO4}SOX4​ gets 1. Two ammonium groups need a bracket → (NHX4)X2SOX4\ce{(NH4)2SO4}(NHX4​)X2​SOX4​.

  7. 7

    (d) Calcium hydrogencarbonate

    Ions: CaX2+\ce{Ca^2+}CaX2+ (+2) and HCOX3X−\ce{HCO3-}HCOX3​X− (−1).

  8. 8

    Cross over: Ca gets 1, HCOX3\ce{HCO3}HCOX3​ gets 2 → Ca(HCOX3)X2\ce{Ca(HCO3)2}Ca(HCOX3​)X2​.

  9. Check: charges add to zero — (a) 3 × (+1) − 3 = 0; (b) +3 − 3 × 1 = 0; (c) 2 × (+1) − 2 = 0; (d) +2 − 2 × 1 = 0 ✔.

  10. Answer (a) HX3POX4\ce{H3PO4}HX3​POX4​ (b) Al(OH)X3\ce{Al(OH)3}Al(OH)X3​ (c) (NHX4)X2SOX4\ce{(NH4)2SO4}(NHX4​)X2​SOX4​ (d) Ca(HCOX3)X2\ce{Ca(HCO3)2}Ca(HCOX3​)X2​

    Common trap: Writing NHX4SOX4\ce{NH4SO4}NHX4​SOX4​ or CaHCOX3\ce{CaHCO3}CaHCOX3​ — the charges would not add to zero.

Worked example 2

Balancing a neutralisation with a group

Question. Balance Mg(OH)X2+HNOX3→Mg(NOX3)X2+HX2O\ce{Mg(OH)2 + HNO3 -> Mg(NO3)2 + H2O}Mg(OH)X2​+HNOX3​​Mg(NOX3​)X2​+HX2​O and add state symbols for: solid magnesium hydroxide, dilute nitric acid, a solution of magnesium nitrate, and water.

Given: a skeletal equation and the states in words. Asked: the balanced equation with state symbols.

Concept used: balance by counting atoms, nitrate as one block, H and O last. Why it applies: the formulae are already correct, so only the coefficients can change.

  1. 1

    Count the nitrate blocks: left 1, right 2 → put 2 in front of HNOX3\ce{HNO3}HNOX3​.

  2. 2

    Count Mg: 1 | 1 ✔.

  3. 3

    Count H on the left: 2 (in Mg(OH)X2\ce{Mg(OH)2}Mg(OH)X2​) + 2 (in 2 HNOX3\ce{2HNO3}2HNOX3​) = 4. Right: 2 (in one HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O) → put 2 in front of HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O.

  4. 4

    Count O outside the nitrate blocks: left 2 (in (OH)X2\ce{(OH)2}(OH)X2​); right 2 (in 2 HX2O\ce{2H2O}2HX2​O) ✔.

  5. 5

    Add the states: solid (s), dilute acid (aq), solution (aq), water (l).

    Mg(OH)X2(s)+2 HNOX3(aq)→Mg(NOX3)X2(aq)+2 HX2O(l)\ce{Mg(OH)2(s) + 2HNO3(aq) -> Mg(NO3)2(aq) + 2H2O(l)}Mg(OH)X2​(s)+2HNOX3​(aq)​Mg(NOX3​)X2​(aq)+2HX2​O(l)Swipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: Mg 1 | 1; N 2 | 2; H 2 + 2 = 4 | 4; O 2 + 6 = 8 | 6 + 2 = 8 ✔.

  7. Answer Mg(OH)X2(s)+2 HNOX3(aq)→Mg(NOX3)X2(aq)+2 HX2O(l)\ce{Mg(OH)2(s) + 2HNO3(aq) -> Mg(NO3)2(aq) + 2H2O(l)}Mg(OH)X2​(s)+2HNOX3​(aq)​Mg(NOX3​)X2​(aq)+2HX2​O(l)

    Common trap: "Balancing" by writing MgNOX3\ce{MgNO3}MgNOX3​ on the right. That changes the formula — magnesium always needs two nitrate groups.

The correct formula of calcium carbonate is:

Common mistakes!

  • CaOHX2\ce{CaOH2}CaOHX2​ or MgNOX32\ce{MgNO32}MgNOX32​

    Without a bracket, the 2 doubles only the last atom.

    Bracket a group you need more than once: Ca(OH)X2\ce{Ca(OH)2}Ca(OH)X2​, Mg(NOX3)X2\ce{Mg(NO3)2}Mg(NOX3​)X2​.

  • Changing a formula to make atoms match

    A new formula is a different substance.

    Change only the coefficients in front.

  • Forgetting the charge check in ionic equations

    Charge, like atoms, cannot appear or vanish.

    Add up the charges on each side; they must be equal.

  • HX2O(aq)\ce{H2O(aq)}HX2​O(aq)

    Water is the solvent, a liquid.

    Write HX2O(l)\ce{H2O(l)}HX2​O(l).

Key points

  • Acids give HX+\ce{H+}HX+ in water; hydroxide bases give OHX−\ce{OH-}OHX−; salts = positive ion of the base + negative ion of the acid.
  • Criss-cross the charges, simplify, bracket polyatomic ions; the charges must add up to zero.
  • Balance by changing only coefficients; check atoms — and charge, for ionic equations.
  • (s), (l), (g), (aq): "solution" means (aq); water is (l).
