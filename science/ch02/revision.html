The whole chapter on one page

Indicators — show a different colour (or smell) in acid and base. Blue litmus → red in acid; red litmus → blue in base. Phenolphthalein: colourless in acid, pink in base. Methyl orange: red in acid, yellow in base. Turmeric: yellow → reddish-brown in base. Red cabbage: reddish-pink in acid, green in base. Olfactory (onion, vanilla, clove oil): smell disappears in base.

Six reaction patterns

  1. acid + metal → salt + hydrogen (pop test)
  2. NaOH + zinc/aluminium → salt + hydrogen
  3. acid + carbonate/hydrogencarbonate → salt + carbon dioxide + water (lime water milky; clear again in excess)
  4. acid + base → salt + water (neutralisation)
  5. acid + metal oxide → salt + water → metal oxides are basic
  6. base + non-metal oxide → salt + water → non-metal oxides are acidic

Acids and bases in water — acids give HX+(aq)\ce{H+(aq)}HX+(aq), written HX3OX+\ce{H3O+}HX3​OX+ (hydronium); bases give OHX−(aq)\ce{OH-(aq)}OHX−(aq). Alkali = water-soluble base. Acid/alkali solutions conduct (ions); glucose and alcohol solutions do not. Dry HCl gas does not change dry litmus — ions form only in water. Dissolving acids/bases in water is highly exothermic: add acid to water, slowly, stirring. Dilution lowers ion concentration.

pH — 0 to 14; 7 neutral; below 7 acidic (smaller = more acidic); above 7 basic. Universal indicator gives a colour for each pH. Strong/weak = how many ions an acid/base releases (HCl, HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​, HNOX3\ce{HNO3}HNOX3​, NaOH, KOH strong; acetic, citric, carbonic acids and NHX4OH\ce{NH4OH}NHX4​OH weak). Concentrated/dilute = how much water. Body 7.0–7.8 · acid rain < 5.6 · tooth decay < 5.5 · antacids are mild bases (Mg(OH)X2\ce{Mg(OH)2}Mg(OH)X2​) · toothpaste basic · acidic soil + quicklime/slaked lime/chalk · bee sting (acid) + baking soda.

Salts — positive ion of the base + negative ion of the acid. Strong acid + strong base → neutral (NaCl); strong acid + weak base → acidic (NHX4Cl\ce{NH4Cl}NHX4​Cl, CuSOX4\ce{CuSO4}CuSOX4​); weak acid + strong base → basic (NaX2COX3\ce{Na2CO3}NaX2​COX3​, NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​, CHX3COONa\ce{CH3COONa}CHX3​COONa).

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Indicator: a substance with a different colour or smell in acidic and basic solutions.
  • Acid: a substance that gives HX+(aq)\ce{H+(aq)}HX+(aq) (HX3OX+\ce{H3O+}HX3​OX+) ions in water. Base: gives OHX−\ce{OH-}OHX− ions. Alkali: a water-soluble base.
  • Neutralisation: acid + base → salt + water.
  • pH scale: a scale (0–14) that shows how acidic or basic a solution is; 7 neutral.
  • Strong / weak acid: gives many / few HX+\ce{H+}HX+ ions. Concentrated / dilute: much / little acid per volume of water.
  • Dilution: adding water, which decreases the concentration of ions per unit volume.
  • Water of crystallisation: the fixed number of water molecules in one formula unit of a salt.
