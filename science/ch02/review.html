Week 1

Set 2 — Week 1

5 questions, about 8 minutes

Focus: applying the ideas — slightly harder.

W1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

Write the balanced equation: zinc + dilute hydrochloric acid.

Answer: Zn+2 HCl→ZnClX2+HX2\ce{Zn + 2HCl -> ZnCl2 + H2}Zn+2HCl​ZnClX2​+HX2​ (Topic 3)

Working:

  1. Pattern 1: acid + metal → salt + hydrogen. Salt: ZnX2+\ce{Zn^2+}ZnX2+ + ClX−\ce{Cl-}ClX− → ZnClX2\ce{ZnCl2}ZnClX2​.
  2. Chlorine: 1 on the left, 2 on the right → put 2 in front of HCl.
  3. Check: Zn 1 | 1; H 2 | 2; Cl 2 | 2 ✔.
Did you get it?

W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

A bottle is labelled "dilute nitric acid". Is it a strong or a weak acid? Explain the difference between the two labels.

Answer: Strong — nitric acid is a strong acid whatever its concentration. "Dilute" tells you there is a lot of water; "strong" tells you the acid releases a large number of HX+\ce{H+}HX+ ions. (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

W1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

Predict whether solutions of (a) copper sulphate and (b) potassium nitrate are acidic, basic or neutral.

Answer: (a) Acidic — HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​ strong, Cu(OH)X2\ce{Cu(OH)2}Cu(OH)X2​ weak. (b) Neutral — HNOX3\ce{HNO3}HNOX3​ and KOH both strong. (Topic 6)

Did you get it?

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Why is a concentrated acid always added to water, and not the other way round?

Answer: The mixing is highly exothermic. Water added to acid can boil and splash out, causing burns, and can crack the container; acid added slowly to water lets the heat spread through the water. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

W1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

Write the equation for Plaster of Paris setting with water. Why must PoP be stored dry?

Answer: CaSOX4 ⋅ 12 HX2O+1 12 HX2O→CaSOX4 ⋅ 2 HX2O\ce{CaSO4.1/2H2O + 1 1/2H2O -> CaSO4.2H2O}CaSOX4​⋅21​HX2​O+1 21​HX2​O​CaSOX4​⋅2HX2​O. Moisture would turn it into hard gypsum inside the container. (Topic 7)

Did you get it?

Month 1

Set 3 — Month 1

5 questions, about 10 minutes

Focus: mixed, exam-style — the way board questions combine ideas.

M1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

A white solid X gives a gas with dilute sulphuric acid. The gas turns lime water milky. The other products are water and sodium sulphate. Identify X (a normal carbonate) and write the equation.

Answer: X = sodium carbonate. NaX2COX3+HX2SOX4→NaX2SOX4+HX2O+COX2\ce{Na2CO3 + H2SO4 -> Na2SO4 + H2O + CO2}NaX2​COX3​+HX2​SOX4​​NaX2​SOX4​+HX2​O+COX2​.

Working (Topic 3 — Pattern 3):

  1. Lime water milky → carbon dioxide → X is a carbonate (or hydrogencarbonate).
  2. Product sodium sulphate → X contains sodium; the question says a normal carbonate → NaX2COX3\ce{Na2CO3}NaX2​COX3​.
  3. Check: Na 2 | 2; C 1 | 1; O 7 | 4 + 1 + 2 = 7; H 2 | 2; S 1 | 1 ✔.
Did you get it?

M1.2 · 2 of 5●●● Advanced

Dilute HCl is added slowly to sodium hydroxide solution containing universal indicator until the acid is in excess. Describe how the pH and the colour change.

Answer: pH falls from about 13 → 7 at neutralisation → below 7 with excess acid. Colour: violet/blue → green at pH 7 → yellow/orange → red. (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

M1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

Give one experiment that shows acids give ions in water, and one that shows water is needed for those ions to form.

Answer: (1) Conductivity test — the bulb glows with dilute HCl but not with glucose solution. (2) Dry vs moist litmus in HCl gas — dry litmus does not change; moist blue litmus turns red. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

M1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Trace bleaching powder back to common salt in two equations.

Answer: 2 NaCl+2 HX2O→2 NaOH+ClX2+HX2\ce{2NaCl + 2H2O -> 2NaOH + Cl2 + H2}2NaCl+2HX2​O​2NaOH+ClX2​+HX2​ (chlor-alkali: chlorine from the anode), then 2 Ca(OH)X2+2 ClX2→Ca(ClO)X2+CaClX2+2 HX2O\ce{2Ca(OH)2 + 2Cl2 -> Ca(ClO)2 + CaCl2 + 2H2O}2Ca(OH)X2​+2ClX2​​Ca(ClO)X2​+CaClX2​+2HX2​O (chlorine on dry slaked lime). (Topics 6–7)

Did you get it?

M1.5 · 5 of 5●●● Advanced

A friend suggests treating a bee sting with lemon juice "because lemon is natural". Evaluate the suggestion and give a better remedy.

Answer: Not a good idea. A bee sting leaves an acid; lemon juice is also an acid and would not neutralise it. A mild base such as baking soda neutralises the acid and relieves the pain. (Topic 5)
