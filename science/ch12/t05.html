Topic 5 of 6

Direct current and alternating current

About 20 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Distinguish direct current (one direction; cells) from alternating current (reverses periodically; household supply).
  • State that the AC supply in India is 220 V at 50 Hz and find how often it changes direction (100 times a second, once every 1/100 s).
  • State the advantage of AC over DC.

The current from a torch cell always flows the same way round the circuit. The current in the plug socket at home does something surprising: it keeps turning round and flowing back the other way — a hundred times every second. Why would anyone want a current like that?

Quick reminder (frequency)

If something repeats regularly, its frequency is the number of complete repeats (cycles) in one second. Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz): 1 Hz = one cycle per second.

About this topic

The CBSE syllabus for 2026-27 lists "Direct current. Alternating current: frequency of AC. Advantage of AC over DC." Your 2026-27 textbook keeps only one line on it (in What you have learnt): "In our houses we receive AC electric power of 220 V with a frequency of 50 Hz." The other facts below come from the NCERT Exemplar and the earlier edition of your textbook, and are marked where they appear.

  1. Direct current (DC)

A direct current always flows in one direction. The current from a cell or a battery is direct current: in every circuit of Chapter 11, the current leaves the + terminal and returns to the − terminal, and it never turns round.

  1. Alternating current (AC)

An alternating current reverses its direction periodically — it flows one way, then the other way, then back again, over and over, at regular intervals. The difference in one line (NCERT Exemplar):

Direct current always flows in one direction, whereas alternating current reverses its direction periodically.

The electricity supplied to our homes is AC. In India it has a potential difference of 220 V and a frequency of 50 Hz.

  1. How often does the household current change direction?

  • A frequency of 50 Hz means 50 complete cycles every second.
  • In each cycle the current flows one way and then the other way, so it changes direction twice per cycle.
  • So in one second it changes direction 2×50=1002 \times 50 = 1002×50=100 times.
  • That is, the AC in India changes direction after every 1100\dfrac{1}{100}1001​ second (NCERT Exemplar). One complete cycle takes 150\dfrac{1}{50}501​ second.

  1. The advantage of AC over DC

An important advantage of AC over DC is that electric power can be transmitted over long distances without much loss of energy. (Earlier edition of your textbook; the syllabus asks for this point.)

Power stations are often far from the towns that use the electricity, so this matters a great deal (earlier edition of your textbook: most power stations constructed these days produce AC).

Worked example 2

DC or AC?

Question. Say whether each of these gives direct current or alternating current, with a reason: (a) the cells in a torch, (b) the socket in a classroom wall, (c) a car battery.

Given: three sources. Asked: DC or AC for each.

Concept used: DC flows one way only; the household supply is AC (this topic).

  1. 1

    (a) Torch cells → a cell drives the current from its + terminal round to its − terminal, always the same way → DC.

  2. 2

    (b) Wall socket → it is part of the mains supply (220 V, 50 Hz), which keeps reversing → AC.

  3. 3

    (c) Car battery → a battery is a set of cells; the current flows one way → DC.

  4. Check: only a supply that keeps reversing is AC — of the three, only the wall socket is part of the 50 Hz mains; both the others are made of cells ✔.

  5. Answer (a) DC (b) AC (c) DC

    Common trap: "a battery gives AC because it is strong". Strength has nothing to do with it — only the direction of flow.

Common mistakes!

  • "50 Hz means the current changes direction 50 times a second."

    50 Hz is 50 cycles; each cycle has two reversals.

    Reversals per second = 2×50=1002 \times 50 = 1002×50=100; once every 1100\dfrac{1}{100}1001​ s.

  • Mixing up 220 V and 50 Hz.

    220 V is the potential difference; 50 Hz is the frequency.

    "220 volts, 50 hertz" — learn them as a pair.

  • "AC is better because it never stops."

    Both kinds keep flowing while the switch is on; the difference is that AC keeps reversing its direction.

    Advantage of AC: power sent over long distances without much loss of energy.

Key points

  • Direct current (DC) always flows in one direction — for example, from a cell or battery.
  • Alternating current (AC) reverses its direction periodically. The household supply in India is AC at 220 V and 50 Hz.
  • At 50 Hz the current changes direction twice in each cycle: 100 times a second, i.e. after every 1100\dfrac{1}{100}1001​ s (one cycle takes 150\dfrac{1}{50}501​ s).
  • Advantage of AC over DC: electric power can be transmitted over long distances without much loss of energy.
