Intext p.196, Q1A compass near a bar magnet2 marks

Why does a compass needle get deflected when brought near a bar magnet?

Given: a compass needle near a bar magnet. Asked: why the needle turns.

Concept used: a compass needle is a small magnet; the magnetic field of the bar magnet exerts forces on its poles (Topic 1). Why it applies: the needle is inside the region where the magnet's force can be detected.

  1. 1

    The needle is a magnet → a compass needle is a small bar magnet with a north and a south pole.

  2. 2

    The bar magnet has a field → the region round it is its magnetic field, where its force acts.

  3. 3

    Forces on the poles → like poles repel and unlike poles attract, so the bar magnet pushes one end of the needle and pulls the other.

  4. 4

    Result → the needle turns until it lies along the magnet's field — it is deflected.

  5. Check: a non-magnetic needle (for example, of brass) would not turn — the effect needs a magnet ✔.

  6. Answer A compass needle is itself a small magnet. Near a bar magnet it lies in the magnet's magnetic field, which exerts forces on the needle's poles (like poles repel, unlike poles attract). These forces turn the needle until it points along the field, so it is deflected.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    The compass needle is a small magnet. (1) The magnetic field of the bar magnet exerts forces on its poles (attraction/repulsion), which turn the needle along the field. (1)

    Common trap: "because the bar magnet attracts iron". The needle turns because it is a magnet — its two poles feel forces in opposite directions.

Intext p.200, Q1Field lines around a bar magnet2 marks

Draw magnetic field lines around a bar magnet.

Given: a bar magnet. Asked: a drawing of its field lines.

Concept used: field lines leave N and enter S outside, run S to N inside, never cross, crowd near the poles (Topic 1). Why it applies: these four rules decide every feature of the drawing.

  1. 1

    Magnet → draw a rectangle; label one end N and the other S.

  2. 2

    Lines → draw at least four smooth curves from the N end round to the S end — two above the magnet and two below, plus a short line out of each end along the axis.

  3. 3

    Arrows → on every line outside the magnet, the arrow points from N towards S; on the line inside, from S to N.

  4. 4

    Spacing → keep the lines close together near the poles and wider apart away from the magnet.

  5. 5

    No crossing → no two lines touch or cross.

  6. Check: follow any line with your pencil: out of N, round, into S, and back through the magnet to N — a closed curve ✔.

  7. Answer The drawing is the field-line pattern of a bar magnet (as in Topic 1, Fig 12.4): closed curves out of N and into S outside, S to N inside, crowded at the poles, never crossing.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Correct pattern of closed curves from N to S, crowded near the poles and not crossing (1); arrows from N to S outside the magnet (1)

    Common trap: arrows from S to N outside. Outside the magnet the arrows always point away from N.
Intext p.200, Q2Properties of field lines3 marks

List the properties of magnetic field lines.

Given: magnetic field lines. Asked: their properties.

Concept used: the description of field lines (Topic 1).

  1. 1

    Direction → outside a magnet, field lines emerge from the north pole and merge at the south pole; the direction at a point is the way a compass's north pole points there.

  2. 2

    Closed curves → inside the magnet they run from S to N, so field lines are closed curves.

  3. 3

    Strength → the field is stronger where the lines are closer together (crowded).

  4. 4

    No crossing → no two field lines cross each other.

  5. Check: each property can be seen in the iron-filing and compass patterns of Activities 12.2 and 12.3 ✔.

  6. Answer

  7. 1

    Field lines emerge from the N pole and merge at the S pole outside a magnet (the direction is that of a compass's north pole).

  8. 2

    Inside the magnet they go from S to N; so they are closed curves.

  9. 3

    They are closer together where the field is stronger.

  10. 4

    Two field lines never cross each other.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    Any three: emerge from N and merge at S outside; S to N inside — closed curves; closer where the field is stronger; never intersect. (1 + 1 + 1)

    Common trap: writing "they start at N and end at S". They do not end: they continue through the magnet and are closed.
Intext p.200, Q3Why lines never intersect2 marks

Why don’t two magnetic field lines intersect each other?

Given: two field lines. Asked: why they cannot cross.

Concept used: a field line at a point gives the direction of the field there (Topic 1). Why it applies: a crossing gives two directions at one point.

  1. 1

    At a crossing point → each line has its own direction there, so there would be two directions at that point.

  2. 2

    A compass there → it would have to point in two directions at once.

  3. 3

    Not possible → the field at a point has only one direction.

  4. Check: no iron-filing pattern ever shows two lines meeting ✔.

  5. Answer If two field lines crossed, a compass needle placed at the point of intersection would have to point in two directions at the same time, which is not possible. So two field lines never intersect.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    At the point of intersection there would be two directions of the magnetic field (1); a compass needle placed there would point in two directions, which is not possible (1)

    Common trap: "because they repel each other". Field lines are a way of drawing the field; the reason is the single direction of the field at each point.

Intext p.201, Q1A loop on the table3 marks

Consider a circular loop of wire lying in the plane of the table. Let the current pass through the loop clockwise. Apply the right-hand rule to find out the direction of the magnetic field inside and outside the loop.

Given: loop flat on the table; current clockwise (as seen from above). Asked: field direction inside and outside the loop.

Concept used: right-hand thumb rule applied to a short piece of the loop (Topics 2 and 3). Why it applies: each piece of the loop is a short straight current.

  1. 1

    Pick the part of the loop nearest you → going clockwise (seen from above), the current there flows from right to left.

  2. 2

    Thumb → point your right thumb along it, to the left.

  3. 3

    Fingers → they curl round the wire: on the side away from you (inside the loop) they point down into the table; on the side towards you (outside the loop) they point up out of the table.

  4. 4

    Every part of the loop gives the same result: inside — downwards; outside — upwards.

  5. Check: test your right hand on your textbook's Fig 12.7 first (thumb down along the current → seen from above, the fingers go round clockwise). Then try the far part of the loop, where the clockwise current flows from left to right: thumb pointing right, the fingers again go down on the inside of the loop (towards you) ✔.

  6. Answer Inside the loop the magnetic field is perpendicular to the table, pointing downwards (into the table). Outside the loop it points upwards (out of the table).

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    By the right-hand thumb rule (thumb along the current in a part of the loop, fingers give the field) (1): inside the loop the field is perpendicular to the plane of the table, directed into the table (downwards) (1); outside the loop it is out of the table (upwards) (1)

    Common trap: saying the field is "clockwise" too. The field of a loop passes through the loop, at right angles to the table.

The magnetic field in a given region is uniform. Draw a diagram to represent it.

Given: a uniform field. Asked: a diagram.

Concept used: a uniform field has the same strength and direction everywhere (Topic 1).

  1. 1

    Same direction → draw straight, parallel lines with arrows pointing the same way.

  2. 2

    Same strength → keep the lines equally spaced.

  3. Check: the field inside a long solenoid is uniform and looks exactly like this ✔.

  4. Answer A set of parallel, equally spaced straight lines, with arrows all pointing in the same direction (as in Topic 1, the second panel of the "never cross" diagram).

    Write it in the exam (1 mark):

    Parallel, equally spaced straight lines with arrows in the same direction (1)

    Common trap: lines drawn freehand with uneven gaps — that shows a field that changes in strength.

Intext p.203, Q1A proton in a magnetic field1 mark

Which of the following property of a proton can change while it moves freely in a magnetic field? (There may be more than one correct answer.)
(a) mass (b) speed (c) velocity (d) momentum

Given: a proton moving freely in a magnetic field. Asked: which properties can change.

Concept used: the force of a magnetic field on a moving charge is at right angles to its motion (Topic 4). Why it applies: a sideways push changes the direction of motion only.

  1. 1

    Mass → a property of the particle itself; a field cannot change it.

  2. 2

    Speed → the force is always at right angles to the motion, so it does not speed the proton up or slow it down: the speed stays the same.

  3. 3

    Velocity → velocity includes direction; the sideways force changes the direction, so the velocity changes.

  4. 4

    Momentum → momentum = mass × velocity; the velocity changes, so the momentum changes.

  5. Check: the beams in Topic 4 are deflected (turned), which is a change of direction ✔.

  6. Answer (c) velocity and (d) momentum.

    Write it in the exam (1 mark):

    (c) and (d) — the force is perpendicular to the motion, so only the direction (velocity, momentum) changes, not the speed or mass. (1)

    Common trap: choosing (b) speed. A force at right angles to the motion turns the particle; it does not change how fast it goes.
Intext p.203, Q2Changing the displacement of the rod3 marks

In Activity 12.7, how do we think the displacement of rod AB will be affected if (i) current in rod AB is increased; (ii) a stronger horse-shoe magnet is used; and (iii) length of the rod AB is increased?

Given: the rod-and-magnet set-up of Activity 12.7. Asked: the effect of three changes on the displacement.

Concept used: the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field (Topic 4). Why it applies: a larger force gives a larger displacement.

  1. 1

    (i) Larger current → the force on the rod is larger → the displacement increases.

  2. 2

    (ii) Stronger magnet → a stronger field pushes harder on the same current → the displacement increases.

  3. 3

    (iii) Longer rod (with the extra length also between the poles) → more of the conductor lies in the field, and every part of it is pushed → the total force and the displacement increase.

  4. Check: in every case the direction of the displacement stays the same — only its size changes, because neither the current nor the field is reversed ✔.

  5. Answer In all three cases the displacement of rod AB increases (in the same direction): (i) larger current, (ii) stronger field, (iii) more length of conductor in the field — each gives a larger force.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    (i) Displacement increases — force increases with current. (1) (ii) Increases — a stronger magnetic field gives a larger force. (1) (iii) Increases — a longer conductor in the field experiences a larger force. (1)

    Common trap: saying the direction of displacement changes. Only reversing the current or the field reverses the force.

Intext p.205, Q1Two safety measures2 marks

Name two safety measures commonly used in electric circuits and appliances.

Given: electric circuits and appliances. Asked: two safety measures.

Concept used: the fuse and earthing (Topic 6).

  1. 1

    Electric fuse → in series with the circuit; melts and breaks the circuit when the current becomes too large (short-circuiting or overloading).

  2. 2

    Earthing → the metal body of an appliance is connected to the earth wire, which gives leaked current a low-resistance path to the earth, so the user does not get a severe shock.

  3. Check: these are the two safety devices your textbook describes in §12.4 ✔.

  4. Answer (1) The electric fuse (protects the circuit and appliances from short-circuiting and overloading). (2) Earthing of metal-bodied appliances through the earth wire (protects the user from severe electric shock).

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Electric fuse — melts and breaks the circuit when the current is too high. (1) Earthing — the earth wire gives a low-resistance path for leaked current, preventing severe shock. (1)

    Common trap: naming the "main switch" or the "meter". They are parts of the circuit, not safety measures against faults.

Intext p.205, Q3Avoiding overloading2 marks

What precaution should be taken to avoid the overloading of domestic electric circuits?

Given: a domestic circuit. Asked: precautions against overloading.

Concept used: the causes of overloading (Topic 6). Why it applies: each precaution removes one cause.

  1. 1

    Too many appliances on one socket → do not connect many appliances to a single socket (no multi-plug adapters for heavy loads).

  2. 2

    High-power appliances → use them on the 15 A circuit, not the 5 A circuit.

  3. 3

    Short circuits → keep the insulation of wires in good condition and repair faulty appliances, so live and neutral never touch.

  4. 4

    Protection → use a fuse of the correct rating in every circuit.

  5. Check: these match the three causes of overloading your textbook lists: too many appliances, short-circuiting and a hike in the supply voltage (the fuse guards against the last) ✔.

  6. Answer Do not connect too many appliances to a single socket; run high-power appliances on the 15 A circuit; keep wiring and appliances in good repair to avoid short circuits; and use a fuse of the proper rating in each circuit.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Too many appliances should not be connected to a single socket (1); use a fuse of the correct rating / keep insulation in good condition (1)

    Common trap: "use a fuse of a higher rating". A higher rating lets a dangerous current flow without melting.

Exercise Q63 marks

Imagine that you are sitting in a chamber with your back to one wall. An electron beam, moving horizontally from back wall towards the front wall, is deflected by a strong magnetic field to your right side. What is the direction of magnetic field?

Given: electrons moving horizontally from the back wall to the front wall; force towards your right. Asked: direction of the magnetic field.

Concept used: current opposite to electron motion; Fleming's left-hand rule (Topic 4). Why it applies: a beam of electrons is a current in a magnetic field.

  1. 1

    Current → the electrons move forwards (back wall → front wall), so the current is backwards (front wall → back wall).

  2. 2

    Middle finger (current) → point it backwards, towards the wall behind you.

  3. 3

    Thumb (force) → turn your left hand until the thumb points to your right.

  4. 4

    Forefinger (field) → it now points vertically downwards.

  5. Check: test your left hand on your textbook's Fig 12.13 first (forefinger up for the field, middle finger towards you for the current → the thumb must point to the left). With the forefinger upwards instead, the thumb would point to your left — the opposite of what is seen; and your textbook's answer key gives "vertically downwards" ✔.

  6. Answer The magnetic field is vertically downwards.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    Current is opposite to the motion of electrons, i.e. from the front wall towards the back wall. (1) By Fleming's left-hand rule (middle finger along the current, thumb along the force to the right) (1), the forefinger points vertically downwards: the field is vertically downwards. (1)

    Common trap: putting the middle finger along the electron beam (forwards). That gives "vertically upwards" — the wrong answer.
Exercise Q73 marks

State the rule to determine the direction of a (i) magnetic field produced around a straight conductor-carrying current, (ii) force experienced by a current-carrying straight conductor placed in a magnetic field which is perpendicular to it, and (iii) current induced in a coil due to its rotation in a magnetic field.

Given: three situations. Asked: the rule for each.

Concept used: right-hand thumb rule (Topic 2); Fleming's left-hand rule (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    (i) Field round a straight current → the right-hand thumb rule.

  2. 2

    (ii) Force on a current in a perpendicular field → Fleming's left-hand rule.

  3. 3

    (iii) Induced current in a rotating coil → Fleming's right-hand rule. (Not for the board exam: induced currents — electromagnetic induction and the generator — are in the 2026-27 syllabus for school assessment only, and are not taught in this chapter.)

  4. Check: your textbook's answer key gives (i) right-hand thumb rule and (ii) Fleming's left-hand rule ✔.

  5. Answer

    • (i) Right-hand thumb rule: hold the straight conductor in your right hand with the thumb pointing in the direction of the current; the fingers wrap round the conductor in the direction of the magnetic field lines.
    • (ii) Fleming's left-hand rule: stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand so that they are mutually perpendicular; if the forefinger points along the magnetic field and the middle finger along the current, the thumb points in the direction of the force (motion) on the conductor.
    • (iii) Fleming's right-hand rule (not for the board exam).

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    (i) Right-hand thumb rule — with the statement. (1) (ii) Fleming's left-hand rule — with the statement. (1) (iii) Fleming's right-hand rule. (1)

    Common trap: swapping (i) and (ii). Field round a current → right hand; force on a current → left hand.

Exercise Q93 marks

What is the function of an earth wire? Why is it necessary to earth metallic appliances?

Given: the earth wire; metallic appliances. Asked: the function of the earth wire; why earthing is needed.

Concept used: earthing (Topic 6).

  1. 1

    Earth wire → the green wire, connected to a metal plate deep in the earth near the house.

  2. 2

    Function → it provides a low-resistance conducting path for any current that leaks to the metal body of an appliance.

  3. 3

    Why for metal bodies → if the live wire touches the metal body, the earth wire carries the leaked current to the earth and keeps the body at the potential of the earth.

  4. 4

    Result → a person touching the appliance does not get a severe electric shock.

  5. Check: appliances such as the electric press, toaster, table fan and refrigerator — all with metal bodies — are your textbook's examples ✔.

  6. Answer The earth wire is a safety wire connected to a metal plate deep in the earth. It gives a low-resistance path to the earth for any current leaking to the metal body of an appliance. Metallic appliances are earthed so that, if current leaks to the body, the body stays at earth potential and the user does not get a severe electric shock.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    The earth wire provides a low-resistance conducting path to the earth (it is connected to a metal plate deep in the earth). (1) If current leaks to the metallic body, it flows to the earth and the body stays at the potential of the earth (1), so the user does not get a severe electric shock. (1)

    Common trap: "the earth wire completes the circuit for the appliance". The appliance works through live and neutral; the earth wire is for safety.
