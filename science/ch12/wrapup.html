Wrap-up

Which rule do I use?

About 25 minutes · 6 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • State the properties of field lines.
  • Use the right-hand thumb rule.
  • Find the N and S ends of a solenoid.
  • Use Fleming's left-hand rule, including for beams of charged particles.
  • Find how often the household AC changes direction.
  • Decide whether a circuit or fuse rating is exceeded.

Most questions in this chapter ask for a direction — of a field, of a force, or of a pole — or for a reason about safety at home. Two hand rules answer every direction question, one picture of field lines covers every pattern, and three wires and a fuse cover the house. This page puts the chapter on three cards and then works through four mixed questions.

Card 1 — Which hand rule?

You want… Rule (hand) Set → read
the field round a straight current right-hand thumb rule (right) thumb along the current → curled fingers = field
the N end of a loop or solenoid thumb rule on one turn (right) thumb along the current in a turn → the field inside points to the N end
the force on a current (or a beam) in a field Fleming's left-hand rule (left) forefinger = field, middle finger = current → thumb = force
the field, from a known force and current Fleming's left-hand rule (left) middle finger = current, thumb = force → forefinger = field

Card 2 — Field patterns

Source Shape of the field lines Strength Changes direction when…
Bar magnet out of N, round to S; S to N inside; closed curves strongest near the poles the magnet is turned round
Straight wire concentric circles round the wire ↑ with current; ↓ with distance the current reverses
Circular loop circles near the wire; nearly straight lines at the centre nnn turns → nnn times the field the current reverses
Solenoid like a bar magnet outside; parallel straight lines inside uniform inside; ↑ with current, turns, soft iron core the current reverses (poles swap)

Card 3 — Safety at home

Fuse Earthing
Protects against short-circuiting and overloading electric shock from a leaking metal body
How it is connected in series, in the live wire metal body → earth wire (green) → metal plate deep in the earth
What it does melts (Joule heating) and breaks the circuit gives a low-resistance path; keeps the body at earth potential
Do not replace it with a higher rating or a thick wire leave a metal-bodied appliance without it

Four mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

Two wires side by side

Question. Two long straight wires lie side by side on this page, parallel to each other and a few centimetres apart. Both carry a current up the page. (a) What is the direction of the magnetic field produced by the left wire at the position of the right wire? (b) Use your answer to find the direction of the force on the right wire.

Given: two parallel wires; both currents up the page. Asked: the field of the left wire at the right wire; the force on the right wire.

Concept used: right-hand thumb rule for (a); Fleming's left-hand rule for (b) (Card 1). Why it applies: first the field round a current, then the force on a current in that field.

  1. 1

    (a) Thumb (right hand) up the page along the left wire's current.

  2. 2

    To its right → the curled fingers go into the page. So at the right wire the field is into the page (⊗\otimes⊗).

  3. 3

    (b) Left hand: forefinger into the page (field), middle finger up the page (current in the right wire).

  4. 4

    Thumb → points to the left, towards the left wire.

  5. Check: test both hands on your textbook first — Fig 12.7 (right hand: thumb down → clockwise seen from above) and Fig 12.13 (left hand: field up, current towards you → thumb left). Then: if the right wire's current were reversed (down the page), the thumb would point right — away from the left wire. Reversing one current reverses the push ✔.

  6. Answer (a) Into the page. (b) The force on the right wire is towards the left — towards the other wire.

    Common trap: using one rule for both parts. Part (a) is a field round a current (right hand); part (b) is a force on a current (left hand).

Mixed example 2

A compass at the end of a solenoid

Question. A solenoid lies along the page. Seen from its right end, the current goes round the turns anticlockwise. A compass is placed on the axis just beyond the right end. (a) Which end of the solenoid is N? (b) Which way does the compass needle's north pole point? (c) The current is reversed. Which way does it point now?

Given: current anticlockwise seen from the right end; compass beyond the right end. Asked: the N end; the needle direction; after reversing.

Concept used: field inside from the right-hand thumb rule; lines come out of the N end; a compass points along the field (Cards 1 and 2).

  1. 1

    (a) Field inside → anticlockwise as seen from the right, so the field inside points towards you, i.e. to the right. The lines come out at the right end → the right end is N.

  2. 2

    (b) Beyond the N end → the field points away from the solenoid, to the right; the needle's north pole points right (its S end faces the solenoid).

  3. 3

    (c) Reversed → the right end becomes S; the field there now points into the solenoid, to the left; the needle's north pole turns to point left, towards the solenoid.

  4. Check: the solenoid behaves like a bar magnet; near a bar magnet's N end a compass's S end faces the magnet, and near its S end the compass's N end does ✔.

  5. Answer (a) The right end. (b) To the right, away from the solenoid. (c) To the left, towards the solenoid.

    Common trap: (c) "the compass stays the same". Reversing the current swaps the solenoid's poles.

Mixed example 3

An electron beam

Question. In a laboratory, a beam of electrons travels horizontally towards the east. A magnetic field acts on it vertically downwards. In which direction is the beam deflected?

Given: electrons moving east; field downwards. Asked: direction of the force.

Concept used: current opposite to electron motion; Fleming's left-hand rule (Card 1).

  1. 1

    Current → the electrons move east, so the current is towards the west.

  2. 2

    Left hand: forefinger down (field), middle finger west (current).

  3. 3

    Thumb → points south.

  4. Check: a beam of positive charges moving east would be pushed north — the opposite way, as it should be ✔.

  5. Answer The beam is deflected towards the south.

    Common trap: setting the middle finger towards the east (along the electrons). That gives north — wrong.

Mixed example 4

A busy 15 A circuit

Question. A 220 V, 15 A circuit in a kitchen has a 1.76 kW oven and a 1.21 kW iron switched on. (a) Find the current drawn by each and the total. (b) A 0.55 kW mixer is then switched on. Is the circuit overloaded? (c) The supply is AC at 50 Hz. How many times does the current change direction in the 2 minutes the mixer runs?

Given: V=220V = 220V=220 V; oven 1760 W; iron 1210 W; mixer 550 W; rating 15 A; f=50f = 50f=50 Hz; 2 min. Asked: currents; overload?; reversals.
