The whole chapter on one page

Variation and heredity — heredity passes on a common body design plus small changes, so children resemble their parents but are not identical. Variations are inherited, and new ones are added in every generation, so they accumulate (Fig 8.1). Asexual reproduction gives very similar offspring (only small DNA-copying errors — a sugarcane field); sexual reproduction gives more diversity. Variants do not all survive equally — in a heat wave, heat-tolerant bacteria survive better — so variation helps the species survive. In an asexual population, a trait found in more individuals has probably arisen earlier. Free and attached earlobes are two variants of an inherited human trait.

Mendel's one-trait cross — Mendel was the first to count offspring in each generation. Pure tall (TT) × pure short (tt) → F1 all tall (Tt): no medium-height plants — traits do not blend. F1 self-pollinated → F2 3 tall : 1 short; genetic make-up 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt. Each plant has two copies of each gene (one from each parent); each gamete has one. Dominant trait: shows with one copy (T). Recessive trait: shows only with two copies (tt). Violet × white flowers → F1 violet, F2 3 : 1 → violet dominant. To confirm 1 : 2 : 1, self-pollinate each tall F2 plant: TT gives only tall, Tt gives some short. Working back: tall × short → all tall means the tall parent is TT; about half short means it is Tt. Tall × tall → about 1 short in 4 means both are Tt.

Two traits — tall round × short wrinkled → F1 all tall round; F2 includes new combinations (tall wrinkled, short round). Fig 8.5: RRyy (round, green) × rrYY (wrinkled, yellow) → F1 RrYy (round, yellow) → four gametes RY, Ry, rY, ry → 16 boxes → 9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green. All four combinations appear, 9 : 3 : 3 : 1, and seed shape does not affect seed colour → traits are inherited independently.

How traits are expressed — a gene is a section of DNA that gives the information for one protein. Height: gene → enzyme → amount of growth hormone → height; an altered gene → less efficient enzyme → less hormone → short. Both parents contribute equally: two gene sets in each body cell, one in each germ cell. Genes are on separate chromosomes; each germ cell takes one chromosome of each pair → independent inheritance; fusion restores the number → stable DNA. Dominance needs the right data (pure-line crosses; a trait that reappears) — not one family where the child is like one parent, not a "likely" link, not how common a trait is.

Sex determination — a few reptiles: the temperature of the eggs decides; snails can change sex; humans: largely genetic. 22 pairs + one pair of sex chromosomes: women XX, men XY (Y short). Every egg carries X; sperms carry X or Y. X from the father → girl; Y from the father → boy. Half the children are expected to be girls and half boys; every child is a 50 : 50 chance.

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Heredity: the passing of traits from parents to offspring, by rules that make inheritance reliable.
  • Variation: the differences between individuals of a species; variations are inherited and accumulate.
  • Trait: a characteristic, such as height, seed shape or flower colour.
  • Gene: a section of DNA that provides the information for one protein.
  • F1 generation: the offspring of a cross between two parents. F2 generation: the offspring of the F1 (here, by self-pollination).
  • Dominant trait: the trait expressed even when only one copy of its gene is present.
  • Recessive trait: the trait expressed only when both copies of its gene are of that kind.
  • Pure-breeding (pure) plant: a plant with two identical copies (TT or tt) that gives only its own kind on self-pollination.
  • Independent inheritance: traits are passed on separately, so new combinations of traits appear in the offspring.
  • Chromosome: an independent piece of DNA carrying genes; body cells have two copies of each.
  • Sex chromosomes: the pair that differs between the sexes — XX in women, XY in men.
