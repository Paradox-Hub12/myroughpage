Sex determination

About 30 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Describe how sex is decided in other species — temperature in some reptiles; snails that can change sex.
  • Explain the XX and XY sex chromosomes in humans and show the cross giving half boys and half girls (Fig 8.6).
  • Explain that the sex of a child depends on the chromosome it inherits from the father.

A family with three daughters is sometimes told, "Your next child will surely be a boy." Is that true? And in some reptiles, the sex of the young is not decided by genes at all, but by how warm the eggs were kept. So what decides whether a newborn is a boy or a girl?

Quick reminder (Topic 4)

Each body cell has pairs of chromosomes — one of each pair from the mother and one from the father. Each gamete gets one chromosome from each pair.

  1. Different species, different strategies

  • The surroundings decide (environmental cues): in a few reptiles, the temperature of the fertilised eggs — how warm they are kept — decides whether each young one will be male or female.
  • Sex can change: in some animals, such as snails, an individual can change sex — so sex there is not genetically determined.
  • Genes decide: in human beings, sex is largely genetically determined — the genes a child receives from its parents decide its sex.

  1. The sex chromosomes

If both parents passed on similar gene sets, how could genes decide sex? The answer is that not all human chromosomes are perfectly paired.

  • 22 pairs of human chromosomes each have a maternal copy and a paternal copy that match.
  • One more pair, the sex chromosomes, is not always a perfect pair:
  • women have a perfect pair, both called X → XX;
  • men have a mismatched pair — one normal-sized X and one short chromosome called Y → XY.

  1. The cross — half boys, half girls (Fig 8.6)

Use the cross routine with the sex chromosomes.

  1. Parents: mother XX × father XY.
  2. Gametes: every egg carries X. The sperms are of two kinds in equal numbers — X or Y.
  3. Checkerboard:
Gametes X (sperm) Y (sperm)
X (egg) XX (girl) XY (boy)
  1. Count: one girl box, one boy box → girls : boys = 1 : 1 — half the children will be girls and half boys.

  1. The father's chromosome decides

  • All children get an X from their mother, whether they are boys or girls.
  • So the sex of a child depends on what it inherits from its father:
  • an X from the father → XX → a girl;
  • a Y from the father → XY → a boy.

The mother never gives a Y — she has none.

  1. Every child is a fresh chance

Each time a child is conceived, the egg meets either an X-carrying sperm or a Y-carrying sperm, with equal chances. Earlier children make no difference. So for every child the chance of a girl is 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ (50%) and the chance of a boy is 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ (50%) — even after three daughters.

Worked example 1

After three daughters

Question. A couple has three daughters. What is the chance that their fourth child will be a girl? Explain using sex chromosomes.

Given: three daughters already. Asked: the chance that the next child is a girl; the reason.

Concept used: sex determination in humans (this topic). Why it applies: each child's sex depends on which sperm fertilises the egg.

  1. 1

    Mother (XX) → every egg carries X.

  2. 2

    Father (XY) → sperms carry X or Y, in equal numbers.

  3. 3

    For each child → the egg is fertilised by an X-sperm (→ XX, girl) or a Y-sperm (→ XY, boy), with equal chances.

  4. 4

    Earlier children do not change this.

  5. 5

    Chance of a girl =12= \dfrac{1}{2}=21​ = 50%.

  6. Check: the checkerboard has one girl box and one boy box, every time ✔.

  7. Answer The chance is 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ (50%). Each child gets an X from the mother and, by chance, an X or a Y from the father; earlier children make no difference.

    Common trap: "After three girls, a boy is due." Each child is a new 50 : 50 chance.

Worked example 2

Who gave the Y?

Question. A baby boy has the sex chromosomes XY. From which parent did he get each of them? Could he have got his Y from his mother?

Given: a boy, XY. Asked: the source of X and of Y; whether Y could come from the mother.

Concept used: women XX, men XY (this topic). Why it applies: a child gets one sex chromosome from each parent.

  1. 1

    Mother is XX → she can only give an X.

  2. 2

    So the boy's X came from his mother.

  3. 3

    His Y must have come from his father (XY), the only parent with a Y.

  4. 4

    Could the mother give Y? No — she has no Y chromosome.

  5. Check: XX × XY → the only way to get XY is X (egg) + Y (sperm) ✔.

  6. Answer X from the mother, Y from the father. The mother cannot give a Y.

    Common trap: "The boy got his Y from his mother." Women have no Y chromosome.

Worked example 3

Reptile eggs and snails

Question. A farm keeps the eggs of one kind of reptile in two incubators at different temperatures. Almost all the young from one incubator are male, and almost all from the other are female. How is sex being decided here, and how is this different from humans and from snails?

Given: same kind of eggs; different temperatures; different sexes. Asked: how sex is decided; the difference from humans and snails.

Concept used: strategies of sex determination (this topic). Why it applies: the only difference between the two sets of eggs is the temperature.

  1. 1

    Reptile → the temperature at which the fertilised eggs are kept decides the sex — an environmental cue.

  2. 2

    Humans → sex is largely genetic — decided by the X or Y from the father.

  3. 3

    Snails → an individual can change sex, so sex is not fixed by genes.

  4. Check: these three cases match the three strategies in section 1 ✔.

  5. Answer In this reptile, sex is decided by the temperature of the eggs (the environment). In humans it is decided by genes (the father's X or Y). Snails can change sex, so it is not genetically fixed.

    Common trap: "Every animal's sex is decided by X and Y." Different species use different strategies.

Common mistakes!

  • "The mother decides whether the baby is a boy or a girl."

    Every egg carries the same X.

    The father's sperm (X or Y) decides.

  • "The mother gives the Y to a son."

    Women are XX — they have no Y.

    Son: X from mother, Y from father.

  • "Women are XY and men are XX."

    It is the other way round.

    Women XX (perfect pair); men XY (mismatched).

  • "After three girls, a boy is more likely."

    Each child is a fresh chance.

    Chance of a girl or a boy is 50% every time.

  • "Sex is decided by genes in every animal."

    Some reptiles use temperature; snails can change sex.

    Different species, different strategies.

Key points

  • Strategies differ: in a few reptiles, the temperature of the eggs decides sex; snails can change sex; in humans, sex is largely genetic.
  • Humans: 22 pairs of matching chromosomes + one pair of sex chromosomes.
  • Women XX (perfect pair); men XY (Y is short).
  • Every egg carries X; sperms carry X or Y in equal numbers.
  • X from the father → girl (XX); Y from the father → boy (XY) — the father's chromosome decides.
  • Half the children are expected to be boys and half girls; every child is a 50 : 50 chance.
