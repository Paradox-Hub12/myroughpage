CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q154 marks

Question: In order to study patterns of inheritance, Neha had performed typical Mendelian experiments with pea plants in the field. In experiment one, she crossed a pea plant having white flowers (vv) with another pea plant bearing violet flowers (VV). In another experiment, in addition to the colour of the flower she also focussed on the height of the pea plant. This time she crossed a pea plant having violet flowers and tall height (VVTT) with another pea plant having white flowers and short height (vvtt).
What percentage is expected for the following traits as a result of these experiments? Give a reason.
A. Violet flowers in F1 and F2 generation in experiment one? (1)
B. Tall height plants with white flowers in F2 generation in experiment two? (1)
Attempt either sub-part C or D.
C. Percentage of medium height plants with light violet flowers in F1 and F2 generations in experiment two? Give a reason.
OR
D. Percentage of short height plants with white flowers in F1 and F2 generations in experiment two? (2)

Given: experiment one — vv × VV; experiment two — VVTT × vvtt. Violet and tall are dominant. Asked: percentages in the F1 and F2, with reasons.

Concept used: the one-trait and two-trait cross routines; no blending (Topics 2 and 3). Why it applies: both experiments start with pure parents and go to the F2.

  1. 1

    A — experiment one, F1 → VV × vv → gametes V and v → all Vv → all violet → 100% violet.

  2. 2

    A — experiment one, F2 → Vv × Vv:

    Gametes V v
    V VV (violet) Vv (violet)
    v Vv (violet) vv (white)

    → violet = 3 boxes of 4 → 34×100%=75%\dfrac{3}{4} \times 100\% = 75\%43​×100%=75% (VV 14\dfrac{1}{4}41​ + Vv 24\dfrac{2}{4}42​). Violet is dominant.

  3. 3

    Experiment two, F1 → VVTT gives VT; vvtt gives vt → all VvTt → violet and tall.

  4. 4

    F1 gametes → VT, Vt, vT, vt → a 16-box checkerboard in the F2 → violet tall : violet short : white tall : white short = 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.

  5. 5

    B — tall white in F2 → needs vv and at least one T → vvTT, vvTt, vvTt → 3 boxes of 16 → 316×100%=18.75%\dfrac{3}{16} \times 100\% = 18.75\%163​×100%=18.75% — one dominant trait with one recessive trait.

  6. 6

    C — medium height, light violet → traits do not blend: one copy of T or V gives the same result as two copies → 0% in both the F1 and the F2.

  7. 7

    D — short white → in the F1 every plant is VvTt, showing both dominant traits → 0%. In the F2, short white needs vvtt → 1 box of 16 → 116×100%=6.25%\dfrac{1}{16} \times 100\% = 6.25\%161​×100%=6.25% — both traits recessive.

  8. Check: the four F2 groups of experiment two: 56.25% + 18.75% + 18.75% + 6.25% = 100% ✔.

    Common trap: in D, giving 6.25% for the F1 too. Two pure parents give an F1 in which every plant shows both dominant traits.
A. In experiment one, in F1 all plants (100%) will have violet (Vv) flowers and in F2, 75% (34\dfrac{3}{4}43​) plants will have violet flowers (14\dfrac{1}{4}41​ VV and 24\dfrac{2}{4}42​ Vv) because violet colour of the flower is a dominant trait in pea plant. (1)
B. 316\dfrac{3}{16}163​ or 18.75% pea plants will have tall height and white flowers (vvTT/vvTt) — a combination of a dominant and a recessive trait. (1)
C. In pea plants, no half way characteristics are produced because even a single copy of T or V will produce the same result as two copies of T or V. Thus, no plants with medium height and light violet flowers will be produced in F1 or F2 generation (zero percent). (2) (split inferred: reason 1 · 0% in F1 and F2 1)
OR
D. In F1 generation all plants will have only dominant traits so no plant with short height and white flowers (recessive traits) will be produced (zero percent). In F2 generation 116\dfrac{1}{16}161​ or 6.25% plants will have short height with white flowers (vvtt) as both these traits are recessive. (2) (split inferred: F1 0% with reason 1 · F2 6.25% with reason 1)

CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section A, Q51 mark

Question: In a cross between black furred rabbit (B) and white furred rabbit (b), all offspring were found to have black fur. What can be inferred about the genetic makeup of the parent rabbits?
A. BB X bb
B. Bb X Bb
C. Bb X bb
D. bb X bb

Given: black × white; all offspring black. Asked: the parents' make-up.

Concept used: working backwards (Topic 2). Why it applies: the offspring tell us what gametes the parents made.

  1. 1

    White parent → white is the recessive form here → bb; it gives only b.

  2. 2

    All offspring black → every gamete from the black parent carried B → black parent BB.

  3. 3

    So the cross is BB × bb → A.

  4. 4

    B ✘ — Bb × Bb gives black : white = 3 : 1, and both parents would be black. C ✘ — Bb × bb gives about half white. D ✘ — bb × bb gives only white, and neither parent is black.

  5. Check: BB × bb → all Bb → all black ✔.

    Common trap: choosing C because "a black rabbit is Bb". With Bb × bb, half the offspring would be white.
A. BB x bb
1 mark
CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section A, Q81 mark

Choose: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. B. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A. C. A is true but R is false. D. A is false but R is true.
Question: Assertion (A): Tallness of a pea plant is controlled by an enzyme.
Reason (R): The gene for that enzyme makes proteins which help the plant to be tall.

Given: an assertion about an enzyme and plant height; a reason about the gene. Asked: the correct option.

Concept used: gene → enzyme → hormone → height (Topic 4). Why it applies: this is your textbook's height example.

  1. Strictly, a gene does not make the protein; it gives the information from which the cell makes it. The intended answer is still the one in the marking scheme.

  2. 1

    A → true: plant height depends on the amount of a growth hormone, and the amount depends on how efficiently an enzyme works.

  3. 2

    R → true: the gene gives the information for the enzyme, which is a protein; an efficient enzyme leads to a tall plant.

  4. 3

    R explains A → the enzyme controls height because the gene makes it → A.

  5. Check: the chain gene → enzyme → hormone → height links R to A ✔.

    Common trap: choosing B because "the hormone, not the enzyme, makes the plant tall". The enzyme decides how much hormone is made, so it does control height.
A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
1 mark
CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section A, Q143 marks

Question: In a genetic experiment, plants with pure round green seeds (RRyy) were crossed with plants with wrinkled yellow seeds (rrYY).
(i) Show the gametes formed when F1 was self-pollinated.
(ii) A total of 144 seeds were produced which developed into saplings. Show the ratio in which these traits are independently inherited in these144 sapling.

Given: RRyy × rrYY; F1 self-pollinated; 144 F2 saplings. Asked: (i) F1 gametes; (ii) the F2 ratio and numbers.

Concept used: the two-trait cross (Topic 3). Why it applies: this is the cross of your textbook's Fig 8.5.

  1. 1

    Parents' gametes → RRyy gives Ry; rrYY gives rY → F1 RrYy (round, yellow).

  2. 2

    (i) F1 gametes → RY, Ry, rY, ry.

  3. 3

    (ii) F2 → 16-box checkerboard → 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.

  4. 4

    Numbers from 144 → round yellow 916×144=81\dfrac{9}{16} \times 144 = 81169​×144=81; round green 316×144=27\dfrac{3}{16} \times 144 = 27163​×144=27; wrinkled yellow 27; wrinkled green 116×144=9\dfrac{1}{16} \times 144 = 9161​×144=9.

  5. Check: 81 + 27 + 27 + 9 = 144 ✔.

    Common trap: naming the classes by height. This cross is about seed shape and seed colour — see the note below.
(i) RY, Ry, rY, ry (1)
(ii) Round yellow : 81, Round green : 27, Wrinkled yellow : 27, Wrinkled green : 9 (1) — ratio 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 (1) — 3 marks (split inferred; the scheme gives only the total)

(Note: the official marking scheme names the four classes "Tall round: 81, Tall wrinkled: 27, Short round: 27, Short wrinkled: 9". The question is about seed shape and colour, not height, so the classes above use the correct names; the numbers and the ratio are the same as the scheme's.)
