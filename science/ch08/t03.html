Topic 3 of 5

Two traits at once — independent inheritance

About 45 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Describe the cross of round green and wrinkled yellow seeds — F1 all round yellow, F2 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 (Fig 8.5).
  • Write the four kinds of gametes of the F1 and fill the 16-box checkerboard.
  • Explain how the F2 shows that traits are inherited independently and that new combinations appear.

A pea plant has round seeds and another has wrinkled ones; one has yellow seeds and another green. If round seeds came with green colour in one parent, must they always stay together in the grandchildren? Mendel let the plants answer — and found seeds that neither grandparent had.

Quick reminder (Topic 2)

Each plant has two copies of each gene; each gamete has one copy. Follow the cross routine: parents → gametes → checkerboard → count → ratio → answer.

  1. Two traits in one cross

What happens when the parents differ in two traits instead of one? Your textbook's first example crosses a plant that is tall and has round seeds with one that is short and has wrinkled seeds.

  • F1: every plant is tall with round seeds → tall and round are the dominant traits.
  • F2 (F1 self-pollinated): some tall-round and some short-wrinkled, as in the grandparents — and some new combinations: tall with wrinkled seeds and short with round seeds.

So the two traits did not stay together as they were in the parents.

  1. The seed cross step by step (Fig 8.5)

Your textbook's Fig 8.5 follows two seed traits: shape (R = round, r = wrinkled) and colour (Y = yellow, y = green).

  1. Parents: RRyy (round, green) × rrYY (wrinkled, yellow).
  2. Gametes: RRyy gives only Ry; rrYY gives only rY. Each gamete has one letter for shape and one for colour.
  3. F1: Ry + rY → RrYy — all round and yellow. So round and yellow are the dominant traits; wrinkled and green are recessive.
  4. F1 × F1: RrYy × RrYy (the F1 self-pollinates).

  1. The F1 makes four kinds of gametes

A gamete needs one copy of the shape gene (R or r) and one copy of the colour gene (Y or y). Pair each shape copy with each colour copy:

Y y
R RY Ry
r rY ry

So an RrYy plant makes four kinds of gametes in equal numbers: RY, Ry, rY, ry. Never write a gamete with two letters for the same gene (such as "Rr") or with a gene missing (such as "R").

  1. The 16-box checkerboard

Gametes RY Ry rY ry

rY RrYY RrYy rrYY rrYy
ry RrYy Rryy rrYy rryy

How to sort a box: it is round if it has at least one R, and yellow if it has at least one Y.

So the F2 ratio is 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 (16 parts).

Real counts are close, not exact. Your textbook's figure records 556 seeds: 315 round yellow, 108 round green, 101 wrinkled yellow and 32 wrinkled green. An exact 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 split of 556 would be about 313 : 104 : 104 : 35 — very close to what was counted.

  1. Why this shows independent inheritance

Look at one trait at a time in the 16 boxes:

  • Shape: round 12 boxes, wrinkled 4 → round : wrinkled = 3 : 1.
  • Colour: yellow 12 boxes, green 4 → yellow : green = 3 : 1.

Each trait still gives its own 3 : 1. More important, the two results combine freely: among the 12 round boxes, yellow : green = 9 : 3 = 3 : 1, and among the 4 wrinkled boxes, yellow : green = 3 : 1 again. Knowing a seed's shape tells you nothing about its colour. This happens because the shape copy that enters a gamete does not decide which colour copy goes with it, so all four kinds of gametes form in equal numbers. The traits are inherited independently.

That is why new combinations appear. The parents were round-green and wrinkled-yellow; the F2 also has round-yellow and wrinkled-green seeds. If R and y had to stay linked together, the F1 could make only Ry and rY gametes. Its F2 would then be only round green, round yellow and wrinkled yellow, in the ratio 1 : 2 : 1. A wrinkled green seed (rryy) could never appear, and there would be no 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.

  1. From 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 to numbers

Multiply each fraction by the total. For 320 F2 plants:

  • 916×320=180\dfrac{9}{16} \times 320 = 180169​×320=180, 316×320=60\dfrac{3}{16} \times 320 = 60163​×320=60, 316×320=60\dfrac{3}{16} \times 320 = 60163​×320=60, 116×320=20\dfrac{1}{16} \times 320 = 20161​×320=20.
  • Check: 180 + 60 + 60 + 20 = 320 ✔.

Worked examples

Worked example 1

Green axial × yellow terminal

Question. A pure plant with green pods and axial flowers (GGAA) is crossed with a pure plant with yellow pods and terminal flowers (ggaa). (a) Write the make-up of the F1 and how it looks. (b) Write the gametes of the F1. (c) If 640 F2 plants are grown, how many are expected to have yellow pods and axial flowers?

Given: GGAA × ggaa; green pods and axial flowers dominant; 640 F2 plants. Asked: F1; F1 gametes; the number of yellow-pod, axial-flower plants.

Concept used: the two-trait cross routine (this topic). Why it applies: two traits are followed together.

  1. 1

    Gametes of the parents → GGAA gives GA; ggaa gives ga.

  2. 2

    (a) F1 → GA + ga = GgAa → green pods and axial flowers.

  3. 3

    (b) F1 gametes → pair each pod-colour copy with each flower-position copy → GA, Ga, gA, ga.

  4. 4

    F2 → GgAa × GgAa gives a 16-box checkerboard with green axial : green terminal : yellow axial : yellow terminal = 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.

  5. 5

    (c) Yellow pods with axial flowers is one of the "3" groups → 316\dfrac{3}{16}163​ of the F2 → 316×640=120\dfrac{3}{16} \times 640 = 120163​×640=120 plants.

  6. Check: yellow pods with axial flowers must have gg (yellow) and at least one A (axial): in the checkerboard these are ggAA, ggAa, ggAa — 3 boxes of 16 ✔.

  7. Answer (a) GgAa, green pods and axial flowers (b) GA, Ga, gA, ga (c) 120 plants.

    Common trap: using 116\dfrac{1}{16}161​ for yellow axial. 116\dfrac{1}{16}161​ is only for the plant with both recessive traits (yellow pods, terminal flowers).

Worked example 2

How many kinds of gametes?

Question. Write all the kinds of gametes made by pea plants with these make-ups: (a) TtRr (b) TTRr (c) ttrr. (T = tall, R = round seeds.)

Given: three make-ups for two genes. Asked: the kinds of gametes of each.

Concept used: a gamete takes one copy of each gene (Topics 2 and 3). Why it applies: every gamete must have one height letter and one seed-shape letter.

  1. 1

    (a) TtRr → height copy T or t; shape copy R or r → pair each with each → TR, Tr, tR, tr (four kinds).

  2. 2

    (b) TTRr → height copy is always T; shape copy R or r → TR, Tr (two kinds).

  3. 3

    (c) ttrr → always t and always r → tr (one kind).

  4. Check: each gamete has exactly one T-or-t and one R-or-r ✔.

  5. Answer (a) TR, Tr, tR, tr (b) TR, Tr (c) tr

    Common trap: writing "TT" or "Rr" as a gamete. A gamete never carries two copies of the same gene.

Worked example 3

Which combinations are "new"?

Question. Cross A: round yellow (RRYY) × wrinkled green (rryy). Cross B: round green (RRyy) × wrinkled yellow (rrYY). Both F1s are self-pollinated. Which F2 types are new combinations in each cross?

Given: two crosses with different parents. Asked: the new combinations in each F2.

Concept used: independent inheritance and new combinations (this topic). Why it applies: "new" means not seen in the parents of that cross.

  1. 1

    Both F1s are RrYy, so both F2s have round yellow, round green, wrinkled yellow and wrinkled green in the ratio 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.

  2. 2

    Cross A parents: round yellow and wrinkled green → new = round green and wrinkled yellow.

  3. 3

    Cross B parents: round green and wrinkled yellow → new = round yellow and wrinkled green.

  4. Check: in each cross the "new" types are exactly the two that neither parent showed ✔.

  5. Answer Cross A: round green and wrinkled yellow. Cross B: round yellow and wrinkled green.

    Common trap: learning "round green and wrinkled yellow are always the new types". It depends on which parents were crossed.

In the F2 of a round green × wrinkled yellow cross, how do the results show that seed shape and seed colour are inherited independently?

Answer: The F2 shows all four combinations in the ratio 9 : 3 : 3 : 1, including new combinations (round yellow, wrinkled green) not seen in the parents. Among round seeds and among wrinkled seeds, yellow : green is the same 3 : 1, so seed shape does not affect seed colour; the two copies go into gametes independently.

Common mistakes!

  • "RrYy makes only RY and ry gametes."

    Shape and colour copies sort independently.

    Four kinds: RY, Ry, rY, ry.

  • Writing a gamete as "Rr" or "RrY".

    A gamete has one copy of each gene.

    One shape letter + one colour letter.

  • "Wrinkled green is 25% of the F2."

    It is 1 box out of 16.

    116\dfrac{1}{16}161​ = 6.25%; 316\dfrac{3}{16}163​ = 18.75%; 916\dfrac{9}{16}169​ = 56.25%.

  • "The F1 shows 9 : 3 : 3 : 1."

    The F1 is all RrYy — all round yellow.

    9 : 3 : 3 : 1 is the F2.

  • "Round always goes with yellow (traits stay linked)."

    The F2 shows round green and wrinkled yellow too.

    Traits are inherited independently → new combinations.

  • "The new combinations are always round green and wrinkled yellow."

    "New" depends on the parents of that cross.

    New = the types neither parent showed.

Key points

  • Two traits: tall round × short wrinkled → F1 all tall round; F2 includes new combinations (tall wrinkled, short round).
  • Fig 8.5: RRyy (round, green) × rrYY (wrinkled, yellow) → F1 RrYy (round, yellow).
  • The F1 makes four kinds of gametes: RY, Ry, rY, ry.
  • 16-box checkerboard → 9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green.
  • 916\dfrac{9}{16}169​ = 56.25%, 316\dfrac{3}{16}163​ = 18.75%, 116\dfrac{1}{16}161​ = 6.25%.
  • Each trait alone gives 3 : 1, and the colour ratio is the same among round and wrinkled seeds → 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 with new combinations → the traits are inherited independently.
