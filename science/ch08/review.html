W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

A violet-flowered plant crossed with a white-flowered plant (vv) gives about half violet and half white offspring. What is the make-up of the violet parent? Why?

Answer: Vv — it gives v to half its gametes, so about half the offspring are vv (white). A VV parent would give all violet. (Topic 2)

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

A father with straight hair and a mother with curly hair have one child with straight hair. (Suppose hair type is controlled by one gene with two forms.) Does this show that straight hair is dominant? Why?

Answer: No — if straight were dominant, the father's straight copy would show in the child; if curly were dominant, the mother could carry a hidden straight copy and pass it on. Both fit. Many families or crosses are needed. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

M1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

Explain how chromosomes make both of these possible: (a) both parents contribute equally; (b) traits are inherited independently.

Answer: (a) Body cells have two copies of each chromosome, one from each parent; each germ cell takes one of each pair, and fusion gives the zygote one set from each parent. (b) Each germ cell takes one chromosome from each pair separately, so genes on different chromosomes are sorted independently → new combinations. (Topics 3 and 4)

Did you get it?

M1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

"If tall and short plants are crossed, the F2 will be tall, medium and short in the ratio 1 : 2 : 1." Correct this statement.

Answer: There are no medium plants — traits do not blend. The F2 is 3 tall : 1 short; 1 : 2 : 1 is the ratio of TT : Tt : tt. (Topic 2)

Did you get it?

M1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

"In every animal, sex is decided by the X or Y chromosome from the father." Give one reason why this is not true, and one reason why it is true for humans.

Answer: Not every animal: in a few reptiles the temperature of the eggs decides (or: snails can change sex). For humans: every egg carries X, so the father's X or Y decides. (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

M1.5 · 5 of 5●●● Advanced

A Tt pea plant is tall even though it has one t. Use the gene → enzyme → hormone idea to suggest why one T copy is enough.

Answer: A reasonable suggestion: the T copy gives an efficient enzyme, so enough growth hormone is made for the plant to grow tall; the t copy gives a less efficient enzyme, but one working T copy is enough. Only a tt plant, with no efficient enzyme copy, makes less hormone and is short. (Topics 2 and 4)
