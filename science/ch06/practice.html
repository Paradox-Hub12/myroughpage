P11●● StandardShort answer · 3 marksOriginal

Walking barefoot, Sunil steps on a sharp stone and lifts his foot at once; a moment later he feels the pain. (a) Write the path of the impulse in this reflex, naming five parts in order. (b) Where is the reflex arc made? (c) Why does he feel the pain only a moment after lifting his foot?

Full solution

Given: a reflex involving the foot. Asked: the path; where the arc is; why the pain comes later.

Concept used: the reflex arc and the message to the brain (Topic 2). Why it applies: the reflex and the feeling travel by different routes.

  1. 1

    (a) Receptors (pain receptors in the skin of the sole) → sensory neuron → relay neuron in the spinal cord → motor neuron → effector (muscles of the leg).

  2. 2

    (b) In the spinal cord.

  3. 3

    (c) The reflex uses the short route through the spinal cord, so the foot moves at once. The information also travels up to the brain; the brain is where the pain is felt, so awareness comes slightly later.

  4. Check: the pathway card's side arrow — "message to brain → you feel pain afterwards" ✔.

  5. Answer (a) Receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron (spinal cord) → motor neuron → leg muscle. (b) Spinal cord. (c) The reflex is made in the spinal cord; the message reaching the brain, where pain is felt, takes a little longer.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    (a) Correct sequence of five parts (1) (b) Spinal cord (1) (c) Reflex arc in spinal cord acts first; information reaches the brain afterwards, where pain is felt (1).

    Common trap: Sending the message to the brain first. In a reflex the spinal cord acts first.

P19●● StandardShort answer · 3 marksOriginal

A message must reach every cell of an animal's body, including cells not connected to any nerve, and must keep acting for several hours. Should it be sent as a nerve impulse or as a hormone? Give three reasons.

Full solution

Given: a message for all cells, lasting hours. Asked: nerve or hormone; three reasons.

Concept used: limits of electrical impulses; features of chemical communication (Topics 4 and 5). Why it applies: the two kinds of message differ in reach and duration.

  1. 1

    Choice → a hormone.

  2. 2

    Reach → nerve impulses reach only cells connected by nervous tissue; a hormone carried in the blood can reach all cells.

  3. 3

    Duration → a neuron must reset after each impulse and cannot keep sending impulses continuously; hormones act steadily and persistently.

  4. 4

    Speed not needed → hormones are slower, but this message does not have to be instant.

  5. Check: adrenaline is exactly such a message — it prepares many organs at once and its effects last ✔.

  6. Answer A hormone — it reaches all cells through the blood, not just nerve-connected ones; it acts steadily and persistently, whereas neurons cannot send impulses continuously; its slower speed does not matter here.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    Hormone + three reasons — 1 mark each for the reasons (reach all cells; steady/persistent; nerves need to reset). (Choosing "hormone" alone earns no marks.)

    Common trap: "Hormones are faster." Nerve impulses are faster; hormones win on reach and duration.

P23●● StandardShort answer · 3 marksOriginal

Design a simple experiment to show that roots grow downwards in response to gravity, whichever way the seed is placed. Write what you do, what you would observe and what it shows.

Full solution

Given: geotropism of roots. Asked: an experiment.

Concept used: geotropism; a fair test (Topic 4). Why it applies: changing the seed's position tests whether the root follows gravity.

  1. 1

    What you do → pin four soaked bean seeds on a damp blotting paper fixed inside a glass jar, each turned a different way (root tip pointing up, down, left and right). Keep the paper moist and the jar in the dark, so light cannot affect the result.

  2. 2

    What you observe → after 3–4 days, every root has curved to grow downwards, whatever its starting direction; the young shoots curve upwards.

  3. 3

    What it shows → roots respond to gravity by growing downwards — they are positively geotropic (shoots are negatively geotropic).

  4. Check: keeping the jar dark removes light as a cause; the seeds differ only in starting direction, yet all roots end up pointing down ✔.

  5. Answer Seeds placed in different directions on damp blotting paper in a dark jar all send their roots downwards after a few days — roots are positively geotropic.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    Set-up (seeds in different directions, kept dark) (1) · observation (all roots bend down) (1) · conclusion (positive geotropism) (1).

    Common trap: Keeping the jar on a sunny window-sill — then light could be the cause.
