Section A · what to look at again

Question 1 · MCQ

After a head injury, a man can think and speak clearly, but he sways when he stands and cannot walk in a straight line. Which part of his brain is most likely injured?

Correct: (b)

Posture, balance and walking in a straight line depend on the cerebellum. The medulla (a) controls blood pressure, salivation and vomiting; the cerebrum (c) is the thinking part — and he thinks clearly; the hypothalamus (d) releases hormones that act on the pituitary; it does not control balance.

Question 2 · MCQ

Which part releases hormones that stimulate the pituitary gland to release its hormones?

Correct: (c)

Releasing hormones come from the hypothalamus and stimulate the pituitary. The thyroid (a) makes thyroxin; the adrenal gland (b) adrenaline; the pancreas (d) insulin.

Question 3 · MCQ

In a reflex arc, the motor neuron carries the impulse from the:

Correct: (b)

The motor neuron carries the impulse from the spinal cord to the effector (muscle). (a) is the sensory neuron's path; (c) and (d) run the wrong way.

Question 4 · MCQ

Which statement about plant hormones is correct?

Correct: (b)

Plant hormones are made away from where they act and diffuse there. Auxin is made at the shoot tip (a ✘); abscisic acid inhibits growth (c ✘); making shady-side cells grow longer is auxin's job, while cytokinins promote cell division (d ✘).

Question 5 · A–R
AOn touching a hot object, we pull the hand away before we become aware of the pain.
RIn a reflex action, the information from the receptors never reaches the brain.

Correct: (C)

A is true: the reflex arc in the spinal cord acts first. R is false: the information also goes on to the brain, which is how we become aware of the pain afterwards.

Question 6 · A–R
AIn Activity 6.2, the roots of the bean seedlings bend away from the light.
RRoots are positively geotropic.

Correct: (B)

A is true: roots bend away from light (phototropism). R is true: roots grow towards gravity. But R is about gravity, not light, so it does not explain A.
