The whole chapter on one page

Why control and coordination — organisms detect a change (stimulus), choose the right response and act. Animals use nervous and muscular tissue and hormones; plants use hormones (and electrical-chemical signals from cell to cell).

Receptors and neurons — receptors are specialised tips of nerve cells in sense organs: gustatory (taste), olfactory (smell), heat/pain (skin), inner ear. A neuron: dendrite → cell body (nucleus) → axon → nerve ending. Inside a neuron the message is an electrical impulse; at the nerve ending chemicals are released, cross the synapse and start an impulse in the next dendrite (one way only). Neurons pass messages to muscles at the neuromuscular junction.

Reflex action — a sudden action in response to a stimulus, without thinking. Reflex arc: receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron in the spinal cord → motor neuron → effector (muscle/gland). Made in the spinal cord (where nerves first meet); the message also goes to the brain, which is informed. Reflex arcs evolved because thinking is too slow.

Kinds of action — voluntary (writing, talking — brain, fore-brain); involuntary (heartbeat, breathing, salivation, vomiting, blood pressure — mid-brain and hind-brain, many by the medulla); reflex (spinal cord).

Brain — CNS = brain + spinal cord; PNS = cranial nerves + spinal nerves. Fore-brain (cerebrum): thinking; sensory areas (hearing, smell, sight); association areas; motor areas for voluntary muscles; hunger centre. Mid-brain: involuntary actions. Hind-brain: medulla (blood pressure, salivation, vomiting), cerebellum (posture, balance, precision of voluntary actions), pons. Protection: cranium + fluid-filled balloon (brain); vertebral column (spinal cord). Muscle cells shorten when special proteins change their shape and arrangement.

Plants — no nerves, no muscles. Sensitive plant: quick, no growth; information passed cell to cell by electrical-chemical means; cells change shape by changing water content. Tropisms (directional growth): photo- (shoot towards light, root away), geo- (root down — positively; shoot up — negatively), hydro- (towards water), chemo- (pollen tube to ovule). Tendrils: the side touching the support grows less; the other side grows more → coils. Plant hormones are made away from where they act and diffuse: auxin (shoot tip; cells grow longer; moves to the shady side → shoot bends towards light), gibberellins (stem growth), cytokinins (cell division; fruits, seeds), abscisic acid (inhibits growth; wilting).

Hormones in animals — endocrine glands secrete hormones into the blood → target organs. Adrenaline (adrenal glands): heart faster, blood diverted from gut and skin to skeletal muscles, faster breathing. Thyroxin (thyroid; needs iodine — lack → goitre). Growth hormone (pituitary; deficiency in childhood → dwarfism). Testosterone (testes), oestrogen (ovaries) — puberty. Insulin (pancreas) — blood sugar; too little → blood sugar rises (diabetes). Releasing hormones (hypothalamus) → pituitary. Feedback: sugar up → more insulin → sugar down → less insulin.

Definition sheet

This chapter has no equations. Learn the two pathways and the definitions below instead.

Pathways to copy

  • Reflex arc: stimulus → receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron (spinal cord) → motor neuron → effector → response (a copy of the message goes to the brain).
  • Insulin feedback: blood sugar rises → pancreas makes more insulin → blood sugar falls → less insulin is released.

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Stimulus: a change in the surroundings that an organism responds to.
  • Receptor: the specialised tip of a nerve cell that detects a stimulus.
  • Neuron: a nerve cell, specialised to carry information as electrical impulses.
  • Synapse: the gap between two neurons, crossed by chemicals.
  • Reflex action: a sudden action in response to a stimulus, made without thinking.
  • Reflex arc: the pathway from receptor to effector through the spinal cord in a reflex action.
  • Effector: the muscle or gland that carries out the response.
  • Central nervous system: the brain and the spinal cord.
  • Peripheral nervous system: the cranial and spinal nerves linking the CNS to the body.
  • Tropism (tropic movement): directional growth of a plant part towards or away from a stimulus.
  • Plant hormones: chemicals that coordinate growth, development and responses in plants; made away from where they act.
  • Hormone (animal): a chemical secreted by an endocrine gland into the blood that acts on target organs.
  • Feedback mechanism: control in which the effect of a hormone regulates the amount of hormone released.
