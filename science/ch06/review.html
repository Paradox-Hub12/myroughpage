Week 1

Set 2 — Week 1

5 questions, about 8 minutes

Focus: applying the ideas — slightly harder.

W1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

Why can a person with damaged heat receptors in the fingers get burnt more easily?

Answer: The receptors cannot detect the heat, so no impulse is sent to the spinal cord and brain; the reflex that would pull the hand away does not happen, and the person does not feel the danger. (Topic 1)

Did you get it?

W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

Both the heartbeat and pulling your hand back from a flame happen without thinking. Give one way in which they differ.

Answer: Any one: the hand withdrawal is a sudden response to a stimulus through a reflex arc in the spinal cord; the heartbeat is an involuntary action that goes on all the time and is controlled by the mid-brain and hind-brain. (Topic 2)

Did you get it?

W1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

Name the two parts of the central nervous system, and say what protects each of them.

Answer: Brain — protected by the cranium (bony box) and a fluid-filled balloon that absorbs shocks. Spinal cord — protected by the vertebral column (backbone). (Topic 3)

Did you get it?

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

The leaflets of chhui-mui fold when touched. How do the cells change shape without muscles?

Answer: The cells change the amount of water in them, so they swell or shrink, which changes their shape and moves the leaflets. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

W1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

When adrenaline is released, why does less blood go to the skin and digestive system?

Answer: Muscles around the small arteries of these organs contract, reducing their blood supply; the blood is diverted to the skeletal muscles, which need more oxygen to act in an emergency. (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

Month 1

Set 3 — Month 1

5 questions, about 12 minutes

Focus: linking topics — the kind of thinking the board exam rewards.

M1.1 · 1 of 5●●● Advanced

Nerves and hormones both use chemicals. Where does each use them, and why is the nervous message still much faster?

Answer: Nerves use chemicals only to cross the tiny synapse; along the neuron the message is a fast electrical impulse. Hormones are chemicals that travel the whole way in the blood to the target organs, which is much slower. (Topics 1 and 5)

Did you get it?

M1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

In Activity 6.2, the flask is turned round after the shoots have bent towards the light. Explain why only the new growth changes direction.

Answer: Bending is a growth movement: auxin from the shoot tip moves to the shady side and makes those cells grow longer. Only growing parts can respond; the old parts have stopped growing. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

M1.3 · 3 of 5●●● Advanced

Muscle cells and the cells at the base of a chhui-mui leaf both change shape to cause movement. How is the way they change shape different?

Answer: Muscle cells change shape when special proteins change their shape and arrangement, making the cells shorter; plant cells change shape by changing their water content (swelling or shrinking). (Topics 3 and 4)

Did you get it?

M1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Without looking, list the labels on (a) your textbook's brain diagram and (b) its endocrine-gland diagram of a female. Then check against the wrap-up's "Draw it in the exam" card.

Answer: (a) Cerebrum, Cranium (skull), Fore-Brain, Mid-brain, Hypothalamus, Pituitary gland, Hind-brain, Pons, Medulla, Cerebellum, Spinal cord. (b) Hypothalamus, Pineal gland, Pituitary gland, Thyroid gland, Parathyroid glands, Thymus, Adrenal glands, Pancreas, Uterus, Ovary. (Topics 3 and 5)

Did you get it?

M1.5 · 5 of 5●●● Advanced

"Hormones only make things grow." Use one plant hormone and two animal hormones to show this is wrong.

Answer: Abscisic acid inhibits growth (wilting of leaves). Adrenaline prepares the body for an emergency (faster heartbeat and breathing). Insulin regulates the blood sugar level. (Topics 4 and 5)

Did you get it?
