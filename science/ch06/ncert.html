Intext p.105, Q1Reflex action and walking2 marks

What is the difference between a reflex action and walking?

Given: two actions — a reflex action and walking. Asked: how they differ.

Concept used: reflex and voluntary actions (Topic 2); the cerebellum (Topic 3). Why it applies: walking is a voluntary action; a reflex is not.

  1. 1

    Thinking → a reflex action happens without thinking, as a sudden response to a stimulus; walking is a voluntary action — we decide to walk.

  2. 2

    Control → a reflex is controlled by a reflex arc in the spinal cord; walking is controlled by the brain (the fore-brain decides; the cerebellum keeps it precise and balanced).

  3. 3

    Speed and purpose → a reflex is a very quick, usually protective response (pulling the hand from a flame); walking is a planned movement that we can start, stop or change at will.

  4. Check: compare with the table of three kinds of action (Topic 2) — reflex: no thinking, spinal cord; voluntary: thinking, brain ✔.

  5. Answer A reflex action is a sudden, automatic response made without thinking, through a reflex arc in the spinal cord. Walking is a voluntary action, done when we decide to, and controlled by the brain (with the cerebellum for balance and precision).

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Reflex action: sudden, automatic response without thinking; controlled by the spinal cord (reflex arc). (1)
    • Walking: voluntary action, under conscious control of the brain (cerebellum maintains balance and precision). (1)
    Common trap: "Walking is involuntary because we don't think about each step." We choose to walk; the cerebellum only keeps it smooth.
Intext p.105, Q2What happens at a synapse2 marks

What happens at the synapse between two neurons?

Given: a synapse between two neurons. Asked: what happens there.

Concept used: how a message travels across a synapse (Topic 1). Why it applies: the synapse is the gap where the message changes form.

  1. 1

    Impulse arrives → an electrical impulse reaches the end of the axon (nerve ending) of the first neuron.

  2. 2

    Chemicals released → the impulse sets off the release of chemicals there.

  3. 3

    Chemicals cross the gap → these chemicals cross the synapse.

  4. 4

    New impulse → on reaching the next neuron's dendrite, they set off a new electrical impulse there.

  5. Check: the order matches the synapse close-up (Topic 1): electrical → chemical → electrical ✔.

  6. Answer At the synapse, the impulse arriving at the axon's end makes the first neuron release chemicals; they cross the gap and set off a new electrical impulse in the next neuron's dendrite.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Electrical impulse at the axon end releases chemicals. (1)
    • Chemicals cross the synapse and start a similar electrical impulse in the dendrite of the next neuron. (1)
    Common trap: "The electrical impulse jumps across the synapse." It is chemicals that cross.

Intext p.105, Q4Smelling an agarbatti2 marks

How do we detect the smell of an agarbatti (incense stick)?

Given: the smell of an agarbatti. Asked: how it is detected.

Concept used: receptors and the neuron (Topic 1); sensory areas of the fore-brain (Topic 3). Why it applies: smell is information from the surroundings, picked up by receptors.

  1. 1

    Receptors → olfactory receptors in the nose — the specialised tips of nerve cells — detect the smell.

  2. 2

    Impulse → this starts a chemical reaction in the receptor, producing an electrical impulse.

  3. 3

    Nerves → the impulse travels along neurons (crossing synapses) to the brain.

  4. 4

    Brain → the fore-brain has a separate area for smell, which interprets the impulse, and we recognise it as the smell of agarbatti.

  5. Check: the route follows receptor → sensory neurons → brain, the input half of the nervous system ✔.

  6. Answer The olfactory receptors in the nose detect the smell and produce electrical impulses, which travel along nerves to the smell area of the fore-brain, where the smell is interpreted.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Olfactory receptors in the nose detect the smell. (1)
    • Impulses are carried by nerves to the fore-brain (smell area), where they are interpreted. (1)
    Common trap: "Gustatory receptors detect smell." Gustatory = taste; olfactory = smell.
Intext p.105, Q5The brain's role in a reflex2 marks

What is the role of the brain in reflex action?

Given: a reflex action. Asked: what the brain does in it.

Concept used: the reflex arc and the "message to brain" (Topic 2). Why it applies: the reflex arc is in the spinal cord, but the information also goes to the brain.

  1. 1

    The quick response is not made by the brain → the reflex arc is formed in the spinal cord, linking the sensory neuron to the motor neuron, because the brain's thinking is too slow.

  2. 2

    The brain is informed → the incoming information also travels on to the brain.

  3. 3

    What the brain does with it → we become aware of what happened (for example, we feel the pain of the burn) and can then decide on further, voluntary actions, such as cooling the burnt finger.

  4. Check: on the reflex-arc diagram, the arrow labelled "Message to brain" leaves the spinal cord after the reflex connection ✔.

  5. Answer The brain does not make the reflex response — the reflex arc in the spinal cord does. The information also reaches the brain, which becomes aware of the stimulus and the action (we feel pain) and can plan further voluntary action.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Reflex arc is formed in the spinal cord; the response is made without involving the thinking brain. (1)
    • The information also reaches the brain, which becomes aware of it (e.g. feels pain) and may plan further action. (1)
    Common trap: "The brain sends the order to the muscle in a reflex." The spinal cord does.

Intext p.108, Q1Plant hormones2 marks

What are plant hormones?

Given: the term "plant hormones". Asked: its meaning.

Concept used: chemical coordination in plants (Topic 4). Why it applies: plants use chemicals, not nerves, to coordinate.

  1. 1

    What they are → chemical compounds used by plants for control and coordination.

  2. 2

    What they do → they coordinate the plant's growth, development and responses to its surroundings.

  3. 3

    How they work → each is made in one part of the plant and diffuses to the part where it acts.

  4. 4

    Examples → auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid.

  5. Check: auxin fits all three points — a chemical, made at the shoot tip, diffusing to the cells below it that grow longer ✔.

  6. Answer Plant hormones are chemicals that coordinate a plant's growth, development and responses to its surroundings. They are made at one place and diffuse to the area where they act. Examples: auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin, abscisic acid.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Chemical substances that coordinate growth, development and responses to the environment; made away from where they act and diffuse to it. (1)
    • Any two examples: auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin, abscisic acid. (1)
    Common trap: Defining them as "chemicals that make plants grow". Abscisic acid is a plant hormone that inhibits growth.
Intext p.108, Q2Sensitive plant vs shoot2 marks

How is the movement of leaves of the sensitive plant different from the movement of a shoot towards light?

Given: two plant movements. Asked: how they differ.

Concept used: immediate response vs movement due to growth (Topic 4). Why it applies: only one of the two involves growth.

  1. Point Leaves of the sensitive plant Shoot bending towards light
    Growth no growth involved movement due to growth
    Speed very quick slow
    Direction not related to the direction of the touch directional — towards the light (a tropism)
    Cause cells change shape by changing their water content auxin makes cells on the shady side grow longer

  2. Check: if growth were stopped, the shoot could not bend — but the leaves would still fold ✔.

  3. Answer The sensitive plant's leaves fold quickly, with no growth, because cells change shape by losing or gaining water. A shoot bends towards light slowly, by directional growth, because auxin makes the shady side grow longer.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Any two matched differences — 1 mark each (growth / speed / direction / cause).
    Common trap: Writing only one side, e.g. "the sensitive plant has no growth". Give both plants for each point.
Intext p.108, Q3A growth-promoting hormone1 mark

Give an example of a plant hormone that promotes growth.

Given: plant hormones. Asked: one that promotes growth.

Concept used: the four plant hormones (Topic 4). Why it applies: three of them promote growth; one inhibits it.

  1. 1

    Auxin helps cells grow longer. (Gibberellins — stem growth — and cytokinins — cell division — are also correct answers.)

  2. Check: abscisic acid is the only one of the four that inhibits growth, so it must not be given ✔.

  3. Answer Auxin (or gibberellin, or cytokinin).

    • Auxin / gibberellin / cytokinin (any one). (1)
    Common trap: Giving abscisic acid — it inhibits growth.
Intext p.108, Q4Auxin and the tendril2 marks

How do auxins promote the growth of a tendril around a support?

Given: a tendril touching a support. Asked: how auxins make it grow round the support.

Concept used: tendrils are sensitive to touch; auxin makes cells grow longer; unequal growth causes bending (Topic 4). Why it applies: coiling is unequal growth on the two sides.

  1. 1

    Touch → tendrils are sensitive to touch; the support is the stimulus.

  2. 2

    Side in contact → the side of the tendril touching the support grows more slowly.

  3. 3

    Side away from the support → auxin stimulates the cells on the side away from the support to grow longer, faster.

  4. 4

    Result → with the outer side growing more than the inner side, the tendril circles round the support and clings to it.

  5. Check: the outer side of a coil is longer than the inner side, which fits faster growth on the side away from the support ✔.

  6. Answer When a tendril touches a support, the side in contact grows slowly, while auxin makes the cells on the side away from the support grow longer faster. This unequal growth makes the tendril coil round the support.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Part in contact with the support grows less rapidly than the part away from it. (1)
    • Auxin stimulates faster growth (elongation) on the side away from the support, so the tendril coils round it. (1)
    Common trap: "Cells touching the support grow faster." They grow more slowly.
Intext p.108, Q5Designing a hydrotropism experiment3 marks

Design an experiment to demonstrate hydrotropism.

Given: hydrotropism — growth in response to water. Asked: an experiment to show it.

Concept used: tropic movements; a fair test with a control (Topic 4, virtual lab). Why it applies: we must show roots growing towards water and rule out gravity.

  1. 1

    Set-up → fill a deep box (at least 15 cm) with slightly damp soil or sawdust; sow soaked seeds (e.g. gram) in a line across the middle.

  2. 2

    Source of water on one side → bury a porous (unglazed) clay pot filled with water at one end, so the soil near it becomes much wetter than elsewhere.

  3. 3

    Control → set up a second tray in exactly the same way without the clay pot.

  4. 4

    Wait → keep both trays in the same place for about a week.

  5. 5

    Observe → wash away the soil gently. In the test tray, the roots bend sideways towards the clay pot; in the control tray, they grow straight down.

  6. 6

    Conclusion → roots grow towards water: they show hydrotropism. Sideways bending rules out gravity, which would only send them downwards.

  7. Check: the only difference between the trays is the clay pot, so the difference in root direction must be due to water ✔.

  8. Answer Seeds are grown in slightly damp soil with a water-filled porous pot at one end; a control tray has no pot. Roots in the test tray bend towards the pot, while control roots grow straight down — showing hydrotropism.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Set-up: seeds in damp soil with a source of water (porous pot) on one side; a control without it. (1)
    • Observation: roots bend towards the water source (control roots grow straight down). (1)
    • Conclusion: roots grow towards water — hydrotropism. (1) (A labelled sketch helps.)
    Common trap: Watering the whole tray evenly from above — then roots simply grow down (geotropism) and nothing is shown about water.

Intext p.111, Q1Chemical coordination in animals2 marks

How does chemical coordination take place in animals?

Given: chemical coordination in animals. Asked: how it happens.

Concept used: endocrine glands and hormones (Topic 5). Why it applies: hormones are the chemicals that coordinate.

  1. 1

    Glands → endocrine glands secrete chemicals called hormones.

  2. 2

    Transport → hormones are released directly into the blood, which carries them all over the body.

  3. 3

    Target → they act on specific target organs or tissues, bringing about particular changes (e.g. adrenaline makes the heart beat faster).

  4. 4

    Control → feedback mechanisms decide when and how much hormone is released.

  5. Check: adrenaline fits every step — adrenal glands → blood → heart (target) ✔.

  6. Answer Endocrine glands secrete hormones directly into the blood, which carries them to target organs, where they bring about specific responses; the amount released is regulated by feedback.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Endocrine glands secrete hormones directly into the blood. (1)
    • Blood carries them to target organs/tissues, where they act; release regulated by feedback (example: adrenaline/insulin). (1)
    Common trap: "Hormones travel through nerves." They travel in the blood.
Intext p.111, Q2Iodised salt2 marks

Why is the use of iodised salt advisable?

Given: iodised salt. Asked: why it is advised.

Concept used: thyroxin needs iodine (Topic 5). Why it applies: iodine is needed by the thyroid gland.

  1. 1

    Iodine is needed to make thyroxin → the thyroid gland needs iodine to make the hormone thyroxin.

  2. 2

    Thyroxin's job → it controls how the body uses carbohydrates, proteins and fats (metabolism), keeping the right balance for growth.

  3. 3

    If iodine is lacking → thyroxin is not made properly and a person may suffer from goitre (swollen neck).

  4. 4

    So → iodised salt supplies the iodine our diet may lack.

  5. Check: the symptom (swollen neck) matches the position of the thyroid gland on the endocrine-gland diagram ✔.

  6. Answer The thyroid gland needs iodine to make thyroxin, the hormone that controls carbohydrate, protein and fat metabolism. Iodised salt prevents iodine deficiency, which can cause goitre.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Iodine is essential for the synthesis of thyroxin by the thyroid gland; thyroxin regulates carbohydrate, protein and fat metabolism. (1)
    • Deficiency of iodine may cause goitre (swollen neck). (1)
    Common trap: "Iodine is a hormone." Iodine is a mineral used to make thyroxin.
Intext p.111, Q3The body's response to adrenaline3 marks

How does our body respond when adrenaline is secreted into the blood?

Given: adrenaline in the blood. Asked: the body's responses.

Concept used: adrenaline and its target organs (Topic 5). Why it applies: adrenaline prepares the body for an emergency.

  1. 1

    Heart → the heart beats faster, supplying more oxygen to the muscles.

  2. 2

    Gut and skin → muscles around the small arteries of the digestive system and skin contract, so less blood goes there.

  3. 3

    Skeletal muscles → this diverts blood to the skeletal muscles.

  4. 4

    Breathing → the breathing rate increases because of contractions of the diaphragm and rib muscles.

  5. 5

    Overall → the body is ready to deal with the emergency — to fight or run away.

  6. Check: every change sends more blood or oxygen to the skeletal muscles ✔.

  7. Answer The heart beats faster (more oxygen to muscles); less blood goes to the gut and skin and more to the skeletal muscles; breathing becomes faster. The body becomes ready to deal with the emergency.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Heart beats faster, supplying more oxygen to the muscles. (1)
    • Blood to the digestive system and skin reduced (contraction of muscles round small arteries); diverted to skeletal muscles. (1)
    • Breathing rate increases (contraction of diaphragm and rib muscles); body prepared for emergency. (1)
    Common trap: "Blood supply to the stomach increases." It is reduced — the blood goes to the skeletal muscles.
Intext p.111, Q4Insulin injections2 marks

Why are some patients of diabetes treated by giving injections of insulin?

Given: patients with diabetes. Asked: why insulin is injected.

Concept used: insulin and blood sugar (Topic 5). Why it applies: diabetes is linked to insulin from the pancreas.

  1. 1

    Insulin's job → insulin, made by the pancreas, helps regulate the blood sugar level.

  2. 2

    In these patients → insulin is not secreted in proper amounts.

  3. 3

    Result → blood sugar climbs too high and harms the body — diabetes.

  4. 4

    Treatment → injections of insulin supply the hormone the body lacks, helping to bring the blood sugar level under control.

  5. Check: in a healthy person the pancreas releases more insulin when sugar rises (feedback); the injection replaces this missing response ✔.

  6. Answer In these patients the pancreas does not secrete enough insulin, so blood sugar rises (diabetes). Injected insulin makes up for the shortage and helps regulate the blood sugar level.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Insulin, secreted by the pancreas, regulates blood sugar; in diabetes it is not secreted in proper amounts, so blood sugar rises. (1)
    • Insulin injections supply the hormone and help control the blood sugar level. (1)
    Common trap: "Insulin is given to increase blood sugar." Insulin helps lower a high blood sugar level.

Exercise Q4Receptors and what goes wrong3 marks

What is the function of receptors in our body? Think of situations where receptors do not work properly. What problems are likely to arise?

Given: receptors. Asked: their function; situations where they fail; the problems.

Concept used: receptors (Topic 1). Why it applies: receptors are where all information about the surroundings enters.

  1. 1

    Function → receptors are the specialised tips of nerve cells, located in sense organs, that detect information (stimuli) from the surroundings and start an electrical impulse that is sent to the spinal cord or brain.

  2. 2

    Situation 1 → olfactory receptors blocked during a cold → we cannot smell, and food seems tasteless; we might not smell smoke or a gas leak.

  3. 3

    Situation 2 → heat/pain receptors in the skin not working → we might hold a hot object and get burnt, or not notice an injury.

  4. 4

    Situation 3 → gustatory receptors damaged → we cannot tell the taste of food, and might eat spoilt food.

  5. 5

    Problem in general → the body cannot respond correctly, so it cannot protect itself, because no information reaches the nervous system.

  6. Check: every problem comes from information not entering — the first step of detect → choose → act (Topic 1) ✔.

  7. Answer Receptors detect stimuli from the surroundings and send the information to the CNS as impulses. If they fail (e.g. blocked nose, damaged skin receptors), information is not detected, so the body cannot respond — we may not smell danger, may get burnt, or may not taste spoilt food.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Receptors detect stimuli from the environment and pass the information (as impulses) to the brain/spinal cord. (1)
    • Any two situations with their problems — 1 mark each (e.g. olfactory receptors blocked in a cold — no smell; heat receptors damaged — burns). (2)
    Common trap: Giving only "the sense organs won't work". Name the receptor and the problem for each situation.

Exercise Q6How phototropism occurs3 marks

How does phototropism occur in plants?

Given: phototropism. Asked: how it happens.

Concept used: auxin and unequal growth (Topic 4, auxin diagram). Why it applies: phototropism is a growth movement controlled by auxin.

  1. 1

    Meaning → phototropism is the directional growth of a plant part in response to light: shoots bend towards light, roots away from it.

  2. 2

    Auxin is made → auxin, a plant hormone, is made at the shoot tip and helps cells grow longer.

  3. 3

    Light from one side → auxin spreads to the shaded side of the shoot.

  4. 4

    Unequal growth → the higher concentration of auxin makes the cells on the shady side grow longer than those on the lit side.

  5. 5

    Bending → so the shoot bends towards the light.

  6. Check against the auxin diagram (Topic 4): auxin dots on the side away from the light; longer cells there; shoot curves towards the light ✔.

  7. Answer When light falls on a shoot from one side, auxin made at the shoot tip diffuses to the shady side; the cells there grow longer, so the shoot bends towards the light.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Definition: growth movement in response to light; shoots towards, roots away. (1)
    • Auxin synthesised at shoot tip diffuses to the shady side. (1)
    • Cells on the shady side elongate more, so the shoot bends towards light. (1)
    Common trap: "Auxin collects on the lit side" or "light pulls the shoot". Auxin moves to the shady side.
Exercise Q7A spinal cord injury2 marks

Which signals will get disrupted in case of a spinal cord injury?

Given: an injured spinal cord. Asked: which signals are disrupted.

Concept used: the spinal cord carries signals between the body and the brain and contains reflex arcs (Topics 2 and 3). Why it applies: an injury cuts this path.

  1. 1

    Signals from the body to the brain → impulses from receptors (touch, pain, heat) in the parts below the injury cannot reach the brain, so those parts lose sensation.

  2. 2

    Signals from the brain to the body → orders from the brain to the muscles below the injury cannot get through, so voluntary movement of those parts is lost.

  3. 3

    Reflex actions → reflex arcs that pass through the damaged part of the spinal cord are also disrupted.

  4. Check: the spinal cord is the meeting place of nerves on their way to and from the brain — both directions of traffic are cut ✔.

  5. Answer Both sensory signals from receptors to the brain and motor signals from the brain to the muscles (for parts below the injury) are disrupted; reflex actions that use the injured part are also affected.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Messages from receptors to the brain (sensory) are disrupted. (1)
    • Messages from the brain to muscles (motor) and reflex actions through the spinal cord are disrupted. (1)
    Common trap: Mentioning only movement. Sensation is lost too.

How does chemical coordination occur in plants?

Concept used: plant hormones (Topic 4). Why it applies: plants coordinate using hormones.

  1. 1

    No nerves → plants have no nervous system, so they coordinate mainly by chemicals — plant hormones.

  2. 2

    How hormones work → each is made in one part and diffuses to where it acts; together they coordinate the plant's growth, development and responses to its surroundings.

  3. 3

    Auxin → made at the shoot tip; helps cells grow longer; causes phototropism (moves to the shady side).

  4. 4

    Gibberellins → help the stem grow.

  5. 5

    Cytokinins → promote cell division; found in large amounts in fruits and seeds.

  6. 6

    Abscisic acid → inhibits growth; causes wilting of leaves.

  7. Check: the four hormones in the plant-hormone table (Topic 4): three promote, one inhibits ✔.

  8. Answer Plants coordinate through plant hormones, which are made in one part and diffuse to where they act. Auxin (cell elongation, phototropism), gibberellins (stem growth) and cytokinins (cell division) promote growth; abscisic acid inhibits growth (wilting of leaves).

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Plant hormones coordinate growth, development and responses; synthesised away from the site of action and diffuse to it. (1)
    • Any two hormones with roles — 1 mark each (auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin, abscisic acid). (2)
    Common trap: Writing "abscisic acid promotes growth". It inhibits growth.
Exercise Q9Why control and coordination are needed2 marks

What is the need for a system of control and coordination in an organism?

Given: an organism living in a changing environment. Asked: why it needs control and coordination.

Concept used: detect → choose → act (Topic 1). Why it applies: every response needs the right organs to work together.

  1. 1

    Responding to change → the surroundings keep changing; each change must be detected and must get the correct response (pulling away from heat, whispering rather than shouting).

  2. 2

    Working together → in a multicellular organism, many organs must act together and in the right order (e.g. in an emergency, the heart, lungs and muscles).

  3. 3

    Body activities → internal activities (heartbeat, breathing, digestion, growth, blood sugar) must be kept in balance.

  4. 4

    So → specialised systems — the nervous system and hormones in animals, hormones in plants — provide control and coordination.

  5. Check: without such a system, a burnt hand might not be pulled away, or the heart might not speed up in danger ✔.

  6. Answer An organism must detect changes in its surroundings and respond to each with the correct action, with its organs working together, and must keep its internal activities in balance. The nervous and hormonal systems provide this control and coordination.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • To detect changes in the environment and produce the appropriate response (protection, survival). (1)
    • To make different organs/systems work together in a coordinated way and keep body functions balanced — by nervous system and hormones. (1)
    Common trap: Answering only "for movement". Coordination also covers internal activities such as heartbeat and blood sugar.
Exercise Q10Involuntary actions vs reflex actions2 marks

How are involuntary actions and reflex actions different from each other?

Given: involuntary and reflex actions. Asked: the difference.

Concept used: three kinds of action (Topic 2). Why it applies: both happen without thinking, but are controlled differently.

  1. Point Involuntary action Reflex action
    Nature body activities we cannot control even if we try (heartbeat, breathing, digestion, salivation, vomiting) a sudden, quick response to a stimulus, usually protective
    Controlled by mid-brain and hind-brain (many by the medulla) a reflex arc in the spinal cord
    Examples heartbeat, breathing, salivation, vomiting, blood pressure pulling the hand away from a flame

  2. Check: both are done without thinking — which is why they are confused — but they differ in control centre (brain vs spinal cord) ✔.

  3. Answer Involuntary actions (heartbeat, breathing, salivation) go on without our thinking control and are controlled by the mid-brain and hind-brain (medulla). Reflex actions are sudden responses to a stimulus, made through a reflex arc in the spinal cord (e.g. pulling the hand away from a flame).

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    • Involuntary actions: controlled by mid-brain and hind-brain (medulla); e.g. heartbeat, salivation. (1)
    • Reflex actions: sudden response to a stimulus, controlled by the spinal cord (reflex arc); e.g. withdrawing hand from a hot object. (1)
    Common trap: "There is no difference — both are without thinking." Control centre and trigger differ.
Exercise Q11Nervous and hormonal mechanisms5 marks

Compare and contrast nervous and hormonal mechanisms for control and coordination in animals.

Given: two control mechanisms. Asked: similarities ("compare") and differences ("contrast").

Concept used: nervous coordination (Topics 1–3); hormonal coordination (Topic 5); wrap-up Card 1. Why it applies: the two work side by side in animals.

  1. 1

    Similarities (compare)

    Both control and coordinate the activities of the body and respond to changes.

  2. 2

    Both use chemicals at some stage — chemicals cross the synapse; hormones are chemicals.

  3. 3

    They work together — for example, the hypothalamus (brain) releases hormones that act on the pituitary.

    Differences (contrast)

    Point Nervous mechanism Hormonal mechanism
    Made up of neurons — brain, spinal cord, nerves endocrine glands
    Message electrical impulse chemical (hormone)
    Carried along nerve fibres in the blood
    Reaches only cells connected by nerves all target cells of the body
    Speed fast slow
    Effect short-lived; neurons must reset before the next impulse steady and long-lasting

  4. Check: the reflex (fast, short) and adrenaline (body-wide, lasting) examples fit the two columns ✔.

  5. Answer Both control and coordinate the body and use chemicals at some point. The nervous mechanism sends fast electrical impulses along neurons to connected cells, with short-lived effects; the hormonal mechanism sends chemical hormones in the blood to all target cells, more slowly but with steady, lasting effects.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    • Two similarities — ½ mark each. (1)

Exercise Q12Sensitive plant vs our legs3 marks

What is the difference between the manner in which movement takes place in a sensitive plant and the movement in our legs?

Given: two movements — leaves of the sensitive plant; our legs. Asked: how the manner differs.

Concept used: immediate movement in plants (Topic 4); muscle action (Topic 3). Why it applies: both are quick movements, but the mechanisms differ.

  1. Point Sensitive plant Our legs
    How the information travels electrical-chemical means from cell to cell; no specialised tissue through nerves — specialised nervous tissue
    What changes shape plant cells alter their shape by gaining or losing water (swelling or shrinking) muscle cells: special proteins change their shape and arrangement, so the cells shorten
    Muscles none muscle tissue does the work
    Control centre no brain or spinal cord brain (voluntary movement; cerebellum for balance) or spinal cord (reflex)

  2. Check: both are quick and involve no growth — the difference lies in how information is carried and how cells change shape ✔.

  3. Answer In the sensitive plant, information passes from cell to cell by electrical-chemical means and the cells change shape by changing their water content; there are no nerves or muscles. In our legs, nerves carry impulses from the brain or spinal cord, and muscle cells shorten when their special proteins change shape and arrangement.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    • Any three matched differences — 1 mark each.
    Common trap: "The sensitive plant moves by growth." Its movement involves no growth.
