The whole chapter on one page

Covalent bonding — carbon (2, 4) cannot easily form CX4−\ce{C^4-}CX4− or CX4+\ce{C^4+}CX4+, so it shares electrons. A shared pair = one covalent bond. Single (1 pair: HX2\ce{H2}HX2​, ClX2\ce{Cl2}ClX2​, HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O, NHX3\ce{NH3}NHX3​, CHX4\ce{CH4}CHX4​), double (2 pairs: OX2\ce{O2}OX2​, COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​), triple (3 pairs: NX2\ce{N2}NX2​). Valencies: C 4, N 3, O 2, S 2, H 1, Cl 1. Covalent compounds: strong bonds inside molecules, weak forces between them → low melting and boiling points; no ions → poor conductors.

Versatile carbon — catenation (C bonds to C: chains, branches, rings) and tetravalency (four bonds). Small atom → strong C–C bonds; silicon's Si–Si bonds are weaker (short, reactive chains). Saturated = only single C–C bonds (alkanes, not very reactive); unsaturated = C=C (alkenes) or C≡C\ce{C#C}C≡C (alkynes), more reactive. Rings: cyclohexane CX6HX12\ce{C6H12}CX6​HX12​ (saturated), benzene CX6HX6\ce{C6H6}CX6​HX6​ (unsaturated). Structural isomers: same molecular formula, different structures — butane 2, pentane 3.

Functional groups and series — –Cl/–Br (haloalkane), –OH (alcohol), –CHO (aldehyde, chain end), –CO– (ketone, inside chain), –COOH (carboxylic acid, chain end). Homologous series: same functional group; members differ by CHX2\ce{CH2}CHX2​ (14 u); one general formula; chemical properties similar, physical properties change gradually. Alkanes CnH2n+2\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2}Cn​H2n+2​, alkenes CnH2n\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}Cn​H2n​, alkynes CnH2n−2\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n-2}Cn​H2n−2​.

Naming — stem (meth, eth, prop, but, pent, hex) + group: chloro-/bromo- (prefix); -ol, -al, -one, -oic acid (drop the final e); -ene, -yne. Count every carbon, including the one in –CHO, –CO–, –COOH. No methanone/ethanone (ketones need 3 C); no methene (alkenes need 2 C).

Reactions — combustion (→ COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ + HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O + heat + light; blue flame = enough air; yellow sooty flame = limited air or unsaturated). Oxidation: alcohol → carboxylic acid with alkaline KMnOX4\ce{KMnO4}KMnOX4​ or acidified KX2CrX2OX7\ce{K2Cr2O7}KX2​CrX2​OX7​ (oxidising agents). Addition: unsaturated + HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ with Ni/Pd → saturated (vegetable oil → vanaspati). Substitution: alkane + ClX2\ce{Cl2}ClX2​ in sunlight → chloroalkane + HCl.

Ethanol — liquid; solvent (tincture iodine, cough syrups); soluble in water; + Na → sodium ethoxide + HX2\ce{H2}HX2​; + hot conc. HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​ (443 K) → ethene (dehydrating agent). Ethanoic acid — acetic acid; vinegar = 5–8%; glacial (m.p. 290 K); weak acid; + NaOH → sodium ethanoate + water; + carbonate/hydrogencarbonate → salt + water + COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​. Esterification: acid + alcohol → sweet-smelling ester (acid catalyst). Saponification: ester + NaOH → alcohol + sodium salt (soap-making). Tell ethanol from ethanoic acid with NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​ or litmus — not sodium.

Soaps and detergents — soap = sodium/potassium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid: hydrophobic tail + hydrophilic ionic head. Micelle: tails in the oil, heads outside in water → emulsion → rinsed away. Hard water (CaX2+\ce{Ca^2+}CaX2+, MgX2+\ce{Mg^2+}MgX2+) + soap → scum (wastes soap). Detergents (sodium salts of sulphonic acids / ammonium salts with ClX−\ce{Cl-}ClX−, BrX−\ce{Br-}BrX−) form no scum → work in hard water. Soap tests hard water (little lather + scum); soap solution is basic (red litmus → blue).

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Covalent bond: a bond formed by the sharing of a pair of electrons between two atoms.
  • Catenation: the ability of carbon atoms to bond with other carbon atoms, forming chains, branches and rings. Tetravalency: having a valency of four.
  • Saturated / unsaturated compound: only single bonds between carbon atoms / at least one double or triple bond between carbon atoms.
  • Hydrocarbon: a compound of carbon and hydrogen only. Alkane / alkene / alkyne: saturated / with C=C / with C≡C\ce{C#C}C≡C.
  • Structural isomers: compounds with the same molecular formula but different structures.
  • Functional group: an atom or group of atoms that gives a compound its characteristic properties, whatever the chain length.
  • Homologous series: a series of compounds with the same functional group, successive members differing by CHX2\ce{CH2}CHX2​.
  • Oxidising agent: a substance that adds oxygen to another substance.
  • Catalyst: a substance that changes the rate of a reaction without itself being used up.
  • Addition reaction: atoms add across a double or triple bond. Substitution reaction: one atom or group takes the place of another.
  • Esterification / saponification: acid + alcohol → ester + water / ester + alkali → alcohol + salt of the acid.
  • Micelle: a cluster of soap molecules with tails inside (round oily dirt) and ionic heads outside, facing the water.
  • Scum: the insoluble substance formed when soap reacts with the calcium and magnesium ions in hard water.
