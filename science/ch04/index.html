Chapter 4 · Unit I

Carbon and its Compounds

8 topicsAbout 9 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

The LPG in your kitchen, the petrol in a scooter, the ethanol in a hand sanitiser, the vinegar in a jar of pickles, the soap at the wash-basin and the plastic of your water bottle — all are compounds of one element, carbon. So are the proteins, fats and DNA in your own body. Chemists know millions of carbon compounds, more than those of all the other elements together. Why can carbon do what no other element can?

This chapter answers with two big ideas. The first is the covalent bond: carbon shares electrons instead of giving them away, and it can bond to other carbon atoms again and again, forming chains, branches and rings. The second is the functional group: a small group of atoms that gives a whole family of compounds its name and its reactions. It is part of Unit I, Chemical Substances – Nature and Behaviour, and its electron-dot structures, names, reactions and soap questions are common in CBSE sample papers and board-style questions.

What you'll learn

  • Write electron arrangements and valencies; tell ionic from covalent bonds; check the number of bonds round each atom.
  • Explain why carbon shares electrons; draw electron-dot structures with single, double and triple bonds; explain the properties of covalent compounds.
  • Explain catenation and tetravalency; tell saturated from unsaturated hydrocarbons; draw chains, branches, rings and isomers.
  • Recognise functional groups; write the next member of a series; describe how properties change along a series.
  • Name compounds from their structures and draw structures from their names; avoid impossible names.
  • Write equations for combustion, oxidation, addition and substitution, with their conditions.
  • Describe their properties, uses and reactions, including esterification and saponification; tell them apart.
  • Explain how soap cleans (micelles), why it forms scum in hard water, and why detergents do not.
  • Use the naming card and reaction cards to solve "identify the compound" problems.
