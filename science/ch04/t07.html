Topic 7 of 8

Ethanol and ethanoic acid

About 60 minutes · 4 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • State the properties and uses of ethanol, and write equations for its reactions with sodium and with hot concentrated sulphuric acid (dehydration to ethene).
  • State the properties and uses of ethanoic acid (vinegar, glacial acetic acid, a weak acid), and write equations for its reactions with sodium hydroxide, carbonates and hydrogencarbonates.
  • Describe esterification and saponification, with equations.
  • Distinguish ethanol from ethanoic acid by experiment.

Two clear, colourless liquids sit in your home: the ethanol in a hand sanitiser and the vinegar in a jar of pickles. One evaporates off your hands with a sharp smell; the other smells sour and keeps mango pickle from spoiling for months. They differ by just one oxygen atom and two hydrogen atoms — and that is enough to make one an alcohol and the other an acid.

Quick reminder (Chapter 2)

Acid + base → salt + water. Acid + carbonate or hydrogencarbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide; carbon dioxide turns lime water milky. A strong acid is completely ionised in water; a weak acid only partly.

  1. Ethanol, CX2HX5OH\ce{C2H5OH}CX2​HX5​OH

Properties and uses. At room temperature ethanol is a liquid (melting point 156 K, boiling point 351 K). Its everyday name is alcohol, and it is what makes alcoholic drinks intoxicating. Because it dissolves many substances well (a good solvent), it is found in medicines such as tincture iodine, cough syrups and many tonics. It is soluble in water in all proportions.

Drinking even small amounts of dilute ethanol causes drunkenness. Pure ethanol (absolute alcohol) can kill even in a small quantity, and drinking alcohol regularly over many years causes serious health problems.

Reaction with sodium. Alcohols react with sodium, giving off hydrogen. With ethanol the other product is sodium ethoxide.

NCERT information box

Large amounts of alcohol slow the body's processes and depress the nervous system, causing loss of coordination, confusion and drowsiness. Methanol is far more dangerous: even a small amount can kill, because the liver turns it into methanal, which damages cells, and it can cause blindness. Industrial ethanol is made unfit to drink by adding methanol and a blue dye — this is denatured alcohol.

Did you know?Not in board exam
NCERT information box

Sugarcane turns sunlight into chemical energy very efficiently. Its juice gives molasses, which is fermented to make ethanol. Some countries add ethanol to petrol, because it is a cleaner fuel that gives only carbon dioxide and water when it burns in enough air.

  1. Ethanoic acid, CHX3COOH\ce{CH3COOH}CHX3​COOH

Properties and uses. The everyday name of ethanoic acid is acetic acid; it is a member of the carboxylic acid family. A 5–8% solution of acetic acid in water is vinegar, widely used as a preservative in pickles. Pure ethanoic acid melts at 290 K (about 17 °C), so it often freezes in cold winters — which is why it is called glacial acetic acid.

A weak acid. Mineral acids such as hydrochloric acid ionise fully in water. Carboxylic acids ionise only partly, so they are weak acids:

CHX3COOH⇌CHX3COOX−+HX+\ce{CH3COOH <=> CH3COO- + H+}CHX3​COOH​CHX3​COOX−+HX+Swipe to see the whole equation →

Dilute ethanoic acid and dilute hydrochloric acid both turn blue litmus red, but universal indicator shows that ethanoic acid has a higher pH — it is the weaker acid (NCERT Activity 4.7).

Reaction with a base. Just as a mineral acid would, ethanoic acid neutralises sodium hydroxide, forming a salt, sodium ethanoate (commonly called sodium acetate), and water:

  1. Esterification and saponification

Esterification. An ester usually forms when an acid reacts with an alcohol. Warm ethanoic acid with absolute ethanol and an acid catalyst (a few drops of concentrated sulphuric acid) and you get an ester and water:

Saponification. When an ester is treated with sodium hydroxide (an alkali), it is converted back into the alcohol and the sodium salt of the carboxylic acid:

CHX3COOCX2HX5+NaOH→CX2HX5OH+CHX3COONa\ce{CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH -> C2H5OH + CH3COONa}CHX3​COOCX2​HX5​+NaOH​CX2​HX5​OH+CHX3​COONaSwipe to see the whole equation →

This reaction is called saponification (from the Latin sapo, soap) because it is used to make soap. Soaps are sodium or potassium salts of long-chain carboxylic acids (Topic 8).

  1. Virtual lab — CBSE Practical 8: properties of acetic acid

What you do: Take a little dilute acetic acid (ethanoic acid) and test:

  1. Odour — waft the vapour gently towards your nose.
  2. Solubility — add 1 mL to 5 mL of water in a test tube and shake.
  3. Effect on litmus — put a drop on blue litmus paper and on red litmus paper.
  4. Reaction with sodium hydrogencarbonate — add a pinch of solid sodium hydrogencarbonate; pass the gas through lime water.

What you observe:

Test Observation
Odour sharp, sour smell of vinegar
Solubility mixes completely; a clear solution — no layers
Blue litmus turns red
Red litmus no change
Sodium hydrogencarbonate brisk effervescence; the gas turns lime water milky

What it shows: acetic acid is soluble in water, is acidic and reacts with a hydrogencarbonate to give carbon dioxide.

Safety

use dilute acetic acid; never taste anything in the laboratory; glacial acetic acid is corrosive and its vapour stings the eyes.

  1. Telling ethanol from ethanoic acid

Test Ethanol Ethanoic acid
Smell sharp, spirit-like sour, like vinegar
Blue litmus no change turns red
Sodium hydrogencarbonate no reaction brisk effervescence of COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ (lime water turns milky)
Melting point 156 K — never freezes in winter 290 K — may freeze in winter

The sodium hydrogencarbonate test (or sodium carbonate) is the most reliable. Sodium metal is no use for telling them apart: both give hydrogen with sodium.

Worked example 1

From an alcohol to an alkene

Question. Compound X has the formula CX2HX6O\ce{C2H6O}CX2​HX6​O. It is a liquid used in cough syrups. A tiny piece of sodium dropped into X gives a gas that burns with a pop. When X is heated at 443 K with excess concentrated sulphuric acid, it gives a gas Y. Identify X and Y and write the two equations. What is the role of the sulphuric acid?

Given: CX2HX6O\ce{C2H6O}CX2​HX6​O; solvent in medicines; gives hydrogen with Na; gives Y with hot conc. HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​. Asked: X, Y, equations, role of acid.

Concept used: properties and reactions of ethanol (this topic). Why it applies: CX2HX6O\ce{C2H6O}CX2​HX6​O used as a solvent in medicines is ethanol.

  1. 1

    X = ethanol, CHX3CHX2OH\ce{CH3CH2OH}CHX3​CHX2​OH.

  2. 2

    With sodium it gives sodium ethoxide and hydrogen. Skeletal equation:

    Na+CHX3CHX2OH→CHX3CHX2ONa+HX2\ce{Na + CH3CH2OH -> CH3CH2ONa + H2}Na+CHX3​CHX2​OH​CHX3​CHX2​ONa+HX2​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    Hydrogen: right has 5 + 2 = 7, an odd number; put 2 in front of CHX3CHX2ONa\ce{CH3CH2ONa}CHX3​CHX2​ONa.

  4. 4

    Then 2 ethanol molecules are needed → put 2 in front of CHX3CHX2OH\ce{CH3CH2OH}CHX3​CHX2​OH.

  5. 5

    Sodium: right now 2 → put 2 in front of Na.

    2 Na+2 CHX3CHX2OH→2 CHX3CHX2ONa+HX2\ce{2Na + 2CH3CH2OH -> 2CH3CH2ONa + H2}2Na+2CHX3​CHX2​OH​2CHX3​CHX2​ONa+HX2​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    With hot concentrated sulphuric acid, water is removed → Y = ethene:

    CHX3−CHX2OH→443 Khot conc.  HX2SOX4CHX2=CHX2+HX2O\ce{CH3-CH2OH ->[\text{hot conc. } H2SO4][\text{443 K}] CH2=CH2 + H2O}CHX3​−CHX2​OHhot conc.  HX2​SOX4​443 K​CHX2​=CHX2​+HX2​OSwipe to see the whole equation →
  7. 7

    The acid removes water → dehydrating agent.

  8. Check: first equation — Na 2 | 2, C 4 | 4, H 12 | 10 + 2 = 12, O 2 | 2 ✔. Second — C 2 | 2, H 6 | 4 + 2 = 6, O 1 | 1 ✔. Y fits the clues: an unsaturated gas formed when water leaves ethanol ✔.

  9. Answer X = ethanol; Y = ethene; equations as in steps 5 and 6; conc. HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​ is a dehydrating agent.

    Common trap: Calling the product with sodium "sodium ethanoate". Sodium + ethanol gives sodium ethoxide; sodium ethanoate comes from ethanoic acid.

Worked example 2

Ester and back again

Question. (a) Write the equation for the reaction of ethanoic acid with ethanol in the presence of a few drops of concentrated sulphuric acid. Name the type of reaction and the role of the sulphuric acid. (b) The product is boiled with sodium hydroxide solution. Write the equation and name this reaction.

Given: ethanoic acid + ethanol (acid catalyst); then the product + NaOH. Asked: equations, names, role.

Concept used: acid + alcohol → ester + water (esterification); ester + NaOH → alcohol + sodium salt (saponification). Why it applies: these are the two reactions of this topic that go in opposite directions.

  1. 1

    (a)

    The –OH of the acid and the H of the alcohol's –OH leave as water; the rest join through an O atom → the ester ethyl ethanoate.

    CHX3COOH+CHX3CHX2OH⇌acidCHX3COOCHX2CHX3+HX2O\ce{CH3COOH + CH3CH2OH <=>[\text{acid}] CH3COOCH2CH3 + H2O}CHX3​COOH+CHX3​CHX2​OHacid​CHX3​COOCHX2​CHX3​+HX2​OSwipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Reaction type: esterification. Concentrated sulphuric acid is an acid catalyst.

  3. 3

    (b)

    The ester is split by the alkali into ethanol and the sodium salt of ethanoic acid:

    CHX3COOCX2HX5+NaOH→CX2HX5OH+CHX3COONa\ce{CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH -> C2H5OH + CH3COONa}CHX3​COOCX2​HX5​+NaOH​CX2​HX5​OH+CHX3​COONaSwipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Reaction type: saponification.

  5. Check: (a) C 2 + 2 = 4 | 4, H 4 + 6 = 10 | 8 + 2 = 10, O 2 + 1 = 3 | 2 + 1 = 3 ✔. (b) C 4 | 2 + 2, H 9 | 6 + 3, O 3 | 1 + 2, Na 1 | 1 ✔. Reaction (b) gives back the alcohol that went into (a) ✔.

  6. Answer (a) esterification (equation in step 1); conc. HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​ = acid catalyst (b) saponification (equation in step 3)

    Common trap: Swapping the two names. Ester-ification makes an ester; sapon-ification ("soap-making") breaks an ester with an alkali.

Worked example 3

Two unlabelled bottles

Question. Two bottles have lost their labels. One holds ethanol and the other ethanoic acid. Describe two tests to tell them apart, with the result for each liquid. Write the equation for the test that produces a gas.

Given: ethanol and ethanoic acid. Asked: two distinguishing tests; equation.

Concept used: ethanoic acid is acidic; ethanol is not (this topic). Why it applies: a test is useful only if the two liquids give different results.

  1. 1

    Litmus test: put a drop of each on blue litmus paper. Ethanoic acid turns it red; ethanol causes no change.

  2. 2

    Sodium hydrogencarbonate test: add a pinch of NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​ to a little of each. Ethanoic acid gives brisk effervescence, and the gas turns lime water milky; ethanol gives no reaction.

  3. 3

    Equation for the gas:

    CHX3COOH+NaHCOX3→CHX3COONa+HX2O+COX2\ce{CH3COOH + NaHCO3 -> CH3COONa + H2O + CO2}CHX3​COOH+NaHCOX3​​CHX3​COONa+HX2​O+COX2​Swipe to see the whole equation →

  4. Check: C 3 | 2 + 1, H 5 | 3 + 2, O 5 | 2 + 1 + 2, Na 1 | 1 ✔. Neither test gives the same result for both liquids ✔.

  5. Answer Litmus: ethanoic acid turns blue litmus red, ethanol does not. NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​: ethanoic acid fizzes (COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​), ethanol does not (equation in step 3).

    Common trap: Suggesting sodium metal. Both liquids give hydrogen with sodium, so it cannot tell them apart.

Common mistakes!

  • Swapping esterification and saponification

    They are opposite reactions.

    Acid + alcohol → ester (esterification); ester + NaOH → alcohol + salt (saponification).

  • "Conc. HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​ is a catalyst when ethanol is heated at 443 K."

    Here it removes water from ethanol.

    Dehydration: dehydrating agent. Esterification: acid catalyst.

  • "Ethanoic acid is a strong acid."

    It is only partly ionised.

    It is a weak acid (higher pH than HCl of the same strength).

  • "Vinegar is pure acetic acid."

    Vinegar is a dilute solution.

    Vinegar = 5–8% acetic acid in water.

  • Using sodium to tell ethanol from ethanoic acid

    Both give hydrogen with sodium.

    Use NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​ (or litmus).

  • Sodium + ethanol → "sodium ethanoate"

    The product is sodium ethoxide, CHX3CHX2ONa\ce{CH3CH2ONa}CHX3​CHX2​ONa.

    Sodium ethanoate comes from ethanoic acid + NaOH or a carbonate.

  • Writing the ester as CX2HX5COOCHX3\ce{C2H5COOCH3}CX2​HX5​COOCHX3​

    That is a different ester.

    Ethanoic acid + ethanol → CHX3COOCX2HX5\ce{CH3COOC2H5}CHX3​COOCX2​HX5​ (ethyl ethanoate).

Key points

  • Ethanol: liquid, solvent (tincture iodine, cough syrups), soluble in water; + Na → sodium ethoxide + HX2\ce{H2}HX2​; + hot conc. HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​ at 443 K → ethene + water (dehydrating agent).
  • Ethanoic acid: acetic acid; vinegar = 5–8% solution (pickle preservative); glacial — freezes at 290 K; a weak acid.
  • Ethanoic acid + NaOH → sodium ethanoate + water; + carbonate or hydrogencarbonate → salt + water + COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​.
  • Esterification: acid + alcohol → sweet-smelling ester + water (acid catalyst). Saponification: ester + NaOH → alcohol + sodium salt (soap-making).
  • Tell them apart with NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​ (only the acid fizzes) or litmus — not with sodium.
