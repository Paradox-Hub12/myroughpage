CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section B, Q191 mark

Question: Manjeet wants to wash clothes, but the water available is hard. He compares the action of soap and detergent in hard water, to decide which one to use for washing his clothes. He observes:

  • Soap forms scum in hard water.
  • Detergent produces lather easily in hard water.

Which of the following inferences is correct?
A. CaX2+\ce{Ca^2+}CaX2+ and MgX2+\ce{Mg^2+}MgX2+ ions react with soap, forming insoluble compounds, whereas detergents do not form such insoluble salts.
B. Soap is acidic, and detergents are basic.
C. Soap react with NaX+\ce{Na+}NaX+ ions in hard water to form scum while detergents do not react with NaX+\ce{Na+}NaX+ ions.
D. Detergents form lather easily because they are ionic compounds and react with CaX2+\ce{Ca^2+}CaX2+ and MgX2+\ce{Mg^2+}MgX2+ ions while soaps do not react with hard water.

Given: soap → scum in hard water; detergent → lather. Asked: the correct explanation.

Concept used: hard water contains calcium and magnesium ions; soap forms insoluble salts with them, detergents do not (Topic 8). Why it applies: the two observations are exactly this difference.

  1. 1

    Scum is an insoluble compound of soap with the calcium and magnesium ions of hard water → A's first half is right.

  2. 2

    Detergents' charged ends do not form insoluble salts with these ions, so they lather → A's second half is right → A.

  3. 3

    B is not the reason (and soap solution is in fact basic). C blames sodium ions — but soap is itself a sodium salt. D says detergents react with CaX2+\ce{Ca^2+}CaX2+/MgX2+\ce{Mg^2+}MgX2+ and that soap does not react with hard water — both are the reverse of the truth.

  4. Check: A explains both observations in the stem — scum with soap and lather with detergent ✔.

    Common trap: Choosing D because it mentions CaX2+\ce{Ca^2+}CaX2+ and MgX2+\ce{Mg^2+}MgX2+. Read it carefully: it has the roles of soap and detergent swapped.
A. CaX2+\ce{Ca^2+}CaX2+ and MgX2+\ce{Mg^2+}MgX2+ ions react with soap, forming insoluble compounds, whereas detergents do not form such insoluble salts.

CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section B, Q252 marks

Question: Carbon forms millions of compounds whereas silicon, which is just below it in the Periodic Table, forms a few compounds. Explain the reason for the difference in their ability to form compounds.

Given: carbon forms millions of compounds; silicon only a few. Asked: the reason.

Concept used: catenation depends on strong bonds between atoms of the same element; smaller atoms form stronger bonds (Topic 3).

  1. 1

    Carbon has a small atomic size, so its nucleus holds shared electron pairs tightly.

  2. 2

    This makes C–C bonds strong and stable, so carbon shows extensive catenation — long chains, branches and rings.

  3. 3

    Silicon is larger, so Si–Si bonds are weaker; silicon catenates only to a small extent (chains of about seven or eight atoms, which are very reactive).

  4. 4

    Fewer stable chains → far fewer compounds.

  5. Check: the textbook gives the same explanation: "One reason for the formation of strong bonds by carbon is its small size… The bonds formed by elements having bigger atoms are much weaker." ✔

    Common trap: Answering only "carbon shows catenation" without the reason — the marking scheme wants small size → strong C–C bonds.
Carbon forms millions of compounds because it has small atomic size; therefore it forms strong and stable C–C bonds, showing extensive catenation. Silicon, being larger in size, forms weaker Si–Si bonds and cannot show catenation to the same extent, so it forms fewer compounds. — 2 marks (split inferred: carbon's small size and strong C–C bonds 1; silicon's larger size and weak Si–Si bonds 1)
