Wrap-up

Identify the compound

About 40 minutes · 9 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Draw electron-dot structures of covalent molecules.
  • Draw structural isomers.
  • Name compounds from their structures.
  • Write combustion equations.
  • Describe addition reactions.
  • Describe substitution reactions.
  • Describe the reactions of ethanol.
  • Describe esterification and saponification.
  • Explain micelles and the cleaning action of soap.

Board questions in this chapter love a chain of clues: "Compound A, CX2HX6O\ce{C2H6O}CX2​HX6​O, … on heating with concentrated sulphuric acid gives B, which adds hydrogen in the presence of nickel to give C. Identify A, B and C and write the equations." Every clue points to one family and one reaction. This page puts the chapter on two cards and a clue table, then works through six mysteries.

Six mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

The fizzing liquid

Question. Compound X has the molecular formula CX2HX4OX2\ce{C2H4O2}CX2​HX4​OX2​. Its 5–8% solution in water is used to preserve pickles. X gives brisk effervescence with sodium hydrogencarbonate. When X is warmed with an alcohol Y (CX2HX6O\ce{C2H6O}CX2​HX6​O) and a few drops of concentrated sulphuric acid, a sweet-smelling compound Z forms. Identify X, Y and Z and write the equations for the two reactions.

Given: CX2HX4OX2\ce{C2H4O2}CX2​HX4​OX2​, pickle preservative, fizzes with NaHCOX3\ce{NaHCO3}NaHCOX3​; Y = CX2HX6O\ce{C2H6O}CX2​HX6​O; sweet-smelling Z. Asked: X, Y, Z; two equations.

Concept used: properties of ethanoic acid; esterification (Topic 7). Why it applies: vinegar is a 5–8% solution of ethanoic acid, and sweet smells come from esters.

  1. 1

    A 5–8% solution used in pickles is vinegar → X = ethanoic acid, CHX3COOH\ce{CH3COOH}CHX3​COOH (formula CX2HX4OX2\ce{C2H4O2}CX2​HX4​OX2​ ✔).

  2. 2

    Acid + hydrogencarbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide:

    CHX3COOH+NaHCOX3→CHX3COONa+HX2O+COX2\ce{CH3COOH + NaHCO3 -> CH3COONa + H2O + CO2}CHX3​COOH+NaHCOX3​​CHX3​COONa+HX2​O+COX2​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    An alcohol with formula CX2HX6O\ce{C2H6O}CX2​HX6​O → Y = ethanol, CX2HX5OH\ce{C2H5OH}CX2​HX5​OH.

  4. 4

    Acid + alcohol with an acid catalyst → ester + water → Z = ethyl ethanoate (an ester):

    CHX3COOH+CX2HX5OH→acidCHX3COOCX2HX5+HX2O\ce{CH3COOH + C2H5OH ->[\text{acid}] CH3COOC2H5 + H2O}CHX3​COOH+CX2​HX5​OHacid​CHX3​COOCX2​HX5​+HX2​OSwipe to see the whole equation →

  5. Check: first equation — C 3 | 2 + 1, H 5 | 3 + 2, O 5 | 2 + 1 + 2, Na 1 | 1 ✔; second — C 4 | 4, H 10 | 8 + 2, O 3 | 2 + 1 ✔.

  6. Answer X ethanoic acid, Y ethanol, Z ethyl ethanoate (an ester); equations above.

    Common trap: Naming Z "sodium ethanoate". Sodium ethanoate is the salt from the first reaction; the sweet-smelling product is the ester.

Mixed example 2

A chain of three reactions

Question. An alcohol P has the formula CX3HX8O\ce{C3H8O}CX3​HX8​O, with its –OH group on an end carbon. When P is heated with excess concentrated sulphuric acid, it gives a hydrocarbon Q, CX3HX6\ce{C3H6}CX3​HX6​. Q adds hydrogen in the presence of nickel to give R. R reacts with chlorine in sunlight to give S and hydrogen chloride. Identify P, Q, R and S, and write the three equations, naming each type of reaction.

Given: P CX3HX8O\ce{C3H8O}CX3​HX8​O; P → Q (conc. HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​); Q + HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ (Ni) → R; R + ClX2\ce{Cl2}ClX2​ (sunlight) → S + HCl. Asked: P–S; equations; reaction types.

Concept used: dehydration of alcohols, addition and substitution (Topics 6–7). Why it applies: each step is one of these reactions, carried over from ethanol to the three-carbon member of the series.

  1. 1

    P = propanol, CHX3CHX2CHX2OH\ce{CH3CH2CH2OH}CHX3​CHX2​CHX2​OH. Losing water gives the alkene Q = propene:

    CHX3CHX2CHX2OH→hot conc.  HX2SOX4CHX3−CH=CHX2+HX2O\ce{CH3CH2CH2OH ->[\text{hot conc. } H2SO4] CH3-CH=CH2 + H2O}CHX3​CHX2​CHX2​OHhot conc.  HX2​SOX4​​CHX3​−CH=CHX2​+HX2​OSwipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    Type: dehydration (sulphuric acid is the dehydrating agent).

  3. 3

    Propene adds hydrogen across its double bond → R = propane:

    CHX3−CH=CHX2+HX2→NiCHX3−CHX2−CHX3\ce{CH3-CH=CH2 + H2 ->[\text{Ni}] CH3-CH2-CH3}CHX3​−CH=CHX2​+HX2​Ni​CHX3​−CHX2​−CHX3​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Type: addition (hydrogenation).

  5. 5

    In sunlight, chlorine replaces one hydrogen of propane → S = chloropropane:

    CHX3−CHX2−CHX3+ClX2→sunlightCHX3−CHX2−CHX2Cl+HCl\ce{CH3-CH2-CH3 + Cl2 ->[\text{sunlight}] CH3-CH2-CH2Cl + HCl}CHX3​−CHX2​−CHX3​+ClX2​sunlight​CHX3​−CHX2​−CHX2​Cl+HClSwipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    Type: substitution. (Chlorine can replace an H on the middle carbon too, so a mixture of chloropropanes forms; one is shown.)

  7. Check: Q is CX3HX6\ce{C3H6}CX3​HX6​ ✔, fitting CnH2n\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}Cn​H2n​ for an alkene; R is CX3HX8\ce{C3H8}CX3​HX8​ ✔, fitting CnH2n+2\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2}Cn​H2n+2​. Atoms in step 5: C 3 | 3, H 8 | 7 + 1, Cl 2 | 1 + 1 ✔.

  8. Answer P propanol, Q propene, R propane, S chloropropane; dehydration → addition → substitution.

    Common trap: Writing the third step as an addition. Propane is saturated — chlorine can only replace its hydrogens.

Mixed example 3

The fuel in the cylinder

Question. One molecule of a hydrocarbon burns completely to give 4 molecules of carbon dioxide and 5 molecules of water. (a) Find its molecular formula and name it. (b) Write the balanced equation for its combustion. (c) Is it saturated or unsaturated? Name the reaction it undergoes with chlorine in sunlight. (d) Draw its two structural isomers.

Given: 1 molecule → 4 COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ + 5 HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O. Asked: (a)–(d).

Mixed example 4

Two non-metals

Question. Element X has atomic number 6 and element Y has atomic number 17. (a) Write the formula of the compound formed by X and Y. (b) What type of bond does it have? Draw its electron-dot structure. (c) Will it conduct electricity? Will it have a high or a low boiling point? Give reasons.

Given: Z = 6 and Z = 17. Asked: formula; bond; electron-dot structure; two properties.

Concept used: valency (Topic 1); covalent bonding and its properties (Topic 2). Why it applies: both elements are non-metals.

  1. 1

    (a)

    X: 2, 4 → carbon, valency 4. Y: 2, 8, 7 → chlorine, valency 1.

  2. 2

    One carbon bonds to four chlorine atoms → XYX4\ce{XY4}XYX4​, i.e. CClX4\ce{CCl4}CClX4​ (carbon tetrachloride).

  3. 3

    (b)

    Both are non-metals, so they share electrons → covalent bonds. Carbon shares one pair with each chlorine; each chlorine keeps three lone pairs:

    Low boiling point: the forces between CClX4\ce{CCl4}CClX4​ molecules are weak.

Mixed example 6

From a sweet smell to soap

Question. A liquid L, warmed with ethanoic acid and a few drops of concentrated sulphuric acid, gives a sweet-smelling compound M. When M is boiled with sodium hydroxide solution, L forms again, together with a salt S. (a) Identify L, M and S if L has two carbon atoms. (b) Name the reaction M → L + S. (c) What is a soap, and how is it related to S?

Given: L + ethanoic acid → M (sweet); M + NaOH → L + S. Asked: (a)–(c).

Concept used: esterification and saponification (Topic 7); soaps (Topic 8). Why it applies: sweet smell = ester; ester + NaOH = saponification.

  1. 1

    (a)

    A two-carbon liquid that forms an ester with an acid → L = ethanol.

  2. 2

    M = ethyl ethanoate, CHX3COOCX2HX5\ce{CH3COOC2H5}CHX3​COOCX2​HX5​.

  3. 3

    Ester + NaOH → alcohol + sodium salt of the acid → S = sodium ethanoate, CHX3COONa\ce{CH3COONa}CHX3​COONa:

    CHX3COOCX2HX5+NaOH→CX2HX5OH+CHX3COONa\ce{CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH -> C2H5OH + CH3COONa}CHX3​COOCX2​HX5​+NaOH​CX2​HX5​OH+CHX3​COONaSwipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    (b)

    Saponification.

  5. 5

    (c)

    A soap is a sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid. S is the sodium salt of a short-chain acid — the same kind of compound, but with a chain too short to clean. Soaps are made by saponification of fats and oils, which are esters of long-chain acids.

  6. Check: atoms — C 4 | 2 + 2, H 9 | 6 + 3, O 3 | 1 + 2, Na 1 | 1 ✔; L appears on both sides of the two-step story, as the question says ✔.

  7. Answer (a) L ethanol, M ethyl ethanoate, S sodium ethanoate (b) saponification (c) soap = sodium/potassium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid; S is a short-chain salt of the same kind.

    Common trap: Calling M a salt. The sweet smell is the clue to an ester; the salt only forms in the second step.
