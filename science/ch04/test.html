Section A · what to look at again

Question 1 · MCQ

How many electrons are shared in all in one molecule of carbon dioxide?

Correct: (c)

O=C=O\ce{O=C=O}O=C=O has two double bonds = 4 shared pairs = 8 electrons. (a) is one shared pair; (b) counts only one double bond; (d) is the total number of valence electrons (4 + 6 + 6), including the lone pairs that are not shared.

Question 2 · MCQ

The correct name of CHX3−CHX2−CHX2−CHX2−CHO\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-CH2-CHO}CHX3​−CHX2​−CHX2​−CHX2​−CHO is:

Correct: (b)

Four carbons in CHX3−CHX2−CHX2−CHX2X−\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-CH2-}CHX3​−CHX2​−CHX2​−CHX2​X− plus the carbon in –CHO = 5 → pentanal. (a) forgets the –CHO carbon. (c) -one is for –CO– inside a chain. (d) -ol is for –OH.

Question 3 · MCQ

Which reaction needs sunlight to take place?

Correct: (b)

Substitution of a saturated hydrocarbon by chlorine needs sunlight. (a) needs a Ni/Pd catalyst; (c) needs hot concentrated sulphuric acid; (d) needs an acid catalyst and warming.

Question 4 · MCQ

Soap is added to water that contains magnesium sulphate. What is observed?

Correct: (b)

Magnesium ions make the water hard and form insoluble scum with soap, so little lather forms. (a) happens in soft water. (c) soap solution is basic, and scum formation does not make it acidic. (d) in water, micelles have their heads outside.

Question 5 · A–R
AAlkanes react with chlorine in sunlight by substitution, not by addition.
RAlkanes have only single bonds between their carbon atoms.

Correct: (A)

Both true, and R explains A: with only single bonds there is no double or triple bond to add across, so a chlorine atom can only replace a hydrogen.

Question 6 · A–R
AA detergent can be used to find out whether a sample of water is hard.
RDetergents do not form insoluble precipitates with calcium and magnesium ions.

Correct: (D)

A is false: a detergent lathers in both hard and soft water, so it cannot show hardness (soap is used). R is true.
