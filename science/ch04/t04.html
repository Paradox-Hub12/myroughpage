Topic 4 of 8

Functional groups and homologous series

About 45 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Identify the functional groups halo (–Cl, –Br), alcohol (–OH), aldehyde (–CHO), ketone (–CO–) and carboxylic acid (–COOH), and the C=C and C≡C bonds.
  • Define a homologous series, write the next or previous member, state the –CH₂– (14 u) difference and describe how physical and chemical properties change along the series.

Methanol, ethanol, propanol and butanol all react with sodium to give hydrogen, and all burn with a clean flame. Yet methanol boils at about 65 °C and butanol at about 118 °C. Why do these four behave alike in reactions but not in boiling? One small group of atoms — and one chain that keeps growing — explains both.

Quick reminder (Topic 3)

Carbon chains can be straight, branched or ringed, and their carbons are joined to hydrogen and to each other by covalent bonds.

  1. Heteroatoms and functional groups

Atoms of other elements can take the place of one or more hydrogen atoms on a hydrocarbon chain — such as chlorine, bromine or oxygen — in a way that keeps every carbon's valency at four. The element that replaces hydrogen is called a heteroatom.

A heteroatom, or a group of atoms containing it, gives the compound its own characteristic properties, whatever the length of the carbon chain. Such an atom or group is called a functional group. It is attached to the chain through its free valency, in place of a hydrogen atom.

Three groups all contain C=O\ce{C=O}C=O, so look carefully at what else is on that carbon:

  • Aldehyde: the C=O carbon also carries an H — it is always at the end of a chain.
  • Ketone: the C=O carbon sits between two carbons — never at the end of a chain.
  • Carboxylic acid: the C=O carbon also carries an –OH — always at the end of a chain.

  1. Homologous series

Because the functional group decides the chemical properties, compounds with the same functional group behave alike. The chemical properties of CHX3OH\ce{CH3OH}CHX3​OH, CX2HX5OH\ce{C2H5OH}CX2​HX5​OH, CX3HX7OH\ce{C3H7OH}CX3​HX7​OH and CX4HX9OH\ce{C4H9OH}CX4​HX9​OH are very similar.

A homologous series is a series of compounds in which the same functional group substitutes for hydrogen in carbon chains of increasing length. Its members:

  1. have the same functional group and so similar chemical properties;
  2. differ from the next member by a −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​− unit, which is a difference of 12 + 2 × 1 = 14 u in molecular mass;
  3. can be described by one general formula;
  4. show a gradual change in physical properties as the molecular mass increases — melting and boiling points rise, and solubility in a particular solvent changes step by step.

General formulas of the hydrocarbon series

Series General formula First members
Alkanes CnH2n+2\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2}Cn​H2n+2​ CHX4\ce{CH4}CHX4​, CX2HX6\ce{C2H6}CX2​HX6​, CX3HX8\ce{C3H8}CX3​HX8​, CX4HX10\ce{C4H10}CX4​HX10​
Alkenes CnH2n\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}Cn​H2n​ (nnn = 2, 3, 4 …) CX2HX4\ce{C2H4}CX2​HX4​, CX3HX6\ce{C3H6}CX3​HX6​, CX4HX8\ce{C4H8}CX4​HX8​, CX5HX10\ce{C5H10}CX5​HX10​
Alkynes CnH2n−2\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n-2}Cn​H2n−2​ (nnn = 2, 3, 4 …) CX2HX2\ce{C2H2}CX2​HX2​, CX3HX4\ce{C3H4}CX3​HX4​, CX4HX6\ce{C4H6}CX4​HX6​

(The alkene and alkyne series start at two carbons, because a double or triple bond needs two carbon atoms.)

The alcohol series — a ladder of −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​− steps

Alcohol Formula Molecular mass (u) Boiling point (about)
Methanol CHX3OH\ce{CH3OH}CHX3​OH 32 65 °C
Ethanol CX2HX5OH\ce{C2H5OH}CX2​HX5​OH 46 78 °C
Propanol CX3HX7OH\ce{C3H7OH}CX3​HX7​OH 60 97 °C
Butanol CX4HX9OH\ce{C4H9OH}CX4​HX9​OH 74 118 °C

Each step adds −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​− and 14 u, and the boiling point climbs. But every member still has –OH, so every member reacts with sodium, burns, and can be oxidised to an acid in the same way.

Physical properties change gradually; chemical properties stay similar. The chain length controls the size of the molecule (and so its physical properties); the functional group controls its reactions.

Worked example 1

Spot the functional group

Question. Name the class of compound and the functional group in each:
(a) CHX3−CHX2−CHX2−Br\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-Br}CHX3​−CHX2​−CHX2​−Br (b) CHX3−CH(OH)−CHX3\ce{CH3-CH(OH)-CH3}CHX3​−CH(OH)−CHX3​ (c) CHX3−CHX2−CHX2−CHX2−CHO\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-CH2-CHO}CHX3​−CHX2​−CHX2​−CHX2​−CHO (d) CHX3−CHX2−CO−CHX2−CHX3\ce{CH3-CH2-CO-CH2-CH3}CHX3​−CHX2​−CO−CHX2​−CHX3​ (e) CHX3−CHX2−CHX2−CHX2−COOH\ce{CH3-CH2-CH2-CH2-COOH}CHX3​−CHX2​−CHX2​−CHX2​−COOH (f) CHX3−C≡C−CHX3\ce{CH3-C#C-CH3}CHX3​−C≡C−CHX3​

Given: six condensed formulae. Asked: class and functional group.

Concept used: the functional-group table; look at what is attached to a C=O carbon. Why it applies: each formula contains exactly one group from the table.

  1. 1

    (a) –Br on the chain → haloalkane (bromo group).

  2. 2

    (b) –OH on a carbon (here the middle one) → alcohol (hydroxyl group).

  3. 3

    (c) –CHO at the end: C=O with H on the same carbon → aldehyde.

  4. 4

    (d) –CO– between two carbons → ketone.

  5. 5

    (e) –COOH at the end: C=O with –OH on the same carbon → carboxylic acid.

  6. 6

    (f) C≡C\ce{C#C}C≡C between carbons → alkyne (triple bond).

  7. Check: in (c) and (e) the group is at the chain end, as aldehyde and acid groups must be; in (d) the C=O carbon has carbons on both sides, as a ketone needs ✔.

  8. Answer (a) haloalkane, –Br (b) alcohol, –OH (c) aldehyde, –CHO (d) ketone, –CO– (e) carboxylic acid, –COOH (f) alkyne, C≡C\ce{C#C}C≡C

    Common trap: Calling (e) an alcohol because it contains "OH". In –COOH the OH sits on a C=O carbon, which makes it a carboxylic acid.

Worked example 2

Climbing the ladder

Question. Write the formula of the next member of each homologous series, and find the difference in molecular mass between the two members: (a) propanol, CX3HX7OH\ce{C3H7OH}CX3​HX7​OH; (b) propene, CX3HX6\ce{C3H6}CX3​HX6​; (c) ethanoic acid, CHX3COOH\ce{CH3COOH}CHX3​COOH. (C = 12 u, H = 1 u, O = 16 u)

Given: three compounds. Asked: next member; mass difference.

Concept used: successive members differ by −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​−. Why it applies: each compound belongs to a homologous series.

  1. 1

    (a) Alcohols

    Add −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​− to the chain: CX3HX7OH\ce{C3H7OH}CX3​HX7​OH → CX4HX9OH\ce{C4H9OH}CX4​HX9​OH (butanol).

  2. 2

    Masses: CX3HX7OH\ce{C3H7OH}CX3​HX7​OH = 3 × 12 + 8 × 1 + 16 = 60 u; CX4HX9OH\ce{C4H9OH}CX4​HX9​OH = 4 × 12 + 10 × 1 + 16 = 74 u.

  3. 3

    Difference: 74 − 60 = 14 u.

  4. 4

    (b) Alkenes

    CX3HX6\ce{C3H6}CX3​HX6​ → CX4HX8\ce{C4H8}CX4​HX8​ (butene). Masses 42 u and 56 u → difference 14 u.

  5. 5

    (c) Carboxylic acids

    Add −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​− between CHX3\ce{CH3}CHX3​ and –COOH: CHX3COOH\ce{CH3COOH}CHX3​COOH → CHX3CHX2COOH\ce{CH3CH2COOH}CHX3​CHX2​COOH (propanoic acid).

  6. 6

    Masses: 2 × 12 + 4 × 1 + 2 × 16 = 60 u; 3 × 12 + 6 × 1 + 2 × 16 = 74 u → difference 14 u.

  7. Check: (b) by the general formula CnH2n\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}Cn​H2n​: nnn = 4 gives CX4HX8\ce{C4H8}CX4​HX8​ ✔. Every difference equals the mass of −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​− = 12 + 2 = 14 u ✔.

  8. Answer (a) CX4HX9OH\ce{C4H9OH}CX4​HX9​OH (b) CX4HX8\ce{C4H8}CX4​HX8​ (c) CHX3CHX2COOH\ce{CH3CH2COOH}CHX3​CHX2​COOH — each 14 u heavier.

    Common trap: Adding CHX3\ce{CH3}CHX3​ (15 u) instead of CHX2\ce{CH2}CHX2​. A new link in the middle of a chain carries only two hydrogens.

Worked example 3

Which series?

Question. Assuming each is an open chain (no ring), say which homologous series each hydrocarbon belongs to: CX5HX12\ce{C5H12}CX5​HX12​, CX4HX6\ce{C4H6}CX4​HX6​, CX6HX12\ce{C6H12}CX6​HX12​, CX3HX8\ce{C3H8}CX3​HX8​. Which two belong to the same series?

Given: four molecular formulae. Asked: series; the pair in the same series.

Concept used: general formulas — alkanes CnH2n+2\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2}Cn​H2n+2​, alkenes CnH2n\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}Cn​H2n​, alkynes CnH2n−2\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n-2}Cn​H2n−2​. Why it applies: each is a hydrocarbon.

  1. 1

    CX5HX12\ce{C5H12}CX5​HX12​: nnn = 5; 2 × 5 + 2 = 12 ✔ → alkane.

  2. 2

    CX4HX6\ce{C4H6}CX4​HX6​: nnn = 4; 2 × 4 − 2 = 6 ✔ → alkyne.

  3. 3

    CX6HX12\ce{C6H12}CX6​HX12​: nnn = 6; 2 × 6 = 12 ✔ → alkene.

  4. 4

    CX3HX8\ce{C3H8}CX3​HX8​: nnn = 3; 2 × 3 + 2 = 8 ✔ → alkane.

  5. Check: CX3HX8\ce{C3H8}CX3​HX8​ and CX5HX12\ce{C5H12}CX5​HX12​ differ by CX2HX4\ce{C2H4}CX2​HX4​ = two −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​− units, as two alkanes two places apart should ✔.

  6. Answer CX5HX12\ce{C5H12}CX5​HX12​ and CX3HX8\ce{C3H8}CX3​HX8​ alkanes; CX4HX6\ce{C4H6}CX4​HX6​ alkyne; CX6HX12\ce{C6H12}CX6​HX12​ alkene. CX3HX8\ce{C3H8}CX3​HX8​ and CX5HX12\ce{C5H12}CX5​HX12​ are in the same series.

    Common trap: Grouping compounds by the number of carbons. CX4HX6\ce{C4H6}CX4​HX6​ and CX4HX10\ce{C4H10}CX4​HX10​ have the same number of carbons but belong to different series.

Quick check · 3 of 3Short answer2 marks

Ethanol and propanol react with sodium in the same way, but propanol has the higher boiling point. Explain both facts using the idea of a homologous series.

Answer: Both contain the same functional group, –OH, which decides the chemical properties, so they react alike. Propanol has a longer chain and a higher molecular mass (by 14 u); in a homologous series, physical properties such as boiling point increase gradually with molecular mass.

Functional group → chemical properties; chain length / molecular mass → physical properties.

Common mistakes!

  • "Successive members differ by CHX3\ce{CH3}CHX3​."

    An extra link in a chain is −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​−.

    Difference = −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​− = 14 u.

  • "Chemical properties change gradually along a series."

    The functional group, which decides reactions, is the same in every member.

    Physical properties change gradually; chemical properties stay similar.

  • Confusing –CHO and –CO–

    Both have C=O.

    –CHO has H on the C=O carbon (end of chain); –CO– sits between two carbons.

  • Calling –COOH an alcohol

    Its OH is on a C=O carbon.

    –COOH = carboxylic acid.

  • Putting compounds with the same number of carbons in one series

    A series is defined by its functional group.

    Check the general formula or the group first.

Key points

  • A functional group (–Cl, –Br, –OH, –CHO, –CO–, –COOH, C=C, C≡C\ce{C#C}C≡C) gives a compound its characteristic chemical properties, whatever the chain length.
  • Aldehyde and carboxylic acid groups are always at the end of a chain; a ketone group is never at the end.
  • A homologous series has the same functional group, members differing by −CHX2−\ce{-CH2-}−CHX2​− (14 u), one general formula, similar chemical properties and gradually changing physical properties.
  • General formulas: alkanes CnH2n+2\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2}Cn​H2n+2​, alkenes CnH2n\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n}Cn​H2n​, alkynes CnH2n−2\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n-2}Cn​H2n−2​.
  • Melting and boiling points increase with molecular mass along a series.
