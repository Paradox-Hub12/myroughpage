Week 1

Set 2 — Week 1

5 questions, about 8 minutes

Focus: the reasons behind the facts.

W1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

What would happen to the soil of a forest if all its decomposers disappeared?

Answer: Dead leaves and dead animals would pile up without rotting, and the substances in them would not return to the soil, so the soil would not be replenished and plants would run short of minerals. (Topic 1)

Did you get it?

W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

Write a food chain of three trophic levels with a fox at the top, and name the fox's trophic level.

Answer: grass → rabbit → fox — the fox is at the third trophic level (a secondary consumer). (Topic 2)

Did you get it?

W1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

Why do food chains usually have only three or four steps?

Answer: Only about 10 % of the energy passes on at each step; the loss is so great that very little usable energy remains after four trophic levels. (Topic 3)

Did you get it?

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Why does a fish-eating bird have a higher concentration of a non-degradable pesticide than the fish it eats?

Answer: The pesticide is not broken down, so it accumulates at each trophic level; the bird eats many fish and is at a higher level (biological magnification). (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

W1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

Why are refrigerators now made CFC-free?

Answer: CFCs have been linked to the depletion of the ozone layer, which shields the earth from harmful UV radiation; since 1987 their production has been controlled worldwide. (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

M1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

In the chain trees → deer → tiger, what happens to the trees if all the tigers are removed?

Answer: The deer are no longer eaten, so they multiply and eat more of the trees' leaves and young plants — the trees suffer. (Topic 2)

Did you get it?

M1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

"If all our waste were biodegradable, there would be no waste problem." Do you agree? Give a reason.

Answer: No. Biodegradable waste takes time to break down; in large heaps it rots, smells and spreads disease. It still has to be separated and composted. (Topic 6)

Did you get it?

M1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

Suggest two ways in which your school can reduce the waste it sends to the garbage dump.

Answer: Any two: separate biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste; compost food and garden waste; send paper and plastic for recycling; use refillable pens and reusable lunch boxes instead of disposables. (Topic 6)
