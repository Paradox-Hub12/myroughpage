How every solution is laid out: Given / Asked → Concept used (and why it applies here) → Steps, one per line → Check → ✅ Final answer → Write it in the exam (the points a marking scheme looks for, with marks) → Common trap. Intext p.212, Q1Trophic levels3 marks

What are trophic levels? Give an example of a food chain and state the different trophic levels in it.

Given: the idea of a food chain. Asked: the meaning of trophic levels; one food chain with its levels.

Concept used: each step of a food chain is a trophic level; levels are numbered from the producers (Topic 2). Why it applies: the question asks for both the definition and its use on a real chain.

  1. 1

    Definition → the various steps (levels) in a food chain at which energy is transferred, from the producers upwards, are called trophic levels. Each step or level of a food chain forms a trophic level.

  2. 2

    Example chain: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle.

  3. 3

    Levels: grass: first trophic level — producer; grasshopper: second — herbivore (primary consumer); frog: third — secondary consumer; snake: fourth — tertiary consumer; eagle: fifth trophic level.

  4. Check: read each arrow as "is eaten by" — grass is eaten by the grasshopper, the grasshopper by the frog, the frog by the snake, the snake by the eagle ✔; and the producer is numbered 1.

  5. Answer Trophic levels are the steps or levels of a food chain at which the transfer of food (energy) takes place. Example: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle. Grass — first trophic level (producer); grasshopper — second (primary consumer); frog — third (secondary consumer); snake — fourth (tertiary consumer); eagle — fifth trophic level.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    Each step or level of a food chain where transfer of energy takes place is a trophic level. (1) Example: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle (1). Grass — producer (1st level); grasshopper — primary consumer (2nd); frog — secondary consumer (3rd); snake — tertiary consumer (4th); eagle — 5th level. (1)

    Common trap: numbering the grasshopper as the first level because it is the first animal. The producer is always the first trophic level.
Intext p.212, Q2Role of decomposers2 marks

What is the role of decomposers in the ecosystem?

Given: decomposers. Asked: their role.

Concept used: decomposers break down dead remains and wastes into simple inorganic substances that return to the soil (Topic 1).

  1. 1

    Who they are → microorganisms — bacteria and fungi.

  2. 2

    What they do → they break down the dead remains and waste products of organisms — complex organic substances into simple inorganic substances.

  3. 3

    Why it matters → these simple substances go into the soil and are used up once more by the plants, so the soil is replenished; and garbage and dead bodies do not pile up.

  4. Check: without decomposers (Topic 1, Worked example 3) dead matter stays unchanged and the soil is not replenished — the two jobs named above are exactly what goes missing ✔.

  5. Answer Decomposers (bacteria and fungi) break down the dead remains and waste products of organisms, turning complex organic substances into simple inorganic substances. These go into the soil and are used again by plants. So decomposers clean up the ecosystem and replenish the soil (recycle materials).

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Decomposers break down complex organic substances of dead remains and wastes into simple inorganic substances. (1) These return to the soil and are used again by plants — natural replenishment of the soil / recycling of materials. (1)

    Common trap: "decomposers eat dead animals" (that is a scavenger such as a vulture). Decomposers are microorganisms that break substances down into simple inorganic ones.

Intext questions — page 214

Intext p.214, Q1Why some substances are not biodegradable2 marks

Why are some substances biodegradable and some non-biodegradable?

Given: two kinds of substances. Asked: the reason for the difference.

Concept used: decomposers break substances down with enzymes, and enzymes are specific (Topic 6).

  1. 1

    How decomposers work → bacteria and other saprophytes break substances down using enzymes.

  2. 2

    Enzymes are specific → each enzyme acts only on a particular substance.

  3. 3

    Biodegradable → natural substances (peels, paper, cotton) are broken down because the decomposers have enzymes for them.

  4. 4

    Non-biodegradable → many human-made materials such as plastics are not acted upon by any enzyme of bacteria or other saprophytes, so they are not broken down; they persist for a long time.

  5. Check: this is why a buried peel rots in weeks but a buried milk packet stays unchanged (Activity 13.5) ✔.

  6. Answer Substances are broken down by decomposers (bacteria and other saprophytes) with the help of enzymes, and enzymes are specific in their action. Substances for which decomposers have suitable enzymes are biodegradable; materials such as plastics, which no enzyme of these organisms can act on, are not broken down — they are non-biodegradable and persist for a long time.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Substances that are broken down by biological processes (by bacteria and other saprophytes) are biodegradable; others are non-biodegradable. (1) Enzymes are specific; decomposers have no enzymes to break down materials such as plastics. (1)

    Common trap: "plastic is non-biodegradable because it is hard". The reason is chemical — the decomposers' enzymes cannot act on it.
Intext p.214, Q2Effects of biodegradable substances2 marks

Give any two ways in which biodegradable substances would affect the environment.

Given: biodegradable substances. Asked: two effects on the environment.

Concept used: effects of biodegradable waste (Topic 6). Why it applies: the substances rot — usefully, if handled; harmfully, if dumped in heaps.

  1. 1

    Useful effect → when broken down by decomposers they return simple inorganic substances to the soil, which plants use again (they can be made into compost).

  2. 2

    Harmful effect 1 → in large heaps they rot and give a foul smell.

  3. 3

    Harmful effect 2 → rotting heaps attract flies and other carriers of disease germs, spreading disease.

  4. Check: every effect listed comes from the substance rotting — the one thing that does not happen to non-biodegradable waste ✔.

  5. Answer Any two: (1) they are broken down and return nutrients to the soil (useful as compost); (2) in heaps they rot and smell foul; (3) rotting heaps breed flies and disease germs.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Any two: broken down by decomposers and return nutrients to the soil (compost) / heaps of rotting waste give a foul smell / they attract flies and spread disease. (1 + 1)

    Common trap: writing "they persist for a long time" — that describes non-biodegradable substances.
Intext p.214, Q3Effects of non-biodegradable substances2 marks

Give any two ways in which non-biodegradable substances would affect the environment.

Given: non-biodegradable substances. Asked: two effects on the environment.

Concept used: non-biodegradable substances persist or harm members of the ecosystem; biological magnification (Topics 4 and 6).

  1. 1

    They persist → they are not broken down, so they remain in the environment for a long time and keep piling up (litter, garbage heaps).

  2. 2

    They harm organisms → for example, animals that swallow plastic bags can be harmed or killed.

  3. 3

    They enter food chains → harmful non-degradable chemicals such as some pesticides accumulate at each trophic level (biological magnification).

  4. Check: each effect follows from "not broken down" — something that rots would not persist, pile up or accumulate up a food chain ✔.

  5. Answer Any two: (1) they persist for a long time and keep accumulating as garbage; (2) they harm organisms — for example, animals that eat plastic bags; (3) harmful ones such as some pesticides enter food chains and accumulate at each trophic level (biological magnification).

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Any two: remain in the environment for a long time (pollute soil and water) / harm members of the ecosystem, e.g. animals eating plastic / enter food chains and get accumulated — biological magnification. (1 + 1)

    Common trap: "they make the soil fertile" — that is a result of biodegradable waste being decomposed.

Intext p.216, Q1Ozone and the ecosystem3 marks

What is ozone and how does it affect any ecosystem?

Given: ozone. Asked: what it is; how it affects ecosystems.

Concept used: ozone and the ozone layer (Topic 5).

  1. 1

    What it is → ozone (OX3\ce{O3}OX3​) is a molecule formed by three atoms of oxygen.

  2. 2

    How it forms → at the higher levels of the atmosphere, UV radiation splits OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ into free oxygen atoms, which combine with OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ to form ozone: OX2→UVO+O\ce{O2 ->[UV] O + O}OX2​UV​O+O; O+OX2→OX3\ce{O + O2 -> O3}O+OX2​​OX3​.

  3. 3

    Effect on ecosystems → high up, the ozone layer shields the surface of the earth from the Sun's UV radiation, which is highly damaging to organisms (it causes skin cancer in humans). So it protects all the organisms of every ecosystem.

  4. 4

    Near the ground → ozone is a deadly poison if breathed.

  5. Check: the equations balance — 1 + 2 = 3 oxygen atoms in the second step ✔.

  6. Answer Ozone (OX3\ce{O3}OX3​) is a molecule made of three oxygen atoms, formed in the upper atmosphere when UV radiation splits OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ and the free oxygen atoms combine with OX2\ce{O2}OX2​. Though poisonous to breathe, the ozone layer shields the earth from harmful UV radiation of the Sun, which can cause skin cancer and damage other organisms — so it protects every ecosystem. If it is depleted, more UV reaches the earth and harms living things.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    Ozone is a molecule of three oxygen atoms (OX3\ce{O3}OX3​), formed in the upper atmosphere by UV radiation acting on oxygen. (1) It shields the earth's surface from the Sun's UV radiation. (1) UV radiation is highly damaging to organisms (e.g. skin cancer), so the ozone layer protects all ecosystems. (1)

    Common trap: "ozone is the oxygen we breathe". We breathe OX2\ce{O2}OX2​; ozone is a deadly poison to breathe.
Intext p.216, Q2Reducing the waste problem2 marks

How can you help in reducing the problem of waste disposal? Give any two methods.

Given: the waste-disposal problem. Asked: two methods you can use.

Concept used: ways of dealing with waste (Topic 6).

  1. 1

    Separate biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste at home, so each can be handled properly.

  2. 2

    Compost biodegradable kitchen waste into manure.

  3. 3

    Recycle and reuse — send paper, plastic, glass and metal for recycling; reuse things instead of throwing them away.

  4. 4

    Reduce — avoid disposable items and plastic bags; carry a cloth bag for shopping.

  5. Check: each method either reduces the amount of waste made or changes where it ends up (compost, recycling) instead of a dump ✔.

  6. Answer Any two: (1) separate biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste at home; (2) compost kitchen waste; (3) recycle and reuse paper, plastic, glass and metal; (4) reduce the use of disposable items and plastic bags — use cloth bags.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Any two: segregation of biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste / composting kitchen waste / recycling and reuse / using cloth bags instead of plastic bags. (1 + 1)

    Common trap: "burn the garbage". Burning does not remove the problem; it pollutes the air.

Exercise Q11 mark

Which of the following groups contain only biodegradable items?
(a) Grass, flowers and leather (b) Grass, wood and plastic (c) Fruit-peels, cake and lime-juice (d) Cake, wood and grass

Asked: the groups with only biodegradable items (more than one group may be correct).

Concept used: biodegradable = broken down by biological processes (Topic 6).

  1. Reasoning for each option:

    • (a) ✔ Grass, flowers and leather (an animal product) are all broken down by decomposers.
    • (b) ✘ Plastic is non-biodegradable.
    • (c) ✔ Fruit-peels, cake and lime-juice are all broken down by decomposers.
    • (d) ✔ Cake, wood and grass are all biodegradable.

  2. Answer (a), (c) and (d) — the NCERT answer key gives all three. Only (b) contains a non-biodegradable item (plastic).

    Common trap: stopping at the first correct option. Here three groups are correct.
Exercise Q2MCQ1 mark

Which of the following constitute a food-chain?

Choose your answer first — the full solution opens after.

All three are plants (producers); none eats another.
Grass (producer) is eaten by the goat (herbivore), and goat meat is eaten by humans: grass → goat → human. The goat is at the second trophic level and the human at the third.
All three are herbivores; none eats another.
Goats do not eat fish, and fish do not eat goats — the three cannot be joined as who eats whom.

Asked: the group that forms a food chain.

Concept used: a food chain starts with a producer, and each member eats the one before it (Topic 2).

  1. Reasoning for each option:

    • (a) ✘ All three are plants (producers); none eats another.
    • (b) ✔ Grass (producer) is eaten by the goat (herbivore), and goat meat is eaten by humans: grass → goat → human. The goat is at the second trophic level and the human at the third.
    • (c) ✘ All three are herbivores; none eats another.
    • (d) ✘ Goats do not eat fish, and fish do not eat goats — the three cannot be joined as who eats whom.

  2. Answer (b) Grass, goat and human

Exercise Q43 marks

What will happen if we kill all the organisms in one trophic level?

Given: all the organisms of one trophic level are killed. Asked: the result.

Concept used: members of an ecosystem depend on each other; removing an organism affects the levels above and below (Topic 2).

  1. 1

    Level above → the organisms that fed on the removed level lose their food; their numbers fall, and they may die out or be forced to depend on other foods.

  2. 2

    Level below → the organisms that were eaten by the removed level are no longer eaten; their numbers increase sharply, which in turn affects the level below them (for example, more herbivores would overgraze the plants).

  3. 3

    Whole ecosystem → the flow of energy through the chain is broken and the balance of the ecosystem is disturbed.

  4. Check: apply it to grass → deer → tiger with all the deer killed: the tigers lose their food (level above), and the grass is no longer eaten and grows unchecked (level below) ✔.

  5. Answer The transfer of food and energy to the next level stops: organisms of the higher level lose their food and decline, while organisms of the lower level are no longer eaten and multiply unchecked. The balance of the ecosystem is disturbed, and the effects spread to other levels.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    The higher trophic level will be deprived of food and its population will fall. (1) The population of the lower trophic level will increase as it is no longer eaten. (1) The flow of energy is broken and the balance of the ecosystem is disturbed. (1)

    Common trap: describing only the level above. The level below is affected too.
Exercise Q53 marks

Will the impact of removing all the organisms in a trophic level be different for different trophic levels? Can the organisms of any trophic level be removed without causing any damage to the ecosystem?

Given: removal of one whole trophic level. Asked: whether the impact differs from level to level; whether any level can be removed safely.

Concept used: interdependence; energy enters through the producers (Topics 1–3).

  1. 1

    Removing the producers: no food or energy enters the ecosystem at all, so every other level starves — the whole ecosystem collapses. This is the worst damage.

  2. 2

    Removing the herbivores: the carnivores lose their food and decline; the plants grow unchecked.

  3. 3

    Removing the top carnivores: the levels below them multiply (for example, too many herbivores overgraze and destroy the plants), which harms the whole ecosystem later.

  4. 4

    So the impact is different for different levels — but every level does some damage when removed.

  5. Check: in each case the damage spreads through the arrows into and out of the removed level; for no level do both arrows vanish without effect ✔.

  6. Answer Yes, the impact differs: removing the producers is the most damaging, because no energy can enter the ecosystem and all other levels starve; removing consumers of a level causes the level below to multiply and the level above to lose food. No — the organisms of no trophic level can be removed without damaging the ecosystem, because all levels are interdependent.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    Yes, the impact differs — removal of producers would collapse the ecosystem, as all other levels depend on them for food and energy. (1) Removal of other levels causes an increase in the level below and a shortage of food for the level above. (1) No level can be removed without damage, since all trophic levels are interdependent. (1)

    Common trap: "the top carnivores can be removed safely because nothing eats them". Their prey would multiply and upset the levels below.
Exercise Q63 marks

What is biological magnification? Will the levels of this magnification be different at different levels of the ecosystem?

Given: biological magnification. Asked: its meaning; whether it differs from level to level.

Concept used: non-degradable chemicals accumulate at each trophic level (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    Meaning → harmful non-degradable chemicals (such as some pesticides) enter a food chain through plants (from soil and water) and accumulate progressively at each trophic level — their concentration increases at each higher level. This is biological magnification.

  2. 2

    Different at different levels? → Yes. The concentration is lowest at the producers and rises at each level, being highest at the top level — human beings, who occupy the top of most food chains.

  3. Check: if the levels were the same everywhere, the word "magnification" (making larger) would not fit; the textbook says the chemicals accumulate "progressively at each trophic level" ✔.

  4. Answer Biological magnification is the increase in the concentration of harmful non-degradable chemicals (such as pesticides) at each successive trophic level of a food chain. Yes, the levels differ: the concentration is least at the first trophic level and greatest at the highest level (in human beings at the top).

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    Accumulation of harmful non-biodegradable chemicals in increasing concentration at each trophic level of a food chain is called biological magnification. (1 + 1) Yes — the concentration is maximum at the highest trophic level (humans) and least at the producer level. (1)

    Common trap: "the level with most energy has most pesticide". It is the reverse: the top level has the least energy and the most pesticide.
Exercise Q73 marks

What are the problems caused by the non-biodegradable wastes that we generate?

Given: non-biodegradable waste. Asked: the problems it causes.

Concept used: effects of non-biodegradable waste (Topics 4 and 6).

  1. 1

    They persist → not broken down by decomposers, they remain in the environment for a long time; heaps of garbage keep growing and litter towns and tourist places.

  2. 2

    They pollute → they spoil the soil and water.

  3. 3

    They harm organisms → animals that swallow plastic can be harmed or killed.

  4. 4

    They enter food chains → harmful chemicals such as some pesticides accumulate at each trophic level (biological magnification), reaching humans at the top.

  5. Check: every problem listed comes from the waste not being broken down — none of them would happen to a peel that rots in weeks ✔.

  6. Answer Non-biodegradable wastes persist for a long time and pile up as garbage; they pollute soil and water; they harm animals (for example, those that eat plastic); and harmful ones such as some pesticides enter food chains and accumulate at each trophic level (biological magnification), affecting human beings at the top.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    Any three: remain in the environment for a long time and accumulate as garbage / pollute soil and water / harm animals that consume them / enter food chains — biological magnification. (1 + 1 + 1)

    Common trap: writing only "they cause pollution". Name how — persistence, harm to organisms, biological magnification.
Exercise Q83 marks

If all the waste we generate is biodegradable, will this have no impact on the environment?

Given: all our waste is biodegradable. Asked: would there be no impact?

Concept used: effects of biodegradable waste (Topic 6).

  1. 1

    It still has an impact → decomposers need time to break the waste down, and we produce very large amounts every day; the waste piles up faster than it rots.

  2. 2

    Rotting heaps → give a foul smell and attract flies and other carriers of disease germs.

  3. 3

    If managed (separated and composted) → its impact is small, and it even returns nutrients to the soil.

  4. Check: the answer does not depend on the waste being non-biodegradable — every effect above comes from rotting in large amounts, which is exactly what biodegradable waste does ✔.

  5. Answer No — it will still have an impact. Even biodegradable waste takes time to decompose; produced in large amounts, it piles up, rots and smells, and breeds flies and disease germs. Its impact is small only if it is managed properly — collected separately and composted, when it returns nutrients to the soil.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    No — it will still have an impact. (1) Large amounts take time to decompose and pile up, causing foul smell and spreading disease through flies/germs. (1) Its impact can be reduced by proper management, e.g. composting. (1)

    Common trap: "biodegradable waste is harmless because it rots". It is the amount and how it is handled that matter.
Exercise Q93 marks

Why is damage to the ozone layer a cause for concern? What steps are being taken to limit this damage?

Given: damage to the ozone layer. Asked: why it matters; steps taken.

Concept used: ozone layer, UV and CFCs (Topic 5).

  1. 1

    Why it matters → the ozone layer shields the earth from the Sun's UV radiation. If it is damaged, more UV reaches the earth; UV is highly damaging to organisms and causes skin cancer in human beings.

  2. 2

    Cause → the damage has been linked to CFCs, used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers.

  3. 3

    Steps taken → in 1987, the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) forged an agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels; it is now mandatory for all manufacturing companies to make CFC-free refrigerators throughout the world.

  4. Check: the steps act on the cause named in step 2 (CFCs), which is what makes them steps "to limit this damage" ✔.

  5. Answer The ozone layer shields the earth from harmful UV radiation; if it is damaged, more UV reaches the earth and harms organisms — for example, it causes skin cancer in humans. The damage is linked to CFCs. To limit it, UNEP brought about an agreement in 1987 to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels, and it is now mandatory worldwide to make CFC-free refrigerators.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    The ozone layer protects the earth from the Sun's harmful UV radiation; its depletion lets more UV reach the earth, causing e.g. skin cancer. (1) Depletion is caused by CFCs (refrigerants, fire extinguishers). (1) UNEP agreement (1987) to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels; CFC-free refrigerators made mandatory. (1)

    Common trap: "the ozone layer stops global warming". In your textbook its job is to block UV radiation.
