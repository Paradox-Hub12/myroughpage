The whole chapter on one page

Ecosystem — all the interacting organisms of an area together with the non-living constituents of the environment. Biotic components: living organisms. Abiotic components: temperature, rainfall, wind, soil, minerals. Natural ecosystems: forest, pond, lake. Human-made: garden, crop-field, aquarium. Producers (green plants, certain bacteria) make food from inorganic substances using sunlight; consumers — herbivores, carnivores, omnivores, parasites — depend on them directly or indirectly; decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead remains and wastes into simple inorganic substances that return to the soil and are used again by plants. All components are interdependent.

Food chains and webs — a food chain is a series of organisms feeding on one another; each arrow points from the food to the eater, and the chain starts with a producer. Each step is a trophic level: producers (1st), primary consumers/herbivores (2nd), secondary consumers/small carnivores (3rd), tertiary consumers/larger carnivores (4th). The Sun and the decomposers are not levels. Organisms usually have several eaters, so chains join into a food web. Removing a level affects the levels above and below it.

Energy flow — green plants capture about 1 % of the sunlight falling on their leaves. At each step much energy is lost as heat; on average only 10 % passes to the next level (10 per cent law). Energy flow is unidirectional and diminishes at each level; materials are recycled, energy is not. Food chains have only three or four steps; lower levels have more individuals.

Biological magnification — non-degradable chemicals such as some pesticides enter food chains through plants (from soil and water) and accumulate at each trophic level; humans, at the top, get the maximum concentration. Residues in grains, vegetables, fruits and meat cannot always be washed off.

Ozone — OX3\ce{O3}OX3​, three oxygen atoms; a deadly poison, but high up it shields the earth from UV radiation (which causes skin cancer). Formed by UV acting on OX2\ce{O2}OX2​. Ozone dropped sharply in the 1980s, linked to CFCs (refrigerants, fire extinguishers). 1987: UNEP agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels; CFC-free refrigerators mandatory.

Garbage — biodegradable: broken down by biological processes (peels, paper, wood, leather); non-biodegradable: not broken down (plastics, glass, DDT) because enzymes are specific. Non-biodegradable waste persists or harms organisms; biodegradable waste in heaps rots, smells, spreads disease. Lifestyle, disposable items and packaging increase waste. Solutions: separate, compost, recycle, reuse, cloth bags, treat sewage and industrial waste.

Rules and equations

  • Sunlight → producers: producers' energy ≈ 1 % of the sunlight falling on the leaves (÷ 100).
  • One level up: energy at the next level = 10 % (÷ 10). One level down: × 10.
  • Count the steps, not the organisms: a chain of nnn organisms has n−1n - 1n−1 steps.
  • Energy not passed on at a step = energy at that level − energy at the next level (about 90 %).
  • Ozone formation: OX2→UVO+O\ce{O2 ->[UV] O + O}OX2​UV​O+O; then O+OX2→OX3\ce{O + O2 -> O3}O+OX2​​OX3​.

Key definitions

(as in your textbook — learn the words in bold)

  • Ecosystem: all the interacting organisms in an area together with the non-living constituents of the environment.
  • Producers: all green plants and certain bacteria which can produce food by photosynthesis.
  • Consumers: organisms which consume the food produced, either directly from producers or indirectly by feeding on other consumers.
  • Decomposers: microorganisms (bacteria and fungi) that break down the dead remains and waste products of organisms — complex organic substances into simple inorganic substances.
  • Food chain: a series of organisms taking part at various biotic levels, each feeding on the one before it.
  • Trophic level: each step or level of the food chain.
  • Food web: feeding relationships shown as a series of branching lines (many food chains joined) instead of a straight-line chain.
  • Biological magnification: accumulation of non-degradable chemicals progressively at each trophic level of a food chain.
  • Biodegradable: substances that are broken down by biological processes. Non-biodegradable: substances that are not broken down in this manner.
  • Ozone: a molecule formed by three atoms of oxygen (OX3\ce{O3}OX3​).
