CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q71 mark

Question: A fish living in a pond ecosystem feeds on small aquatic insects (larvae). These insect larvae, in turn, feed on green algae and small photosynthetic bacteria. In this specific food chain, what is the trophic level classification of fish?
A. Producer B. Primary Consumer C. Secondary Consumer D. Tertiary Consumer

Given: who eats whom in a pond. Asked: the fish's place in this chain.

Concept used: trophic levels; consumer name = level − 1 (Topic 2). Why it applies: we must first write the chain, then count.

  1. 1

    Producers: green algae and photosynthetic bacteria (they make food by photosynthesis).

  2. 2

    Chain: green algae → insect larvae → fish.

  3. 3

    Count: algae 1st, insect larvae 2nd (primary consumers), fish 3rd — a secondary consumer.

  4. Check: read the arrows: the algae are eaten by the larvae, the larvae by the fish ✔; and the third level is the secondary consumers' level.

    Common trap: choosing "Primary Consumer" because the fish is the first animal you read about in the question. Start counting from the producers.
C. Secondary Consumer
1 mark
CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q91 mark

Question: Assertion (A): In an ecosystem, 1% of energy is available for transfer from one trophic level to the next.
Reason (R): An average of 10% of the food eaten by organisms is available for flow in the form of chemical energy.

Given: an assertion about 1 %; a reason about 10 %. Asked: the correct option.

Concept used: about 1 % of the sunlight falling on leaves is captured; about 10 % passes from one trophic level to the next (Topic 3).

  1. 1

    A → false: the share passed from one trophic level to the next is about 10 %. The 1 % figure is the share of sunlight captured by plants.

  2. 2

    R → true: on average 10 % of the food eaten is turned into the body of the eater and made available to the next level — as the chemical energy of food.

  3. 3

    A false, R true → D.

  4. Check: the two numbers belong to two different steps — sunlight to plants (1 %) and one level to the next (10 %) — so A has used the wrong one ✔.

    Common trap: choosing C because "1 %" sounds familiar. The 1 % is for sunlight to producers, not for one trophic level to the next.
D. A is false but R is true.

CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section A, Q143 marks

Question: Study the food chain given below:
[Figure described: a food chain printed in one line — Phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fishes → large fish.]
A. In the given food chain, the amount of energy available at the fourth trophic level is 10 kJ, what will be the energy available at the producer level?
B. Is it possible to have three more trophic levels in the above food chain? Justify.
C. Which of these trophic levels will have the maximum amount of pesticide, if it had washed down in the water body from agricultural fields. Why?

Given: phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fishes → large fish; level 4 (large fish) = 10 kJ. Asked: energy at the producers; whether three more levels are possible; where a pesticide is most concentrated, and why.

Concept used: the 10 per cent law (Topic 3); very little usable energy after four levels (Topic 3); biological magnification (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    A — levels: phytoplankton 1, zooplankton 2, small fishes 3, large fish 4. From level 4 down to level 1: 3 steps, × 10 each:

    10×10×10×10=10 000 kJ10 \times 10 \times 10 \times 10 = 10\,000\ \text{kJ}10×10×10×10=10000 kJSwipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    B → No. Three more levels would be levels 5, 6 and 7; level 7 would get 1010×10×10=0.01\dfrac{10}{10 \times 10 \times 10} = 0.0110×10×1010​=0.01 kJ. The loss of energy at each step is so great that very little usable energy would be left — not enough to support more levels.

  3. 3

    C → the large fish (the top level). The pesticide is not degradable, so it accumulates at each trophic level — biological magnification.

  4. Check: forward from the producers: 10 000→1000→100→1010\,000 \to 1000 \to 100 \to 1010000→1000→100→10 kJ — the large fish's 10 kJ ✔.

    Common trap: in A, multiplying by 10 four times (four organisms) and getting 100 000 kJ. Four organisms make three steps.
A. 10 000 kJ (10 kJ × 10³). (1)
B. No — the loss of energy at each step is so much that very little usable energy will be left. (1)
C. Large fish, due to biological magnification of the pesticide (it accumulates at each trophic level). (1) — 3 marks (split inferred: the marking scheme gives the three answers and the total only; it writes the unit "KJ")
CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section A, Q61 mark

Question: Which are the correct statements related to ozone?
(i) Ozone layer helps in increasing the UV radiations reaching earth.
(ii) Ozone is a deadly poison.
(iii) Ozone layer shields the earth from UV radiations.
(iv) Ozone layer prevents UV rays which cause skin cancer.
(v) Ozone is formed with the help of Chloroflurocarbons.

Options: A. (i), (ii), (iii) B. (ii), (iii), (iv) C. (iii), (iv), (v) D. (i), (iv), (v)

(Statement (v) is printed "Chloroflurocarbons"; the word is chlorofluorocarbons.)

Given: five statements about ozone. Asked: the correct ones.

Concept used: ozone and the ozone layer (Topic 5).

  1. 1

    (i) false — the layer reduces the UV reaching the earth.

  2. 2

    (ii) true — ozone is a deadly poison.

  3. 3

    (iii) true — it shields the earth from UV.

  4. 4

    (iv) true — it keeps out UV, which causes skin cancer.

  5. 5

    (v) false — ozone forms when UV acts on oxygen; CFCs are linked to its depletion.

  6. 6

    True set: (ii), (iii), (iv) → B.

  7. Check: the only option with none of the false statements (i) and (v) is B ✔.

    Common trap: accepting (v) because CFCs are "connected with ozone". They destroy it; they do not make it.
B. (ii), (iii), (iv)

Question: Which of the following human activities has resulted in an increase of non-biodegradable substances?

Given: four human activities. Asked: the one that increased non-biodegradable substances.

Concept used: changes in packaging have made much of our waste non-biodegradable (Topic 6).

  1. 1

    A, B, D → organic farming, planting trees and composting all involve biodegradable materials; they do not add non-biodegradable waste.

  2. 2

    C → plastic packaging is not broken down by decomposers; your textbook says changes in packaging have made much of our waste non-biodegradable → C.

  3. Check: C is the only option that adds a material from the non-biodegradable column (plastics) ✔.

C. Use of plastic as packaging material.

Question: Assertion (A): Vulture will always have the least amount of pesticides in a food chain.
Reason (R): Vulture occupies the last trophic level and it gets only 10% of energy of the previous trophic level.

Given: an assertion about a vulture and pesticides; a reason about its energy. Asked: the correct option.

Concept used: biological magnification (Topic 4); 10 per cent law (Topic 3).

  1. 1

    A → false: the vulture, at the last trophic level, has the most pesticide — non-degradable chemicals accumulate at each level.

  2. 2

    R → true: it is at the last level and gets only about 10 % of the energy of the level below it.

  3. 3

    A false, R true → D.

  4. Check: energy and pesticide concentration go opposite ways up a chain; A wrongly makes them go the same way ✔.

    Common trap: reasoning "least energy, so least pesticide". Energy is used up at each level; the pesticide is not — it builds up.
D. A is false but R is true.

CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section A, Q122 marks

Question: About 100 acres of forest land was declared as Natural reserve park. The following organisms were predominant in the Natural reserve park:
rabbit, frog, grass, fish, fox, water insects, zebra, peacock, snake, trees, bird, owl, insects, tiger, vulture, duck.
Create a food web comprising two separate food chains with different producers by using the above data.

Given: a list of organisms with two producers (grass, trees). Asked: two food chains with different producers, joined into a food web.

Concept used: food chains and food webs (Topic 2). Why it applies: a web is formed when chains share an organism.

  1. 1

    Chain 1 (producer: grass): grass → insects → frog → snake → owl.

  2. 2

    Chain 2 (producer: trees): trees → insects → bird → owl.

  3. 3

    Join them into a web: the two chains share the insects (which eat both grass and tree leaves) and the owl (which eats both snakes and birds). Draw the two producers side by side at the bottom, with arrows from both to the insects; from the insects, one arrow to the frog and one to the bird; from the frog to the snake; from the snake and from the bird to the owl.

  4. Check: every arrow is a real "is eaten by" link — grass and tree leaves are eaten by insects, insects by frogs and birds, frogs by snakes, snakes and birds by owls ✔; and the two chains start with different producers, as asked.

    Common trap: drawing two chains with the same producer (grass in both). The question asks for different producers.
Chain 1: grass → insects → frog → snake → owl (½)
Chain 2: trees → insects → bird → owl (½)
Food web: the two chains joined at the common organisms (insects, owl), with arrows from food to eater (1) — 2 marks (split inferred: the marking scheme gives the total only)

The marking scheme's own example is "tree, zebra, tiger" with "grass, zebra, tiger", joined at the zebra, and it accepts "any other food chain". Zebras mainly graze on grass, so the answer above uses a tree chain that is easier to defend.

Question: Which of the following group of organisms are not included in ecological food chain?

Given: four groups. Asked: the group left out of food chains.

Concept used: decomposers (saprophytes) act on every level and are not given a level (Topics 1–2).

  1. 1

    Carnivores, herbivores and predators all occupy steps in a food chain.

  2. 2

    Saprophytes — organisms such as fungi and bacteria that feed on dead matter — are the decomposers, which are not placed in the chain → (b).

  3. Check: your textbook names bacteria and other saprophytes as the organisms that break down waste (Topic 6) — the decomposers ✔.

    Common trap: choosing (d) because predators "are not a trophic level". Predators are carnivores — they are in food chains.
(b) Saprophytes

Question: Recycling of paper is a good practice but recycled paper should not be used as food packaging because

Given: recycled paper used to wrap food (new options). Asked: why it is unsuitable.

Concept used: recycling (Topic 6). Why it applies: only a reason about the safety of the food answers the question.

    Options (a), (b) and (d) are not about the food's safety, and are not generally true.

  2. 2

Question: The given graph shows the amount of waste generated, dumped and treated in percentage. Identify the reason of low success rate of waste management process.
[Figure described: a bar graph with a vertical scale from 0 to 70. Above "Total generation" (value printed under it: 62) one tall green bar labelled "Generation", reaching about 62. Above "Total Collected" (value printed: 42.9) two bars: a blue bar "Dumped" reaching about 31 and a short red bar "Treated" reaching about 12.]

Given: a graph labelled "in percentage": total generation 62; total collected 42.9, of which about 31 is dumped and about 12 treated. Asked: the reason the waste-management process has low success.

Concept used: reading a graph; treating waste (Topic 6).

  1. 1

    Read the graph as the question says — in percentage: about 42.9 % of the waste is collected, which is less than 60 % → statement (b) is true.

  2. 2

    Treated: the red bar is small (about 12–15) — only a small share is processed → statement (a) is true.

  3. 3

    Both are reasons for low success → (d).

  4. Check: statement (c) ("more than 60 % is collected") contradicts step 1, so it cannot also be true ✔.

    Common trap: choosing only (a). Low collection is a reason for low success too, so both (a) and (b) count.
(d) both a and b
