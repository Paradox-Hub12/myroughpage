Topic 4 of 6

Biological magnification

About 25 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Describe how pesticides and other chemicals enter food chains.
  • Define biological magnification and explain why the concentration is highest at the top level (human beings).
  • Suggest ways to reduce our intake of pesticides (Activity 13.3).

A farmer sprays a pesticide on his vegetable field to kill insects. Months later, traces of that same chemical turn up in the milk of a cow that grazed nearby, in fish from a pond downstream — and in the people who ate them. The amount in each person is far higher than in the field's soil. How does a chemical climb a food chain and become more concentrated on the way up?

Quick reminder (how chemicals reach soil and water)

Farmers use pesticides and other chemicals to protect crops from diseases and pests. Rain and irrigation water carry some of these chemicals down into the soil and into water bodies — ponds, rivers and lakes — which is one of the ways water gets polluted. (Your textbook refers to Class IX here; this is the idea you need.)

  1. How the chemicals enter a food chain

  • From the soil: the chemicals are absorbed by plants along with water and minerals.
  • From water bodies: they are taken up by aquatic plants and animals.

So the chemicals enter the first trophic level, and every animal that eats those plants, or eats animals that ate them, takes them in too.

  1. Why they pile up at each level

These chemicals are not degradable — they are not broken down in the bodies of organisms. So they get accumulated progressively at each trophic level.

An explanation that helps: an animal eats a great many organisms of the level below it during its life, and the chemical from all of them stays and collects in its body. The animal at the next level eats many of these animals, so it collects even more.

Biological magnification

the increase in the concentration of harmful, non-degradable chemicals (such as some pesticides) at each successive trophic level of a food chain.

  1. Human beings are at the top

Human beings occupy the top level in any food chain, so the maximum concentration of these chemicals gets accumulated in our bodies. That is why food grains such as wheat and rice, vegetables and fruits, and even meat, contain varying amounts of pesticide residues — and these cannot always be removed by washing or other means.

Energy and pesticide go opposite ways up a chain:

Going up a food chain Energy Concentration of a non-degradable pesticide
What happens at each level decreases (only about 10 % passes on) increases (it accumulates)
Where it is greatest producers (first level) top level (for example, human beings)

Worked examples

Worked example 1

Where does the spray end up?

Question. A non-degradable pesticide is sprayed on a vegetable field. In the field, this food chain is found: crop plants → grasshoppers → frogs → snakes → hawk. (a) How does the pesticide enter the food chain? (b) Which organism will have the highest concentration of the pesticide? (c) Give the reason.

Given: a non-degradable pesticide; a five-level chain. Asked: how it enters; where it is most concentrated; why.

Concept used: chemicals enter through plants (from the soil) and accumulate at each trophic level; biological magnification (this topic).

  1. 1

    (a) Entry: some of the spray reaches the soil; the crop plants absorb it from the soil along with water and minerals (some also stays on the leaves). The grasshoppers that eat the plants take it in.

  2. 2

    (b) Highest concentration: the organism at the top level — the hawk.

  3. 3

    (c) Reason: the pesticide is not degradable, so it is not broken down in the organisms' bodies; it accumulates progressively at each trophic level, and the hawk, at the fifth level, collects the most.

  4. Check: compare with energy, which goes the opposite way — the hawk has the least energy but the most pesticide; any answer that puts the most pesticide at the producers' end has reversed the trend ✔.

  5. Answer (a) The plants absorb it from the soil with water and minerals, and it passes to every animal that eats them. (b) The hawk. (c) The pesticide is non-degradable, so it accumulates at each trophic level (biological magnification); the hawk is at the top.

    Common trap: "the grasshoppers have the most because they eat the sprayed leaves directly". Being first to eat it does not give the highest concentration; the chemical keeps building up at each level above.

Worked example 2

The fish and the bird

Question. In a lake polluted with a non-degradable pesticide, the food chain is algae → water fleas → small fish → heron. A test finds more of the pesticide in each gram of the heron's body than in each gram of the small fish. Explain this result.

Given: a four-level lake chain; pesticide concentration higher in the heron than in the fish. Asked: why.

Concept used: biological magnification (this topic).

  1. 1

    Entry: the algae take up the pesticide from the water.

  2. 2

    Build-up: the water fleas eat many algae, the small fish eat many water fleas, and the heron eats many small fish. The chemical is not broken down at any stage.

  3. 3

    Result: at each step the chemical collects in a smaller number of larger animals, so its concentration rises — the heron, at the top (fourth level), has the highest.

  4. Check: list the chain from the bottom: algae (1) → water fleas (2) → small fish (3) → heron (4). The heron's number is the largest, so it must have the highest concentration; an answer naming the fish or the algae puts the most pesticide at a lower level ✔.

  5. Answer The pesticide is non-degradable, so it is not broken down in any organism. It accumulates at each trophic level as each animal eats many organisms of the level below. The heron is at the top of this chain (fourth level), so it has the highest concentration — this is biological magnification.

    Common trap: "the heron has more because it is bigger". Concentration means the amount in each gram; it is higher because the heron is higher in the food chain, not because it is larger.

Which statement about pesticide residues in our food is correct?

Common mistakes!

  • "The top level gets the least pesticide because it gets the least energy."

    Energy decreases up a chain, but a non-degradable chemical accumulates — it increases.

    Energy: most at the bottom. Pesticide concentration: most at the top.

  • "Washing removes all the pesticide from food."

    Residues get inside the grains, fruits and meat and cannot always be removed by washing.

    Washing helps, but the real answer is to use less of such chemicals.

  • Confusing biological magnification with the energy pyramid.

    Magnification is about the concentration of chemicals, not about energy.

    Define it with the words non-degradable, accumulate and each trophic level.

Key points

  • Pesticides and other chemicals used on crops are washed into the soil and water bodies; plants absorb them from the soil with water and minerals, and aquatic plants and animals take them up from water.
  • These chemicals are not degradable, so they accumulate progressively at each trophic level — biological magnification.
  • Human beings occupy the top level in any food chain, so the maximum concentration collects in our bodies.
  • Wheat, rice, vegetables, fruits and meat contain pesticide residues, which cannot always be removed by washing.
  • Going up a chain, energy decreases but the concentration of non-degradable chemicals increases.
