Topic 5 of 6

The ozone layer and its depletion

About 25 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Describe ozone (O3), how it forms at higher levels of the atmosphere, and its role as a shield against UV radiation.
  • Explain the cause of ozone depletion (CFCs) and the steps taken to limit it.

Look at the back of a new refrigerator or air conditioner and you may find the words "CFC-free". Why would anyone print that on a fridge? The answer lies high above your head, in a thin layer of a gas that is poisonous to breathe — yet without it, life on land would suffer badly.

Quick reminder (Chapter 1)

The oxygen we breathe is made of molecules of two oxygen atoms, OX2\ce{O2}OX2​. A free oxygen atom is written O. In an equation, the substances on the left react to give the substances on the right; a condition written on the arrow (such as UV) is what makes the reaction happen.

  1. What is ozone?

Ozone (OX3\ce{O3}OX3​) is a molecule formed by three atoms of oxygen.

  • Ordinary oxygen, OX2\ce{O2}OX2​, is needed by every organism that respires aerobically.
  • Ozone is a deadly poison if breathed in.
  • Yet high up in the atmosphere, ozone does a vital job: it shields the earth's surface from the Sun's ultraviolet (UV) radiation.

  1. Why the shield matters

UV radiation harms living things badly — in human beings, for example, it can cause skin cancer. The ozone layer stops much of it from reaching the ground, so it protects people, animals and plants — the whole ecosystem.

  1. How ozone forms high up

High in the atmosphere, ozone is made by UV radiation acting on oxygen (OX2\ce{O2}OX2​) molecules:

  1. The higher-energy UV radiation splits some OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ molecules into free oxygen atoms:
OX2→UVO+O\ce{O2 ->[UV] O + O}OX2​UV​O+OSwipe to see the whole equation →
  1. These atoms combine with molecular oxygen to form ozone:
O+OX2→OX3\ce{O + O2 -> O3}O+OX2​​OX3​Swipe to see the whole equation →

  1. Ozone depletion — and what was done about it

  • In the 1980s the amount of ozone started falling sharply.
  • The fall has been linked to chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) — human-made chemicals used as refrigerants (for example in refrigerators) and in fire extinguishers.
  • In 1987, the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) brought countries to an agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels.
  • Today every manufacturer in the world must make CFC-free refrigerators.

Worked example 1

Reading the ozone equations

Question. Look at the two equations for the formation of ozone in the upper atmosphere. (a) What does the UV radiation do in the first step? (b) What combines in the second step? (c) How many oxygen atoms are there in one molecule of ozone?

Given: OX2→UVO+O\ce{O2 ->[UV] O + O}OX2​UV​O+O and O+OX2→OX3\ce{O + O2 -> O3}O+OX2​​OX3​. Asked: the job of UV; what combines; atoms in ozone.

Concept used: formation of ozone (this topic); reading an equation (Chapter 1).

  1. 1

    (a) Step 1: the UV radiation splits a molecule of oxygen (OX2\ce{O2}OX2​) into two free oxygen atoms.

  2. 2

    (b) Step 2: a free oxygen atom combines with a molecule of oxygen (OX2\ce{O2}OX2​).

  3. 3

    (c) Count the atoms in OX3\ce{O3}OX3​: the subscript 3 means three oxygen atoms.

  4. Check: count atoms in step 2 — left side: 1 (in O) + 2 (in OX2\ce{O2}OX2​) = 3; right side: 3 in OX3\ce{O3}OX3​ ✔.

  5. Answer (a) It splits OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ into free oxygen atoms. (b) A free oxygen atom and an oxygen molecule. (c) Three.

    Common trap: writing that ozone is "ordinary oxygen". Oxygen gas is OX2\ce{O2}OX2​ (two atoms); ozone is OX3\ce{O3}OX3​ (three atoms) and is poisonous to breathe.

Worked example 2

Harmful near us, helpful up high

Question. A news report says that ozone near the ground in a crowded city is harmful, while another says that the loss of ozone is a serious danger. Explain how both can be true.

Given: ozone near the ground; ozone high in the atmosphere. Asked: why one is harmful and the other helpful.

Concept used: ozone is a deadly poison; at higher levels it shields the earth from UV (this topic).

  1. 1

    Near the ground: we would breathe the ozone, and ozone is a deadly poison — so it is harmful there.

  2. 2

    High in the atmosphere: nobody breathes it, and it performs an essential function: it shields the earth's surface from the Sun's UV radiation.

  3. 3

    Losing the high ozone lets more UV reach the ground, which damages organisms (for example, causing skin cancer).

  4. Check: both statements describe the same gas in different places; swap the places and neither statement makes sense any more ✔.

  5. Answer Ozone is poisonous to breathe, so near the ground it is harmful. High in the atmosphere it forms a layer that shields the earth from UV radiation, which causes skin cancer; losing that ozone is therefore dangerous. Both statements are true because the gas is in two different places.

    Common trap: "the ozone layer keeps the earth warm". Its job in your textbook is to block UV radiation, not to keep heat in.

Worked example 3

Why "CFC-free"?

Question. Ravi's family buys a new refrigerator labelled "CFC-free". (a) What are CFCs, and what were they used for? (b) Why should refrigerators be CFC-free? (c) What international step was taken about CFCs?

Given: a "CFC-free" refrigerator. Asked: what CFCs are and their uses; why avoid them; the international step.

Concept used: ozone depletion (this topic).

  1. 1

    (a) CFCs = chlorofluorocarbons, synthetic chemicals used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers.

  2. 2

    (b) Reason: the sharp fall in ozone since the 1980s has been linked to CFCs; less ozone means more UV radiation reaches the earth.

  3. 3

    (c) International step: in 1987, UNEP brought about an agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels; now all manufacturers must make CFC-free refrigerators.

  4. Check: cover the question and say the chain aloud: CFCs → less ozone → more UV → skin cancer. If any arrow is reversed (for example "CFCs make ozone"), the sentence becomes false ✔.

  5. Answer (a) Chlorofluorocarbons — synthetic chemicals used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers. (b) CFCs deplete the ozone layer, which shields the earth from harmful UV radiation. (c) In 1987 the UNEP agreement froze CFC production at 1986 levels; making CFC-free refrigerators is now mandatory worldwide.

    Common trap: "CFCs help make ozone". UV acting on oxygen makes ozone; CFCs are linked to its destruction.

Common mistakes!

  • "Ozone is good everywhere" or "ozone is just oxygen".

    Ozone (OX3\ce{O3}OX3​) is a deadly poison to breathe; it is useful only high up, as a shield.

    Say where: high up — protective shield; near us — poisonous.

  • "The ozone layer keeps heat in."

    Its role is to shield the earth from UV radiation.

    Ozone layer → blocks UV → prevents damage such as skin cancer.

  • "CFCs make ozone" or "carbon monoxide/methane are the main cause of depletion."

    Ozone is made by UV acting on OX2\ce{O2}OX2​; the depletion has been linked to CFCs.

    Cause of depletion: CFCs (refrigerants, fire extinguishers).

Key points

  • Ozone (OX3\ce{O3}OX3​) is a molecule of three oxygen atoms. It is a deadly poison, but at higher levels of the atmosphere it shields the earth from the Sun's UV radiation.
  • UV radiation is highly damaging to organisms; it can cause skin cancer in human beings.
  • Ozone forms high up: OX2→UVO+O\ce{O2 ->[UV] O + O}OX2​UV​O+O, then O+OX2→OX3\ce{O + O2 -> O3}O+OX2​​OX3​.
  • The amount of ozone began to drop sharply in the 1980s; the decrease is linked to CFCs, used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers.
  • In 1987, UNEP brought about an agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels; making CFC-free refrigerators is now mandatory throughout the world.
