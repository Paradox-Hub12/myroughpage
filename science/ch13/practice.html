Full solution

Given: energy in an ecosystem. Asked: the flow; why it is one-way.

Concept used: flow of energy (Topic 3; Fig 13.4).

  1. 1

    Flow: Sun → producer → herbivore → carnivore.

  2. 2

    Capture: producers capture about 1 % of the sunlight falling on their leaves and store it as chemical energy in food.

  3. 3

    Passing on: at each step only about 10 % passes to the next level; the rest is used in life activities and lost as heat.

  4. 4

    Why one-way: the energy captured by the autotrophs does not revert to the Sun, and the energy passed to the herbivores does not come back to the autotrophs; as it moves up, it is no longer available to the previous level. Also, the energy gets less at each higher level, so it cannot flow back down.

  5. Check: the diagram's arrows all point the same way — from Sunlight up to the top carnivores — with no arrow returning ✔.

  6. Answer Energy flows Sun → producers → herbivores → carnivores (→ top carnivores). The flow is unidirectional because energy passed to a higher level never returns to the level before it — the energy captured by plants does not go back to the Sun, and the herbivores' energy does not go back to the plants. At each level much energy is lost as heat, so the available energy decreases at every higher level.
