The whole chapter on one page

Atoms and ions — shells fill 2, 8, 8 (elements 1–20). Metals (1–3 valence electrons) lose electrons → positive ions (NaX+\ce{Na+}NaX+, MgX2+\ce{Mg^2+}MgX2+, AlX3+\ce{Al^3+}AlX3+); non-metals (5–7) gain electrons → negative ions (ClX−\ce{Cl-}ClX−, OX2−\ce{O^2-}OX2−, SX2−\ce{S^2-}SX2−). Formula: criss-cross the charges.

Physical properties — metals: lustrous, hard, malleable (sheets), ductile (wires), good conductors of heat and electricity, sonorous, high melting points. Non-metals: dull, brittle, poor conductors; solids or gases. Best heat conductors: silver, copper; poor: lead, mercury. Exceptions: mercury (liquid metal); gallium, caesium (melt in the palm); Li, Na, K (soft, cut with a knife); iodine (lustrous non-metal); diamond (hardest natural substance); graphite (conducts electricity); bromine (liquid non-metal).

With oxygen — metal + oxygen → metal oxide (basic); AlX2OX3\ce{Al2O3}AlX2​OX3​ and ZnO are amphoteric; NaX2O\ce{Na2O}NaX2​O, KX2O\ce{K2O}KX2​O dissolve to give alkalis. Non-metal oxides are acidic or neutral (CO). K, Na catch fire in air → kept under kerosene; Mg, Al, Zn, Pb have a protective oxide layer; Fe filings burn; Cu forms black CuO; Ag, Au do not react.

With water — cold: K, Na (violent, HX2\ce{H2}HX2​ catches fire), Ca (less violent, floats) · hot: Mg (floats) · steam: Al, Zn, Fe (give oxides) · none: Pb, Cu, Ag, Au.

With acids — metals above hydrogen give salt + hydrogen (Mg > Al > Zn > Fe); Cu does not react with dilute HCl. Nitric acid usually gives no hydrogen (strong oxidising agent) — except Mg and Mn with very dilute HNOX3\ce{HNO3}HNOX3​.

Reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au. A metal displaces any metal below it from its salt solution. Practical 3: Al > Zn > Fe > Cu.

Ionic compounds — formed by transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal; ions held by strong electrostatic forces. Hard, brittle solids; high melting and boiling points; soluble in water, insoluble in kerosene and petrol; conduct when molten or dissolved, not as solids.

Metallurgy — mineral; ore (profitable); gangue (impurities). Ore → enrichment → by position: top (K–Al) electrolysis of molten chloride/oxide; middle (Zn, Fe, Pb) roasting (sulphide, excess air) or calcination (carbonate, limited air) → oxide → reduction (carbon, or a more reactive metal — thermit); bottom (Cu, Hg) heat the sulphide in air (copper oxide can also be reduced by carbon); Au, Ag found free. Electrolytic refining: impure anode, pure cathode, salt solution electrolyte, anode mud.

Corrosion — silver → black silver sulphide; copper → green basic copper carbonate; iron → brown flaky rust. Rusting needs air and water. Prevention: painting, oiling, greasing, galvanising (zinc — protects even when scratched), chrome plating, anodising (aluminium), alloying.

Alloys — homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal. Steel (Fe + C), stainless steel (Fe + Ni + Cr), brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn), solder (Pb + Sn, low melting point), amalgam (contains Hg). Alloys have lower conductivity and melting points than pure metals.

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Malleable: can be beaten into thin sheets. Ductile: can be drawn into thin wires. Sonorous: rings when struck.
  • Amphoteric oxide: a metal oxide that reacts with both acids and bases to give salt and water (AlX2OX3\ce{Al2O3}AlX2​OX3​, ZnO).
  • Reactivity series: a list of metals in order of decreasing reactivity.
  • Ionic (electrovalent) compound: formed by the transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal.
  • Mineral: a naturally occurring element or compound in the earth's crust. Ore: a mineral from which a metal can be extracted profitably. Gangue: impurities in an ore.
  • Metallurgy: extracting metals from their ores and refining them for use.
  • Roasting: heating a sulphide ore strongly in excess air. Calcination: heating a carbonate ore strongly in limited air.
  • Corrosion: the slow attack on a metal's surface by moist air and other substances. Rust: the brown flaky coating on iron.
  • Galvanising: coating iron or steel with a thin layer of zinc. Alloy: a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal. Amalgam: an alloy containing mercury.
