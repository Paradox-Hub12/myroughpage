Topic 4 of 7

Metals with acids and salt solutions; the reactivity series

About 55 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Write equations for metals with dilute acids; explain why copper gives no hydrogen and why nitric acid usually gives no hydrogen (except with magnesium and manganese in very dilute acid).
  • Predict whether a displacement reaction will happen from the reactivity series, and describe what is seen.
  • Recall the reactivity series (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, [H], Cu, Hg, Ag, Au) and use it to rank metals from experimental results.

Drop a shiny iron nail into blue copper sulphate solution and come back after twenty minutes: the nail wears a brown coat and the blue has faded to pale green. Now put a copper wire into pale green iron sulphate solution: nothing happens, however long you wait. Same two metals, opposite results. By the end of this topic you will be able to predict results like these for any pair of common metals — from one list.

Quick reminder (Chapters 1 and 2)

Metal + dilute acid → salt + hydrogen (hydrogen burns with a pop). In a displacement reaction, a more reactive element pushes a less reactive one out of its compound.

  1. Metals with dilute acids

Lab: metals in dilute hydrochloric acid (NCERT Activity 3.11)

Lab notebook · do → see → shows
Do
Rub samples of magnesium, aluminium, zinc, iron and copper clean with sandpaper. Put each in a test tube of dilute hydrochloric acid with a thermometer dipping in the acid. (Sodium and potassium are never used — they react violently even with cold water.)
See
Bubbles form fastest with magnesium, and its temperature rises most. Aluminium, zinc and iron bubble more and more slowly. With copper there are no bubbles and no change in temperature.
Shows
Reactivity with dilute acids decreases in the order Mg > Al > Zn > Fe. Copper does not react with dilute HCl.

Why copper gives no hydrogen. A metal can push hydrogen out of an acid only if it is more reactive than hydrogen. Copper, mercury, silver and gold are less reactive than hydrogen, so they give no hydrogen with dilute hydrochloric or sulphuric acid.

Nitric acid is a special case. Metals usually do not give hydrogen with nitric acid. Being a strong oxidising agent, nitric acid turns any hydrogen formed into water and is itself reduced to oxides of nitrogen (NX2O\ce{N2O}NX2​O, NO or NOX2\ce{NO2}NOX2​). The exceptions are magnesium and manganese, which give hydrogen with very dilute nitric acid:

Mg+2 HNOX3→Mg(NOX3)X2+HX2\ce{Mg + 2HNO3 -> Mg(NO3)2 + H2}Mg+2HNOX3​​Mg(NOX3​)X2​+HX2​Swipe to see the whole equation →
Did you know?Not in board exam
NCERT information box — not examined on its own, but you need it to answer NCERT Exercise Question 15

Aqua regia ("royal water" in Latin) is a freshly made mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid in the ratio 3 : 1. It dissolves gold and platinum, although neither acid can do this alone. It is a highly corrosive, fuming liquid.

  1. Metals with salt solutions: displacement

Lab: copper and iron swap places — or don't (NCERT Activity 3.12)

Lab notebook · do → see → shows
Do
Put a clean copper wire in iron(II) sulphate solution, and a clean iron nail in copper(II) sulphate solution. Wait 20 minutes.
See
The iron nail gets a reddish-brown coating and the blue solution turns pale green. The copper wire and the iron sulphate solution show no change.
Shows
Iron displaces copper from copper sulphate, so iron is more reactive than copper. Copper cannot displace iron.
Fe(s)+CuSOX4(aq)→FeSOX4(aq)+Cu(s)\ce{Fe(s) + CuSO4(aq) -> FeSO4(aq) + Cu(s)}Fe(s)+CuSOX4​(aq)​FeSOX4​(aq)+Cu(s)Swipe to see the whole equation →

The rule, in words:

Metal A + salt solution of metal B → salt solution of A + metal B — only if A is more reactive than B.

Displacement is the best test of reactivity, because it works even for metals that do not react with water or acids.

  1. The reactivity series

Putting the results of all these experiments together gives a list of metals in order of decreasing reactivity — the reactivity series (or activity series):

Metal
K Potassium most reactive
Na Sodium
Ca Calcium
Mg Magnesium
Al Aluminium
Zn Zinc reactivity decreases ↓
Fe Iron
Pb Lead
[H] [Hydrogen] (not a metal — included as a reference point)
Cu Copper
Hg Mercury
Ag Silver
Au Gold least reactive

Memory aid (initials of the English names): Please Send Cats, Monkeys And Zebras Into Large Hot Cages; Make Sure Gates (are shut) → Potassium, Sodium, Calcium, Magnesium, Aluminium, Zinc, Iron, Lead, Hydrogen, Copper, Mercury, Silver, Gold.

One ladder, many uses. Think of the series as a ladder. The higher a metal stands:

Higher on the ladder means… Example
reacts more vigorously with oxygen K and Na catch fire in air; Ag and Au never react
reacts with colder water K, Na, Ca: cold water · Mg: hot water · Al, Zn, Fe: steam · Pb and below: none
gives hydrogen with dilute acids if above [H] Mg, Al, Zn, Fe give hydrogen; Cu, Hg, Ag, Au do not
displaces every metal below it from its salt solution Zn displaces Cu; Cu cannot displace Zn
is harder to extract from its ore Topic 6

Worked example 1

Three metals, three acids

Question. Predict what happens and write a balanced equation where a reaction occurs: (a) aluminium foil (cleaned with sandpaper) in dilute sulphuric acid; (b) magnesium ribbon in very dilute nitric acid; (c) copper turnings in dilute hydrochloric acid.

Given: three metal–acid pairs. Asked: reaction or not; equations.

Concept used: metals above hydrogen give salt + hydrogen with dilute hydrochloric and sulphuric acids; nitric acid gives hydrogen only with Mg and Mn when very dilute. Why it applies: each case tests one of these rules.

    (a)

    Aluminium is above hydrogen → it gives aluminium sulphate and hydrogen.

  2. 2

    Salt: AlX3+\ce{Al^3+}AlX3+ and SOX4X2−\ce{SO4^2-}SOX4​X2− → AlX2(SOX4)X3\ce{Al2(SO4)3}AlX2​(SOX4​)X3​. Skeletal: Al+HX2SOX4→AlX2(SOX4)X3+HX2\ce{Al + H2SO4 -> Al2(SO4)3 + H2}Al+HX2​SOX4​​AlX2​(SOX4​)X3​+HX2​.

  4. 4

  5. 5

    Hydrogen: left now 6, right 2 → put 3 in front of HX2\ce{H2}HX2​.

Worked example 2

Will it displace?

Question. Predict whether a reaction takes place. Write the balanced equation where it does. (i) zinc + copper(II) sulphate solution; (ii) silver + copper(II) sulphate solution; (iii) lead + silver nitrate solution; (iv) iron + zinc sulphate solution.

Given: four metal + salt-solution pairs. Asked: yes/no, with reason; equations.

Concept used: a metal displaces another from its salt solution only if it is higher in the reactivity series. Why it applies: each pair is a metal and a salt of a different metal.

  1. 1

    (i) Zn is above Cu → yes.

    Zn(s)+CuSOX4(aq)→ZnSOX4(aq)+Cu(s)\ce{Zn(s) + CuSO4(aq) -> ZnSO4(aq) + Cu(s)}Zn(s)+CuSOX4​(aq)​ZnSOX4​(aq)+Cu(s)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  2. 2

    (ii) Ag is below Cu → no reaction.

  3. 3

    (iii) Pb is above Ag → yes. Silver nitrate is AgNOX3\ce{AgNO3}AgNOX3​; lead nitrate is Pb(NOX3)X2\ce{Pb(NO3)2}Pb(NOX3​)X2​. Skeletal: Pb+AgNOX3→Pb(NOX3)X2+Ag\ce{Pb + AgNO3 -> Pb(NO3)2 + Ag}Pb+AgNOX3​​Pb(NOX3​)X2​+Ag.

  4. 4

    Nitrate: left 1, right 2 → put 2 in front of AgNOX3\ce{AgNO3}AgNOX3​.

  5. 5

    Silver: left now 2, right 1 → put 2 in front of Ag.

    Pb(s)+2 AgNOX3(aq)→Pb(NOX3)X2(aq)+2 Ag(s)\ce{Pb(s) + 2AgNO3(aq) -> Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2Ag(s)}Pb(s)+2AgNOX3​(aq)​Pb(NOX3​)X2​(aq)+2Ag(s)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  6. 6

    (iv) Fe is below Zn → no reaction.

  7. Check: (i) Zn 1, Cu 1, S 1, O 4 on each side ✔. (iii) Pb 1 | 1, Ag 2 | 2, N 2 | 2, O 6 | 6 ✔. The two "no" cases are the reverse of reactions that do happen (Cu would displace Ag; Zn would displace Fe) ✔.

  8. Answer (i) yes — Zn+CuSOX4→ZnSOX4+Cu\ce{Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu}Zn+CuSOX4​​ZnSOX4​+Cu (ii) no (iii) yes — Pb+2 AgNOX3→Pb(NOX3)X2+2 Ag\ce{Pb + 2AgNO3 -> Pb(NO3)2 + 2Ag}Pb+2AgNOX3​​Pb(NOX3​)X2​+2Ag (iv) no

    Common trap: Reading the pair the wrong way round. Ask: "Is the free metal higher than the metal in the salt?" Only then does displacement happen.

Worked example 3

Ranking four mystery metals

Question. Four metals P, Q, R and S were each placed in solutions of the salts of the other three. The results:

  1. Metal Salt of P Salt of Q Salt of R Salt of S
    P — displaced displaced no reaction
    Q no reaction — no reaction no reaction
    R no reaction displaced — no reaction
    S displaced displaced displaced —

    Arrange the metals in order of decreasing reactivity. Which metal could be copper and which zinc, if the four are zinc, iron, copper and silver?

    Given: a displacement table. Asked: reactivity order; matching to real metals.

    Concept used: the more metals a metal displaces, the higher it is in the series. Why it applies: each "displaced" means the row metal is more reactive than the column metal.

  2. 1

    Count the displacements in each row: P 2, Q 0, R 1, S 3.

  3. 2

    S displaces all three → most reactive.

  4. 3

    P displaces Q and R but not S → second.

  5. 4

    R displaces only Q → third.

  6. 5

    Q displaces none → least reactive.

  7. 6

    Order: S > P > R > Q.

  8. 7

    Matching to Zn > Fe > Cu > Ag: S = zinc, P = iron, R = copper, Q = silver.

  9. Check: every "no reaction" agrees with this order — P cannot displace S, R cannot displace P or S, Q displaces nothing ✔.

  10. Answer S > P > R > Q; S = zinc, R = copper.

    Common trap: Counting the columns (how often a metal is displaced) and reversing the order. Count down each row — that is what the metal itself can do.

Quick check · 3 of 5Short answer2 marks

Zinc gives hydrogen with dilute sulphuric acid, but neither copper with dilute sulphuric acid nor zinc with nitric acid gives hydrogen. Explain both.

Answer: Copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series, so it cannot displace hydrogen from the acid. Nitric acid is a strong oxidising agent: it oxidises the hydrogen formed to water and is itself reduced to oxides of nitrogen.

Two different reasons: copper fails because of its position; nitric acid fails because of what the acid does to hydrogen.

Lab check 2. Zinc granules are left in copper sulphate solution. Which set of observations is correct?

The blue fades because copper ions are used up; nothing silvery forms.
Copper is displaced (reddish-brown) and the blue copper ions are replaced by colourless zinc ions.

Common mistakes!

  • "Copper displaces iron from iron sulphate."

    Copper is below iron.

    Free metal must be higher than the metal in the salt.

  • Order Zn > Al or Fe > Zn

    Al is above Zn; Zn is above Fe.

    Learn the ladder with the memory aid: …Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, [H], Cu…

  • Forgetting hydrogen's place

    [H] sits between lead and copper.

    Above [H] → hydrogen from dilute acids; below → none.

  • "Copper reacts with dilute HCl to give hydrogen."

    Copper is below hydrogen.

    No reaction.

  • "All metals give hydrogen with nitric acid."

    Nitric acid oxidises the hydrogen to water.

    Only Mg and Mn, with very dilute nitric acid.

  • Fe+CuSOX4→FeX2(SOX4)X3+Cu\ce{Fe + CuSO4 -> Fe2(SO4)3 + Cu}Fe+CuSOX4​​FeX2​(SOX4​)X3​+Cu

    Iron gives iron(II) here, and the equation is not balanced.

    Fe+CuSOX4→FeSOX4+Cu\ce{Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu}Fe+CuSOX4​​FeSOX4​+Cu.

  • Iron + copper sulphate: "solution turns colourless"

    Iron(II) sulphate is pale green.

    Blue → pale green (iron); blue → colourless (zinc, aluminium).

Key points

  • Metals above hydrogen give salt + hydrogen with dilute HCl and HX2SOX4\ce{H2SO4}HX2​SOX4​ (Mg > Al > Zn > Fe); Cu, Hg, Ag, Au do not.
  • Nitric acid usually gives no hydrogen (strong oxidising agent); only Mg and Mn do, with very dilute HNOX3\ce{HNO3}HNOX3​.
  • A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one from its salt solution — never the other way round.
  • Reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au.
  • Practical 3 result: Al > Zn > Fe > Cu.
