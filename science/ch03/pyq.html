CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section B, Q231 mark

Question: Electrical wires made of aluminum

Given: aluminium wires. Asked: whether they corrode easily, and why.

Concept used: aluminium is reactive, but at room temperature it is covered by a thin protective oxide layer that prevents further oxidation (Topic 3). Why it applies: the options test both the observation and the reason.

  1. 1

    Aluminium wires and utensils last for years → they do not corrode easily. This rules out A and B.

  2. 2

    The reason is the thin, tough layer of aluminium oxide that forms on the surface and protects the metal → C.

  3. 3

    D gives a false comparison: aluminium is more reactive than zinc (Al > Zn in the series), so "less reactive than zinc" is wrong.

  4. Check: C agrees with the textbook statement that magnesium, aluminium, zinc and lead are covered by a protective oxide layer ✔.

    Why the other options are wrong: A ignores the oxide layer; B describes rust on iron (porous and flaky), not aluminium oxide; D misplaces aluminium below zinc.
    Common trap: Choosing A because "aluminium is a very reactive metal". It is — but its oxide layer protects it.
C. do not corrode easily as aluminium oxide forms a protective layer.
1 mark
CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section B, Q284 marks

Question: Aavya and Vihan are studying the properties of metals in school. They decide to design and conduct an experiment to test the relative conduction of heat by metals. They use the apparatus (Figure) with rods of five different metals aluminium, silver, copper, iron and zinc attached to a central point. This central point is heated using a burner. The metal rods are of equal length and thickness. They observe that the candles at the end of the rods fall at different times. (Study the graph)

[Figure described: five metal rods radiating from a central point heated by a burner, each with a candle stuck at its far end. Bar graph "Time take for the candle to drop" — time in seconds: silver 10, copper 15, aluminium 30, zinc 50, iron 60.]

A. On the basis of the graph obtained, which metal is the best conductor of heat?
B. Gold is a better conductor of heat than aluminium but it is not as good as copper. The time taken for the candle to drop if one of the rods were made of gold would have been:
(i) 15 s (ii) 20 s (iii) 35 s (iv) 10 s
Attempt either sub-part C or D.
C. The length of all the metal rods is increased by 20% without any change in the thickness. Will the time required for the candles to drop be affected? How and why?
OR
D. Mayank tries the experiment with an apparatus similar to figure (1). He ensures the length of all metal rods is the same. However, the thickness of rods is not the same in his apparatus. Will the inference he draws on the basis of his experiment be correct? Justify your answer.

Given: times for the candle to fall from equal rods: Ag 10 s, Cu 15 s, Al 30 s, Zn 50 s, Fe 60 s. Asked: best conductor; time for gold; effect of longer rods, or of unequal thickness.

Concept used: metals conduct heat; the better the conductor, the sooner heat reaches the far end and melts the wax holding the candle (Topic 2, as in NCERT Activity 3.5). Why it applies: a shorter time means faster conduction.

  1. 1

    A

    The candle on the silver rod fell first (10 s) → silver conducts heat best (as the textbook says: silver and copper are the best conductors).

  2. 2

    B

    Gold conducts better than aluminium → its candle falls sooner than 30 s.

  3. 3

    Gold conducts less well than copper → its candle falls later than 15 s.

  4. 4

    The only option between 15 s and 30 s is (ii) 20 s.

  5. 5

    C

    Heat is passed along a rod by its particles vibrating and passing energy to their neighbours (conduction).

  6. 6

    A longer rod means the heat must travel a longer distance → every candle takes more time to fall.

  7. 7

    All rods are lengthened equally, so the order in which the candles fall stays the same.

  8. 8

    D

    For a fair test, only one thing (the metal) may change; all the rods' dimensions must be the same.

  9. 9

    A thicker rod takes more time to conduct heat to its end, so differences in time could be due to thickness, not the metal.

  10. 10

    So Mayank's inference will not be correct.

  11. Check: the graph order Ag < Cu < Al < Zn < Fe (time) matches the textbook: silver and copper best, iron poorer ✔; 20 s lies strictly between 15 s and 30 s ✔.

    Common trap: In B, choosing 35 s by thinking "a better conductor takes longer". Better conductor → less time.
A. Silver
1 mark
B. (ii) 20 s
1 mark
C. Yes, the time required for the candles to drop will increase for all the metals. The order of falling of candles will remain the same. Particles vibrate and transfer heat to one another in conduction; heat has to travel a longer distance, so conduction from one end to the other takes more time.
2 marks
OR D. No, if the thickness of the rods is changed the inference will not be correct. The dimensions of the rods have to be the same. A thicker rod will take more time to conduct heat.

The other end of the rod is heated and time required for the heat sensor to beep is noted for all metal rods. The heat sensor at the end of B rod beeps in 10 seconds, C rod in 15 seconds, A a 30 seconds, E in 50 seconds and D sensor beeps in 60 seconds.
A. On the basis of the experimental result, which metal is the best conductor of heat?
B. Metal G is a better conductor of heat than metal A but it is not as good as metal C. The time taken for the sensor to beep if one of the rods were made of G would have been:
(i) 15 s (ii) 20 s (iii) 35 s (iv) 10 s
Attempt either sub-part C or D.
C. The length of all the metal rods is increased by 20% without any change in the thickness. Will the time required for the sensor to beep be affected? How and why?
OR
D. Mayank tries the experiment with little variation. He keeps the length of all the rods of the same length, but, the thickness of rods is not the same. Will the inference he draws on the basis of his experiment be correct? Justify your answer.

Given: beep times B 10 s, C 15 s, A 30 s, E 50 s, D 60 s. Asked: as in the main version.

Concept used: shorter time = better conductor of heat (Topic 2).

  1. 1

    A: B's sensor beeps first (10 s) → metal B is the best conductor.

  2. 2

    B: G is better than A (so less than 30 s) but not as good as C (so more than 15 s) → (ii) 20 s.

  3. 3

    C: longer rods → heat travels further → every sensor takes longer; the order stays the same.

  4. 4

    D: unequal thickness → not a fair test (a thicker rod takes longer) → the inference will not be correct.

  5. Check: ordering the times gives B > C > A > E > D as conductors; 20 s fits between C (15 s) and A (30 s) ✔.

    Common trap: Reading the letters in alphabetical order. Rank by time, shortest first.

Question: The following activity is set-up in the science lab by the teacher. He clamped an aluminium wire on a stand and fixed a pin to the free end of the wire using wax. Then he heated the wire with a burner from the end where the wire is clamped. Students observed the pin fall off.
[Figure described: an aluminium wire clamped to a stand, a pin fixed with wax at its free end, and a burner heating the wire near the clamp.]
A. If the teacher replaces aluminium wire by silver wire, will the students' observation change? Justify your answer.
B. Will the aluminium wire melt? Give reason for your answer.

Given: NCERT Activity 3.5 with an aluminium wire; the pin falls. Asked: effect of using silver; whether the wire melts.

Concept used: metals are good conductors of heat (silver is among the best) and have high melting points (Topic 2).

  1. 1

    A

    Silver also conducts heat, so the wax will still melt and the pin will still fall.

  2. 2

    Silver is a better conductor than aluminium → heat reaches the wax sooner → the pin falls in less time.

  3. 3

    B

    A spirit lamp or burner melts the wax, but metals have high melting points → the aluminium wire does not melt.

  4. Check: this matches the 2026-27 graph above, where silver's candle (10 s) falls well before aluminium's (30 s) ✔.

    Common trap: In A, answering "no change" — the pin still falls, but the time changes.
A. The pin will drop but will take less time to drop, because silver is a better conductor of heat than aluminium. — 1 mark (split inferred)
B. No, the aluminium wire will not melt, because metals have high melting points. — 1 mark (split inferred)

CBSE SQP 2025-26, Section B, Q26 option B3 marks

Question: B. The domes of many building in Europe are made of copper. These domes now appear greenish in colour.
(i) Why do the domes appear greenish though copper is orange-red in colour?
(ii) In your opinion, should the copper domes be replaced by iron domes to overcome the problem of change of colour of copper domes?
(iii) Domes used to be made from thin sheets of metals. Why did the ancient architects use copper to make domes?

Given: green copper domes. Asked: (i) why green; (ii) should iron replace copper; (iii) why copper was chosen.

Concept used: corrosion of copper and iron (Topic 7); malleability (Topic 2).

  1. 1

    (i)

    Copper reacts slowly with moist carbon dioxide in the air and forms a green coat of basic copper carbonate → the domes look green.

  2. 2

    (ii)

    Iron would rust: the reddish-brown rust flakes off, exposing fresh iron, which rusts again → the dome would weaken and not stay stable.

  3. 3

    Copper's green coating stays on and protects the copper underneath from further corrosion.

  4. 4

    So no, iron domes would be worse.

  5. 5

    (iii)

    Copper is highly malleable — it can be beaten into thin sheets and shaped into curved roofs such as domes.

  6. Check: each answer uses one property: (i) and (ii) corrosion products (protective vs flaky), (iii) malleability ✔.

    Common trap: In (ii), saying "yes, iron does not change colour". Iron changes colour too — and its rust flakes off.
(i) Copper gets oxidised/corroded to basic copper carbonate, which is greenish in colour. — 1 mark (split inferred)
(ii) No, iron will rust and the reddish layer of rust will come off, exposing iron to air; the dome will not be stable. Copper on corrosion forms a protective layer which does not allow further corrosion. — 1 mark (split inferred)
(iii) Copper is a highly malleable metal; its thin sheets can be used to give different shapes to roofs, like the shape of a dome. — 1 mark (split inferred)
CBSE SQP 2025-26, Q29 option B — parts (a)–(c) (3 of the question's 5 marks)3 marks

Question: B. The electronic structures of atoms P and Q are shown below
[Figure described: atom P has two shells with 2 and 6 electrons; atom Q has three shells with 2, 8 and 1 electrons.]
Based on the information given above, answer the following questions:
(a) If P and Q combine to form a compound, what type of bond is formed between them?
(b) Give the chemical formula of the compound formed.
(c) The compound so formed is dissolved in water. Is the resultant solution acidic or basic in nature? Justify your answer.

Given: P is 2, 6; Q is 2, 8, 1. Asked: bond type; formula; nature of its solution.

Concept used: metals lose and non-metals gain electrons → ionic bond (Topics 1 and 5); metal oxides are basic (Topic 3).

  1. 1

    P: 2, 6 → 8 electrons → oxygen, a non-metal that gains 2 electrons → PX2−\ce{P^2-}PX2−.

  2. 2

    Q: 2, 8, 1 → 11 electrons → sodium, a metal that loses 1 electron → QX+\ce{Q+}QX+.

  3. 3

    (a) Electrons are transferred from the metal to the non-metal → ionic bond.

  4. 4

    (b) Criss-cross QX+\ce{Q+}QX+ and PX2−\ce{P^2-}PX2− → QX2P\ce{Q2P}QX2​P (that is, NaX2O\ce{Na2O}NaX2​O).

  5. 5

    (c) A metal oxide dissolves to give a base: NaX2O+HX2O→2 NaOH\ce{Na2O + H2O -> 2NaOH}NaX2​O+HX2​O​2NaOH → the solution is basic.

  6. Check: charges 2 × (+1) − 2 = 0 ✔; NaX2O+HX2O→2 NaOH\ce{Na2O + H2O -> 2NaOH}NaX2​O+HX2​O​2NaOH: Na 2 | 2, O 2 | 2, H 2 | 2 ✔.

    Common trap: Writing QPX2\ce{QP2}QPX2​ by mixing up which atom needs two partners. Q gives only one electron, and P needs two, so two Q atoms are needed.
(a) Ionic bond — 1 mark (split inferred)
(b) QX2P\ce{Q2P}QX2​P — 1 mark (split inferred)
(c) Basic; metallic oxides are basic in nature. — 1 mark (split inferred)
CBSE SQP 2025-26, Q29 option B, version for visually impaired students — parts (b)–(d) (3 of the question's 5 marks)3 marks

Question: B. Oxygen can combine with both metals and non-metals. It combines with Calcium to form CaO and with carbon to form COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​.
(b) Identify the type of bond formed between Calcium and oxygen.
(c) Which of the above compounds will be a good conductor of electricity in molten state and why?
(d) Comment on the physical state (solid, liquid or gas) of CaO and COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​.

Given: CaO (metal + non-metal) and COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ (non-metal + non-metal). Asked: bond in CaO; which conducts when molten; physical states.

Concept used: ionic compounds and their properties (Topic 5).

  1. 1

    (b) Calcium (metal) transfers 2 electrons to oxygen (non-metal) → ionic bond.

  2. 2

    (c) CaO: in the molten state its ions (CaX2+\ce{Ca^2+}CaX2+, OX2−\ce{O^2-}OX2−) are free to move and carry current. COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ has no ions.

  3. 3

    (d) CaO is a solid (ionic, melting point 2850 K); COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ is a gas at room temperature.

  4. Check: CaO's melting point of 2850 K (textbook table) confirms it is a high-melting solid ✔.

    Common trap: In (c), saying "CaO conducts because it has free electrons". Ionic compounds conduct by moving ions.
(b) Ionic bond — 1 mark (split inferred)
(c) CaO, due to the presence of free ions in the molten state. — 1 mark (split inferred)
(d) CaO is a solid while COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ is a gas. — 1 mark (split inferred)

In the above discussed reaction, what is the nature of MnOX2\ce{MnO2}MnOX2​?

Given: MnOX2\ce{MnO2}MnOX2​ reacts with hydrochloric acid to give a salt (MnClX2\ce{MnCl2}MnClX2​) and water. Asked: the nature of MnOX2\ce{MnO2}MnOX2​ in this reaction.

Concept used: a metal oxide that reacts with an acid to give a salt and water behaves as a basic oxide (Topic 3; Chapter 2). Why it applies: the reaction shown is with an acid only.

  1. 1

    Manganese is a metal → MnOX2\ce{MnO2}MnOX2​ is a metal oxide.

  2. 2

    It reacts with an acid (HCl) to form a salt (MnClX2\ce{MnCl2}MnClX2​) and water — towards the acid it behaves as a basic oxide → (b). (Chlorine is also given off, because MnOX2\ce{MnO2}MnOX2​ is an oxidising agent as well — so this is not a simple neutralisation.)

  3. Check: the case gives no reaction with a base, so there is no evidence for amphoteric behaviour; and it does not behave as an acid ✔.

    Teacher's note

    This is the question bank's key. Manganese dioxide is not in the textbook, and in this reaction it also acts as an oxidising agent (Chapter 1). At this level, judge it only by what the case shows: a metal oxide reacting with an acid → basic.

    Common trap: Choosing (d) amphoteric because "manganese is a metal like zinc". Only aluminium oxide and zinc oxide are named as amphoteric in this chapter, and the case shows no reaction with a base.
(b) Basic oxide
