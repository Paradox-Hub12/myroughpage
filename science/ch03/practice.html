P8● FoundationShort answer · 2 marksOriginal

Solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity, but a solution of sodium chloride in water does. Explain.

Full solution

Given: solid vs dissolved NaCl. Asked: why only the solution conducts.

Concept used: current in an ionic compound is carried by moving ions (Topic 5). Why it applies: NaCl is made of NaX+\ce{Na+}NaX+ and ClX−\ce{Cl-}ClX− ions.

  1. 1

    Conduction needs charged particles that can move.

  2. 2

    In solid NaCl, the ions are held in a rigid structure — they cannot move.

  3. 3

    In water, the ions separate and move freely, towards the oppositely charged electrodes.

  4. 4

    So the solution conducts and the solid does not.

  5. Check: molten NaCl also conducts — the ions are free there too — which confirms that "free ions" is the deciding factor ✔.

  6. Answer The ions in the solid are fixed in place; in solution they are free to move and carry the current.

    Common trap: Saying "the solid has no ions". The ions are present in both — only their freedom to move differs.
