Wrap-up

Identify the element

About 35 minutes · 7 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Write equations for metals with oxygen and classify oxides.
  • Describe the reactions of metals with water.
  • Predict displacement reactions from the reactivity series.
  • Use the reactivity series to identify and rank metals.
  • Show the formation of an ionic compound by electron transfer.
  • Choose the extraction method from a metal's position.
  • Recognise corrosion products.

Board questions in this chapter love a mystery: "An element X is stored in kerosene… a metal M does not react with dilute acid but… Identify X and write the equation." Every clue points to a rung on one ladder. This page puts the whole chapter on two cards and a clue table, then works through six mysteries.

Displacement rule: any metal displaces every metal below it from a salt solution — never one above it.

Card 2 — From ore to pure metal

Ore → enrichment (remove gangue) →

  • High reactivity (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al): electrolytic reduction (molten chloride; Al from its oxide) → metal at the cathode.
  • Medium reactivity (Zn, Fe, Pb): sulphide → roasting (excess air, gives SOX2\ce{SO2}SOX2​); carbonate → calcination (limited air, gives COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​) → oxide → reduction with carbon (or a more reactive metal: thermit) → refining.
  • Low reactivity (Cu, Hg): heat the sulphide in air → metal → refining (copper oxide can also be reduced by carbon). Ag, Au: often free.

Electrolytic refining: impure metal = anode; pure strip = cathode; salt solution = electrolyte; insoluble impurities = anode mud.

Six mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

The kerosene metal

Question. Element X has the electron arrangement 2, 8, 8, 1. It is soft enough to cut with a knife and is stored under kerosene. (a) Identify X. (b) Write the equation for its reaction with water. (c) How is X extracted, and why not with carbon?

Given: arrangement 2, 8, 8, 1; soft; stored in kerosene. Asked: X; equation; extraction method and reason.

Concept used: arrangement → element (Topic 1); reactions with water (Topic 3); extraction by position (Topic 6). Why it applies: every clue points to the top of the ladder.

  1. 1

    2 + 8 + 8 + 1 = 19 electrons → atomic number 19 → X = potassium.

  2. 2

    One valence electron → a very reactive metal; soft and stored in kerosene confirms it.

  3. 3

    Water reaction (violent; hydrogen catches fire):

    2 K(s)+2 HX2O(l)→2 KOH(aq)+HX2(g)\ce{2K(s) + 2H2O(l) -> 2KOH(aq) + H2(g)}2K(s)+2HX2​O(l)​2KOH(aq)+HX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Potassium is at the top of the series. It has more affinity for oxygen than carbon, so carbon cannot reduce its compounds.

  5. 5

    It is obtained by electrolysis of molten potassium chloride — potassium forms at the cathode.

  6. Check: K 2 | 2, H 4 | 2 + 2 = 4, O 2 | 2 ✔. The method agrees with the ladder card (K: electrolysis) ✔.

  7. Answer (a) potassium (b) 2 K+2 HX2O→2 KOH+HX2\ce{2K + 2H2O -> 2KOH + H2}2K+2HX2​O​2KOH+HX2​ (c) electrolysis of the molten chloride; carbon cannot take oxygen away from such a reactive metal.

    Common trap: Counting 2, 8, 8, 1 as sodium. Sodium is 2, 8, 1 (11 electrons).

Mixed example 2

The metal that won't make hydrogen

Question. A reddish-brown metal M does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid. When heated in air, it gets a black coating, and when a strip of it is placed in silver nitrate solution, shiny crystals form on it and the solution slowly turns blue. Identify M and write the two equations.

Given: no reaction with dilute HCl; black coating on heating; displaces silver; solution turns blue. Asked: M; two equations.

Concept used: the ladder card and the displacement rule (Topics 3–4). Why it applies: M is below hydrogen but above silver.

  1. 1

    No hydrogen with dilute HCl → M is below hydrogen: Cu, Hg, Ag or Au.

  2. 2

    M displaces silver → M is above silver: Cu or Hg.

  3. 3

    Reddish-brown solid metal with a black oxide coating, giving a blue solution → M = copper.

  4. 4

    Heating in air:

    2 Cu+OX2→2 CuO\ce{2Cu + O2 -> 2CuO}2Cu+OX2​​2CuOSwipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Displacement: skeletal Cu+AgNOX3→Cu(NOX3)X2+Ag\ce{Cu + AgNO3 -> Cu(NO3)2 + Ag}Cu+AgNOX3​​Cu(NOX3​)X2​+Ag.

  6. 6

    Nitrate: left 1, right 2 → put 2 in front of AgNOX3\ce{AgNO3}AgNOX3​.

  7. 7

    Silver: left now 2 → put 2 in front of Ag.

    Cu(s)+2 AgNOX3(aq)→Cu(NOX3)X2(aq)+2 Ag(s)\ce{Cu(s) + 2AgNO3(aq) -> Cu(NO3)2(aq) + 2Ag(s)}Cu(s)+2AgNOX3​(aq)​Cu(NOX3​)X2​(aq)+2Ag(s)Swipe to see the whole equation →

  8. Check: Cu 1 | 1, Ag 2 | 2, N 2 | 2, O 6 | 6 ✔. Copper(II) salts are blue, matching the colour change ✔.

  9. Answer M = copper; 2 Cu+OX2→2 CuO\ce{2Cu + O2 -> 2CuO}2Cu+OX2​​2CuO and Cu+2 AgNOX3→Cu(NOX3)X2+2 Ag\ce{Cu + 2AgNO3 -> Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag}Cu+2AgNOX3​​Cu(NOX3​)X2​+2Ag

    Common trap: Thinking "no reaction with acid" means "no reaction with anything". Copper still displaces the metals below it.

Mixed example 3

The steam-only metal

Question. Metal Z does not react with cold or hot water but reacts with steam. Its oxide reacts with both hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide. Z displaces iron from iron(II) sulphate solution but cannot displace aluminium from aluminium sulphate. Identify Z, and write equations for (a) Z with steam, (b) its oxide with sodium hydroxide.

Given: steam only; amphoteric oxide; above Fe, below Al. Asked: Z and two equations.

Concept used: the clue table, amphoteric oxides (Topic 3) and displacement (Topic 4). Why it applies: the clues pin Z to one rung.

  1. 1

    Steam only → Al, Zn or Fe.

  2. 2

    Amphoteric oxide → Al or Zn.

  3. 3

    Displaces Fe but not Al → between Al and Fe → Z = zinc.

  4. 4

    With steam (the oxide forms):

    Zn(s)+HX2O(g)→ZnO(s)+HX2(g)\ce{Zn(s) + H2O(g) -> ZnO(s) + H2(g)}Zn(s)+HX2​O(g)​ZnO(s)+HX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Oxide with sodium hydroxide — salt (sodium zincate) and water:

    ZnO+2 NaOH→NaX2ZnOX2+HX2O\ce{ZnO + 2NaOH -> Na2ZnO2 + H2O}ZnO+2NaOH​NaX2​ZnOX2​+HX2​OSwipe to see the whole equation →

  6. Check: (a) Zn 1, H 2, O 1 each side ✔ (b) Zn 1 | 1, O 3 | 3, Na 2 | 2, H 2 | 2 ✔.

  7. Answer Z = zinc; (a) Zn+HX2O→ZnO+HX2\ce{Zn + H2O -> ZnO + H2}Zn+HX2​O​ZnO+HX2​ (b) ZnO+2 NaOH→NaX2ZnOX2+HX2O\ce{ZnO + 2NaOH -> Na2ZnO2 + H2O}ZnO+2NaOH​NaX2​ZnOX2​+HX2​O

    Common trap: Choosing aluminium because of the amphoteric oxide. The last clue rules it out: a metal that cannot displace aluminium must sit below aluminium.

Mixed example 4

The dazzling metal

Question. Element E has atomic number 12. (a) Identify E and the flame it gives when burnt. (b) Write the equation for its burning and say whether the product is acidic or basic. (c) Show the formation of its chloride by electron transfer. (d) How is E extracted?

Given: Z = 12. Asked: (a)–(d).

Concept used: arrangement (Topic 1), metals with oxygen (Topic 3), electron-dot structures (Topic 5), extraction (Topic 6).

  1. 1

    12 electrons: 2, 8, 2 → E = magnesium. It burns with a dazzling white flame.

  2. 2

    Burning:

    2 Mg(s)+OX2(g)→2 MgO(s)\ce{2Mg(s) + O2(g) -> 2MgO(s)}2Mg(s)+OX2​(g)​2MgO(s)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    MgO dissolves a little in water to give magnesium hydroxide, which turns red litmus blue → basic oxide.

  4. 4

    Chloride: Mg loses 2 electrons; each Cl gains 1 → two Cl atoms:

    ×Mg×  +  2 ∙∙Cl∙∙∙∙∙⟶  [Mg]2+  2[∙∙Cl∙∙∙∙×∙]−\begin{aligned} &{}^{\times}\mathrm{Mg}^{\times} \;+\; 2\,{}^{\bullet}_{\bullet}\overset{\bullet\bullet}{\underset{\bullet\bullet}{\mathrm{Cl}}}{}^{\bullet} \\ &\longrightarrow\; \left[\mathrm{Mg}\right]^{2+}\;2\left[{}^{\bullet}_{\bullet}\overset{\bullet\bullet}{\underset{\bullet\bullet}{\mathrm{Cl}}}{}^{\bullet}_{\times}\right]^{-} \end{aligned}​×Mg×+2∙∙​∙∙Cl​∙∙​∙⟶[Mg]2+2[∙∙​∙∙Cl​∙∙​×∙​]−​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  5. 5

    Magnesium is high in the series → electrolysis of molten magnesium chloride; magnesium forms at the cathode, chlorine at the anode.

  6. Check: equation — Mg 2 | 2, O 2 | 2 ✔; electron-dot — electrons lost (2) = electrons gained (1 + 1) ✔; charges +2 − 1 − 1 = 0 ✔.

  7. Answer (a) magnesium, dazzling white flame (b) 2 Mg+OX2→2 MgO\ce{2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO}2Mg+OX2​​2MgO, basic (c) shown above, MgClX2\ce{MgCl2}MgClX2​ (d) electrolysis of molten MgClX2\ce{MgCl2}MgClX2​.

    Common trap: Drawing MgCl with one chlorine. Two electrons leave magnesium, so two chlorine atoms are needed.

Mixed example 5

Three tarnished objects

Question. A silver spoon has turned black, a copper statue has a green coat, and an iron railing is covered in brown flakes. Name the substance formed in each case, say what in the air causes it, and suggest one way to protect the railing.

Given: three corroded objects. Asked: the corrosion product and cause for each; protection for iron.

Concept used: corrosion of silver, copper and iron; prevention (Topic 7).

  1. 1

    Silver → silver sulphide (black), caused by sulphur compounds in the air.

  2. 2

    Copper → basic copper carbonate (green), caused by moist carbon dioxide in the air.

  3. 3

    Iron → rust (brown, flaky), caused by air (oxygen) and water together.

  4. 4

    Railing: paint it — the paint keeps air and water away from the iron.

  5. Check: the iron answer agrees with the three-tube test: rust only where both air and water reach the iron ✔.

  6. Answer silver sulphide (sulphur in air); basic copper carbonate (moist COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​); rust (air + water) — protect the railing by painting.

    Common trap: Calling all three "rust". Only iron rusts.

Mixed example 6

From a carbonate ore to the metal

Question. A metal X occurs as a carbonate ore, XCOX3\ce{XCO3}XCOX3​. When the ore is heated strongly with air kept out, it gives the oxide XO and a gas that turns lime water milky. The oxide is then heated with coke. X displaces copper from copper sulphate solution but not zinc from zinc sulphate solution, and its sulphate solution is pale green. Identify X, name the process, and write the equations for the two heating steps.

Given: carbonate ore; heated with air kept out → oxide + COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​; reduced with coke; between Zn and Cu; pale green sulphate. Asked: X; process; two equations.

Concept used: calcination and reduction with carbon (Topic 6); displacement (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    X is between zinc and copper and is reduced with carbon → Fe or Pb.

  2. 2

    A pale green sulphate solution is iron(II) sulphate → X = iron, ore FeCOX3\ce{FeCO3}FeCOX3​.

  3. 3

    Heating a carbonate ore strongly to drive out carbon dioxide is calcination (the textbook says "in limited air"; for iron, air is kept out so that iron(II) oxide does not react further):

    FeCOX3(s)→heatFeO(s)+COX2(g)\ce{FeCO3(s) ->[\text{heat}] FeO(s) + CO2(g)}FeCOX3​(s)heat​FeO(s)+COX2​(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Reducing the oxide with coke:

    FeO(s)+C(s)→Fe(s)+CO(g)\ce{FeO(s) + C(s) -> Fe(s) + CO(g)}FeO(s)+C(s)​Fe(s)+CO(g)Swipe to see the whole equation →

  5. Check: calcination — Fe 1, C 1, O 3 | 1 + 2 = 3 ✔; reduction — Fe, O, C one each side ✔; the gas turns lime water milky, as COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​ should ✔.

  6. Answer X = iron; calcination: FeCOX3→FeO+COX2\ce{FeCO3 -> FeO + CO2}FeCOX3​​FeO+COX2​; reduction: FeO+C→Fe+CO\ce{FeO + C -> Fe + CO}FeO+C​Fe+CO

    Common trap: Calling this step roasting. Roasting is for sulphide ores and gives SOX2\ce{SO2}SOX2​; a carbonate is calcined and gives COX2\ce{CO2}COX2​.
