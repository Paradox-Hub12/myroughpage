Week 1

Set 2 — Week 1

5 questions, about 8 minutes

Focus: applying the ideas — slightly harder.

W1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

Write the balanced equation for sodium reacting with water, and say why the hydrogen catches fire.

Answer: 2 Na+2 HX2O→2 NaOH+HX2\ce{2Na + 2H2O -> 2NaOH + H2}2Na+2HX2​O​2NaOH+HX2​. The reaction is so exothermic that the hydrogen formed catches fire. (Topic 3)

Working:

  1. Products: sodium hydroxide and hydrogen.
  2. Hydrogen on the right: 1 + 2 = 3, an odd number → put 2 in front of NaOH.
  3. Hydrogen on the right is now 4 → put 2 in front of HX2O\ce{H2O}HX2​O.
  4. Sodium on the right is 2 → put 2 in front of Na.
  5. Check: Na 2 | 2; H 4 | 4; O 2 | 2 ✔.
Did you get it?

W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

An iron nail is placed in copper(II) sulphate solution. Describe two observations and write the equation.

Answer: The blue solution turns pale green; a reddish-brown coating of copper forms on the nail. Fe+CuSOX4→FeSOX4+Cu\ce{Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu}Fe+CuSOX4​​FeSOX4​+Cu. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

W1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

Why do ionic compounds have high melting points, and why are they brittle?

Answer: The oppositely charged ions are held by strong electrostatic forces — a lot of energy is needed to separate them (high melting point), and the same strong, rigid arrangement breaks rather than bends when pressure is applied (brittle). (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Write the equation for the calcination of zinc carbonate, and say how calcination differs from roasting.

Answer: ZnCOX3→heatZnO+COX2\ce{ZnCO3 ->[\text{heat}] ZnO + CO2}ZnCOX3​heat​ZnO+COX2​. Calcination heats a carbonate ore in limited air; roasting heats a sulphide ore in excess air. (Topic 6)

Did you get it?

W1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

What is galvanising, and why does it protect iron even when the coating is scratched?

Answer: Coating iron with a thin layer of zinc. Zinc is more reactive than iron, so at a scratch the zinc corrodes instead of the iron. (Topic 7)

Set 3 — Month 1

5 questions, about 10 minutes

Focus: mixed, exam-style — the way board questions combine ideas.

M1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

Metal X displaces copper from copper(II) sulphate solution but does not displace iron from iron(II) sulphate solution. (a) Where does X sit on the reactivity ladder? (b) Which metal in the textbook series could X be? (c) Could X be zinc?

Answer: (a) Below iron and above copper. (b) Lead (Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu). (c) No — zinc is above iron and would displace it. (Topic 4)

M1.3 · 3 of 5●●● Advanced

A student says: "Carbon is the cheapest reducing agent, so we should use it to make aluminium too." Evaluate this.

Answer: Not possible. Aluminium has more affinity for oxygen than carbon, so carbon cannot remove oxygen from aluminium oxide. Aluminium is obtained by electrolytic reduction of aluminium oxide. (Topic 6)

Did you get it?

M1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Aluminium is high in the reactivity series, yet aluminium electrical wires do not corrode easily and its oxide reacts with sodium hydroxide. Explain both facts.

Answer: A thin protective oxide layer forms on aluminium and stops further corrosion. Aluminium oxide is amphoteric, so it reacts with bases as well as acids: AlX2OX3+2 NaOH→2 NaAlOX2+HX2O\ce{Al2O3 + 2NaOH -> 2NaAlO2 + H2O}AlX2​OX3​+2NaOH​2NaAlOX2​+HX2​O. (Topic 3)

Did you get it?

M1.5 · 5 of 5●●● Advanced

A kitchen knife made of pure iron would rust and bend easily. Suggest a better material, say what it is made of, and explain why it is better.

Answer: Stainless steel — iron mixed with nickel and chromium. As an alloy it is hard (pure iron is soft) and it does not rust. (Topic 7)
