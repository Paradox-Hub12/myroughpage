Week 1

Set 2 — Week 1

5 questions, about 8 minutes

Focus: the reasons behind the facts.

W1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

In a circuit, in which direction does the conventional current flow outside the cell, and in which direction do the electrons move?

Answer: Conventional current: from the + terminal round to the − terminal. Electrons: the opposite way, from − to +. (Topic 1)

Did you get it?

W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

Give two reasons why the heating elements of toasters are made of alloys.

Answer: Alloys have a higher resistivity than pure metals, and they do not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperatures. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

W1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

A wire is replaced by one of the same material and thickness but twice as long, on the same cell. What happens to the resistance and to the current?

Answer: The resistance doubles (R∝lR \propto lR∝l), so the current halves (I=VRI = \dfrac{V}{R}I=RV​). (Topics 3 and 4)

Did you get it?

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

The current in a resistor is halved; the resistance and time stay the same. What happens to the heat produced, and why?

Answer: It becomes one-quarter, because H=I2RtH = I^2RtH=I2Rt depends on the square of the current. (Topic 6)

Did you get it?

W1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

Give two reasons why the appliances in a house are joined in parallel and not in series.

Answer: In parallel each appliance gets the full supply p.d. and draws the current it needs; and each works independently — one failing does not switch off the others. (Topic 5)
