Current — the rate of flow of charge: I=QtI = \dfrac{Q}{t}I=tQ​. Charge in coulomb (C), current in ampere (A); 1 A = 1 C/s; 1 mA = 10−310^{-3}10−3 A, 1 µA = 10−610^{-6}10−6 A. In metal wires the charges are electrons (e=1.6×10−19e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}e=1.6×10−19 C; 1 C ≈ 6×10186 \times 10^{18}6×1018 electrons). Conventional current flows from + to − outside the cell — opposite to electron flow. A circuit is a continuous, closed path; current is not used up. Ammeter: in series.

Potential difference — work done per unit charge: V=WQV = \dfrac{W}{Q}V=QW​; volt: 1 V = 1 J/C. A cell maintains a p.d. by chemical action. Voltmeter: in parallel. Circuit diagrams use the standard symbols (cell: long line +).

Ohm's law — V∝IV \propto IV∝I for a metallic wire provided its temperature remains the same: V=IRV = IRV=IR. Resistance R=VIR = \dfrac{V}{I}R=IV​, in ohm (Ω). V–I graph: straight line through the origin; slope = RRR. A rheostat varies resistance to control current. Electrons are slowed by the atoms they move among.

Resistivity — R=ρlAR = \rho\dfrac{l}{A}R=ρAl​: R∝lR \propto lR∝l, R∝1AR \propto \dfrac{1}{A}R∝A1​, depends on the material (and temperature). Resistivity ρ\rhoρ: property of the material, unit Ω m. Most metals about 10−810^{-8}10−8 Ω m; alloys higher (nichrome 10−410^{-4}10−4 Ω m); insulators 101210^{12}1012–101710^{17}1017 Ω m. Alloys (nichrome): high resistivity and do not oxidise readily — heating elements. Tungsten: bulb filaments. Copper and aluminium: transmission lines (low resistivity).

Series — same current; V=V1+V2+V3V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3V=V1​+V2​+V3​; Rs=R1+R2+R3R_s = R_1 + R_2 + R_3Rs​=R1​+R2​+R3​ (bigger than each); one failure stops all. Parallel — same p.d.; I=I1+I2+I3I = I_1 + I_2 + I_3I=I1​+I2​+I3​; 1Rp=1R1+1R2+1R3\dfrac{1}{R_p} = \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2} + \dfrac{1}{R_3}Rp​1​=R1​1​+R2​1​+R3​1​ (smaller than each); appliances at home are in parallel (full p.d. each, independent).

Heating effect — in a purely resistive circuit all the energy becomes heat: H=VIt=I2RtH = VIt = I^2RtH=VIt=I2Rt (Joule's law: H∝I2H \propto I^2H∝I2, ∝R\propto R∝R, ∝t\propto t∝t). Uses: iron, toaster, kettle, heater; bulb filament (tungsten, m.p. 3380 °C; nitrogen and argon; thermally isolated); fuse — a wire of suitable melting point, in series, melts when the current is too large (ratings 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 5 A, 10 A …).

Power and energy — P=VI=I2R=V2RP = VI = I^2R = \dfrac{V^2}{R}P=VI=I2R=RV2​; watt: 1 W = 1 V × 1 A. Energy E=PtE = PtE=Pt; 1 kW h = 3.6×1063.6 \times 10^63.6×106 J (one "unit"). Cost = units × rate. A rating "V, P" gives I=PVI = \dfrac{P}{V}I=VP​ and R=V2PR = \dfrac{V^2}{P}R=PV2​; choose the fuse just above PV\dfrac{P}{V}VP​.

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Electric current: the rate of flow of electric charge through a cross-section of a conductor.
  • 1 ampere: the current when 1 coulomb of charge flows through a cross-section in 1 second.
  • Electric circuit: a continuous and closed path of an electric current.
  • Potential difference: the work done to move a unit charge from one point to another in a circuit.
  • 1 volt: the p.d. when 1 joule of work is done to move 1 coulomb of charge between two points.
  • Ohm's law: the p.d. across a metallic wire is directly proportional to the current through it, provided its temperature remains the same.
  • Resistance: the property of a conductor to resist the flow of charges through it; 1 ohm = 1 V/1 A.
  • Resistivity: the constant ρ\rhoρ in R=ρlAR = \rho\dfrac{l}{A}R=ρAl​ — the resistance of a wire of the material of unit length and unit area of cross-section; unit Ω m.
  • Joule's law of heating: H=I2RtH = I^2RtH=I2Rt — heat ∝ square of current, ∝ resistance, ∝ time.
  • Electric power: the rate at which electrical energy is used; 1 watt = power used by a device carrying 1 A at 1 V.
  • Kilowatt hour: the commercial unit of electrical energy; 1 kW h = 3.6×1063.6 \times 10^63.6×106 J.
