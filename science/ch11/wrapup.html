Wrap-up

Which formula do I use?

About 25 minutes · 6 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Use V = IR in all three forms.
  • Use R = ρl/A.
  • Solve mixed series–parallel networks.
  • Calculate the heat produced in a resistor.
  • Use P = VI = I²R = V²/R.
  • Calculate electrical energy and its cost.

Almost every question in this chapter is about four quantities — VVV, III, RRR and PPP — and how they share out round a circuit. Once you know which two you have and whether things are in series or in parallel, the formula almost chooses itself. This page puts the chapter on three cards and then works through four mixed questions.

A network with power

Mixed example 2

From resistance to the bill

Question. An immersion heater rod has a resistance of 22 Ω and is used on the 220 V mains. Find (a) the current, (b) its power, (c) the heat it produces in 1 minute, and (d) the cost of using it for 30 minutes a day for 30 days at Rs 6 per unit.

Mixed example 3

Two bulbs, two ways

Question. Two identical bulbs, each rated "220 V, 40 W", are connected (a) in series and (b) in parallel across the 220 V mains. Find the power used by each bulb in each case. In which case do they glow more brightly?

Given: two identical bulbs, each 220 V, 40 W; supply 220 V. Asked: power per bulb in series and in parallel.

Concept used: rating → R=V2PR = \dfrac{V^2}{P}R=PV2​; series shares the p.d., parallel gives each the full p.d. (Cards 2 and 3). Why it applies: the bulbs' resistance stays the same; only the p.d. across each changes.

    P=(110 V)21210 Ω=121001210 W=10 WP = \dfrac{(110\ \text{V})^2}{1210\ \Omega} = \dfrac{12100}{1210}\ \text{W} = 10\ \text{W}P=1210 Ω(110 V)2​=121012100​ W=10 WSwipe to see the whole equation →
  3. 3

    (b) Parallel — each bulb gets the full 220 V, its rated voltage.

    P=40 WP = 40\ \text{W}P=40 WSwipe to see the whole equation →
  4. 4

    Brightness → more power, more light → brighter in parallel.

  5. Check: in series the current is 2202420=111\dfrac{220}{2420} = \dfrac{1}{11}2420220​=111​ A, and I2R=1121×1210=10I^2R = \dfrac{1}{121} \times 1210 = 10I2R=1211​×1210=10 W ✔.

Mixed example 4

Cut the wire and rejoin it
