Electricity

7 topicsAbout 9–10 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Every switch you press — the fan, the mixer, the phone charger — starts a flow of charge through a closed loop of wire. Why does the heater glow while its cord stays cool? Why does one fused bulb darken a whole string of festival lights, but not the rest of your house? And what exactly is the "unit" that the electricity bill charges you for?

This chapter answers these questions with a handful of ideas and formulas. You will learn what electric current and potential difference are, how to draw circuit diagrams, how current depends on voltage (Ohm's law), what decides the resistance of a wire, how resistors behave in series and in parallel, how a current produces heat, and how power and energy are calculated. It belongs to Unit IV, Effects of Current. In the CBSE sample papers it supplies an MCQ, a 2-mark choice and a 3-mark circuit (2026-27), and a 2-mark circuit choice, a 3-mark resistivity numerical and the 5-mark last question (2025-26).

What you'll learn

  • Define current, use I=QtI = \dfrac{Q}{t}I=tQ​, count electrons in a charge, and explain the direction of current and how an ammeter is connected.
  • Define potential difference and the volt, use V=WQV = \dfrac{W}{Q}V=QW​, and draw circuit diagrams with the standard symbols.
  • Describe the Ohm's-law experiment, plot the V–I graph, and use V=IRV = IRV=IR.
  • Explain how length, thickness and material change resistance, use R=ρlAR = \rho\dfrac{l}{A}R=ρAl​, and choose materials from their resistivity.
  • Find equivalent resistance, currents and p.d.s in series, parallel and mixed circuits, and explain why homes use parallel connection.
  • Use Joule's law H=I2RtH = I^2RtH=I2Rt and explain heaters, bulb filaments and fuses.
  • Use P=VI=I2R=V2RP = VI = I^2R = \dfrac{V^2}{R}P=VI=I2R=RV2​, read power ratings, find units and cost, and choose a fuse.
  • Decide which formula to use, and solve any series–parallel circuit with one routine.

Most of the marks in this chapter come from four skills, and every topic practises them:

  1. Convert units first — mA to A, minutes to seconds, mm and mm² to m and m², kW to W.
  2. Pick the formula from what you are given — the P–V–I–R card in the wrap-up.
  3. Series or parallel? — same current in series, same p.d. in parallel; reduce networks step by step.
  4. Draw circuit diagrams — standard symbols, ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel.
