Topic 6 of 7

Images formed by lenses

About 55 minutes · 4 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Distinguish convex (converging) and concave (diverging) lenses and identify the optical centre, principal axis, foci F₁ and F₂, 2F₁ and 2F₂, and focal length.
  • State the three standard rays for lenses.
  • Draw ray diagrams for all six object positions for a convex lens and two for a concave lens, and state the image in each case.
  • Relate the uses of lenses (magnifying glass, camera, projector, spectacles) to the images they form.

A watch-repairer at a roadside stall squints through a tiny lens to fix a hair-thin spring. The same kind of lens, held in sunlight, can burn a hole in a dry leaf. The camera in your phone uses one to put the whole street onto a sensor smaller than your fingernail. A lens does with refraction what a curved mirror does with reflection.

What is a lens?

A lens is a piece of transparent material (glass, plastic, even water) with at least one curved (spherical) surface. It forms images by refracting light at its surfaces.

Type Shape Effect on parallel light Other name
Convex lens (double convex) thicker in the middle, bulging out on both sides brings rays together to a point converging lens
Concave lens (double concave) thinner in the middle, curved in on both sides spreads rays apart diverging lens
Memory tip

feel the middle — thick middle = convex = converging.

Lens vocabulary

  • Centres of curvature (C1C_1C1​, C2C_2C2​) — each surface of the lens is part of a sphere; their centres are C1C_1C1​ and C2C_2C2​.
  • Principal axis — the straight line through C1C_1C1​ and C2C_2C2​.
  • Optical centre (O) — the central point of the lens on the principal axis. A ray through O goes straight on without changing direction.
  • Principal focus —
  • Convex lens: rays parallel to the axis, after passing through the lens, meet at a point on the other side: the principal focus (real).
  • Concave lens: rays parallel to the axis spread out after the lens; traced backwards they seem to come from a point on the same side as the incoming light: the principal focus (virtual).
  • Two foci, F1F_1F1​ and F2F_2F2​ — light can enter from either side, so a lens has a principal focus on each side, at equal distances from O. We use F1F_1F1​ on the object's side and F2F_2F2​ on the other side.
  • Focal length (fff) — the distance from O to a principal focus.
  • 2F12F_12F1​ and 2F22F_22F2​ — points at twice the focal length from O. They play the part that C played for mirrors: the same-size point.
  • Aperture — the effective diameter of the lens. We only use thin lenses with small apertures.

Thin-lens drawing rule (used in every diagram in this chapter): a real lens bends light at both of its surfaces. For a thin lens we show the bending once, at the lens's centre line (the vertical line through O). Board examiners accept this, and it keeps diagrams clean. Some textbook figures show bending at both surfaces; both give the same image.

Activity: burning a spot with a convex lens (Activity 9.11, CBSE Practical 10)

Safety

Never look straight at the Sun — with or without a lens. Do this with a teacher, away from anything that can catch fire.

Lab notebook · do → see → shows
Do
Hold a convex lens towards the Sun and move a sheet of paper behind it until the brightest, sharpest spot forms. Keep both still for a while. (In the lab, instead of the Sun, focus a distant window or tree on a screen.)
See
A tiny bright spot (a real image of the Sun) forms; the paper starts to smoke and may catch fire. With a distant object, a small, sharp, inverted image appears on the screen.
Shows
Light from a very distant object arrives parallel; a convex lens converges it at its principal focus. The lens-to-image distance gives the approximate focal length.

The three easy rays for lenses

Ray Convex lens Concave lens
Ray 1: comes in parallel to the principal axis passes through F2F_2F2​ on the other side spreads out as if it came from F1F_1F1​ on the object's side (draw the extension back to F1F_1F1​ dashed)
Ray 2: passes through F1F_1F1​ (convex) / is aimed at F2F_2F2​ (concave) comes out parallel to the axis comes out parallel to the axis
Ray 3: passes through O goes straight on, undeviated goes straight on, undeviated

Rays 1 and 3 are the easiest pair for both lenses.

Images formed by a convex lens — six positions

The pattern is the same as for a concave mirror, with 2F2F2F in place of C and the real image on the other side of the lens.

Object position Image position Size Nature
At infinity At F2F_2F2​ Highly diminished, point-sized Real, inverted
Beyond 2F12F_12F1​ Between F2F_2F2​ and 2F22F_22F2​ Diminished Real, inverted
At 2F12F_12F1​ At 2F22F_22F2​ Same size Real, inverted
Between F1F_1F1​ and 2F12F_12F1​ Beyond 2F22F_22F2​ Enlarged Real, inverted
At F1F_1F1​ At infinity Infinitely large (highly enlarged) Real, inverted
Between F1F_1F1​ and O On the same side as the object Enlarged Virtual, erect

The same two patterns as before:

  • Object outside F1F_1F1​ → real, inverted, on the other side. Object inside F1F_1F1​ → virtual, erect, enlarged, on the same side.
  • 2F12F_12F1​ is the "same size" point.

Images formed by a concave lens — two positions

Object position Image position Size Nature
At infinity At F1F_1F1​ (same side as the object) Highly diminished, point-sized Virtual, erect
Between infinity and O Between F1F_1F1​ and O (same side) Diminished Virtual, erect

The one-line rule: a concave lens always gives a virtual, erect, diminished image on the same side as the object, between F1F_1F1​ and O — just like a convex mirror.

Activity: images on a table-top optical bench (Activities 9.12 and 9.13)

Lab notebook · do → see → shows
Do
Find the focal length of a convex lens from a distant object. Chalk five lines one focal length apart and stand the lens on the middle line, so the lines mark 2F12F_12F1​, F1F_1F1​, O, F2F_2F2​, 2F22F_22F2​. Place a candle far beyond 2F12F_12F1​, then just beyond 2F12F_12F1​, at 2F12F_12F1​, between F1F_1F1​ and 2F12F_12F1​, at F1F_1F1​, and between F1F_1F1​ and O; each time try to catch a sharp image on a screen on the other side. Then repeat with a concave lens, looking through it at the candle.
See
With the convex lens, sharp inverted flames appear on the screen at the positions in the table (none at F1F_1F1​). With the candle inside F1F_1F1​, no image forms on the screen, but looking through the lens you see a large, upright flame. With the concave lens, no image ever forms on the screen; looking through it you always see a small, upright flame, which gets smaller as the candle moves away.
Shows
A convex lens behaves like a concave mirror (real images outside F, a virtual magnified image inside F). A concave lens behaves like a convex mirror (always virtual, erect, diminished).

Uses of lenses — and which image each one uses

Use Lens Where the object is Image used
Magnifying glass (reading small print, watch repair) convex, short focal length inside F1F_1F1​ virtual, erect, enlarged
Camera (and the eye — Chapter 10) convex far beyond 2F12F_12F1​ real, inverted, diminished on the sensor
Projector convex between F1F_1F1​ and 2F12F_12F1​ real, inverted, enlarged on a screen (slide put in upside down)
Spectacles convex or concave — correct defects of vision (Chapter 10)
Door peephole (wide view) (Beyond the syllabus) concave lens system outside, far away virtual, erect, diminished — a wide field of view

"Cover half the lens and you lose half the image" — not true

If you cover the lower half of a convex lens with black paper, the whole image still forms. Every part of the lens receives light from every point of the object, so the uncovered half still brings light from the top, middle and bottom of the object to their image points. Only half as much light gets through, so the image is fainter — not cut in half.

Worked examples

Worked example 1

Convex lens: object between F₁ and 2F₁

Question. A 2 cm tall object is placed 18 cm from a convex lens of focal length 12 cm. Draw the ray diagram and describe the image.

Given: convex lens, f = 12 cm (so 2F2F2F is at 24 cm on each side); object 2 cm tall at 18 cm. Asked: ray diagram and description of the image.

Concept used: ray 1 (parallel → through F2F_2F2​) and ray 3 (through O → straight on), bending once at the centre line. Why it applies: both rays have known paths; the object lies between F1F_1F1​ (12 cm) and 2F12F_12F1​ (24 cm).

  1. 1

    Draw the principal axis and the lens. Mark O, then F1F_1F1​ (12 cm) and 2F12F_12F1​ (24 cm) on the left, F2F_2F2​ and 2F22F_22F2​ on the right.

  2. 2

    Draw AB, 2 cm tall, at 18 cm on the left — between F1F_1F1​ and 2F12F_12F1​.

  3. 3

    Ray 1: from B, parallel to the axis, to the lens's centre line; then bend it so that it passes through F2F_2F2​ and continues. Arrowheads on both parts.

  4. 4

    Ray 3: from B, a straight line through O, continued to the right. Arrowheads.

  5. 5

    The two rays cross below the axis, beyond 2F22F_22F2​. Mark B′; draw A′B′ (solid) from the axis down to B′.

  6. Check: table row "between F1F_1F1​ and 2F12F_12F1​ → beyond 2F22F_22F2​, enlarged, real and inverted" ✔. To scale, A′B′ is about 36 cm from O and about 4 cm tall; you will calculate these in Topic 7.

  7. Answer Image on the other side, beyond 2F₂ (about 36 cm from the lens); enlarged (about 4 cm); real and inverted.

    Common trap: Drawing ray 1 through F1F_1F1​ (the object's side). After a convex lens, a parallel ray goes through the focus on the other side.

Worked example 2

Convex lens as a magnifying glass

Question. A 2 cm tall object is placed 6 cm from a convex lens of focal length 12 cm. Draw the ray diagram and describe the image. What is this arrangement used for?

Given: convex lens, f = 12 cm; object 2 cm tall at 6 cm (inside F1F_1F1​). Asked: ray diagram, description and use.

Concept used: rays 1 and 3; if the refracted rays diverge, extend them backwards (dashed). Why it applies: the object is inside F1F_1F1​, so the rays will not meet on the other side.

  1. 1

    Draw the axis, the lens, O, F1F_1F1​ and F2F_2F2​ (12 cm), 2F12F_12F1​ and 2F22F_22F2​ (24 cm).

  2. 2

    Draw AB, 2 cm tall, at 6 cm — between F1F_1F1​ and O.

  3. 3

    Ray 1: from B parallel to the axis; at the centre line bend it through F2F_2F2​.

  4. 4

    Ray 3: from B straight through O.

  5. 5

    On the right, the two rays move apart — they never meet.

  6. 6

    Extend both refracted rays backwards to the left as dashed lines. They meet above the axis on the object's side, farther from the lens than the object. Mark B′ and draw A′B′ (dashed, upright).

  7. Check: table row "between F1F_1F1​ and O → same side, enlarged, virtual and erect" ✔. To scale, A′B′ is about 12 cm from the lens (at F1F_1F1​) and about 4 cm tall.

  8. Answer Image on the same side as the object (about 12 cm from the lens); enlarged (about 4 cm); virtual and erect. Used as a magnifying glass (simple microscope).

    Common trap: Looking for the image on the right-hand side because "lenses form images on the other side". Only real images do.

Worked example 3

Concave lens

Question. A 3 cm tall object is placed 24 cm from a concave lens of focal length 12 cm. Draw the ray diagram and describe the image.

Given: concave lens, f = 12 cm (F1F_1F1​ at 12 cm on the object's side, 2F12F_12F1​ at 24 cm); object 3 cm tall at 24 cm. Asked: ray diagram and description.

Concept used: ray 1 (parallel → appears to come from F1F_1F1​) and ray 3 (through O → straight). Why it applies: a concave lens always spreads the rays out, so the image is found from backward extensions.

  1. 1

    Draw the axis, the concave lens, O, F1F_1F1​ (12 cm, left), F2F_2F2​ (12 cm, right) and 2F12F_12F1​ (24 cm, left).

  2. 2

    Draw AB, 3 cm tall, at 24 cm — at 2F12F_12F1​.

  3. 3

    Ray 1: from B parallel to the axis to the centre line; bend it upwards and away from the axis, along the line that comes from F1F_1F1​. Draw the backward extension from the lens to F1F_1F1​ as a dashed line.

  4. 4

    Ray 3: from B straight through O, continuing to the right.

  5. 5

    The dashed extension of ray 1 crosses ray 3 (the straight line from B to O) on the left, between F1F_1F1​ and O, above the axis. Mark B′; draw A′B′ (dashed, upright).

  6. Check: concave-lens rule: virtual, erect, diminished, between F1F_1F1​ and O ✔. To scale, A′B′ is about 8 cm from the lens and about 1 cm tall.

  7. Answer Image on the same side as the object, between F₁ and O (about 8 cm from the lens); diminished (about 1 cm); virtual and erect.

    Common trap: Treating the object at 2F12F_12F1​ as a "same size" case. That rule is for a convex lens; a concave lens always gives a smaller image.

Quick check

Answer without scrolling up
Quick check · 1 of 3MCQ1 mark

A ray of light passes through the optical centre of a thin concave lens. After passing through the lens it:

That is what happens to a ray coming in parallel to the axis of a concave lens.
No ray converges to a focus after a concave lens.
At the optical centre the two faces are parallel, so for a thin lens the ray goes straight through — for both convex and concave lenses.
Only a ray aimed at the focus comes out parallel.
Quick check · 2 of 3MCQ1 mark

To get a real image of the same size as the object with a convex lens of focal length 12 cm, the object should be placed:

At F1F_1F1​ the image forms at infinity.
At 2F12F_12F1​ (2 × 12 = 24 cm) the image forms at 2F22F_22F2​, real, inverted and the same size.
Between F1F_1F1​ and 2F12F_12F1​ the image is enlarged.
Inside F1F_1F1​ the image is virtual — it cannot be caught on a screen.
Quick check · 3 of 3Short answer2 marks

A camera and a projector both use a convex lens. Compare where the object is placed and the kind of image formed in each.

Answer: Camera: the object (the scene) is far away, beyond 2F12F_12F1​ → image real, inverted and diminished on the sensor. Projector: the slide is placed between F1F_1F1​ and 2F12F_12F1​ → image real, inverted and enlarged on a distant screen (the slide is put in upside down so the picture appears upright).

Both images are real (they fall on a sensor or screen); the difference is diminished versus enlarged.

Did you get it?

Common mistakes!

  • These are the ray-diagram errors examiners see most often with lenses.

  • Bending the ray at both surfaces, or at a random point

    Confusing, and the image often lands in the wrong place.

    Thin-lens rule: bend each ray once, at the centre line.

  • A parallel ray through F1F_1F1​ after a convex lens

    After refraction it goes through the focus on the other side.

    Parallel → F2F_2F2​ (convex); appears from F1F_1F1​ (concave).

  • Bending the ray through O

    A thin lens does not deviate a ray through its optical centre.

    Ray through O: straight line.

  • Virtual image drawn on the far side

    Virtual images from lenses form on the object's side.

    Extend rays backwards (dashed) to the object's side.

  • Missing arrowheads; dashed and solid lines mixed up

    Direction of light is unclear.

    Arrowheads on real rays; extensions dashed.

  • "A convex lens always forms a real image"

    Inside F1F_1F1​ it forms a virtual, magnified image.

    Check the object against F1F_1F1​ first.

  • "Covering half the lens gives half the image"

    Every part of the lens forms the whole image.

    Full image, but dimmer.

Key points

  • Convex lens = converging (thick middle); concave lens = diverging (thin middle).
  • Three easy rays: parallel → through F2F_2F2​ (convex) / as if from F1F_1F1​ (concave); through F1F_1F1​ (or aimed at F2F_2F2​) → parallel; through O → straight.
  • Draw bending once, at the lens's centre line (thin-lens rule).
  • Convex lens: outside F1F_1F1​ → real, inverted, other side; at 2F12F_12F1​ → same size; inside F1F_1F1​ → virtual, erect, enlarged.
  • Concave lens: always virtual, erect, diminished, same side, between F1F_1F1​ and O.
  • Uses: magnifying glass (inside F), camera (far beyond 2F), projector (between F and 2F).
