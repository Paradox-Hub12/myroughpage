The whole chapter on one page

Reflection — angle of incidence = angle of reflection (from the normal); incident ray, normal and reflected ray in one plane. Holds for all mirrors. Plane mirror image: virtual, erect, same size, the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front, laterally inverted.

Spherical mirrors — concave (caves in, converging) and convex (bulges out, diverging). Pole P on the mirror; centre of curvature C; radius of curvature R = PC; principal axis through P and C; principal focus F where parallel rays meet (concave, real) or seem to come from (convex, virtual); focal length f = PF; R = 2f.

Ray diagrams — two easy rays from the top of the object: parallel → through F; through F → parallel; through C → back on itself; to P → equal angles. Real rays solid with arrowheads; extensions dashed. Describe every image by position, size, nature.

Images — concave mirror and convex lens: object outside F → real, inverted (same size at C or 2F); object inside F → virtual, erect, enlarged. Convex mirror and concave lens: always virtual, erect, diminished.

Sign convention — origin at P (or O); light from left to right; right and up +, left and down −. uuu always negative.

Refraction — change in direction of light passing obliquely from one medium to another, caused by a change in speed. Into a denser medium (slower) → towards the normal; into a rarer medium (faster) → away. Glass slab: e=ie = ie=i, emergent ray parallel to incident ray, lateral shift. Snell's law: sin⁡isin⁡r\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}sinrsini​ = constant.

Lenses — convex (thick middle, converging) and concave (thin middle, diverging). Optical centre O (rays pass straight through); foci F1F_1F1​, F2F_2F2​; 2F12F_12F1​, 2F22F_22F2​. Draw bending once at the centre line.

Key definitions

(your own words are fine — just keep every key term)

  • Principal focus (concave mirror): the point on the principal axis where rays parallel to the axis meet after reflection.
  • Focal length: distance from the pole (or optical centre) to the principal focus.
  • Real image: formed where rays actually meet; can be caught on a screen. Virtual image: rays only appear to meet; cannot be caught on a screen.
  • Magnification: ratio of image height to object height.
  • Refraction: change in direction of light passing obliquely from one transparent medium into another, due to a change in speed.
  • Absolute refractive index: speed of light in vacuum ÷ speed of light in the medium.
  • Lateral shift: sideways distance between the incident ray (extended) and the emergent ray from a glass slab.
  • Power of a lens: reciprocal of its focal length in metres. 1 dioptre: power of a lens of focal length 1 m.
