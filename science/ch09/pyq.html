CBSE Sample Question Paper 2026-27

CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section C, Q301 mark

Question: A student wants to determine the focal length of a convex lens using sunlight. Which of the following practical procedures will give the most accurate result?

Given: a convex lens and sunlight. Asked: the procedure that gives the focal length most accurately.

Concept used: rays parallel to the principal axis meet at the principal focus of a convex lens; the focal length is the distance from the optical centre to that point (Topic 6). Why it applies: the Sun is so far away that its rays reach the lens almost parallel.

  1. 1

    Sunlight falls on the lens as (almost) parallel rays.

  2. 2

    A convex lens converges these rays to one point — its principal focus, F2F_2F2​.

  3. 3

    If a sheet of paper is held exactly at F2F_2F2​, all the light falls on one tiny spot: the smallest, brightest spot.

  4. 4

    Move the lens up and down until that spot is formed, then measure the distance from the lens to the paper. That distance is the focal length.

  5. 5

    This is procedure B.

  6. Check: if the paper is nearer or farther than F2F_2F2​, the light is spread over a bigger, dimmer circle — so "smallest and brightest" marks the focus exactly ✔.

    Why the other options are wrong: A — a blurred circle means the screen is not at the focus. C — the thickness of a lens does not tell you its focal length. D — looking through the lens measures nothing (and you must never look at the Sun through a lens).
    Common trap: Measuring when any bright patch appears. Keep adjusting until the spot is as small and sharp as possible.
B. Hold the lens above a sheet of paper and adjust until the smallest, brightest spot is formed.
1 mark
CBSE SQP 2026-27, Section C, Q321 mark

Question: Answer by selecting the appropriate option: A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. B. Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A. C. A is true but R is false. D. A is false but R is true.
Assertion (A): The apparent depth of a coin in water is less than its real depth.
Reason (R): Light bends away from the normal when it travels from water to air.

Given: a coin at the bottom of water. Asked: whether A and R are true, and whether R explains A.

Concept used: refraction — light going from a denser medium (water) into a rarer medium (air) bends away from the normal (Topic 5). Why it applies: the light from the coin leaves the water and enters the air before reaching your eye.

  1. 1

    Is R true? Water is optically denser than air. Light going from denser to rarer bends away from the normal → R is true.

  2. 2

    Is A true? Rays from the coin bend away from the normal at the water surface and reach your eye.

  3. 3

    Your eye assumes light travels in straight lines, so it traces the bent rays straight back.

  4. 4

    These traced-back lines meet at a point above the real coin. The coin looks raised → apparent depth < real depth → A is true.

  5. 5

    Does R explain A? Yes — the coin looks raised because the rays bend away from the normal (step 2–4).

  6. 6

    Option A.

  7. Check: this is the same effect that makes a pool look shallower than it is and a pencil look bent at the water surface ✔.

    Why the other options are wrong: B — R is exactly the reason for A. C and D — both statements are true.
    Common trap: Choosing B because R does not mention "depth". Ask: why does the coin look raised? Because the light bends away from the normal — that is R.
A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
1 mark
