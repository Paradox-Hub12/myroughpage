Chapter 9 · Unit III

Light – Reflection and Refraction

7 topicsAbout 9–10 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Look at your face in the back of a steel spoon, then in the hollow side. One image is small and upright; the other is upside down. A coin at the bottom of a bucket of water seems closer than it really is. A small magnifying glass can make newspaper letters look huge. All of these come from just two things light does: it bounces off shiny surfaces (reflection) and it bends when it passes from one material into another (refraction).

This chapter shows you how curved mirrors and lenses form images, how to draw those images with two simple rays, and how to calculate where an image forms and how big it is. It also prepares you for Chapter 10, where the lens in your own eye does the same job.

What you'll learn

  • Tell a concave mirror from a convex mirror; name and mark the pole, centre of curvature, radius of curvature, principal axis, principal focus, focal length and aperture; use R = 2f.
  • Draw ray diagrams for all six object positions using two easy rays, describe each image, and link the images to uses such as torches and shaving mirrors.
  • Draw the two ray diagrams for a convex mirror and explain why it is used as a rear-view mirror.
  • Use the New Cartesian Sign Convention, the mirror formula and magnification to find where an image forms, how big it is, and whether it is real or virtual. Includes a skippable fractions and signs toolkit.
  • Explain why light bends when its speed changes, trace a ray through a glass slab, state the laws of refraction and calculate refractive index or the speed of light in a medium.
  • Draw ray diagrams for a convex lens (six positions) and a concave lens (two positions) and describe each image.
  • Use the lens formula and magnification, and find the power of a lens in dioptres.
  • Decide quickly "mirror or lens?", pick the right formula and signs, and solve any numerical with one routine.

Linked practical

CBSE Practical 10 asks you to find the focal length of a concave mirror and of a convex lens by forming the image of a distant object on a screen (Topic 1, Topic 6). Practical 11 asks you to trace the path of a ray through a rectangular glass slab for different angles of incidence and measure the angles of incidence, refraction and emergence (Topic 5). Both are described in the lessons as What you do → What you observe → What it shows.
