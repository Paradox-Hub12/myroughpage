Memory tip

"Bracket, LCM, simplify, flip." Do these four in order in every numerical.

Two students solve the same mirror problem. One gets "the image is 20 cm away". The other gets "−20 cm". Only one of them can tell you where the image is — in front of the mirror, where you could catch it on a screen, or behind it, where you never could. That little minus sign carries real information. In this topic you learn to read it.

The New Cartesian Sign Convention

Treat the mirror like a graph. The pole P is the origin, and the principal axis is the x-axis.

  1. The object is always placed on the left of the mirror, so light from the object travels from left to right towards the mirror.
  2. All distances along the axis are measured from the pole.
  3. Distances measured to the right of P (along +x) are positive; distances to the left (along −x) are negative.
  4. Heights measured upwards from the axis (+y) are positive; heights measured downwards (−y) are negative.

What this means for every mirror problem

Quantity Symbol Sign Why
Object distance uuu always negative the object is always on the left of P
Focal length, concave mirror fff negative F is in front, on the left
Focal length, convex mirror fff positive F is behind, on the right
Radius of curvature RRR same sign as fff C is on the same side as F
Image distance, real image vvv negative a real image forms in front (left)
Image distance, virtual image vvv positive a virtual image forms behind (right)
Object height hhh positive the object stands upright on the axis
Image height, erect image h′h'h′ positive above the axis
Image height, inverted image h′h'h′ negative below the axis

Make it a habit: the sign table. Before you put any number into a formula, write a small table of uuu, vvv, fff, hhh, h′h'h′ with their signs — and leave the unknown as "?". Every worked example below does this. It takes 20 seconds and prevents most errors.

The mirror formula

For any spherical mirror (small aperture), the object distance uuu, image distance vvv and focal length fff are linked by:

1v+1u=1f\dfrac{1}{v} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}v1​+u1​=f1​Swipe to see the whole equation →
  • It works for concave and convex mirrors and for every object position — the signs take care of the differences.
  • You do not need to derive it (syllabus: derivation not required).
  • Rearranged for each unknown:
1v=1f−1u1u=1f−1v1f=1v+1u\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{u} \qquad \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{v} \qquad \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{v} + \dfrac{1}{u}v1​=f1​−u1​u1​=f1​−v1​f1​=v1​+u1​Swipe to see the whole equation →

Magnification

Magnification (mmm) tells you how many times bigger (or smaller) the image is than the object, and whether it is the right way up:

m=h′hand, for a mirror,m=−vum = \dfrac{h'}{h} \qquad\text{and, for a mirror,}\qquad m = -\dfrac{v}{u}m=hh′​and, for a mirror,m=−uv​Swipe to see the whole equation →

Reading magnification — two questions:

Look at… If… Then the image is…
the sign of mmm mmm negative real and inverted
mmm positive virtual and erect
the size of mmm (ignore the sign) more than 1 enlarged
equal to 1 same size
less than 1 diminished

Magnification is a ratio of two lengths, so it has no unit.

Reading vvv: vvv negative → image in front of the mirror (real). vvv positive → image behind the mirror (virtual). The signs of vvv and mmm must agree: for a mirror, real ⇔ v<0v < 0v<0 ⇔ m<0m < 0m<0.

A plane mirror fits the same rules

For a plane mirror the image is as far behind as the object is in front, so v=−uv = -uv=−u. Then m=−vu=−(−u)u=+1m = -\dfrac{v}{u} = -\dfrac{(-u)}{u} = +1m=−uv​=−u(−u)​=+1: virtual, erect and the same size — exactly what you know.

Answer: (a) mmm positive → virtual and erect; size 0.5 (less than 1) → diminished (half the size). (b) Convex. A concave mirror's virtual images are always enlarged (object inside F), so a virtual diminished image can only come from a convex mirror.

Common mistakes!

  • These sign-convention errors are the most common reason for losing marks in this chapter.

  • Taking uuu as positive

    The object is always on the left of the pole.

    uuu is always negative. Write it in the sign table first.

  • Taking fff of a concave mirror as positive

    F of a concave mirror is in front (left).

    Concave mirror: f<0f < 0f<0. Convex mirror: f>0f > 0f>0.

  • "Correcting" a positive vvv to negative

    A positive vvv means the image is behind the mirror — virtual.

    Trust the sign; read it as information.

  • Dropping a minus sign while substituting

    Squeezed signs get lost: 1−30\dfrac{1}{-30}−301​ written as 130\dfrac{1}{30}301​.

    Put every negative value in brackets.

  • Forgetting to flip 1v\dfrac{1}{v}v1​ at the end

    1v=120\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{20}v1​=201​ means v=20v = 20v=20, not 120\dfrac{1}{20}201​.

    Last step every time: flip.

  • Writing m=vum = \dfrac{v}{u}m=uv​ for a mirror

    That is the lens formula for mmm.

    Mirror: m=−vum = -\dfrac{v}{u}m=−uv​. (Lens: m=+vum = +\dfrac{v}{u}m=+uv​ — see Topic 7.)

  • Writing a sign on mmm but describing it the other way

    m<0m < 0m<0 always means real and inverted.

    Sign of mmm → nature; size of mmm → enlarged or diminished.

Key points

  • Sign convention: P at the origin; light from left to right; right and up are +, left and down are −. uuu is always negative.
  • Concave mirror: fff and RRR negative. Convex mirror: fff and RRR positive.
  • Mirror formula: 1v+1u=1f\dfrac{1}{v} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}v1​+u1​=f1​ — for all mirrors and all positions.
  • Magnification: m=h′h=−vum = \dfrac{h'}{h} = -\dfrac{v}{u}m=hh′​=−uv​. Negative → real, inverted. Positive → virtual, erect. Size > 1 enlarged, < 1 diminished.
  • Routine: sign table → formula → rearrange → substitute (brackets) → LCM → simplify → flip → interpret.
