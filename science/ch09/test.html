Section A · what to look at again

Question 1 · MCQ

The power of a concave lens of focal length 20 cm is:

Correct: (b) −5 D

Concave → f=−20f = -20f=−20 cm =−0.20= -0.20=−0.20 m; P=1−0.20 m=−5P = \dfrac{1}{-0.20\ \text{m}} = -5P=−0.20 m1​=−5 D. (a) forgets the concave lens's minus sign; (c) uses fff in cm (1−20\dfrac{1}{-20}−201​); (d) both errors together.

Question 2 · MCQ

An object is placed 24 cm from a convex lens of focal length 12 cm. The image is:

Correct: (a)

24 cm =2f= 2f=2f, so the object is at 2F12F_12F1​ and the image is at 2F22F_22F2​: same size, real, inverted. (b) is the case of an object at infinity; (c) needs the object inside F1F_1F1​; (d) needs the object at F1F_1F1​.

Question 3 · MCQ

A ray of light passes obliquely from medium P (refractive index 1.3) into medium Q (refractive index 1.6). The ray:

Correct: (b)

Q has the larger refractive index, so it is optically denser: light slows down and bends towards the normal. (a) is the reverse case (denser to rarer). (c) and (d) pair the speed change with the wrong direction.

Question 4 · MCQ

A spherical mirror forms an image with magnification m=−1.5m = -1.5m=−1.5. The image is:

Correct: (b)

Negative mmm → real and inverted; size 1.5 > 1 → enlarged. (a) and (d) need mmm positive; (c) needs the size less than 1.

Question 5 · A–R
AA ray of light passing through the optical centre of a thin lens comes out without changing direction.
RNear the optical centre, the two faces of a thin lens are almost parallel, like a very thin glass slab.

Correct: (A)

Both are true, and R explains A: near O the lens acts like a thin parallel-sided slab, which produces no change in direction (and, being thin, a negligible sideways shift).

Question 6 · A–R
AFor a lens, the image distance of a real image is positive.
RFor a spherical mirror, magnification is given by m=vum = \dfrac{v}{u}m=uv​.

Correct: (C)

A is true: real images form on the other side of a lens, where distances are positive. R is false: for a mirror m=−vum = -\dfrac{v}{u}m=−uv​; m=vum = \dfrac{v}{u}m=uv​ is the lens formula for magnification.
