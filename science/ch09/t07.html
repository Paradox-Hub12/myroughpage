Lens formula, magnification and power

About 55 minutes · 4 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Apply the sign convention to lenses (f positive for convex, negative for concave).
  • Use the lens formula to find u, v or f.
  • Calculate magnification (m=vum = \dfrac{v}{u}m=uv​) for a lens and interpret it.
  • Define power and the dioptre; calculate power from focal length, converting centimetres to metres.

An eye-test prescription might say "+1.25" for one eye and "−0.75" for the other. Those numbers are not focal lengths — they are powers, in dioptres. Opticians use power because it is quick to read and easy to add. By the end of this topic you will be able to turn a prescription into a focal length, and calculate exactly where any lens forms its image.

Sign convention for lenses

The rules are the same New Cartesian rules as for mirrors (Topic 4), with one change: all distances are measured from the optical centre O, which is the origin.

Quantity Sign Why
uuu (object distance) always negative the object is on the left; light travels left to right
fff, convex lens positive the real focus F2F_2F2​ is on the right
fff, concave lens negative the focus from which rays seem to diverge, F1F_1F1​, is on the left
vvv, real image positive real images from lenses form on the other side (right)
vvv, virtual image negative virtual images form on the object's side (left)
hhh positive upright object
h′h'h′ + erect, − inverted above or below the axis

Notice the flip from mirrors: a real image is at negative vvv for a mirror (in front), but at positive vvv for a lens (behind the lens, on the other side). That is because light bounces back from a mirror but passes through a lens.

Magnification for a lens

m=h′hand, for a lens,m=vum = \dfrac{h'}{h} \qquad\text{and, for a lens,}\qquad m = \dfrac{v}{u}m=hh′​and, for a lens,m=uv​Swipe to see the whole equation →

Reading it is exactly as for mirrors: mmm negative → real and inverted; mmm positive → virtual and erect; size > 1 → enlarged; < 1 → diminished.

Memory tip

"Mirror plus, lens minus" for the formula; "mirror minus, lens plus" for magnification. They swap.

Power of a lens

A lens with a short focal length bends light strongly; a lens with a long focal length bends it gently. Power measures how strongly a lens converges or diverges light:

P=1f(f in metres)P = \dfrac{1}{f} \qquad (f \text{ in metres})P=f1​(f in metres)Swipe to see the whole equation →
  • The SI unit of power is the dioptre (D). 1 D=1 m−11\ \text{D} = 1\ \text{m}^{-1}1 D=1 m−1.
  • 1 dioptre is the power of a lens whose focal length is 1 metre.
  • Convex lens: PPP positive. Concave lens: PPP negative (same sign as fff).
  • fff must be in metres. Convert first: 50 cm = 0.50 m; 20 cm = 0.20 m.

So a "+1.25" prescription is a convex lens of focal length 11.25\dfrac{1}{1.25}1.251​ m = 0.8 m = 80 cm.

Did you know?Not in board exam
NCERT information box

When thin lenses are placed in contact, their powers simply add: P=P1+P2+P3+…P = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \dotsP=P1​+P2​+P3​+… An optician testing your eyes slides lenses of known power into a trial frame and adds them up. For example, +1.75 D and −0.50 D together act like a single lens of +1.25 D. Cameras, microscopes and telescopes use such lens combinations to make sharper images.

An object is placed 28 cm from a convex lens of focal length 14 cm. Find the image distance and magnification, and describe the image.

Answer: v=+28v = +28v=+28 cm; m=−1m = -1m=−1 — the image is 28 cm on the other side, real, inverted and the same size.

Working:

  1. Signs: u=−28u = -28u=−28 cm, f=+14f = +14f=+14 cm, vvv = ?
  2. Rearrange the lens formula: 1v=1f+1u\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} + \dfrac{1}{u}v1​=f1​+u1​.
  3. Substitute: 1v=1(+14 cm)+1(−28 cm)\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{(+14\ \text{cm})} + \dfrac{1}{(-28\ \text{cm})}v1​=(+14 cm)1​+(−28 cm)1​.
  4. LCM 28: 1v=2−128 cm\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{2 - 1}{28\ \text{cm}}v1​=28 cm2−1​.
  5. Subtract: 1v=128 cm\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{28\ \text{cm}}v1​=28 cm1​.
  6. Flip: v=+28v = +28v=+28 cm.
  7. Substitute in m=vum = \dfrac{v}{u}m=uv​: m=(+28 cm)(−28 cm)m = \dfrac{(+28\ \text{cm})}{(-28\ \text{cm})}m=(−28 cm)(+28 cm)​.
  8. Divide: m=−1m = -1m=−1.

The object is at 2F12F_12F1​ (28 cm = 2 × 14 cm), so the table already told you: image at 2F22F_22F2​, same size, real and inverted.

Common mistakes!

  • Power with fff in centimetres and mixing the mirror and lens formulas are two of the most common ways students lose marks in this chapter.

  • P=1fP = \dfrac{1}{f}P=f1​ with fff in cm (e.g. 140=0.025\dfrac{1}{40} = 0.025401​=0.025 D)

    The dioptre is defined with fff in metres; the answer is 100 times too small.

    Convert first: 40 cm = 0.40 m → P=2.5P = 2.5P=2.5 D. Or use P=100f (in cm)P = \dfrac{100}{f\text{ (in cm)}}P=f (in cm)100​.

  • Dropping the sign of power

    A concave lens has negative power.

    Same sign as fff: convex +, concave −.

  • Using 1v+1u=1f\dfrac{1}{v} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}v1​+u1​=f1​ for a lens

    That is the mirror formula.

    Lens: 1v−1u=1f\dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}v1​−u1​=f1​.

  • Using m=−vum = -\dfrac{v}{u}m=−uv​ for a lens

    That is the mirror magnification.

    Lens: m=+vum = +\dfrac{v}{u}m=+uv​.

  • "Negative vvv means real" for a lens

    That is the mirror rule. For a lens, real images form on the other side, at positive vvv.

    Lens: v>0v > 0v>0 real; v<0v < 0v<0 virtual.

  • Taking fff of a convex lens as negative "because a concave mirror is negative"

    Converging mirror and converging lens have opposite signs.

    Convex lens f>0f > 0f>0; concave lens f<0f < 0f<0.

Key points

  • Lens sign convention: origin at O; uuu always negative; convex f>0f > 0f>0, concave f<0f < 0f<0; real image v>0v > 0v>0, virtual image v<0v < 0v<0.
  • Lens formula: 1v−1u=1f\dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}v1​−u1​=f1​. Magnification: m=h′h=vum = \dfrac{h'}{h} = \dfrac{v}{u}m=hh′​=uv​.
  • Mirror plus, lens minus (formula); mirror minus, lens plus (magnification).
  • Power P=1fP = \dfrac{1}{f}P=f1​ with fff in metres; unit dioptre (D) = m⁻¹; convex positive, concave negative.
  • 1 D is the power of a lens of focal length 1 m.
