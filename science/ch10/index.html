Chapter 10 · Unit III

The Human Eye and the Colourful World

6 topicsAbout 7–8 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Hold this page very close to your eyes and the letters blur. Look up at a far-away tree and it is sharp again — your eye has quietly changed its own lens. Why do some of your classmates need spectacles for the board, and your grandparents for the newspaper? Why does a garden-hose spray, with the Sun behind you, show a rainbow? Why do stars twinkle while planets shine steadily, and why is the clear sky blue rather than black?

This chapter uses what you learnt about lenses and refraction in Chapter 9 to answer these questions. You will see how the eye focuses, what goes wrong in myopia, hypermetropia and presbyopia and which lens puts it right, how a glass prism bends light and splits white light into a spectrum, how raindrops make a rainbow, how the atmosphere bends starlight and sunlight, and how tiny particles scatter light. It belongs to Unit III, Natural Phenomena. In the CBSE sample papers it supplies a 5-mark question with a choice between the eye and dispersion (2026-27), and a sky-colour MCQ and a 3-mark question on a corrected defect of vision (2025-26).

What you'll learn

  • Name and label the parts of the eye and say what each does; explain how the eye changes its focal length; state the near point and far point of a normal eye.
  • Explain myopia, hypermetropia and presbyopia, draw the defect and its correction, and calculate the power of the lens that corrects it.
  • Trace a ray through a prism, label the angles, and explain why the ray comes out bent.
  • Explain how a prism splits white light into VIBGYOR, how a second prism joins the colours again, and how raindrops make a rainbow.
  • Explain why stars twinkle and planets do not, and why the Sun is seen before sunrise and after sunset.
  • Explain the Tyndall effect, the blue sky, the dark sky at great heights and red danger signals.
  • Sort any eye or sky question by its cause, and solve corrective-lens numericals with one routine.

Most of the marks in this chapter come from four skills, and every topic practises them:

  1. Draw and label the textbook diagrams — the eye, the two defects and their corrections, the prism, dispersion, recombination, the raindrop and the star.
  2. Explain "why" with a chain — cause → what happens to the light → what we see.
  3. Reason about a defect — what the person can see → which point has moved → where the image falls → the correcting lens.
  4. Calculate the power of a correcting lens, with the right sign and in metres.
