Week 1

Set 2 — Week 1

5 questions, about 8 minutes

Focus: the reasons behind the facts.

W1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

Why does your textbook say forests play a key role in the ecological system?

Answer: Forests are the primary producers, and all other living beings depend on them; with animals and micro-organisms, plants re-create the air, water and soil we need. (Topic 1)

Did you get it?

W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

What was the main thrust of the wildlife protection programme under the 1972 Act?

Answer: Protecting the remaining population of certain endangered species by banning hunting, giving legal protection to their habitats and restricting trade in wildlife. (Topic 2)

Did you get it?

W1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

Who owns or manages most of the country's forests and wild animals, and why does your textbook call them hard to look after?

Answer: Much of them are owned or managed by the government through the Forest Department or other departments; even if we want to conserve them, they are rather difficult to manage, control and regulate. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

What is the "clear lesson" your textbook draws from India's experience of environmental destruction and reconstruction?

Answer: Local communities everywhere have to be involved in natural resource management — but there is still a long way to go before they are at the centre-stage in decision-making. (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

W1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

How has the focus of conservation projects changed, according to your textbook?

Answer: They now focus on biodiversity rather than on a few of its components: insects were added to the protected list (1980 and 1986) and plants for the first time in 1991. (Topic 2)

Did you get it?

Month 1

Set 3 — Month 1

5 questions, about 12 minutes

Focus: mixed questions across the chapter.

M1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

Which one of these is NOT in your textbook's list of states with large percentages of reserved forests: Kerala, Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra?

Answer: Punjab — it is in the list of states with a bulk of their forest under protected forests. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

M1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

Put these in order of year: Project Tiger launched; plants added to the protected list; the Wildlife (Protection) Act; insects added to the protected list.

Answer: The Act (1972) → Project Tiger (1973) → insects (1980 and 1986) → plants (1991). (Topics 2 and 3)

Did you get it?

M1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

A student writes: "Sariska National Park is in Madhya Pradesh." Correct both errors.

Answer: Your textbook calls it Sariska Wildlife Sanctuary (and Sariska Tiger Reserve), and it is in Rajasthan. (Topics 3 and 5)

Did you get it?

M1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Give one measure from your textbook for each problem: (a) poaching; (b) a degraded forest near a village.

Answer: (a) The ban on hunting and restrictions on trade in wildlife (1972 Act). (b) Joint Forest Management — a village institution protects and restores it. (Topics 5 and 6)

Did you get it?

M1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

Why were India and Nepal the main targets of tiger poachers and illegal traders?

Answer: Because together they provide habitat to about two-thirds of the surviving tiger population in the world. (Topic 3)
