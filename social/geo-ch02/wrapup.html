Wrap-up

Forests and wildlife on one page

About 25 minutes · 6 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain why forests and wildlife must be conserved.
  • Describe the provisions of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.
  • Name the six tiger reserves with their states.
  • Classify forests as reserved, protected and unclassed.
  • Describe the community efforts in your textbook.
  • Suggest measures to conserve forests and wildlife.

Almost every question on this chapter asks you to do one of four things: say why we conserve, name who conserves and how (the government or a community), match a reserve with its state, or classify a forest (reserved, protected, unclassed). This page puts the chapter on four cards and works through four mixed questions.

Card 1 — The chapter's chain

Why conserve? (E-L-G-A-F)
Ecological diversity · Life support: water, air, soil · Genetic diversity for growth and breeding · Agriculture: traditional crop varieties · Fisheries: aquatic biodiversity

Four mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

Matching by elimination

Question. Match the reserves with their states and choose the right code. Reserves: 1. Bandhavgarh, 2. Periyar, 3. Corbett, 4. Manas. States: P. Assam, Q. Kerala, R. Madhya Pradesh, S. Uttarakhand. Codes: (A) 1-R, 2-Q, 3-S, 4-P (B) 1-Q, 2-R, 3-P, 4-S (C) 1-S, 2-Q, 3-R, 4-P (D) 1-R, 2-S, 3-Q, 4-P.

Asked: the code that matches all four.

Concept used: The reserve table (Card 3).

  1. 1

    Start with the pair you are surest of: Bandhavgarh → Madhya Pradesh (R). Only (A) and (D) have 1-R.

  2. 2

    Next pair: Periyar → Kerala (Q). Of (A) and (D), only (A) has 2-Q.

  3. 3

    Confirm the rest: Corbett → Uttarakhand (S) ✔; Manas → Assam (P) ✔.

  4. Check: (A) gives four different states to four reserves, and each pair is in the textbook's table.

  5. Answer (A)

    Common trap: stopping after one pair. Two codes often share the first pair; use a second pair to decide.

Mixed example 2

Like Madhya Pradesh

Question. An imaginary state, Girinagar, has 640 sq km of forest. Like Madhya Pradesh, 75 per cent of its forest area is permanent forest. (a) How many sq km are permanent forest? (b) Which two kinds of forest make up that area? (c) How many sq km are left for unclassed forest?

  1. (Girinagar and its forest area are imagined for practice; the 75 per cent and the categories are from your textbook.)

    Asked: an area, the two categories and the remainder.

    Concept used: Permanent forest estates (Card 4; Topic 4).

  2. 1

    (a) 75 per cent of 640 = 0.75 × 640 = 480 sq km.

  3. 2

    (b) Permanent forest = reserved + protected forests.

  4. 3

    (c) 640 − 480 = 160 sq km unclassed.

  5. Check: 480 + 160 = 640 ✔; 480 is three-quarters of 640 (4 × 160 = 640) ✔.

  6. Answer (a) 480 sq km; (b) reserved and protected; (c) 160 sq km.

    Common trap: adding unclassed forest into the permanent forest. Permanent forest estates are reserved and protected only.

Mixed example 3

Both hands at work

Question. "Forests and wildlife in India are conserved both by the government and by local people." Support the statement with examples from your textbook. (5 marks)

Asked: examples of government measures and of community efforts.

Concept used: Card 2.

  1. 1

    Government — law: the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 gave legal protection to habitats, banned hunting and restricted trade in wildlife.

  2. 2

    Government — places: governments set up national parks and wildlife sanctuaries, and launched Project Tiger (1973); India's tiger reserves include Corbett and Periyar.

  3. 3

    Community — law used by people: Sariska villagers fought mining by citing the Wildlife Protection Act.

  4. 4

    Community — movements: the Chipko movement resisted deforestation; five Alwar villages protect the Bhairodev Dakav 'Sonchuri' with their own rules.

  5. 5

    Both together: Joint Forest Management (1988) — village institutions protect degraded forest land of the forest department and share its benefits.

  6. Check: two government points, two community points and one joint point — five different facts for five marks, and the last one shows how the two sides meet.

  7. Answer The government protects by law and reserves; communities protect through movements and their own rules; JFM joins the two.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    1. The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 protected habitats, banned hunting and restricted trade in wildlife. (1)
    2. Governments set up national parks and sanctuaries and launched Project Tiger (1973); tiger reserves include Corbett and Periyar. (1)
    3. In Sariska, Rajasthan, villagers fought against mining by citing the Wildlife Protection Act. (1)
    4. The Chipko movement resisted deforestation, and five Alwar villages guard the Bhairodev Dakav 'Sonchuri' with their own rules. (1)
    5. Under Joint Forest Management, village institutions protect degraded forest land and share its benefits. (1)
    Common trap: writing only government measures. The statement says "both".

Mixed example 4

An assertion and its reason

Question. Assertion (A): Herds of blackbuck, nilgai and peacocks can be seen in and around Bishnoi villages in Rajasthan. Reason (R): In these villages such animals are treated as an integral part of the community, and nobody harms them. Choose: (A) both true, R explains A; (B) both true, R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

Asked: the truth of A and R, and whether R explains A.

Concept used: Sacred groves box (Topic 6).

  1. 1

    A is true: your textbook says herds of blackbuck (chinkara), nilgai and peacocks can be seen in and around Bishnoi villages.

  2. 2

    R is true: they are an integral part of the community and nobody harms them.

  3. 3

    R explains A: the animals are seen there because nobody harms them.

  4. Check: ask "Why can these animals be seen near Bishnoi villages?" — "because nobody harms them" answers it ✔.

  5. Answer (A) — both are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Common trap: choosing (B) because R does not name Rajasthan. R must explain why, not repeat where.
