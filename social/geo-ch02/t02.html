The Wildlife (Protection) Act and protected species

About 35 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Describe the provisions of the Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, and the projects for specific animals.
  • Put the government's conservation steps in order of year and explain the shift from single species to biodiversity.

Imagine a market where anyone may shoot a wild animal, sell its skin and clear its forest for fields — and no law says no. Through the 1960s and 1970s, people who cared for India's wildlife saw numbers falling fast and asked for a national law. In 1972 they got one. This topic is about what that law did, and what the government did after it.

Quick reminder (Class 9, Natural Vegetation and Wildlife). A national park and a wildlife sanctuary are areas set aside where wild plants and animals are protected.

  1. The demand and the Act (1972)

  • In the 1960s and 1970s, conservationists demanded a national wildlife protection programme.
  • The Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act was implemented in 1972.

What the Act did — its main provisions (your textbook):

Provision What it means
various provisions for protecting habitats the places where wild animals live are given legal protection
an all-India list of protected species was published the species that the law protects were named for the whole country
banning hunting to protect the remaining population of certain endangered species
restricting trade in wildlife buying and selling wild animals and their parts was limited

The thrust (main aim) of the programme was protecting the remaining population of certain endangered species by banning hunting, giving legal protection to their habitats, and restricting trade in wildlife.

  1. After the Act: parks, sanctuaries and projects

  • Central and many state governments then established national parks and wildlife sanctuaries.
  • The central government announced projects for protecting specific animals that were gravely threatened:
Animals with special projects (your textbook)
the tiger (Project Tiger — Topic 3)
the one-horned rhinoceros
the Kashmir stag or hangul
three types of crocodiles — fresh water crocodile, saltwater crocodile and the Gharial
the Asiatic lion, and others
  • Most recently, the Indian elephant, black buck (chinkara), the great Indian bustard (godawan) and the snow leopard, etc. have been given full or partial legal protection against hunting and trade throughout India.

Note on the textbook's wording. The textbook prints "black buck (chinkara)" here and "blackbuck, (chinkara)" on p. 17. Many books treat the blackbuck and the chinkara as two different antelopes; in the exam, write the names as your textbook prints them.

  1. From a few animals to the whole of biodiversity

  • Conservation projects now focus on biodiversity rather than on a few of its components, and there is a more intensive search for different conservation measures.
  • Even insects are beginning to find a place in conservation planning: in the notifications of 1980 and 1986 under the Wildlife Act, several hundred butterflies, moths, beetles, and one dragonfly were added to the list of protected species.
  • In 1991, for the first time, plants were also added to the list, starting with six species.

Note on the textbook's wording. The textbook says "the notification under Wildlife Act of 1980 and 1986". The Act itself is of 1972; 1980 and 1986 are the years of the notifications that added insects.

Memory hook — the years in order: 1972 Act → 1973 Project Tiger (Topic 3) → 1980 and 1986 insects → 1988 Joint Forest Management (Topic 5) → 1991 plants. Say it as "72 Act, 73 Tiger, 80 and 86 insects, 88 JFM, 91 plants".

Worked example 1

Put the steps in order

Question. Arrange these in the order in which they happened: (i) plants added to the list of protected species for the first time; (ii) the Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act implemented; (iii) butterflies, moths, beetles and a dragonfly added to the protected list; (iv) Project Tiger launched.

Asked: the order of four events.

Concept used: The years (memory hook, section 3).

  1. 1

    Find each year: (i) 1991; (ii) 1972; (iii) 1980 and 1986; (iv) 1973.

  2. 2

    Sort them: 1972 → 1973 → 1980/1986 → 1991.

  3. Check: the order runs from the law, to one animal, to insects, to plants — from a few species to the wider biodiversity, as the textbook describes.

  4. Answer (ii) → (iv) → (iii) → (i)

    Common trap: putting Project Tiger first because it is famous. The Act came a year earlier, in 1972.

Worked example 2

The forest guard's talk

Question. A forest guard is explaining the 1972 law to a village meeting. Write three things that the 1972 Wildlife (Protection) Act provided for, which she should explain. (3 marks)

Asked: three provisions of the Act.

Concept used: The Act's provisions (section 1).

  1. 1

    Habitats: the Act made various provisions for protecting habitats — legal protection for the places where wild animals live.

  2. 2

    The list: an all-India list of protected species was published.

  3. 3

    Hunting and trade: to protect the remaining population of endangered species, hunting was banned and trade in wildlife was restricted.

  4. Check: three different provisions — habitats, the list, and hunting with trade. "National parks were set up" is something governments did after the Act, so it should not replace one of these.

  5. Answer Legal protection of habitats; an all-India list of protected species; a ban on hunting and restrictions on trade in wildlife.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. The Act made various provisions for giving legal protection to the habitats of wild animals. (1)
    2. An all-India list of protected species was published under it. (1)
    3. It banned hunting and restricted trade in wildlife to protect the remaining populations of endangered species. (1)
    Common trap: writing "the Act gave forests to villagers". The Act is a government law; community efforts come in Topic 5.

Common mistakes!

  • Mixing the years: "Project Tiger 1972", "plants protected in 1980", "JFM 1973".

    Act 1972; Project Tiger 1973; insects 1980 and 1986; JFM 1988; plants 1991.

    Learn "72 Act, 73 Tiger, 80 and 86 insects, 88 JFM, 91 plants".

  • "The Act put forests and wildlife in the Union List."

    Your textbook says nothing of the kind; it lists habitats, the all-India list, the ban on hunting, restricted trade.

    Give only the textbook's provisions.

  • "Insects have been protected since 1972."

    Insects were added by the notifications of 1980 and 1986; plants only in 1991.

    Keep "from a few species to biodiversity" in order of years.

Key points

  • 1960s–70s: conservationists demanded a national programme; the Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 followed.
  • Provisions: protecting habitats; an all-India list of protected species; banning hunting; restricting trade in wildlife — to protect the remaining populations of endangered species.
  • Governments set up national parks and wildlife sanctuaries; projects for the tiger, one-horned rhinoceros, hangul, three crocodiles (fresh water, saltwater, Gharial), Asiatic lion.
  • Recently protected: Indian elephant, black buck (chinkara), great Indian bustard (godawan), snow leopard.
  • The focus moved to biodiversity: insects in 1980 and 1986; plants in 1991 (six species).
