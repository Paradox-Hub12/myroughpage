Biodiversity and why we conserve

About 35 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain biodiversity and the web of life, and why forests are called primary producers.
  • Explain why forests and wildlife must be conserved, using the reasons your textbook gives.

The chapter opens with a Lepcha folk song. The singer asks Narak, "the creator of music", to let him gather music from the springs, rivers, mountains, forests, insects and animals and offer it back. For the Lepchas, nature is not a store-room to empty: it is something to listen to and look after. This topic asks the question the whole chapter answers: why should we look after forests and wildlife at all?

Quick reminder (Geography Ch 1)

Forests and wildlife are biotic resources (they come from living things) and renewable ones (they can grow again) — but only if we do not use them faster than they can recover.

  1. We are one part of a web of life

  • We share this planet with millions of other living beings — from micro-organisms and bacteria, lichens to banyan trees, elephants and blue whales. This whole habitat has immense biodiversity.
  • Humans and all other living organisms form a complex web of ecological system. We are only a part of it, and we are very much dependent on it for our own existence.
  • How we depend on it: plants, animals and micro-organisms re-create the quality of the air we breathe, the water we drink and the soil that produces our food.
  • Forests are the primary producers. They play a key role in the ecological system: all other living beings depend on them.

  1. What biodiversity means

Your textbook's box defines it: biodiversity (or biological diversity) is immensely rich in wildlife and cultivated species, diverse in form and function, but closely integrated in a system through multiple network of interdependencies.

Read the definition in three parts:

Part of the definition What it means for you
wildlife and cultivated species not only wild animals and plants, but also the crops and animals people grow and rear
diverse in form and function living things differ in shape, size and in the work they do in nature
closely integrated … interdependencies they are linked: each depends on others, so harm to one part spreads to the rest

  1. India's forests and wildlife — rich, but under stress

  • India is one of the world's richest countries in terms of its vast array of biological diversity.
  • Forests and wildlife are so well integrated in our daily life that we take them for granted.
  • But lately they are under great stress, mainly due to insensitivity to our environment.
  • NCERT Fig. 2.1 shows three photographs of people and forests: tribal girls using bamboo saplings in a nursery at Mukhali near Silent Valley, tribal women selling minor forest produce, and leaf litter collection by women folk. Look at what the forest gives these people every day.

Note on the textbook's wording. The textbook prints "under great stress mainy due to insensitivity"; read it as "mainly".

  1. Why do we need to conserve forests and wildlife?

Conservation has become essential because of the rapid decline in wildlife population and forestry. Your textbook gives these reasons:

Conservation preserves… Why that matters (your textbook)
ecological diversity the variety of life in nature stays intact
our life support systems — water, air and soil we cannot survive without them
the genetic diversity of plants and animals for better growth of species and breeding
traditional crop varieties in agriculture we are still dependent on traditional crop varieties
aquatic biodiversity fisheries are heavily dependent on it

Memory hook — "E-L-G-A-F": Ecological diversity · Life support (water, air, soil) · Genetic diversity · Agriculture (traditional crops) · Fisheries (aquatic biodiversity).

  1. Reading the newspaper clippings (NCERT p. 16)

The box on p. 16 shows two newspaper headlines: "Gharial on the brink" (the gharial population has been at its lowest since the 1970s) and "Bird deaths blamed on dirty Yamuna" (a Delhi government report points to toxic elements in stagnant water). Read them with three questions:

  • What do they show? A river animal close to the edge, and birds dying near a polluted river.
  • What changed? Numbers have fallen; the river water has become toxic.
  • What does the textbook say? Forests and wildlife are under great stress, mainly because of insensitivity to our environment. Both clippings are about life in and around rivers; the second one names the cause it found — toxic elements in the water.

Worked example 1

The fishing village

Question. In a coastal village, older fishers say their nets now bring up fewer kinds of fish than before, and that a rice variety their grandparents sowed is hard to find. Using your textbook, explain three reasons why conserving biodiversity matters to this village. (3 marks)

Asked: three textbook reasons for conservation that fit the village.

Concept used: Why we conserve (section 4).

  1. 1

    Fish: fisheries are heavily dependent on the maintenance of aquatic biodiversity, so fewer kinds of fish threaten the village's work.

  2. 2

    Rice: in agriculture we are still dependent on traditional crop varieties; conservation keeps the genetic diversity needed for better growth and breeding.

  3. 3

    Everyone: conservation preserves ecological diversity and the life support systems — water, air and soil.

  4. Check: each point names a different textbook reason, and two of them are tied to a clue in the story (fish, rice). If two points both said "fish", one mark would be repeated.

  5. Answer Fisheries need aquatic biodiversity; farming needs traditional crop varieties and genetic diversity; conservation also protects water, air and soil.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Fisheries depend heavily on the maintenance of aquatic biodiversity, so losing kinds of fish harms the village. (1)
    2. Agriculture still depends on traditional crop varieties; conservation keeps their genetic diversity for growth and breeding. (1)
    3. Conservation also preserves ecological diversity and our life support systems — water, air and soil. (1)
    Common trap: writing "to attract tourists". That is not one of the textbook's reasons.

Worked example 2

Correct the definition

Question. A student writes: "Biodiversity means the wild animals of a forest." Correct the statement using your textbook. (2 marks)

Asked: what is wrong, and the correct meaning.

Concept used: The biodiversity box (section 2).

  1. 1

    What is missing: biodiversity includes wildlife and cultivated species — plants as well as animals, and crops as well as wild species — from micro-organisms to blue whales.

  2. 2

    What else it means: these species are diverse in form and function but closely integrated through a network of interdependencies.

  3. Check: the correction adds both missing ideas — "cultivated species" and "interdependencies". Saying only "plants too" would fix half of it.

  4. Answer Biodiversity is the rich variety of wild and cultivated species, diverse in form and function but linked to one another through interdependencies.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. Biodiversity is not only wild animals: it includes wildlife and cultivated species, from micro-organisms to banyan trees and blue whales. (1)
    2. These species are diverse in form and function but closely integrated through a network of interdependencies. (1)
    Common trap: leaving out cultivated species. Crops and the animals people rear are part of biodiversity.

Common mistakes!

  • "Biodiversity means the wild animals of a forest."

    Your textbook includes wildlife and cultivated species, from micro-organisms to blue whales, linked by interdependencies.

    Write all three parts: wild and cultivated, diverse in form and function, interdependent.

  • "Animals are the primary producers" or "humans stand outside the web of life".

    Forests are the primary producers; humans are only a part of the web and depend on it.

    Start any "importance of forests" answer with "forests are primary producers".

  • Giving "tourism" or "they look beautiful" as reasons to conserve.

    The textbook's reasons are ecological diversity, life support (water, air, soil), genetic diversity, traditional crop varieties and aquatic biodiversity.

    Use E-L-G-A-F, one reason per point.

Key points

  • We share the planet with millions of living beings, from micro-organisms to blue whales; humans are only a part of the web of life and depend on it for air, water and soil.
  • Forests are the primary producers on which all other living beings depend.
  • Biodiversity = rich in wildlife and cultivated species, diverse in form and function, closely integrated through interdependencies.
  • India is one of the world's richest countries in biodiversity, but forests and wildlife are under great stress, mainly due to insensitivity to our environment.
  • Why conserve (E-L-G-A-F): ecological diversity; life support — water, air, soil; genetic diversity for growth and breeding; traditional crop varieties in agriculture; aquatic biodiversity for fisheries.
