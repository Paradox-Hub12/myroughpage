Topic 4 of 6

Types and distribution of forests

About 35 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Classify forests as reserved, protected and unclassed, with their shares and what your textbook says about each.
  • Describe where each kind of forest is most common, and Madhya Pradesh's permanent forests.

Two forests can look exactly alike — tall trees, birdsong, a narrow path — and still be treated very differently by law. In one, the Forest Department guards almost every tree; in the next, the forest belongs to a village, which makes its own rules. Who owns a forest, and how strictly it is protected, is the subject of this topic.

Quick reminder (fractions)

"More than half" means more than 50 out of every 100. "One-third" is about 33 out of every 100. If one part is more than half and another is almost one-third, whatever is left must be the smallest part.

  1. Who manages India's forests?

  • Even if we want to conserve our vast forest and wildlife resources, they are rather difficult to manage, control and regulate.
  • In India, much of the forest and wildlife resources are owned or managed by the government, through the Forest Department or other government departments.
  • They are classified into three categories.

  1. Three kinds of forest

Category Share of India's forest (your textbook) What your textbook says about it
Reserved forests more than half of the total forest land regarded as the most valuable as far as the conservation of forest and wildlife resources is concerned
Protected forests almost one-third of the total forest area (declared by the Forest Department) this forest land is protected from any further depletion
Unclassed forests the rest — the smallest share other forests and wastelands belonging to both government and private individuals and communities

Memory hook — "More than half · one-third · the rest": Reserved · Protected · Unclassed, from the largest share to the smallest.

Note on the textbook's wording. The textbook prints "This forest land are protected from any further depletion"; read it as "is protected".

  1. Permanent forest estates

  • Reserved and protected forests together are also called permanent forest estates.
  • They are maintained for the purpose of producing timber and other forest produce, and for protective reasons.
  • Unclassed forests are not part of the permanent forest estates.

  1. Where each kind is most common

What the textbook says States (your textbook)
Largest area under permanent forests Madhya Pradesh — permanent forests are 75 per cent of its total forest area
Large percentages of reserved forests (of their total forest area) Jammu and Kashmir, Andhra Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, Maharashtra
A bulk of their forest under protected forests Bihar, Haryana, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Odisha, Rajasthan
A very high percentage of unclassed forests, managed by local communities all north-eastern states and parts of Gujarat

Read the table carefully. Each list is exactly as your textbook gives it. Do not move a state from one list to another, and do not add states the textbook does not name.

Worked example 1

The forest of an imaginary state

Question. An imaginary state, Vanpur, has 900 sq km of forest: 520 sq km reserved, 300 sq km protected and 80 sq km unclassed. (a) Find the share of each kind, in per cent. (b) Does Vanpur follow the pattern your textbook gives for India? (c) How much of Vanpur's forest is permanent forest estate?

  1. (Vanpur and its numbers are imagined for practice; the India pattern is from your textbook.)

    Asked: three shares, a comparison and a total.

    Concept used: Three kinds of forest and permanent forest estates (sections 2–3).

  2. 1

    (a) Reserved: 520 ÷ 900 × 100 = 57.8 per cent.

  3. 2

    (a) Protected: 300 ÷ 900 × 100 = 33.3 per cent.

  4. 3

    (a) Unclassed: 80 ÷ 900 × 100 = 8.9 per cent.

  5. 4

    (b) Compare: reserved is more than half ✔; protected is about one-third ✔; unclassed is the smallest ✔ — the same pattern as India.

  6. 5

    (c) Permanent forest estate = reserved + protected = 520 + 300 = 820 sq km (91.1 per cent).

  7. Check: the three shares add to 57.8 + 33.3 + 8.9 = 100.0 ✔, and the areas add to 520 + 300 + 80 = 900 ✔.

  8. Answer (a) 57.8, 33.3 and 8.9 per cent; (b) yes; (c) 820 sq km.

    Common trap: adding the unclassed forest to the permanent forest estates. Only reserved and protected forests are permanent forest estates.

Worked example 2

Which kind of forest?

Question. Name the kind of forest in each case: (a) land that the Forest Department has declared safe from any further depletion; (b) forest and wasteland held by a village community and a few private owners; (c) the forest regarded as the most valuable for conserving forest and wildlife.

Asked: three categories.

Concept used: Three kinds of forest (section 2).

  1. 1

    (a) Protected from any further depletion → protected forest.

  2. 2

    (b) Other forests and wastelands of communities and private individuals → unclassed forest.

  3. 3

    (c) Most valuable for conservation → reserved forest.

  4. Check: each description uses one key phrase from the table — "further depletion", "wastelands … communities", "most valuable" — and each phrase belongs to one kind only.

  5. Answer (a) Protected; (b) Unclassed; (c) Reserved.

    Common trap: calling (b) "protected" because the village protects it. The textbook's category for community and private forest land is unclassed.

Worked example 3

Madhya Pradesh and the north-east

Question. Contrast the forests of Madhya Pradesh with those of the north-eastern states, using your textbook. (2 marks)

Asked: one textbook fact for each, showing the difference.

Concept used: Where each kind is most common (section 4).

  1. 1

    Madhya Pradesh: it has the largest area under permanent forests — 75 per cent of its total forest area (reserved and protected).

  2. 2

    North-eastern states: they have a very high percentage of unclassed forests, managed by local communities.

  3. Check: the two points are on the same basis (which kind of forest dominates), so they contrast. Writing "Madhya Pradesh is large" would not be a forest fact.

  4. Answer Madhya Pradesh is mostly permanent forest (75 per cent); the north-east is mostly unclassed forest managed by communities.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. Madhya Pradesh has the largest area under permanent forests, which make up 75 per cent of its total forest area. (1)
    2. All north-eastern states have a very high percentage of unclassed forests, managed by local communities. (1)
    Common trap: saying the north-east has mostly reserved forests. Its forests are mostly unclassed.

Common mistakes!

  • "Unclassed forests make up the largest share."

    Reserved forests are more than half, protected almost one-third; unclassed is the rest, the smallest.

    Say the order: reserved > protected > unclassed.

  • "Permanent forest estates include unclassed forests."

    Permanent forest estates are reserved + protected forests, kept for timber, other forest produce and protective reasons.

    Leave unclassed out of permanent forest estates.

  • Swapping the features: "protected forests are the most valuable"; "reserved forests are protected from further depletion".

    Reserved = most valuable for conservation; protected = protected from any further depletion.

    Learn one key phrase for each kind.

  • "Punjab has mostly reserved forests"; "the north-east has mostly reserved forests".

    Punjab is in the protected list; the north-east is mostly unclassed.

    Learn the three state lists separately (V10).

Key points

  • Most of India's forest and wildlife resources are owned or managed by the government through the Forest Department.
  • Reserved forests: more than half of forest land; the most valuable for conservation.
  • Protected forests: almost one-third; protected from any further depletion.
  • Unclassed forests: other forests and wastelands of government, private individuals and communities.
  • Permanent forest estates = reserved + protected — for timber, other forest produce and protective reasons. Madhya Pradesh has the largest area under them (75 per cent of its forest area).
  • Reserved-heavy: Jammu and Kashmir, Andhra Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, Maharashtra. Protected-heavy: Bihar, Haryana, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Odisha, Rajasthan. Unclassed: all north-eastern states and parts of Gujarat.
