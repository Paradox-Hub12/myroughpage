Topic 5 of 6

Europe becomes the centre of world trade

About 35 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain why thousands fled Europe for America until the nineteenth century.
  • Describe the plantations in America worked by slaves captured in Africa, and read NCERT Fig. 5.
  • Explain how the centre of world trade moved westwards and Europe emerged as its centre.

A crowded port in Europe, a few hundred years ago. A family pushes up the gangway of a ship with everything they own in two bundles. At home there is too little food, fever spreads through the narrow streets, and their church is not the church the rulers allow. Across the ocean lies America — a new start for them. On another ship, people captured in Africa are being taken across the ocean against their will, to grow cotton and sugar for Europe. Both voyages tied America to Europe.

The chain in one line: poverty, hunger, disease and religious persecution in Europe → thousands flee to America → plantations worked by enslaved Africans grow cotton and sugar for Europe; China withdraws and the Americas rise → the centre of world trade moves west, to Europe.

  1. Why thousands fled Europe for America

Your textbook says that until the nineteenth century Europe was a hard place for many people:

Problem in Europe Your textbook's words
Poverty and hunger "poverty and hunger were common in Europe"
Crowded cities "Cities were crowded"
Disease "deadly diseases were widespread"
Religious conflict and persecution "Religious conflicts were common, and religious dissenters were persecuted"

Therefore, thousands fled Europe for America.

Memory hook: "Poverty, hunger, disease, dissent."

New word (from your textbook). Dissenter — one who refuses to accept established beliefs and practices.

Read the time words carefully: "until the nineteenth century". These were the conditions up to that century — this is not a story set in the nineteenth century.

  1. Plantations and slavery in America

In America, by the eighteenth century, plantations worked by slaves captured in Africa were growing cotton and sugar for European markets.

Who From where to where Doing what For whom
Slaves captured in Africa taken from Africa to America working plantations growing cotton and sugar European markets

So America's land, and the forced labour of Africans, now produced goods for Europe.

New word. Plantation — a large farm growing one crop for sale, worked by many labourers (our definition).

  1. Reading a source — NCERT Fig. 5

NCERT Fig. 5, Slaves for sale, New Orleans, from the Illustrated London News, 1851. The caption says a prospective buyer is carefully inspecting slaves lined up before the auction: two children, four women and seven men waiting to be sold. To attract buyers, slaves were often dressed in their best clothes.

Question Answer
What does it show? Enslaved men, women and children lined up for sale at an auction in New Orleans, being inspected by a buyer, in 1851.
What does it tell us? People were bought and sold like goods; even children were sold; they were dressed well only to attract buyers.
What is its limit or point of view? It is a picture in a London magazine: an outsider's view of one moment. It cannot tell us the names, feelings or lives of the people being sold.

Note the date: Fig. 5 is from 1851, but slavery on America's plantations was already at work by the eighteenth century, as the text says.

  1. The centre of world trade moves west

Before What changed After
Until well into the eighteenth century, China and India were among the world's richest countries, and pre-eminent in Asian trade. From the fifteenth century, China is said to have restricted overseas contacts and retreated into isolation. At the same time the Americas rose in importance (silver, crops, plantations — Topic 4 and above). China's reduced role and the rising importance of the Americas gradually moved the centre of world trade westwards. Europe now emerged as the centre of world trade.

Two careful points:

  • The order of cause and effect. China's retreat and the Americas' rise came first; Europe then emerged as the centre. Do not write that China retreated because Europe had become the centre.
  • "Is said to have". Your textbook is careful: China "is said to have" restricted overseas contacts. Keep that phrase.

New word. Pre-eminent — the most important, ahead of all others (our definition).

Worked example 1

A dissenter's decision

Question. Jakob lives in a crowded European town a few centuries ago. He refuses to accept the beliefs of the established church, and fever has already killed two of his neighbours. Using your textbook, give two reasons why someone like Jakob might leave for America. (2 marks) (Jakob is imagined; the reasons are from your textbook.)

Asked: two reasons, linked to the textbook's description of Europe.

Concept used: Why thousands fled Europe for America (this topic, section 1).

  1. 1

    Religious persecution: Jakob is a dissenter — one who refuses to accept established beliefs; your textbook says religious dissenters were persecuted and religious conflicts were common.

  2. 2

    Disease and crowding: cities were crowded and deadly diseases were widespread, as the fever in his town shows.

  3. Check: each reason must be one your textbook gives and must fit Jakob's details. "He wanted to see the plantations" fits neither test.

  4. Answer Religious dissenters were persecuted, and crowded towns with widespread deadly diseases made life dangerous — so thousands fled to America.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. As a dissenter refusing established beliefs, Jakob could face persecution, since religious dissenters were persecuted in Europe. (1)
    2. Crowded cities and widespread deadly diseases, like the fever in his town, made people flee to America. (1)
    Common trap: writing "he went to work as a slave". Slaves were captured in Africa; Europeans like Jakob went to America to escape hardship.

Worked example 2

How Europe became the centre

Question. Explain how the centre of world trade moved westwards and Europe became its centre. (5 marks)

Asked: the steps, in cause → effect order, ending with Europe as the centre.

Concept used: The centre of world trade moves west (section 4), with the sea routes and silver from Topic 4 and the plantations from section 2.

  1. 1

    The old centre: until well into the eighteenth century, China and India were among the world's richest countries and pre-eminent in Asian trade.

  2. 2

    China withdraws: from the fifteenth century, China is said to have restricted overseas contacts and retreated into isolation.

  3. 3

    New routes: in the sixteenth century European sailors found a sea route to Asia and crossed to America, and the world "shrank" (Topic 4).

  4. 4

    The Americas rise: silver from Peru and Mexico enhanced Europe's wealth and financed its trade with Asia; plantations worked by African slaves grew cotton and sugar for European markets.

  5. 5

    The result: China's reduced role and the rising importance of the Americas gradually moved the centre of world trade westwards; Europe emerged as the centre.

  6. Check: five points, each a cause or a step, and the last one the effect. A Check you can do: read your points in reverse — if point 5 could come first, the order is wrong.

  7. Answer China withdrew, while new sea routes and America's silver and plantations made the Americas important — so the centre moved west and Europe became the centre of world trade.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    1. Until well into the eighteenth century, China and India were among the richest countries and pre-eminent in Asian trade. (1)
    2. From the fifteenth century, China is said to have restricted overseas contacts and retreated into isolation. (1)
    3. In the sixteenth century European sailors found a sea route to Asia and crossed the ocean to America. (1)
    4. Silver from Peru and Mexico financed Europe's trade with Asia; slave plantations grew cotton and sugar for European markets. (1)
    5. China's reduced role and the Americas' rising importance gradually moved the centre of world trade westwards to Europe. (1)
    Common trap: stopping at China's isolation. The textbook gives two causes: China's reduced role and the rising importance of the Americas.

Worked example 3

Reading NCERT Fig. 5

Question. Look at NCERT Fig. 5, Slaves for sale, New Orleans (1851). (a) What is happening in the picture? (b) Why, says the caption, were the slaves dressed in their best clothes? (c) What can this picture not tell us? (3 marks)

Asked: three parts — the scene, the reason for the clothes, a limit.

Concept used: Reading a source — Fig. 5 (section 3).

  1. 1

    (a) A buyer is inspecting enslaved men, women and children lined up before an auction, where they will be sold.

  2. 2

    (b) To attract buyers.

  3. 3

    (c) It is one moment drawn for a London magazine; it cannot tell us the names, feelings or lives of the people being sold.

  4. Check: (b) must use the caption's reason. "Because it was a festival" is a guess and fails the Check.

  5. Answer (a) Enslaved people are being inspected before an auction. (b) To attract buyers. (c) It does not tell us who they were or what they felt.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    (a) A prospective buyer inspects enslaved men, women and children lined up before an auction. (1)
    (b) They were dressed in their best clothes to attract buyers. (1)
    (c) It is one outsider's picture of a moment; it cannot tell us the people's names, feelings or lives. (1)

    Common trap: taking the picture as proof that slaves were treated well. The good clothes were for selling, not a sign of good treatment.

By the eighteenth century, what did the plantations in America worked by slaves captured in Africa grow, and for whom?

Potatoes and maize were American foods that travelled to Europe and Asia (Topic 3), not plantation crops in this passage.
Silk and spices are the silk-route goods of Asia (Topic 2).
"…plantations worked by slaves captured in Africa were growing cotton and sugar for European markets."
Your textbook names neither crop nor African markets here.
Quick check · 3 of 3MCQ1 mark

Which pair of causes, according to your textbook, gradually moved the centre of world trade westwards?

These are reasons why people left Europe, not why trade moved there.
"China's reduced role and the rising importance of the Americas gradually moved the centre of world trade westwards."
Your textbook speaks of China's isolation, not India's; the silk routes thrived only till about the fifteenth century.
Neither is given as a cause of the move.

Common mistakes!

  • "People fled Europe in the nineteenth century."

    Your textbook says until the nineteenth century these hardships were common.

    Write "until the nineteenth century".

  • "Slaves were taken from America to Africa."

    Slaves were captured in Africa and worked plantations in America.

    Direction: Africa → America; crops: America → Europe.

  • "China retreated because Europe became the centre of trade."

    China's retreat and the Americas' rise were the causes; Europe's rise was the effect.

    Write the chain: China's reduced role + Americas → centre moves west → Europe.

  • "India withdrew from trade."

    Your textbook speaks of China restricting overseas contacts.

    Keep the country right: China.

  • "China restricted contacts — it is a proven fact."

    Your textbook says China "is said to have" done so.

    Keep "is said to have".

Key points

  • Until the nineteenth century, Europe had poverty and hunger, crowded cities, widespread deadly diseases, and religious conflicts in which dissenters (those who refuse established beliefs and practices) were persecuted — so thousands fled Europe for America.
  • By the eighteenth century, plantations in America worked by slaves captured in Africa grew cotton and sugar for European markets.
  • NCERT Fig. 5 (Illustrated London News, 1851): slaves for sale in New Orleans, inspected by a buyer, dressed in their best clothes to attract buyers.
  • Until well into the eighteenth century, China and India were among the world's richest countries and pre-eminent in Asian trade.
  • From the fifteenth century, China is said to have restricted overseas contacts and retreated into isolation.
  • China's reduced role + the rising importance of the Americas → the centre of world trade moved westwards → Europe emerged as the centre of world trade.
