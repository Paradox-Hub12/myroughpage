How this chapter is asked

Only the board part of the chapter (the pre-modern world, Topics 1–5) is examined. In 2024–2026 it gave 1 to 3 marks in every series: usually a 1-mark item (an MCQ, a statement or assertion–reason item, or a fill in the blank) and/or a 2-mark "explain with two examples" answer; one 3-mark choice in 2024; no 4- or 5-mark question and no case study. The topics asked most: the silk routes, diseases and the conquest of America, food and the potato, the shrinking world and the Indian Ocean, and why people left Europe. Both sample papers give it 3 marks (a 1-mark item and a 2-mark answer).

About the marks

The sample-paper answers follow the official marking schemes. No marking scheme is published for the board papers, so their mark splits are our marking points, written to the CBSE word limits (2 marks — up to 40 words, 3 marks — up to 60). Where an official question is printed with a slip, or has more than one defensible option and no published key, it is quoted exactly and a note explains the textbook answer.

About the 2025-26 sample paper

The 2025-26 answers follow the official marking scheme; the 2025-26 question texts are from the copy of the sample paper on the CBSE academic website.

CBSE Sample Question Paper 2026-27, Section A, Q31 mark

Question: Read the statements given below and choose the correct answer:
Statement I: A good example of thriving pre- modern commercial and cultural ties connecting far flung regions of the world are silk routes.
Statement II: The significance of Chinese silk cargoes traveling the silk route to the west is indicated by the term "silk routes".

Asked: whether each statement is true.

Concept used: What were the silk routes? (Topic 2).

  1. 1

    Statement I — true: your textbook calls the silk routes "a good example of vibrant pre-modern trade and cultural links between distant parts of the world".

  2. 2

    Statement II — true: "The name 'silk routes' points to the importance of West-bound Chinese silk cargoes along this route."

  3. Check: both statements are the textbook's sentences in other words, so the only fit is "both correct".

    Common trap: marking Statement II wrong because other goods also travelled. The statement is only about what the name means, and that is right.
Option (C): Both (I) and (II) are correct
1 mark
CBSE Sample Question Paper 2026-27, Section A, Q52 marks

Question: 'Most powerful weapon of the Spanish conqueror was not a conventional military weapon at all.' Justify the statement by giving two reasons.

Asked: two reasons why germs, not ordinary weapons, were the Spanish conquerors' most powerful weapon.

Concept used: Conquest: germs, not guns (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    The weapon: the germs, such as those of smallpox, that the Spanish carried on their person; once introduced, smallpox spread deep into the continent, ahead even of any Europeans.

  2. 2

    Why it worked: because of their long isolation, America's original inhabitants had no immunity; smallpox killed and decimated whole communities, paving the way for conquest.

  3. Check: the scheme gives two points — the germs spreading ahead of the Europeans, and no immunity leading to conquest. Two reasons, two marks.

    Common trap: writing about the Spaniards' guns and horses. The statement says the weapon was not a conventional one.
  1. The Spanish carried germs such as smallpox, which spread deep into the continent even before any European reached there. (1)
  1. Long isolated, America's original inhabitants had no immunity; smallpox decimated whole communities, paving the way for conquest. (1)
2 marks

Note on the official marking scheme: point 1 says the Spanish "used germs like smallpox", and point 2 calls it "biological warfare". Your textbook says the conquerors carried the germs "on their person", and its Box 1 title asks "'Biological' warfare?" with a question mark. The answer above follows the textbook.

  2. 2

    Match: option A takes two of these words exactly — poverty and deadly diseases.

  3. 3

    The others: wars, climate and natural disasters are not in the textbook's list.

  4. Check: the marking scheme gives A.

CBSE Sample Question Paper 2025-26, Section A, Q5A2 marks

Question: 'The Silk route was a good example of vibrant pre-modern trade and cultural links between distant parts of the world.' Explain the statement with any two examples.

Asked: two examples showing trade and cultural links along the silk routes.

Concept used: Silk routes link the world (Topic 2).

  1. 1

    Trade: Chinese pottery travelled the same route, as did textiles and spices from India and Southeast Asia; gold and silver flowed from Europe to Asia in return.

  2. 2

    Culture — Buddhism: Buddhism emerged from eastern India and spread in several directions through intersecting points on the silk routes.

  3. 3

    Culture — missionaries and preachers: early Christian missionaries almost certainly travelled the route to Asia, and early Muslim preachers a few centuries later.

  4. Check: the scheme lists these three points and asks for any two. One trade example and one cultural example covers both halves of the statement.

    Common trap: two trade examples only. The statement says "trade and cultural links".
  1. Chinese pottery and Indian and Southeast Asian textiles and spices went west; gold and silver flowed from Europe to Asia. (1)
  1. Trade and culture went together: Buddhism spread from eastern India along the silk routes. (1) — 2 marks (any two of the scheme's three points)
CBSE Sample Question Paper 2025-26, Section A, Q5B2 marks

Question: ‘Sometimes the new crops could make the difference between life and death.’. Explain the statement.

Asked: how a new crop could mean life — and how it could mean death.

Concept used: The potato: life and death (Topic 3).

  1. 1

    Life: with the introduction of the humble potato, Europe's poor began to eat better and live longer.

  2. 2

    Death: Ireland's poorest peasants became so dependent on potatoes that when disease destroyed the crop in the mid-1840s, hundreds of thousands died of starvation.

  3. Check: the scheme has exactly these two points: one for life, one for death.

    Common trap: writing two points about the famine and none about the benefit. The statement has two sides.
  1. With the potato, Europe's poor began to eat better and live longer. (1)
  1. Ireland's poorest depended on it so much that when disease destroyed the crop in the mid-1840s, hundreds of thousands starved. (1)
2 marks

Board 2026, 32/1/1 Q2 · 32/1/2 Q3 · 32/1/3 Q31 mark

Question: Two statements are given below. They are Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Read both the statements and choose the correct option: Assertion (A): The Indian subcontinent was a crucial point in 'Indian Ocean trade flows' during sixteenth century. Reason (R): Its geographical location connected East-West Asia and Europe.
(A) Both, (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A). (B) Both, (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A). (C) (A) is true, but (R) is false. (D) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

Asked: whether A and R are true, and whether R explains A.

Concept used: The Indian Ocean trade (Topic 4, section 1).

  1. 1

    A: your textbook says the Indian Ocean had a bustling trade "for centuries before" the sixteenth century, and "the Indian subcontinent was central to these flows and a crucial point in their networks". Read as the textbook's statement, A is true.

  2. 2

    R: your textbook does not give a reason. (Beyond your textbook: the subcontinent's position between West Asia and East and Southeast Asia, in the middle of the ocean's sea lanes, is the usual explanation of its central place.) Accepted as true, R explains why it was a crucial point.

  3. Check: ask "Why was the subcontinent a crucial point?" — R gives a reason of location that answers it.

    Common trap: calling A false because the Europeans had arrived by the sixteenth century. The Europeans expanded or redirected some flows; the textbook still calls the subcontinent central to them.
Our key: the first option — both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
1 mark

Note: No official answer key has been published for this question. Our key, with the reason, is above. Two cautions: your textbook places the subcontinent's central role in the centuries before the sixteenth century, and it gives no reason like R — so the second option can also be argued.

Question: How had globalization begun long before sixteenth century ? Explain.

    Common trap: describing modern globalisation (the last 50 years). The question asks how it began long before.
  1. As early as 3000 BCE, coastal trade linked the Indus valley civilisations with present-day West Asia. (1)
  1. From before the Christian Era, silk routes linked Asia with Europe and northern Africa in trade and culture. (1)

Board 2026, 32/1/1 · 32/1/2 · 32/1/3 Q5 (B)2 marks

Question: How did trade help to spread diseases during the pre-modern world ? Explain.

Asked: how travel for trade carried diseases.

Concept used: The pre-modern world (Topic 1); conquest and disease (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    Germs travelled with goods: traders, travellers and pilgrims carried goods, money, ideas — and even germs and diseases; this long-distance spread can be traced back to the seventh century.

  2. 2

    An example: in the sixteenth century, germs such as smallpox, carried by the Spanish conquerors, spread deep into America and decimated whole communities.

  3. Check: point 1 explains how; point 2 gives a case. Two separate points.

    Common trap: writing only "germs spread". Say who carried them and give the date or the case.
  1. Traders and travellers carried germs and diseases with their goods; this spread is traced back to the seventh century. (1)
  1. In the sixteenth century, smallpox carried by the Spanish spread deep into America, decimating whole communities. (1)

Board 2026, 32/3/1 Q2 · 32/3/2 Q4 · 32/3/3 Q51 mark

Question: Two statements labelled as Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are given below. Read both the statements carefully and choose the correct option: Assertion (A): The Portuguese and Spanish conquest and colonisation of America was decisively under way by the mid-sixteenth century. Reason (R): The Spanish conquerors had the most powerful conventional weapons.
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A). (B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A). (C) Assertion (A) is true, but Reason (R) is false. (D) Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.

Asked: whether A and R are true, and whether R explains A.

Concept used: Conquest: germs, not guns (Topic 4, section 3).

  1. 1

    A is true: these are your textbook's words.

  2. 2

    R is true: your textbook speaks of the conquerors' "superior firepower". But it says conquest was "not just" a result of this; their most powerful weapon was "not a conventional military weapon at all" — it was germs such as smallpox. So R does not explain A.

  3. Check: ask "Does R give the reason for A, in your textbook's words?" The textbook's reason is germs, not conventional weapons — so the explanation fails, while R itself is not denied.

    Common trap: choosing the first option because the Spanish did have guns. Your textbook says conquest was "not just a result of superior firepower".
Our key: the second option — both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
1 mark

Note: No official answer key has been published for this question. Our key, with the textbook reason, is above. The third option (A is true, but R is false) can also be argued, by reading R's "most powerful" against the textbook's line that the most powerful weapon was not a conventional one; but the textbook does not deny that the conquerors had superior firepower.
