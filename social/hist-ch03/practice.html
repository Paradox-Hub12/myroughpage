Full solution

Asked: how the centre moved to Europe despite China and India's wealth.

Concept used: The centre of world trade moves west (Topic 5).

  1. 1

    The starting point: China and India were among the richest countries and pre-eminent in Asian trade.

  2. 2

    China withdraws: from the fifteenth century China is said to have restricted overseas contacts and retreated into isolation.

  3. 3

    The Americas rise: America's silver, crops and plantations grew in importance; China's reduced role and the Americas' rise moved the centre of trade westwards, and Europe emerged as its centre.

  4. Check: point 3 must come after points 1 and 2 — it is the effect.

  5. Answer China withdrew and the Americas rose in importance, so the centre of world trade moved west to Europe.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Until well into the eighteenth century China and India were among the richest countries and pre-eminent in Asian trade. (1)
    2. From the fifteenth century China is said to have restricted overseas contacts and retreated into isolation. (1)
    3. China's reduced role and the rising importance of the Americas moved the centre of trade westwards to Europe. (1)
    Common trap: writing "China retreated because Europe became powerful". China's retreat is a cause, not an effect.

Full solution

Asked: five changes of the sixteenth century, with their causes.

Concept used: Topics 3 and 4. Why it applies: the sea voyages, America's silver and crops, and the conquest all belong to the sixteenth century.

  1. 1

    New routes: European sailors found a sea route to Asia and crossed the western ocean to America; the world "shrank".

  2. 2

    Old flows redirected: in the bustling Indian Ocean trade, the Europeans expanded or redirected some flows towards Europe.

  3. 3

    America's silver: silver from mines in present-day Peru and Mexico enhanced Europe's wealth and financed its trade with Asia.

  4. 4

    New foods: potatoes, maize, tomatoes, chillies and other foods from America reached Europe and Asia.
