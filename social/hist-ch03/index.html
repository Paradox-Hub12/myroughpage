History · Chapter 3

The Making of a Global World

6 topicsAbout 8 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

The potatoes in your sabzi, the chillies in your chutney and the groundnuts in your snack all came to India from America — about five centuries ago. Long before that, silk from China travelled west along the silk routes, cowrie shells from the Maldives were used as money in China and East Africa, and traders, priests and pilgrims crossed vast distances carrying goods, ideas — and germs. This chapter shows that the world was linked up long before modern times, and how, in the sixteenth century, sea voyages, silver, smallpox and slavery shrank the world and made Europe the centre of world trade.

For the board exam, only the first part of the chapter counts: the pre-modern world (your textbook's sections 1 to 1.3 — Topics 1 to 5 here). It is one of the four History chapters in the board exam (History carries 18 marks + 2 map marks). In the CBSE board papers of 2024, 2025 and 2026 it gave 1 to 3 marks in every series — usually a 1-mark question (an MCQ, an assertion–reason or a fill in the blank) and/or a 2-mark "explain with two examples" answer. The rest of the chapter is for a school project (see "Not in the board exam" below).

What you'll learn

  • Explain why the global world is old, and give the early examples: the Indus valley and West Asia, cowries, germs, ships on memorial stones.
  • Describe the silk routes, say what travelled each way, and explain how trade and culture went together.
  • Explain how food shows long-distance contact, name the foods from America, and explain how the potato meant life and then death.
  • Explain why the world "shrank" in the sixteenth century, how America's silver changed trade, and why germs were the conquerors' strongest weapon.
  • Explain why thousands fled Europe for America, describe the slave plantations, and explain how the centre of world trade moved west.
  • Get an overview of the rest of the chapter — 1815 to the 1970s — for your interdisciplinary project.
  • Put the board part of the chapter in order and answer mixed board-style questions.

Most of the marks in this chapter come from four skills, and every topic practises them:

  1. Know what travelled which way — silk and spices west, gold and silver east; foods out of America, germs into America.
  2. Explain with two examples — two separate points, each a textbook fact linked to the statement.
  3. Link cause → effect — no immunity → smallpox → conquest; China's retreat + the Americas → Europe the centre of trade.
  4. Keep the order of events — every topic ends with a mini-timeline; the wrap-up joins them into one.

The spaced-review sets come back to you after 1 day, 1 week and 1 month. Don't skip them — remembering something after a gap is what makes it stick.

Not in the board exam

Only sections 1 to 1.3 of this chapter (the pre-modern world — Topics 1 to 5 here) are in the 2026-27 board exam. Sections 2 to 4.4 — the nineteenth century (1815–1914), the inter-war economy and the Great Depression, and the rebuilding of the world economy up to the end of the Bretton Woods system — are an interdisciplinary project with Geography Chapter 7 and Economics Chapter 4, marked by your school (5 marks of internal assessment). Topic 6 gives a short overview of that part, and your textbook's questions on it are answered on the NCERT solutions page under "For your project — not in the board exam". There is no practice, test or revision question on that part. Everything in sections 1 to 1.3 — including Figs 1–5, Box 1 and the "New words" box — can be asked in the board exam. This chapter has no map items in the board exam.
