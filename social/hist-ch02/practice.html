P14●● StandardShort answer · 3 marksOriginal

Explain the chain of events by which the defeat of Ottoman Turkey in the First World War became part of India's Non-Cooperation Movement.

Full solution

Asked: cause → event → effect, from Turkey's defeat to Non-Cooperation.

Concept used: The Khilafat issue (Topic 2).

  1. 1

    Cause: the war ended with Ottoman Turkey's defeat; rumours spread of a harsh treaty on the Ottoman emperor, the Khalifa — spiritual head of the Islamic world.

  2. 2

    Event: to defend the Khalifa's temporal powers, a Khilafat Committee was formed in Bombay in March 1919; Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali discussed united mass action with Gandhiji.

  3. 3

    Effect: Gandhiji saw a chance to bring Muslims into a unified national movement; at Calcutta (September 1920) he convinced leaders to start non-cooperation for Khilafat as well as swaraj.

  4. Check: the three points follow the dates (1918 defeat → March 1919 committee → September 1920 session); putting the Calcutta session first would turn the effect into a cause.

  5. Answer Turkey's defeat threatened the Khalifa → Khilafat Committee (Bombay, March 1919) → Gandhiji joined the Khilafat cause with swaraj at Calcutta (September 1920), creating the Non-Cooperation–Khilafat Movement.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Ottoman Turkey was defeated in the war, and a harsh treaty on its emperor, the Khalifa, was rumoured. (1)
    2. A Khilafat Committee was formed in Bombay (March 1919) to defend his temporal powers; the Ali brothers discussed united action with Gandhiji. (1)
    3. To bring Muslims into the movement, Gandhiji won support at Calcutta (September 1920) for non-cooperation for Khilafat and swaraj. (1)
    Common trap: writing that the Khilafat movement opposed the Khalifa. It set out to defend him.

  2. 2

  5. Answer Assam workers sought freedom to go home; Gudem tribals sought forest rights through force — neither was in the Congress programme — yet both identified with an all-India struggle through Gandhiji's name.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. For Assam plantation workers, swaraj meant freedom to leave the tea gardens and go home, where "Gandhi Raj" would give them land. (1)

    3. Yet by chanting Gandhiji's name and "Swatantra Bharat", they identified with an all-India movement. (1)

P27●●● AdvancedLong answer · 5 marksOriginal

"The Civil Disobedience Movement could not carry every group with it." Examine five limits of the movement.

Full solution

Asked: five limits, each explained.

Concept used: Topic 5 (all sections). Why it applies: each group that held back, or was held back, is one limit.

  1. 1

    Poor peasants: the Congress would not back their "no rent" campaigns, so the relationship stayed uncertain.

  2. 2

    Industrial workers: they joined in large numbers only in the Nagpur region; the Congress kept their demands out to avoid alienating industrialists.

  3. 3

    Dalits: many wanted separate electorates; their participation was limited, especially in Maharashtra and Nagpur.

  4. 4

    Muslims: many felt alienated after Khilafat and feared domination by a Hindu majority; they did not respond to the call for united struggle.

  5. 5

    Women and rich peasants: women's role stayed symbolic; rich peasants left in 1932 when revenue was not revised.

  6. Check: five separate groups, each with its reason — a list of five group names without reasons would earn little, because each mark is for an explained point.

  7. Answer Poor peasants, workers, dalits and many Muslims held back for their own reasons; women's role stayed symbolic and rich peasants left in 1932.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    1. Poor peasants wanted unpaid rent remitted, but the Congress, unwilling to upset rich peasants and landlords, did not back "no rent" campaigns in most places. (1)
    2. Industrial workers stayed largely aloof except in the Nagpur region; the Congress kept out their demands to avoid alienating industrialists. (1)
    3. Many dalit leaders wanted separate electorates and reserved seats in educational institutions; dalit participation was limited, especially in Maharashtra and Nagpur. (1)
    4. Many Muslims, alienated after the Khilafat movement and fearing a Hindu majority, did not respond to the call for united struggle. (1)
    5. Women's role brought no radical change in their position, and rich peasants refused to rejoin in 1932 as revenue rates were not revised. (1)
    Common trap: writing five points about the successes of the movement. "Limits" means who was left out and why.

(Ramu is an imagined worker; the events around him are from your textbook.)

(a) Which law did not permit workers like Ramu to leave the tea garden? (1)

Full solution

Asked: the law; the meaning of swaraj; what happened and what it shows.

Concept used: Swaraj in the plantations (Topic 3).

  1. 1

    (a) The Inland Emigration Act of 1859.

  2. 2

    (b) The right to move freely in and out of the confined space of the garden and to keep a link with their home villages — they believed "Gandhi Raj" would give them land there.

  3. 3

    (c) What happened: stranded by a railway and steamer strike, they were caught by the police and brutally beaten. What it shows: groups interpreted swaraj in their own ways, beyond the Congress programme, yet felt part of an all-India movement through Gandhiji's name.

  4. Check: (b) must be the workers' meaning; "lower revenue" would be a peasant's meaning and fails.
