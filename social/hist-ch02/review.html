Week 1

Set 2 — Week 1

5 questions, about 8 minutes

Focus: the reasons behind the facts.

W1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

Why did Gandhiji feel, after the Rowlatt satyagraha, that a new kind of movement was needed?

Answer: The Rowlatt satyagraha had stayed mostly in cities and towns; he wanted a more broad-based movement, which could not be built without bringing Hindus and Muslims closer together. (Topic 2)

Did you get it?

W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

Why did the Justice Party of Madras decide to contest the council elections of 1920?

Answer: As the party of the non-Brahmans, it felt that entering the council was a way of gaining some power — something usually only Brahmans had. (Topic 3)

Did you get it?

W1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

How did the worldwide depression affect Indian peasants by 1930?

Answer: Farm prices fell from 1926 and collapsed after 1930; as demand and exports fell, peasants could not sell their harvests or pay their revenue — the countryside was in turmoil. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

On what condition was Jinnah willing to give up the demand for separate electorates?

Answer: If Muslims were assured reserved seats in the Central Assembly and representation in proportion to population in the Muslim-majority provinces (Bengal and Punjab). (Topic 5)

Did you get it?

W1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

Name the three places on the map list that are in Gujarat, from north to south.

Answer: Ahmedabad → Kheda → Dandi ("mill, field, sea"). (Topic 7)

Month 1

Set 3 — Month 1

5 questions, about 12 minutes

Focus: linking ideas across the chapter.

M1.1 · 1 of 5●●● Advanced

Peasants joined both great movements. Compare what peasants wanted in Awadh in 1920–21 with what rich peasants wanted in 1930.

Answer: Awadh (1920–21): tenants under talukdars wanted reduction of revenue, an end to begar and social boycott of oppressive landlords. Rich peasants (1930): producers of commercial crops hit by the Depression wanted the government's revenue demand reduced. Both were rural, but the Awadh movement was against landlords, the 1930 one against the government's revenue. (Topics 3 and 5)

Did you get it?

M1.2 · 2 of 5●●● Advanced

Both great movements were called off by Gandhiji. Give the reason and the year for each.

Answer: Non-Cooperation — February 1922: it was turning violent (Chauri Chaura) and satyagrahis needed training. Civil Disobedience — 1931: amid brutal repression and violent clashes, he called it off and made the Gandhi–Irwin Pact (5 March 1931); relaunched in 1932, it lost momentum by 1934. (Topics 3 and 4)

Did you get it?

M1.3 · 3 of 5●●● Advanced

Give two examples from the chapter of efforts at unity that also made some groups feel left out.

Answer: (1) Bharat Mata and a glorified past drawn from Hindu iconography made other communities feel left out. (2) To keep industrialists in the united anti-imperial front, the Congress kept workers' demands out of its programme, so many industrial workers stayed aloof (except in the Nagpur region). (Topics 5 and 6)

Did you get it?

M1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

Which two of the eight map places are linked with the year 1920, and how are they different?

Answer: Calcutta (September 1920 — Gandhiji convinced the leaders) and Nagpur (December 1920 — the programme was adopted). (Topics 2 and 7)

Did you get it?

M1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

Name two women from the Quit India Movement box and their regions.

Answer: Any two: Matangini Hazra (Bengal), Kanaklata Barua (Assam), Rama Devi (Odisha). (Topic 6)
