Topic 3 of 8

Cropping seasons: rabi, kharif and zaid

About 35 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Distinguish the three cropping seasons by their sowing and harvest time and their crops.
  • Explain why rabi crops succeed in the north and north-west, and name the three paddy crops of Assam, West Bengal and Odisha.

In February, the fields of the Malwa plateau are green with wheat. Come in August and you see paddy standing in water. Come back in May and you may find watermelons and cucumbers. The fields are the same; the season is different. India has three cropping seasons — rabi, kharif and zaid.

Quick reminder (Class 9, Climate)

Most of India's rain comes with the monsoon in the summer and rainy months. In winter, the north and north-west also get a little rain from weather systems coming from the west — your textbook calls them western temperate cyclones.

  1. India's crops reflect its diversity

India's physical diversity and plurality of cultures are reflected in its agricultural practices and cropping patterns. Food and fibre crops, vegetables and fruits, spices and condiments are all grown. India has three cropping seasons — rabi, kharif and zaid.

  1. The three seasons

Season Sown · harvested Crops (your textbook)
Rabi sown in winter, October–December · harvested in summer, April–June wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard
Kharif grown with the onset of monsoon · harvested in September–October paddy, maize, jowar, bajra, tur (arhar), moong, urad, cotton, jute, groundnut, soyabean
Zaid a short season during the summer months, between rabi and kharif watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder crops

Sugarcane does not fit one season: it takes almost a year to grow.

Memory hooks:

  • "Rabi: sow Oct–Dec, reap Apr–Jun; Kharif: sow with the monsoon, reap Sep–Oct; Zaid: summer, in between."
  • Rabi's five crops: "Wheat, Barley, Peas, Gram, Mustard" — winter crops of the north.

  1. Rabi crops: where and why

  • Rabi crops are grown in large parts of India, but states from the north and north-west — Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Uttarakhand and Uttar Pradesh — are important for wheat and other rabi crops.
  • Why they succeed there (two reasons):
    1. Precipitation during the winter months due to the western temperate cyclones;
    2. the success of the green revolution in Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh and parts of Rajasthan (Topic 7).

  1. Kharif crops: where

  • Some of the most important rice-growing regions are Assam, West Bengal, coastal regions of Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Maharashtra (particularly the Konkan coast), along with Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.
  • Recently, paddy has also become an important crop of Punjab and Haryana.
  • Three crops of paddy a year: in Assam, West Bengal and Odisha, three crops of paddy are grown in a year — Aus, Aman and Boro.

  1. Reading the calendar

Month What is happening
October–December rabi sowing (kharif harvest ends by October)
January–March rabi crops growing (winter rain in the north-west helps)
April–June rabi harvest; then the short zaid season in the summer months
onset of monsoon kharif sowing
September–October kharif harvest

(Your textbook gives months for rabi sowing and harvest and for the kharif harvest. It gives no months for kharif sowing — only "with the onset of monsoon" — and none for zaid beyond "the summer months".)

Worked example 1

Name the season from the calendar

Question. An imagined farmer, Gurnam, keeps a diary. Name the season of each entry: (a) "Sowed mustard in the second week of November." (b) "Sowed jowar as soon as the monsoon rains arrived." (c) "Harvested muskmelons in the short gap before the rains." (d) "Cut the gram crop in early April."

Asked: four seasons.

Concept used: The three seasons (section 2).

  1. 1

    (a) Mustard, sown in November (October–December) → rabi.

  2. 2

    (b) Jowar, sown with the onset of monsoon → kharif.

  3. 3

    (c) Muskmelon, in the short summer gap → zaid.

  4. 4

    (d) Gram, harvested in April (April–June) → rabi.

  5. Check: each crop is in its season's list in the table, and each month fits that season.

  6. Answer (a) rabi; (b) kharif; (c) zaid; (d) rabi.

    Common trap: calling (d) kharif because April is hot. Rabi crops are harvested in summer.

Worked example 2

Why wheat in the north-west?

Question. "Punjab and Haryana are important for wheat and other rabi crops." Give two reasons from your textbook. (2 marks)

Asked: two reasons for rabi success in the north-west.

Concept used: Rabi crops: where and why (section 3).

  1. 1

    Winter rain: precipitation during the winter months due to the western temperate cyclones helps rabi crops.

  2. 2

    Green revolution: its success in Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh and parts of Rajasthan has been an important factor.

  3. Check: both reasons are the textbook's own; neither is "the monsoon", which brings the kharif season.

  4. Answer Winter rain from western temperate cyclones, and the success of the green revolution.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. Precipitation in the winter months from the western temperate cyclones helps the rabi crops succeed. (1)
    2. The success of the green revolution in Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh and parts of Rajasthan helped their growth. (1)
    Common trap: writing "heavy monsoon rain". Rabi crops grow in winter; the monsoon belongs to the kharif season.

Worked example 3

Three paddy crops

Question. (a) In which three states does your textbook say three crops of paddy are grown in a year? (b) Name the three crops. (c) In which cropping season does your textbook list moong and urad?

Asked: three states, three crop names, the season of two pulses.

Concept used: Kharif crops: where (section 4).

  1. 1

    (a) Assam, West Bengal and Odisha.

  2. 2

    (b) Aus, Aman and Boro.

  3. 3

    (c) Moong and urad are in the kharif list (with paddy, maize, jowar, bajra, tur, cotton, jute, groundnut and soyabean).

  4. Check: the three states are in the east and north-east; Punjab and Haryana grow paddy too, but your textbook does not say they grow three crops a year.

  5. Answer (a) Assam, West Bengal, Odisha; (b) Aus, Aman, Boro; (c) kharif.

    Common trap: writing "Aus, Aman and Boro are the three seasons of India". They are three paddy crops in three eastern and north-eastern states.

Common mistakes!

  • Rabi and kharif swapped.

    Rabi: sown October–December, harvested April–June. Kharif: sown with the monsoon, harvested September–October.

    Use the hook: "Rabi: sow Oct–Dec, reap Apr–Jun."

  • Gram, peas or mustard called kharif; paddy or maize called rabi.

    Rabi: wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard. Kharif: paddy, maize, jowar, bajra, tur, moong, urad, cotton, jute, groundnut, soyabean.

    Learn the five rabi crops; most others in the list are kharif.

  • "Sugarcane is a zaid crop."

    Your textbook says sugarcane takes almost a year to grow.

    Keep sugarcane out of the three short-season lists.

  • "Rabi crops succeed in Punjab because of the monsoon."

    The reasons are winter precipitation from western temperate cyclones and the green revolution.

    Give both textbook reasons.

  • "Aus, Aman and Boro are grown in Punjab."

    They are three paddy crops grown in Assam, West Bengal and Odisha.

    Learn them with the three eastern and north-eastern states.

Key points

  • Rabi: sown October–December, harvested April–June; wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard; important in Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh; helped by winter rain from western temperate cyclones and the green revolution.
  • Kharif: sown with the onset of monsoon, harvested September–October; paddy, maize, jowar, bajra, tur (arhar), moong, urad, cotton, jute, groundnut, soyabean.
  • Zaid: a short summer season between rabi and kharif; watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder crops. Sugarcane takes almost a year.
  • Rice regions: Assam, West Bengal, coastal Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra (Konkan coast), Uttar Pradesh, Bihar; recently Punjab and Haryana. Aus, Aman, Boro — three paddy crops in Assam, West Bengal, Odisha.
