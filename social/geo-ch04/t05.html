Topic 5 of 8

Food crops other than grains: sugarcane, oil seeds, tea, coffee and horticulture

About 45 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Describe the conditions and major states of sugarcane, tea and coffee.
  • Describe oil seeds (their seasons and uses) and India's horticulture crops with their states.

Your morning cup of tea or coffee, the sugar in it, the oil in the kitchen and the banana in your lunch box all come from farms — but not from grain fields. This topic covers the food crops other than grains: sugarcane, oil seeds, tea, coffee and horticulture (fruits and vegetables).

  1. Sugarcane

Your textbook says
Climate type a tropical as well as a subtropical crop
Climate grows well in a hot and humid climate
Temperature 21°C to 27°C
Rainfall between 75 cm and 100 cm; irrigation is required in regions of low rainfall
Soil can be grown on a variety of soils
Labour needs manual labour from sowing to harvesting
India's rank the second largest producer of sugarcane, only after Brazil
Products the main source of sugar, gur (jaggary), khandsari and molasses
Major states Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Punjab and Haryana

Spelling note: your textbook prints "jaggary"; the usual spelling is jaggery. Sugarcane also takes almost a year to grow (Topic 3). NCERT Fig. 4.8 shows a sugarcane field.

  1. Oil seeds

  • Rank: in 2018 India was the second largest producer of groundnut in the world, after China, and the third largest producer of rapeseed, after Canada and China.
  • Area: different oil seeds cover approximately 12 per cent of the total cropped area of the country.
  • Main oil seeds: groundnut, mustard, coconut, sesamum (til), soyabean, castor seeds, cotton seeds, linseed and sunflower.
  • Uses: most are edible and used as cooking mediums; some are also raw material for soap, cosmetics and ointments.
Oil seed Season (your textbook)
Groundnut kharif; accounts for about half of the major oilseeds; Gujarat was the largest producer, followed by Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu, in 2019–20
Linseed, mustard rabi
Sesamum kharif in the north, rabi in the south
Castor seed both rabi and kharif

NCERT Fig. 4.9 shows groundnut, sunflower and mustard ready for harvest.

  1. Tea

Your textbook says
Type an example of plantation agriculture; an important beverage crop
History introduced in India initially by the British; today most of the tea plantations are owned by Indians
Climate grows well in tropical and sub-tropical climates
Soil deep and fertile well-drained soil, rich in humus and organic matter
Temperature and rain warm and moist frost-free climate all through the year; frequent showers evenly distributed over the year ensure continuous growth of tender leaves
Labour a labour-intensive industry; needs abundant, cheap and skilled labour
Processing processed within the tea garden to restore its freshness
India's rank in 2018, the second largest producer of tea, after China
Major states Assam, the hills of Darjeeling and Jalpaiguri districts (West Bengal), Tamil Nadu and Kerala; also Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Meghalaya, Andhra Pradesh and Tripura

  1. Coffee

Your textbook says
Quality Indian coffee is known in the world for its good quality
Variety Arabica, initially brought from Yemen; in great demand all over the world
History first introduced on the Baba Budan Hills
Where today its cultivation is confined to the Nilgiri in Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu
Your textbook gives no growing conditions for coffee

— no temperature, rainfall or soil. If a question asks you to compare tea and coffee, write tea's conditions from the textbook, coffee's variety, history and limited area, and label anything else Beyond your textbook.

Tea and coffee — keep them apart: tea came with the British; coffee's Arabica came from Yemen. Tea grows in many states (Assam first in your textbook's list); coffee is confined to the Nilgiri in three southern states.

  1. Horticulture crops (fruits and vegetables)

  • In 2018, India was the second largest producer of fruits and vegetables in the world, after China.
  • India grows tropical as well as temperate fruits.
Fruit States or places (your textbook)
Mangoes Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal
Oranges Nagpur; Cherrapunjee (Meghalaya)
Bananas Kerala, Mizoram, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu
Lichi and guava Uttar Pradesh, Bihar
Pineapples Meghalaya
Grapes Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra
Apples, pears, apricots, walnuts Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh
  • Vegetables: India is an important producer of pea, cauliflower, onion, cabbage, tomato, brinjal and potato. NCERT Figs 4.12 and 4.13 show fruits (apricots, apple, pomegranate) and vegetables.

Dates in this topic. The ranks carry the textbook's years — 2018 (groundnut, rapeseed, tea, fruits and vegetables) and 2019–20 (groundnut states). Write the year with the rank.

Worked examples

Worked example 1

Which crop is it?

Question. Name the crop in each description. (a) "We came from Yemen; today we grow only on the Nilgiri of three southern states." (b) "I need 21°C to 27°C, 75–100 cm of rain and manual labour from sowing to harvest; I become gur and khandsari." (c) "I am a kharif oil seed, about half of the major oilseeds; Gujarat led my production in 2019–20." (d) "My leaves must keep growing, so I want frequent showers all year and no frost."

Asked: four crops.

Concept used: Sections 1–4.

  1. 1

    (a) Arabica from Yemen; the Nilgiri in Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu → coffee.

  2. 2

    (b) 21°C–27°C, 75–100 cm, manual labour, gur and khandsari → sugarcane.

  3. 3

    (c) Kharif, half of the major oilseeds, Gujarat largest in 2019–20 → groundnut.

  4. 4

    (d) Continuous growth of tender leaves, frequent showers, frost-free → tea.

  5. Check: each description has a clue that fits only one crop — Yemen (coffee), gur (sugarcane), half of the oilseeds (groundnut), tender leaves (tea).

  6. Answer (a) coffee; (b) sugarcane; (c) groundnut; (d) tea.

    Common trap: writing "tea" for (a) because both are beverage crops. Only coffee came from Yemen.

Worked example 2

Conditions for tea

Question. Using your textbook, explain three conditions a tea garden needs for its bushes to grow well. (3 marks)

Asked: three conditions.

Concept used: Tea (section 3).

  1. 1

    Climate: tropical and sub-tropical, warm and moist, frost-free all through the year.

  2. 2

    Rain: frequent showers evenly distributed over the year, so the tender leaves keep growing.

  3. 3

    Soil: deep, fertile, well-drained soil rich in humus and organic matter.

  4. Check: three different conditions — climate, rain, soil. Labour is a fourth point your textbook gives; it is a need of the industry, so use it only if a fourth point is asked.

  5. Answer Warm, moist, frost-free climate; frequent, evenly distributed showers; deep, fertile, well-drained soil rich in humus.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Tea grows well in tropical and sub-tropical climates that are warm, moist and frost-free all through the year. (1)
    2. Frequent showers evenly distributed over the year ensure continuous growth of tender leaves. (1)
    3. It needs deep, fertile, well-drained soil rich in humus and organic matter. (1)
    Common trap: writing "tea needs heavy rain once a year". Your textbook stresses frequent showers evenly distributed over the year.

Worked example 3

Sorting oil seeds

Question. An imagined farmer in the north wants one oil seed for the kharif season and one for the rabi season. Her cousin in the south wants to grow sesamum. (a) Suggest one kharif and one rabi oil seed for the northern farmer. (b) In which season will the cousin in the south sow sesamum? (2 marks)

Asked: two oil seeds by season; sesamum's season in the south.

Concept used: Oil seeds (section 2).

  1. 1

    (a) Kharif: groundnut (or sesamum, in the north); rabi: mustard or linseed.

  2. 2

    (b) Sesamum is a kharif crop in the north and a rabi crop in the south → rabi.

  3. Check: castor would fit either season (it is grown in both), but the question asks for one clearly kharif and one clearly rabi seed.

  4. Answer (a) groundnut (kharif), mustard or linseed (rabi); (b) rabi.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. Kharif: groundnut; rabi: mustard (or linseed). (1)
    2. In the south, sesamum is a rabi crop (in the north it is kharif). (1)
    Common trap: giving sesamum one season everywhere. It changes: kharif in the north, rabi in the south.

Common mistakes!

  • "India is second to China in sugarcane."

    Second after Brazil.

    Sugarcane–Brazil; most other ranks–China.

  • Sugarcane given 50–75 cm or above 100 cm.

    Sugarcane: 75–100 cm, 21°C–27°C.

    Use the rainfall ladder.

  • "Groundnut is a rabi crop"; "sesamum is kharif everywhere".

    Groundnut is kharif; sesamum is kharif in the north, rabi in the south; castor is both; linseed and mustard are rabi.

    Learn the oil-seed sorter.

  • Tea and coffee mixed: "coffee from the British", "tea from Yemen", "coffee in Assam".

    Tea: introduced by the British, Assam first in your textbook's list; coffee: Arabica from Yemen, the Nilgiri in Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu.

    Learn one history line and one area line for each.

  • Writing coffee's "temperature and rainfall" as if from the textbook.

    Your textbook gives no conditions for coffee.

    Label any such point "beyond your textbook".

  • Fruits placed in the wrong state.

    e.g. pineapples — Meghalaya; apples — Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh.

    Use the fruit table.

Key points

  • Sugarcane: tropical and subtropical; hot and humid; 21°C–27°C; 75–100 cm; variety of soils; manual labour; India second after Brazil; sugar, gur, khandsari, molasses; Uttar Pradesh first in the list of major states.
  • Oil seeds: about 12 per cent of the cropped area; groundnut kharif (about half; Gujarat largest in 2019–20, then Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu); linseed, mustard rabi; sesamum kharif north, rabi south; castor both; used for cooking, soap, cosmetics, ointments.
  • Tea: plantation, beverage; introduced by the British; warm, moist, frost-free all year; frequent showers; deep fertile well-drained soil rich in humus; labour-intensive; processed in the garden; Assam, Darjeeling and Jalpaiguri (West Bengal), Tamil Nadu, Kerala; second after China (2018).
  • Coffee: good quality; Arabica from Yemen; first on the Baba Budan Hills; confined to the Nilgiri in Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu (no conditions given).
  • Horticulture: India second after China in fruits and vegetables (2018); mangoes, oranges (Nagpur, Cherrapunjee), bananas, lichi, guava, pineapples (Meghalaya), grapes, apples (Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh).
