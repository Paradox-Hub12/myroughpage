Agriculture

8 topicsAbout 11 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Two out of every three Indians work on the land, and almost everything on your plate began in a field. This chapter explains how India farms — from slash-and-burn patches in the hills to plantations that feed factories — when it farms (the rabi, kharif and zaid seasons), what it grows and where, and what the government has done to help its farmers. It ends with the crops on the map of India.

You will learn the four types of farming (primitive subsistence, intensive subsistence, commercial and plantation), the three cropping seasons, the conditions and major states of rice, wheat, millets, maize, pulses, sugarcane, oil seeds, tea, coffee, fruits and vegetables, rubber, cotton and jute, the technological and institutional reforms (land reform, the Green and White Revolutions, crop insurance, Grameen banks, the Kissan Credit Card, support prices) and the Bhoodan–Gramdan movement. It is one of the Geography chapters in the board exam (Geography carries 17 marks + 3 map marks), and it has a map item: identify the major areas of rice and wheat, and the major (or leading) producer states of sugarcane, tea, coffee, rubber, cotton and jute. In the board papers of 2024–2026 it was asked 61 times — most often on the types of farming, then on crops and their conditions, the cropping seasons and the government's reforms, with four map items on the leading state for a crop.

What you'll learn

  • Explain why agriculture matters; describe slash-and-burn and intensive subsistence farming.
  • Describe commercial and plantation farming and compare the four types.
  • Tell the three seasons apart by their months and crops.
  • Give the conditions and areas of rice and wheat, and the features of millets, maize and pulses.
  • Describe sugarcane, oil seeds, tea, coffee and horticulture crops.
  • Describe rubber, cotton and jute, and the four fibre crops.
  • Explain why reforms were needed, what they were, and the Bhoodan–Gramdan movement.
  • Identify the rice and wheat areas and the major states of the syllabus crops.
  • Put the chapter on four cards and answer mixed board-style questions.

Most of the marks in this chapter come from six skills, and every topic practises them:

  1. Tell the types of farming apart — features of each, and the differences between two.
  2. Put a crop in its season — rabi, kharif or zaid.
  3. Match a crop with its conditions and states — temperature, rainfall, soil, and where it grows.
  4. Name the reforms and measures — and give measures when measures are asked.
  5. Read a source — a passage on farming or on the seasons.
  6. Find the state on the map — and write to the marks: 2 marks ≈ two points (up to 40 words), 3 marks ≈ three points (up to 60), 5 marks ≈ five points (up to 120), each on its own line.

The spaced-review sets come back to you after 1 day, 1 week and 1 month. Don't skip them — remembering something after a gap is what makes it stick.
