About the question text

Each NCERT question is shown in full above its solution, with its textbook location. The solutions are our own.

How every solution is laid out: Asked → Concept used → Understand it (the reasoning, one step per line) → Check → ✅ Final answer → Write it in the exam (numbered points sized to the marks, with the mark for each point) → Common trap. In the board exam, answers must stay within the word limits: 2 marks — up to 40 words, 3 marks — up to 60 words, 5 marks — up to 120 words.

Marks shown in "Write it in the exam" are our estimate of how a board question of this kind is usually marked; NCERT does not give marks. Where a question asks for your own observation or opinion, or for something your textbook does not give, the answer is marked "One possible answer".

Question p. 30Industries based on agricultural raw material2 marks

Can you name some industries based on agricultural raw material?

Asked: industries that use farm products as raw material.

Concept used: Why agriculture matters (Topic 1); plantation and fibre crops (Topics 2 and 6).

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    Your textbook says agriculture produces raw material for various industries.

  2. 2

    From this chapter: cotton is a main raw material for the cotton textile industry; jute is used for gunny bags, mats, ropes, yarn and carpets; rubber is an industrial raw material; oil seeds go into soap, cosmetics and ointments; sugarcane gives sugar, gur, khandsari and molasses; tea is processed within the tea garden.

  3. Check: each industry named uses a crop from this chapter as its raw material.

  4. Answer The cotton textile industry, jute products (gunny bags, ropes), rubber goods, soap and cosmetics (from oil seeds), and sugar and gur making.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. The cotton textile industry uses cotton; jute is made into gunny bags, mats, ropes, yarn and carpets. (1)
    2. Sugarcane gives sugar, gur and khandsari; oil seeds are used for soap, cosmetics and ointments. (1)
    Common trap: naming industries that do not use farm products (for example, iron and steel).
Question p. 30Names of slash-and-burn farming1 mark

Can you name some such types of farmings?

Asked: the names of primitive subsistence ("slash and burn") farming in India.

Concept used: Primitive subsistence farming and its names (Topic 1).

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    Your textbook answers this in the next line: it is jhumming in north-eastern states like Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Nagaland; Pamlou in Manipur; Dipa in Bastar district of Chhattisgarh and in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

  4. Answer Jhumming (north-east), Pamlou (Manipur), Dipa (Bastar, Chhattisgarh; Andaman and Nicobar Islands), and others such as Bewar, Podu, Kumari and Khil.

    Jhumming (north-eastern states), Pamlou (Manipur) and Dipa (Bastar in Chhattisgarh). (1)

    Note on the textbook's wording. The question prints "farmings"; it means "kinds of farming".

    Common trap: giving the world names (Milpa, Ladang) when the question is about India.
Question p. 31Rinjha's farming1 mark

Can you name the type of farming Rinjha's family is engaged in?

Asked: the type of farming in the Rinjha box.

Concept used: Primitive subsistence farming (Topic 1).

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    The family clears, slashes and burns a patch of land.

  2. 2

    They will search for a fresh patch when the soil's fertility declines.

  3. 3

    These are the features of primitive subsistence ("slash and burn") farming — called jhumming in Assam, where Rinjha lives.

  4. Check: shifting to a fresh patch fits only primitive subsistence farming.

  5. Answer Primitive subsistence (slash and burn) farming — jhumming.

    Write it in the exam (1 mark):

    Primitive subsistence ("slash and burn") farming, called jhumming in Assam. (1)

    Common trap: writing "plantation" because of the bamboo canal. The clue is slash, burn and shift.

Question p. 31Commercial in one region, subsistence in another2 marks

Can you give some more examples of crops which may be commercial in one region and may provide subsistence in another region?

Asked: crops that change role from region to region.

Concept used: One crop, two roles (Topic 2).

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    Your textbook's example: rice is commercial in Haryana and Punjab, but a subsistence crop in Odisha.

  2. 2

    The reason is that the degree of commercialisation varies from one region to another.

  3. 3

    The question asks for more examples, which your textbook does not give. One possible answer, Beyond your textbook: wheat (grown for sale in Punjab, but mainly for the family on small plots elsewhere) and millets such as bajra or ragi (sold in some markets, eaten at home in dry villages).

  4. Check: the extra examples are labelled; each changes role because of who it is grown for.

  5. Answer Rice (textbook); one possible answer: wheat and millets (Beyond your textbook).

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. Rice is a commercial crop in Haryana and Punjab but a subsistence crop in Odisha. (1)
    2. One possible answer (Beyond your textbook): wheat and millets, sold in some regions and eaten at home in others. (1)
    Common trap: naming plantation crops like tea, which are always grown for industry and market.
Question p. 36Kharif and rabi pulses2 marks

Can you distinguish which of these pulses are grown in the kharif season and which are grown in the rabi season?

(The pulses named just before the question: tur (arhar), urad, moong, masur, peas and gram.)

Asked: the season of each pulse.

Concept used: The three seasons (Topic 3); pulses (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    Your textbook's kharif list includes tur (arhar), moong and urad.

  2. 2

    Its rabi list includes peas and gram.

  3. 3

    Masur is not in either list. Beyond your textbook: masur (lentil) is a rabi pulse.

  4. Check: five of the six pulses are sorted from the textbook's own season lists; only masur needs the label.

  5. Answer Kharif: tur (arhar), moong, urad. Rabi: peas, gram (and masur, Beyond your textbook).

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    Common trap: putting gram in kharif. Gram is one of the textbook's five rabi crops.

Exercise 2 (i)A beverage crop and its conditions3 marks

Name one important beverage crop and specify the geographical conditions required for its growth.

Asked: one beverage crop and its conditions.

Concept used: Tea (Topic 5).

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    Crop: tea (your textbook gives conditions for tea, not for coffee).

  2. 2

    Climate: tropical and sub-tropical, warm and moist, frost-free all through the year.

  3. 3

    Rain: frequent showers evenly distributed over the year, for continuous growth of tender leaves.

  4. 4

    Soil: deep, fertile, well-drained, rich in humus and organic matter.

  5. Check: the answer names the crop and gives three conditions — the two parts asked.

  6. Answer Tea: warm, moist, frost-free climate; frequent, evenly distributed showers; deep, fertile, well-drained soil rich in humus.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Tea needs a warm and moist frost-free climate all through the year (tropical and sub-tropical). (1)
    2. Frequent showers evenly distributed over the year keep the tender leaves growing. (1)
    3. It needs deep, fertile, well-drained soil rich in humus and organic matter. (1)
    Common trap: choosing coffee and then inventing its conditions. Your textbook gives no conditions for coffee.

Exercise 2 (iii)Institutional reform programmes3 marks

Enlist the various institutional reform programmes introduced by the government in the interest of farmers.

Asked: a list of institutional reforms.

Concept used: Reforms through time; the 1980s–90s programme (Topic 7).

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    After Independence: collectivisation, consolidation of holdings, cooperation, abolition of zamindari; land reform was the main focus of the First Five Year Plan.

  2. 2

    1980s–1990s: crop insurance (against drought, flood, cyclone, fire and disease); Grameen banks, cooperative societies and banks for loans at lower interest.

  3. 3

    Schemes and prices: Kissan Credit Card, Personal Accident Insurance Scheme; minimum support, remunerative and procurement prices.

  4. Check: every item changes the rules, ownership, credit or prices — none is a seed or a machine.

  5. Answer Land reforms (consolidation, abolition of zamindari, cooperation, collectivisation), crop insurance, Grameen and cooperative banks, KCC, PAIS, support prices.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Land reforms: collectivisation, consolidation of holdings, cooperation and abolition of zamindari. (1)
    2. Crop insurance, and Grameen banks and cooperative societies giving loans at lower interest. (1)
    3. Kissan Credit Card, Personal Accident Insurance Scheme and minimum support prices. (1)

    Note on the textbook's wording. "Enlist" here means "list". The textbook prints "Kissan"; we keep its spelling.

    Common trap: listing high yielding variety (HYV) seeds and fertilisers. Those are technological, not institutional.

Exercise 3 (i)Initiatives to increase agricultural production5 marks

Suggest the initiative taken by the government to ensure the increase in agricultural production.

Asked: the government's steps that raise farm production.

Concept used: Reforms through time; the 1980s–90s programme (Topic 7).

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    Land reforms: consolidation of holdings and abolition of zamindari (land reform: the First Five Year Plan's main focus).

  2. 2

    Green Revolution (package technology) and White Revolution (Operation Flood) in the 1960s–70s.

  3. 3

    Comprehensive land development programme (1980s–90s): crop insurance against drought, flood, cyclone, fire and disease.

  4. 4

    Loans and schemes: Grameen banks, cooperative societies and banks give loans at lower interest; KCC and PAIS are other schemes for farmers.

  5. 5

    Prices and information: minimum support, remunerative and procurement prices; weather bulletins and agricultural programmes on radio and TV.

  6. Check: five different initiatives; each one is a step the government took, not a problem.

  7. Answer Land reforms, the Green and White Revolutions, crop insurance, Grameen and cooperative banks; KCC and PAIS; support prices and weather bulletins.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    1. Land reforms — consolidation of holdings and abolition of zamindari — were the main focus of the First Five Year Plan. (1)
    2. The Green Revolution (package technology) and the White Revolution (Operation Flood) were started in the 1960s and 1970s. (1)
    3. A comprehensive land development programme provided crop insurance against drought, flood, cyclone, fire and disease. (1)
    4. Grameen banks, cooperative societies and banks gave loans at lower interest; KCC and PAIS were introduced. (1)
    5. Minimum support and procurement prices checked middlemen; weather bulletins and farm programmes were broadcast on radio and TV. (1)
    Common trap: writing only "Green Revolution" five ways. Give five different initiatives.
Exercise 3 (ii)Conditions for rice5 marks

Describe the geographical conditions required for the growth of rice.

Asked: the conditions rice needs.

Concept used: Rice (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    Season: rice is a kharif crop.

  2. 2

    Temperature: high, above 25°C.

  3. 3

    Humidity and rain: high humidity with annual rainfall above 100 cm.

  4. 4

    Irrigation: in areas of less rainfall it grows with the help of irrigation — canals and tubewells in Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh and parts of Rajasthan.

  5. 5

    Land: the plains of north and north-eastern India, coastal areas and deltaic regions.

  6. Check: five different conditions — season, temperature, rainfall, irrigation, land.

  7. Answer Kharif; above 25°C; high humidity and above 100 cm of rain; irrigation where rain is less; plains, coasts and deltas.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    1. Rice is a kharif crop, sown with the onset of the monsoon. (1)
    2. The temperature must be high, over 25°C. (1)
    3. It needs high humidity and annual rainfall above 100 cm. (1)
    4. In areas of less rainfall it grows with irrigation, as canals and tubewells allow in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh. (1)
    5. It grows well in the plains of north and north-east India, coastal areas and deltaic regions. (1)
    Common trap: writing wheat's "cool season" or "50–75 cm". Rice needs heat and much water.

Activity, p. 41The word search1 mark

Solve the puzzle by following your search horizontally and vertically to find the hidden answers.

  1. The two staple food crops of India.
  2. This is the summer cropping season of India.
  3. Pulses like arhar, moong, gram, urad contain…
  4. It is a coarse grain.
  5. The two important beverages in India are…
  6. One of the four major fibers grown on black soils.

Asked: six answers hidden in the grid.

Concept used: Topics 3–6.

  1. 1

    Understand it:

    RICE and WHEAT — the staple food crops.

  2. 2

    KHARIF — the word in the grid for clue 2 (see the note).

  3. 3

    PROTEIN — pulses are the major source of protein in a vegetarian diet.

  4. 4

    JOWAR — a millet, a coarse grain.

  5. 5

    TEA and COFFEE — the two beverage crops.

  6. 6

    COTTON — grows in the black cotton soil of the Deccan.

  7. Check: each word appears in the grid, across or down: WHEAT, KHARIF, JOWAR, COFFEE and COTTON across; RICE, PROTEIN and TEA down.

  8. Answer Rice, wheat; kharif; protein; jowar; tea, coffee; cotton.

    Write it in the exam (1 mark):

    Rice and wheat; kharif; protein; jowar; tea and coffee; cotton. (1)

    Note on the textbook's wording. Clue 2 calls the summer season of India the answer, and the grid holds KHARIF. In the text (p. 32), kharif crops are grown with the onset of monsoon, and zaid is the short season during the summer months. For the puzzle write KHARIF; in an exam, use the text's definitions.

    Common trap: looking for ZAID in the grid. It is not there; the puzzle's answer is KHARIF.
