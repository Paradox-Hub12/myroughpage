Map work: crops on the map of India

About 35 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Identify the major areas of rice and wheat on an outline map of India.
  • Identify the major producer states of sugarcane, tea, coffee, rubber, cotton and jute, and name the leading state where a question asks for it.

In the board exam a map question may read: "On the outline map of India, locate and label the leading state in production of jute." You have to find one state on a map with no names on it, mark it and write its name. This topic links each syllabus crop to the states your textbook names, and trains you to find those states on an outline map.

Quick reminder (states on the map)

On an outline map of India, north is at the top. Punjab is in the north-west, next to Pakistan; Uttar Pradesh is the large state on the northern plains; Madhya Pradesh is in the centre; West Bengal reaches from the Himalayas down to the Bay of Bengal; Assam lies in the north-east along the Brahmaputra; Maharashtra is in the west-centre, on the Arabian Sea; Karnataka is south of Maharashtra; Kerala is the narrow state on the south-west coast.

  1. What the syllabus asks

Your syllabus map item for this chapter: "Identify: major areas of rice and wheat; largest/major producer states of sugarcane, tea, coffee, rubber, cotton and jute."

  • Rice and wheat: your textbook has a map for each — "India: Distribution of Rice" (p. 33) and "India: Distribution of Wheat" (p. 35) — with major and minor areas shaded. Study the shaded areas there; the map on this page shows only example points.
  • The other six crops: your textbook gives a list of major producing states for each (Topics 5 and 6).
  • "Largest" or "leading": your textbook never says which state is the largest producer of any of these crops. Every board map question on this chapter so far (2024 and 2025) has asked for the leading state, so this page gives the answer with a label: Beyond your textbook.
  • For candidates with visual impairment, CBSE asks the same item as "Name the leading … state".

  1. The crops and their states

Crop States your textbook names (in its order)
Rice Assam, West Bengal, coastal Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra (Konkan coast), Uttar Pradesh, Bihar; recently Punjab and Haryana
Wheat Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan
Sugarcane Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana
Tea Assam, Darjeeling and Jalpaiguri (West Bengal), Tamil Nadu, Kerala; also Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Meghalaya, Andhra Pradesh, Tripura
Coffee the Nilgiri in Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu ("coffee in Karnataka")
Rubber Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andaman and Nicobar islands, Garo hills of Meghalaya
Cotton Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh
Jute West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha, Meghalaya

The leading states (for "leading" or "largest" questions):

  • Beyond your textbook: rice — West Bengal is usually named the leading state (the top state can change from year to year); on your textbook's rice map the whole of West Bengal is a major area.
  • Beyond your textbook: wheat — Uttar Pradesh.
  • Beyond your textbook: sugarcane — Uttar Pradesh (first in your textbook's list).
  • Beyond your textbook: tea — Assam (first in your textbook's list; "Tea in Assam").
  • Beyond your textbook: coffee — Karnataka (your textbook: "coffee in Karnataka").
  • Beyond your textbook: rubber — Kerala (first in your textbook's list).
  • Beyond your textbook: cotton — Gujarat and Maharashtra are the two biggest (Maharashtra is first in your textbook's list).
  • Beyond your textbook: jute — West Bengal (first in your textbook's list).

  1. Eight example points

These are the points on the map and in the trainer below. Each point is only an example location inside a state — not the whole crop area.

Point Crop (example) State
1 Wheat — Ganga-Satluj plains Punjab
2 Sugarcane — northern plains Uttar Pradesh
3 Tea — north-east Assam
4 Wheat — central India Madhya Pradesh
5 Rice and jute — eastern plains West Bengal
6 Cotton — black cotton soil of the Deccan Maharashtra
7 Coffee — the south-west Karnataka
8 Rubber — the wet south-west Kerala

Where to look on your textbook's maps:

  • Point 1 (Punjab) and point 4 (Madhya Pradesh) lie in the major area of the wheat map, which runs from Punjab and Haryana through western Uttar Pradesh into Madhya Pradesh.
  • Point 5 (West Bengal) lies in the major area of the rice map, which covers the eastern plains and the east coast.

  1. Answering a map item — three steps

  1. Which crop, and what is asked? ("major" → any state your textbook names; "leading" → the one state, beyond your textbook.)
  2. Which state? Use the table in section 2.
  3. Where is it on the outline? Mark a dot (or shade lightly) inside the state, away from its borders, and write the state's name beside it.

Worked example 1

Three marked states

Question. On an outline map, three states are marked. A is the narrow state on the south-west coast; B is the large state in the north-east along the Brahmaputra; C is the state in the west-centre on the Arabian Sea, on the black soil of the Deccan. Name each state, and one syllabus crop your textbook names for it.

Asked: three states and one crop each.

Concept used: The crops and their states (section 2); the quick reminder.

  1. 1

    A — Kerala. A crop your textbook names for Kerala: rubber (also tea, coffee, rice).

  2. 2

    B — Assam. A crop: tea (also rice, jute).

  3. 3

    C — Maharashtra. A crop: cotton (also sugarcane, rice on the Konkan coast).

  4. Check: each crop is in your textbook's list for that state — rubber (Kerala first in its list), tea (Assam first in its list), cotton (Maharashtra first in its list).

  5. Answer A Kerala — rubber; B Assam — tea; C Maharashtra — cotton.

    Common trap: naming the city instead of the state. The item asks for a state.

Worked example 2

Major area or not?

Question. Two places are marked: P in the Gangetic plain of Bihar, and Q in the plains of Haryana. (a) Is P in a major area of rice on your textbook's rice map? (b) Is Q in a major area of wheat on the wheat map? (c) Which season does each crop belong to?

Asked: two map readings and two seasons.

Concept used: What the syllabus asks (section 1); seasons (Topic 3).

  1. 1

    (a) Yes — the rice map shades the plains of Bihar as a major area; your textbook also names Bihar among the important rice-growing regions.

  2. 2

    (b) Yes — the wheat map shades Haryana as a major area; Haryana is in the textbook's list of major wheat-producing states.

  3. 3

    (c) Rice — kharif; wheat — rabi.

  4. Check: both places match a state in the textbook's lists and a major area on its maps.

  5. Answer (a) yes; (b) yes; (c) rice kharif, wheat rabi.

    Common trap: thinking a state named for wheat cannot grow rice. Bihar and Haryana are in both lists (Haryana for rice "recently").

Worked example 3

A "leading state" item

Question. A paper asks for the state that leads India in growing coffee. Answer as you would in the exam, and say what your textbook does and does not tell you.

Asked: one state; what is from the textbook.

Concept used: The leading states (section 2).

  1. 1

    From the textbook: coffee is confined to the Nilgiri in Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu, and "coffee in Karnataka" is one of its plantation examples.

  2. 2

    Not in the textbook: which of the three states produces the most.

  3. 3

    Beyond your textbook: the leading state is Karnataka.

  4. Check: Karnataka is one of the three states your textbook names, so the answer agrees with the textbook.

  5. Answer Karnataka (the leading state is beyond your textbook; Karnataka is named by it for coffee).

    Write it in the exam (1 mark):

    Karnataka (Beyond your textbook: the leading coffee state). (1)

    Common trap: writing "Assam" because tea and coffee are both plantation drinks. Assam is the tea state; coffee is a southern crop.

Quick check · 1 of 3MCQ1 mark

Which state does your textbook name first among the major jute producing states?

Punjab is not in the jute list.
West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha and Meghalaya — West Bengal first in your textbook's list.
Gujarat is named for cotton and groundnut, not jute.

Quick check · 2 of 3MCQ1 mark

A point is marked on the plains of Punjab. On your textbook's maps, which crop has a major area there?

Punjab is not in the tea list.
Rubber needs more than 200 cm of rain; Punjab is not named.
Coffee is confined to the Nilgiri in the south.
The wheat map shades Punjab as a major area; Punjab is first in the wheat list.
Quick check · 3 of 3MCQ1 mark

A state on the south-west coast is marked. Your textbook names it first for rubber. Which state is it?

Kerala is first in the rubber list and lies on the south-west coast.
Assam is in the north-east (tea).
Maharashtra is in the west-centre (cotton).
Uttar Pradesh is on the northern plains (sugarcane).

Common mistakes!

  • Jute marked in Punjab; coffee in Assam; rubber in Rajasthan; tea in Gujarat.

    None of these is in your textbook's lists for that crop.

    Use the crop–state table; jute → West Bengal, first in your textbook's list; coffee → Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu.

  • "The textbook says West Bengal is the largest rice producer."

    Your textbook gives no ranking of states.

    Write the leading state with the label Beyond your textbook.

  • Marking a city instead of a state.

    The item asks for a state.

    Mark inside the state and write the state's name.

  • A mark on the border between two states.

    The examiner cannot tell which state you mean.

    Mark well inside the state.

Key points

  • Syllabus: identify the major areas of rice and wheat (textbook maps, pp. 33 and 35) and the largest/major producer states of sugarcane, tea, coffee, rubber, cotton, jute (textbook lists).
  • First in the textbook's lists: wheat — Punjab; sugarcane — Uttar Pradesh; tea — Assam; rubber — Kerala; cotton — Maharashtra; jute — West Bengal; coffee — the Nilgiri in Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu.
  • Your textbook never names the leading state; when asked, give it with the label Beyond your textbook.
  • Answer a map item in three steps: crop → state → mark and name it.
