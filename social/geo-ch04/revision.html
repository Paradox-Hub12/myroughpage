The whole chapter on one page

Why it matters — two-thirds of India's people are engaged in agriculture (more than 60 per cent depend on it for a livelihood); it is a primary activity giving most of our food, raw material for industries, and exports (tea, coffee, spices).

Types of farming — Primitive subsistence: small patches, hoe, dao, digging sticks, family/community labour, depends on monsoon and natural fertility; slash and burn; shift when fertility falls (Nature replenishes the soil); low productivity (no fertilisers or modern inputs); jhumming (Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland), Pamlou (Manipur), Dipa (Bastar, Chhattisgarh; Andaman and Nicobar Islands). Intensive subsistence: high population pressure, labour-intensive, biochemical inputs and irrigation; inheritance → uneconomical holdings; no other livelihood → maximum output → enormous pressure on land. Commercial: higher doses of modern inputs (high yielding variety (HYV) seeds, chemical fertilisers, insecticides, pesticides) → higher productivity; rice commercial in Haryana and Punjab, subsistence in Odisha. Plantation (a type of commercial farming): single crop, large area; interface of agriculture and industry; capital intensive, migrant labourers; produce → industry; needs transport and communication; tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane, banana.

Seasons — Rabi: sown Oct–Dec, harvested Apr–Jun; wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard; north and north-west (Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh); helped by western temperate cyclones and the green revolution. Kharif: with the monsoon, harvested Sep–Oct; paddy, maize, jowar, bajra, tur, moong, urad, cotton, jute, groundnut, soyabean. Zaid: short summer season; watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder. Sugarcane: almost a year. Aus, Aman, Boro: three paddy crops in Assam, West Bengal, Odisha.

Crops — Rice: staple; second after China; kharif; above 25°C, high humidity, above 100 cm (irrigation where less); plains, coasts, deltas; canals and tubewells in Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh, parts of Rajasthan. Wheat: second most important cereal; rabi; cool growing season, bright sunshine at ripening, 50–75 cm; Ganga-Satluj plains and black soil region of the Deccan. Millets: jowar (third most important food crop; rain-fed), bajra (sandy, shallow black soil), ragi (dry regions; iron, calcium). Maize: food and fodder; 21–27°C; old alluvial soil. Pulses: India largest producer and consumer; protein; less moisture; leguminous, fix nitrogen (your textbook: "all these crops except arhar") → rotation. Sugarcane: hot and humid; 21–27°C; 75–100 cm; manual labour; second after Brazil; sugar, gur, khandsari, molasses. Oil seeds: about 12 per cent of cropped area; groundnut kharif (about half; Gujarat largest in 2019–20); linseed, mustard rabi; sesamum kharif north, rabi south; castor both. Tea: British; warm, moist, frost-free; frequent showers; humus-rich soil; labour-intensive; processed in the garden; Assam, Darjeeling and Jalpaiguri, Tamil Nadu, Kerala. Coffee: Arabica from Yemen; Baba Budan Hills; Nilgiri in Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu. Horticulture: second after China (2018). Rubber: equatorial; over 200 cm, over 25°C; Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andaman and Nicobar, Garo hills. Cotton: original home India; black cotton soil (drier parts); high temperature, light rain or irrigation, 210 frost-free days; kharif, 6–8 months. Jute: golden fibre; flood-plain soils renewed every year; West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha, Meghalaya.

Reforms — after Independence: collectivisation, consolidation of holdings, cooperation, abolition of zamindari; land reform — First Five Year Plan; implementation lukewarm. 1960s–70s: Green Revolution (package technology), White Revolution (Operation Flood) — development concentrated in few areas. 1980s–90s: crop insurance; Grameen banks, cooperative societies and banks; Kissan Credit Card (KCC), Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS); weather bulletins; minimum support, remunerative and procurement prices. Bhoodan–Gramdan: Vinoba Bhave; Pochampalli; 80 acres, 80 landless villagers; villages given; Blood-less Revolution.

Map — rice and wheat areas (textbook maps, pp. 33, 35); first in the lists: wheat Punjab, sugarcane Uttar Pradesh, tea Assam, rubber Kerala, cotton Maharashtra, jute West Bengal; coffee — Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu. "Leading" states are beyond your textbook.

Terms, numbers and places sheet

Key terms (as in your textbook — learn the words in bold)

  • Primitive subsistence farming: slash and burn farming on small patches with primitive tools, for the family; farmers shift when soil fertility decreases.
  • Intensive subsistence farming: labour-intensive farming with high doses of biochemical inputs and irrigation, in areas of high population pressure on land.
  • Commercial farming: farming with higher doses of modern inputs for higher productivity.
  • Plantation: a single crop grown on a large area; an interface of agriculture and industry.
  • Rabi / kharif / zaid: the winter, monsoon and short summer cropping seasons.
  • Leguminous crops: crops (pulses) that fix nitrogen from the air.
  • Sericulture: the rearing of silkworms for the production of silk fibre.
  • Bhoodan / Gramdan: a gift of land / of villages to the landless.

Numbers to know

  • two-thirds of the population in agriculture · more than 60 per cent depend on it
  • rice above 25°C, above 100 cm · wheat 50–75 cm · maize and sugarcane 21°C–27°C · sugarcane 75–100 cm · rubber over 200 cm, over 25°C
  • cotton 210 frost-free days, 6 to 8 months · oil seeds about 12 per cent of the cropped area · groundnut about half of the major oilseeds
  • world ranks: rice 2nd (after China) · sugarcane 2nd (after Brazil) · groundnut 2nd, rapeseed 3rd, tea 2nd, fruits and vegetables 2nd (2018) · cotton 2nd (2017) · pulses 1st · groundnut states 2019–20
  • 80 acres for 80 landless villagers (Bhoodan)

Memory hooks

  • "Primitive moves, intensive squeezes" · "Family–Family–Market–Factory" (who the four farming types grow for)
  • "Rabi: sow Oct–Dec, reap Apr–Jun; Kharif: sow with the monsoon, reap Sep–Oct; Zaid: summer, in between" · rabi five: "Wheat, Barley, Peas, Gram, Mustard"
  • rainfall ladder "Wheat 50–75 · Sugarcane 75–100 · Rice above 100 · Rubber above 200" · "Maize and sugarcane share 21–27°C"
  • "Cotton: 210 days, 6–8 months, black soil" · "Jute: golden fibre, flood-plain soil, West Bengal first in the list"
  • "Land first, then Green and White, then the package of the 80s–90s" · "Bhoodan = land; Gramdan = villages"
