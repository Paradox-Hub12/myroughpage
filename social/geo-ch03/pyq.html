How this chapter is asked

The board papers of 2024–2026 asked this chapter 31 times: most often as a map item on a dam (ten times — every 2026 paper had one), then as dam–river matching or ordering (six times), source questions on the textbook's boxes (Sardar Sarovar twice, the PMKSY paragraph once, the floods safety box twice), rainwater harvesting (bamboo drip three times, tankas once) and scarcity, ancient water works and the Jal Jeevan Mission. The 2026-27 sample paper gives the chapter a 2-mark question and a map item; the 2025-26 sample paper gives it two 1-mark questions and a map item.

Where an official question is printed with a slip, it is quoted exactly and a note explains the textbook answer.

Sample papers 2026-27 and 2025-26

CBSE Sample Question Paper 2026-27, Section B, Q162 marks

Question: A drought-prone district wants to adopt a sustainable water conservation method using traditional knowledge. Suggest two suitable rainwater harvesting practices that could be implemented.

Asked: two traditional rainwater harvesting practices for a dry district.

Concept used: Traditional rainwater harvesting (Topic 5).

  1. 1

    Rooftop rainwater harvesting: rain from rooftops is stored — in Rajasthan, in underground tankas — for drinking water in the dry months.

  2. 2

    Johads or khadins: fields are turned into rain fed storage structures, so water stands, moistens the soil (the marking scheme adds that they recharge groundwater).

  3. Check: both are traditional methods your textbook names for arid and semi-arid (drought-prone) Rajasthan, and both are measures, not causes.

    Common trap: suggesting "build a big dam". The question asks for traditional harvesting practices.
  1. Rooftop rainwater harvesting: rain from rooftops is stored in tanks (tankas) for domestic use in dry periods. (1)
  1. Johads or khadins: fields store rainwater, which moistens the soil and recharges groundwater. (1)

Marking scheme: "1. Rooftop rainwater harvesting … 2. Johads or khadins …" — 1 mark each.

Question: Read the given source and answer the questions that follow :

Sardar Sarovar Project
Sardar Sarovar Dam has been built over the Narmada river in Gujarat. This is one of the largest water resources projects of India covering four states – Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Rajasthan. The Sardar Sarovar Project would meet the requirement of water in drought prone and desert areas. Sardar Sarovar Project will provide irrigation facilities to 18.45 lakh hectare of land, covering 3112 villages in 15 districts of Gujarat. It will also irrigate 2,46,000 hectare of land in the strategic desert districts of Barmer and Jalore in Rajasthan and 37,500 hectare in the tribal hilly tract of Maharashtra through lift. About 75% of the command area in Gujarat is drought prone while entire command in Rajasthan is drought prone. Assured water supply will soon make this area drought proof.

18.1 Why is Sardar Sarovar Project considered important for Gujarat ? (1)
18.2 Why is Sardar Sarovar Project designed to cover four states ? (1)
18.3 In what ways does this project promote sustainability ? (2)

Asked: why the project matters to Gujarat, why it covers four states, and how it supports sustainability.

Concept used: A big project: Sardar Sarovar (Topic 3).

  1. 1

    18.1 It will irrigate 18.45 lakh hectare in 3112 villages of 15 districts of Gujarat, where about 75% of the command area is drought prone.

  2. 2

    18.2 It is meant to meet the water needs of drought-prone and desert areas beyond Gujarat too — Barmer and Jalore in Rajasthan and the tribal hilly tract of Maharashtra (through lift).

  3. 3

    18.3 Assured water supply will make drought-prone areas drought proof, so farming and livelihoods can continue year after year; it brings water to desert and tribal hilly areas that lack it.

  4. Check: every answer uses a line of the passage. The passage does not say what Madhya Pradesh gets, so 18.2 is answered from the areas it does name.

    Common trap: answering 18.3 with "it produces a lot of electricity". The passage is about water supply and irrigation.
18.1 It will irrigate 18.45 lakh hectare in 3112 villages of 15 districts, where about 75% of the command area is drought prone. (1)
18.2 To meet the water needs of drought-prone and desert areas in several states, such as Barmer and Jalore in Rajasthan and Maharashtra's tribal hilly tract. (1)
18.3 Assured water supply will make drought-prone areas drought proof (1), sustaining farming and livelihoods in desert and tribal hilly areas. (1)

Board 2026, 32/5/1 Q18 · 32/5/2 Q18 · 32/5/3 Q184 marks

Question: Read the given source carefully and answer the questions that follow :

Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana
Floods have not only devastated life and property but also caused extensive soil erosion. Sedimentation also meant that the flood plains were deprived of silt, a natural fertilizer, further adding on to the problem of land degradation. It was also observed that the multipurpose projects induced earthquakes, caused water-borne diseases and pests and pollution resulting from excessive use of water.
Irrigation has also changed the cropping pattern of many regions with farmers shifting to water intensive and commercial crops. This has great ecological consequences like salinisation of the soil.
Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana has been started which ensures access to some means to protective irrigation for all agricultural farms in the country, thus bringing much desired rural prosperity. Some of the broad objectives of this programme are to enhance the physical access of water on the farm and expand cultivable area under assured irrigation (har khet ko pani), improve on farm water, use efficiency to reduce wastage and increase availability both in duration and extent, irrigation and other water saving technologies (per drop more crop) and introduce sustainable water conservation practices, etc.

(18.1) Explain the role of floods in land degradation. (1)
(18.2) How has irrigation changed cropping pattern ? (1)
(18.3) Explain any two objectives of the 'Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana'. (2)

Asked: how floods degrade land, how irrigation changed the cropping pattern, and two PMKSY objectives.

Concept used: Dams and floods; irrigation and the cropping pattern; PMKSY (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    18.1 Floods caused extensive soil erosion, and sedimentation deprived the flood plains of silt, a natural fertiliser — both add to land degradation.

  2. 2

    18.2 Farmers shifted to water intensive and commercial crops, which has caused salinisation of the soil.

  3. 3

    18.3 Any two: "har khet ko pani" — physical access to water on the farm and more cultivable area under assured irrigation; on-farm water use efficiency to reduce wastage; "per drop more crop" — water saving technologies; sustainable water conservation practices.

  4. Check: 18.3 asks "explain", so each objective is given with its meaning, not only its slogan.

    Common trap: answering 18.2 with "farmers now grow more food". The passage says they shifted to water intensive and commercial crops, with salinisation as a result.
(18.1) Floods caused extensive soil erosion, and sedimentation deprived flood plains of fertile silt, adding to land degradation. (1)
(18.2) Farmers shifted to water intensive and commercial crops, which caused salinisation of the soil. (1)
(18.3) "Har khet ko pani": water on every farm and more cultivable area under assured irrigation (1); "per drop more crop": water saving technologies to reduce wastage. (1)

Our marking points — no official marking scheme is published for this paper. Note on the official text: the paper prints "Sinchayee" (textbook: "Sinchaee"), "natural fertilizer", and "improve on farm water, use efficiency" — the textbook reads "improve on-farm water use efficiency". Board 2026, 32/1/1 Q18 · 32/1/2 Q18 · 32/1/3 Q184 marks

Question: Read the given source carefully and answer the questions that follow :

FLOODS
Basic safety precautions to be taken :
• Listen to radio/TV for the latest weather bulletins and flood warnings. Pass on the information to others.
• Make a family emergency kit which should include, a portable radio/transistor, torch, spare batteries, a first aid box along with essential medicines, ORS, dry food items, drinking water, matchboxes, candles and other essential items.
• Keep hurricane lamp, ropes, rubber tubes, umbrella and bamboo stick in your house. These could be useful.
• Keep your cash, jewellery, valuables, important documents etc. in a safe place.
• If there is a flood, move along with your family members and cattle to safe areas like relief camps, evacuation centers, elevated grounds where you can take shelter.
• Turn off power and gas connections before leaving your house.
During Floods
• Don't enter into flood waters; it could be dangerous.
• Don't allow children to play in or near flood waters.
• Stay away from sewerage line, gutters, drains, culverts etc.
• Be careful of snakes; snakebites are common during floods.
• Stay away from electric poles and fallen power-lines to avoid electrocution.
• Don't use wet electrical appliances, get them checked before use.
• Eat freshly cooked and dry food. Always keep your food covered.
• Use boiled and filtered drinking water.
• Keep all drains, gutters near your house clean.
• Stagnation of water can breed vector/water-borne diseases. In case of sickness, seek medical assistance.
• Use bleaching powder and lime to disinfect the surroundings.

18.1 Suggest one precaution before using water after floods. (1)
18.2 Mention any two measures related to protection of food items during floods. (½ + ½ = 1)
18.3 Suggest two actions to be taken before leaving the house during a flood warning. (2)

Asked: a water precaution, two food measures and two actions before leaving home.

Concept used: The floods box (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    18.1 Use boiled and filtered drinking water.

  2. 2

    18.2 Eat freshly cooked and dry food; always keep your food covered.

  3. 3

    18.3 Turn off power and gas connections before leaving; move with family and cattle to safe areas like relief camps, evacuation centres or elevated grounds (also: keep cash, valuables and documents in a safe place).

  4. Check: every answer is a line of the source, picked for the right time — water and food during floods, actions before leaving the house.

    Common trap: answering 18.3 with "don't enter flood waters". That is a rule during floods, not an action before leaving.
18.1 Use only boiled and filtered drinking water. (1)
18.2 Eat freshly cooked and dry food (½); always keep food covered. (½)
18.3 Turn off power and gas connections (1); move with family and cattle to a safe place such as a relief camp or elevated ground. (1)
