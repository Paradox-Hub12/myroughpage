Topic 4 of 7

Problems of multi-purpose projects

About 40 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain the ecological and social problems of large dams, the floods that dams have triggered, and inter-state water disputes.
  • Compare the advantages and disadvantages of multi-purpose projects, describe the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchaee Yojana, and read the textbook's floods safety box.

The same dam that was built to hold back a flood can, years later, be blamed for one. The same canal that turned a dry field green can, slowly, turn its soil salty. Your textbook does not ask you to cheer for dams or to hate them — it asks you to weigh both sides. This topic gives you the other side of the scale.

  1. Why big dams came under opposition

In recent years, multi-purpose projects and large dams have come under great scrutiny and opposition for many reasons. Your textbook names these effects on rivers:

What the dam does What follows (your textbook)
Regulating and damming a river affects its natural flow Poor sediment flow downstream and excessive sedimentation at the bottom of the reservoir → rockier stream beds and poorer habitats for the river's aquatic life
Dams fragment rivers It becomes difficult for aquatic fauna to migrate, especially for spawning (laying eggs)
Reservoirs are created on the floodplains They submerge the existing vegetation and soil, which decompose over time

Your textbook's exercise also names a social cost: multi-purpose projects lead to large scale displacements and loss of livelihood (Exercise 1 (ii), option (c)).

  1. Dams and floods

  • Ironically, dams built to control floods have triggered floods due to sedimentation in the reservoir.
  • Big dams have mostly been unsuccessful in controlling floods at the time of excessive rainfall.
  • These floods have devastated life and property and caused extensive soil erosion.
  • Sedimentation also means the flood plains are deprived of silt, a natural fertiliser, adding to land degradation.

The Bhadu song (NCERT p. 22). A popular Bhadu song of the Damodar valley asks the river to "reduce the floods a little" so that boats can sail again. The textbook explains that the Damodar is known as the "river of sorrow" because of its floods.

  1. More problems the textbook lists

Multi-purpose projects were also observed to have:

  • induced earthquakes,
  • caused water-borne diseases and pests, and
  • caused pollution resulting from excessive use of water.

  1. Irrigation and the cropping pattern

  • Irrigation has changed the cropping pattern of many regions: farmers have shifted to water intensive and commercial crops.
  • This has great ecological consequences, like salinisation of the soil (salt building up in the soil).

  1. Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchaee Yojana (PMKSY)

The textbook says PMKSY ensures access to some means of protective irrigation for all agricultural farms, bringing rural prosperity. Its broad objectives (NCERT box):

  1. enhance physical access of water on the farm and expand cultivable area under assured irrigation — "har khet ko pani" (water to every field);
  2. improve on-farm water use efficiency to reduce wastage and increase availability in duration and extent;
  3. irrigation and other water saving technologies — "per drop more crop";
  4. introduce sustainable water conservation practices.

  1. Disputes between states (NCERT box)

Sharing a river's water can bring states into conflict. Your textbook's example: the Krishna-Godavari dispute is due to objections raised by the Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh governments to the diversion of more water at Koyna by the Maharashtra government for a multipurpose project. This would reduce downstream flow in their states, with adverse consequences for agriculture and industry.

  1. Both sides at a glance

Advantages (your textbook) Disadvantages (your textbook)
Irrigation for fields, including water for drought-prone areas Natural flow and sediment flow affected; rockier stream beds; poorer aquatic habitats
Electricity (hydel power) for industries and homes Rivers fragmented — aquatic fauna cannot migrate for spawning
Water supply for homes and industries Vegetation and soil submerged; they decompose
Flood control Floods triggered by sedimentation; flood plains deprived of silt
Recreation, inland navigation, fish breeding Earthquakes, water-borne diseases and pests, pollution
Joining agriculture and the village economy with industry and towns ("temples of modern India") Shift to water intensive crops → salinisation; disputes between states; displacement and loss of livelihood (exercise)

  1. The floods box (NCERT p. 25)

Your textbook prints a box titled "FLOODS — Basic Safety Precautions To Be Taken", with newspaper clippings of a heavy rain in Kolkata and a helicopter rescue. Its main points:

Basic safety precautions (the textbook's first list) During floods
Listen to radio/TV for the latest weather bulletins and flood warnings; pass the information on Don't enter flood waters; don't let children play in or near them
Make a family emergency kit: portable radio/transistor, torch, spare batteries, first aid box with essential medicines, ORS, dry food items, drinking water, matchboxes, candles Stay away from sewerage lines, gutters, drains, culverts; be careful of snakes
Keep a hurricane lamp, ropes, rubber tubes, umbrella and bamboo stick at home Stay away from electric poles and fallen power lines; get wet electrical appliances checked before use
Keep cash, jewellery, valuables and important documents in a safe place Eat freshly cooked and dry food; keep food covered
In a flood, move with family and cattle to relief camps, evacuation centres, elevated grounds Use boiled and filtered drinking water; keep drains clean; stagnant water can breed vector/water-borne diseases — seek medical help
Turn off power and gas connections before leaving the house Use bleaching powder and lime to disinfect the surroundings

Worked example 1

The debate card

Question. A school is holding a debate: "Big dams: blessing or burden?" Prepare a balanced answer using your textbook: give two advantages and three disadvantages of multi-purpose projects. (5 marks)

Asked: two advantages and three disadvantages, from the textbook.

Concept used: Both sides at a glance (section 7).

  1. 1

    Advantage — water: they give irrigation and supply water for homes and industries, including to areas that suffer from scarcity.

  2. 2

    Advantage — power and floods: they generate hydel power and are built to control floods.

  3. 3

    Disadvantage — rivers: damming affects natural flow and sediment flow, giving rockier stream beds and poorer aquatic habitats; fragmented rivers stop fish migrating to spawn.

  4. 4

    Disadvantage — floods: sedimentation has triggered floods, caused soil erosion and deprived flood plains of silt.

  5. 5

    Disadvantage — soil and health: a shift to water intensive crops has caused salinisation; projects have also caused water-borne diseases and pests.

  6. Check: two points from the left column and three from the right, and no point repeats another. The question says "two advantages and three disadvantages" — five points for five marks.

  7. Answer Advantages: irrigation and water supply; hydel power and flood control. Disadvantages: river flow and aquatic life; floods and lost silt; salinisation and diseases.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    1. They provide irrigation and supply water for homes and industries, even to water-scarce areas. (1)
    2. They generate hydel power for industries and homes, and are built to control floods. (1)
    3. Damming affects natural flow and sediment flow, leaving rockier stream beds and poorer habitats; fish cannot migrate to spawn. (1)
    4. Sedimentation has triggered floods, causing soil erosion and depriving flood plains of silt, a natural fertiliser. (1)
    5. A shift to water intensive crops has caused salinisation of the soil, and projects have caused water-borne diseases and pests. (1)
    Common trap: writing five disadvantages (or five advantages). The question asks for both; count each side.

Worked example 2

A flood-control dam and a flood

Question. An imagined reservoir, Kalisagar, could hold 900 million cubic metres of water when it was built. Thirty years later, silt fills 270 million cubic metres of it. (a) What share of the reservoir is now silt? (b) Use your textbook to explain how a dam built to control floods can end up triggering them, and give two results of such floods. (3 marks)

  1. (Kalisagar and its numbers are imagined for practice; the facts about floods are from your textbook.)

    Asked: a share, and an explanation with two results.

    Concept used: Dams and floods (section 2).

  2. 1

    (a) 270 ÷ 900 = 0.3, so 30 per cent of the reservoir is silt.

  3. 2

    (b) Cause: your textbook says dams have triggered floods due to sedimentation in the reservoir. (Beyond your textbook: a reservoir partly full of silt has less room to hold flood water.)

  4. 3

    (b) Results: such floods have devastated life and property and caused extensive soil erosion; downstream flood plains are deprived of silt, a natural fertiliser.

  5. Check: 30 per cent of 900 is 270 ✔. The two results are both from the textbook's own list.

  6. Answer (a) 30 per cent; (b) sedimentation in the reservoir has triggered floods, which destroyed life and property and caused soil erosion.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. (a) 270 ÷ 900 = 0.3, so 30 per cent of the reservoir is silt. (1)
    2. (b) Sedimentation in the reservoir has made dams trigger floods; big dams mostly fail during excessive rainfall. (1)
    3. Such floods devastated life and property, caused soil erosion and deprived flood plains of silt. (1)
    Common trap: writing "dams always stop floods". Your textbook says the opposite has happened.

Worked example 3

Which list?

Question. Using your textbook's floods box, sort each action: (a) Put cash and important papers in a safe place. (b) Drink only boiled and filtered water. (c) Switch off the power and gas before leaving home. (d) Keep away from fallen power lines.

Asked: the list of the box for each action — "Basic Safety Precautions" or "During floods".

Concept used: The floods box (section 8).

  1. 1

    (a) A precaution taken in advance → basic safety precautions.

  2. 2

    (b) A rule for drinking water once water is everywhere → during.

  3. 3

    (c) Done when leaving the house as the flood comes → basic safety precautions (the box lists it there).

  4. 4

    (d) A danger once streets are flooded → during.

  5. Check: the box's "Basic Safety Precautions" list ends with "turn off power and gas connections before leaving your house"; the "During floods" list has the drinking water and power-line rules.

  6. Answer (a) basic safety precautions; (b) during floods; (c) basic safety precautions; (d) during floods.

    Common trap: putting (c) under "during" because power lines are dangerous in a flood. The box places switching off power and gas before leaving the house.

Common mistakes!

  • "Dams are always good" or "dams are always bad".

    Your textbook gives both sides: irrigation, power, water supply and flood control — and ecological, flood and social problems.

    In "compare" answers, write points from both sides.

  • "Dams always stop floods."

    Dams built to control floods have triggered floods due to sedimentation, and have mostly been unsuccessful during excessive rainfall.

    Learn the textbook's word: "ironically".

  • "Dams send more silt to the fields."

    Poor sediment flow downstream; silt settles at the bottom of the reservoir; flood plains are deprived of silt.

    Silt stays behind the dam; fields below lose it.

  • "Irrigation always makes soil better."

    A shift to water intensive and commercial crops has caused salinisation of the soil.

    Pair "changed cropping pattern" with "salinisation".

  • Krishna-Godavari dispute: "between Tamil Nadu and Kerala".

    Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh objected to Maharashtra diverting more water at Koyna.

    Learn "two objectors, one diverter, one place: Koyna".

  • Flood steps in the wrong list — "turn off power and gas" under "during floods".

    The box lists it under basic safety precautions, before leaving your house; boiled and filtered water and staying out of flood waters are during floods.

    Sort each step by when you do it.

  • Writing only one side, or only two points, for a 5-mark "compare" question.

    Five marks need five different points, here from both sides.

    Count the marks; plan the split (e.g. two advantages + three disadvantages).

Key points

  • Large dams came under scrutiny and opposition: natural flow and sediment flow affected, rockier stream beds, poorer aquatic habitats; rivers fragmented (no migration for spawning); vegetation and soil submerged.
  • Ironically, dams have triggered floods through sedimentation; floods caused soil erosion, and flood plains lost silt, a natural fertiliser.
  • Projects have also induced earthquakes and caused water-borne diseases, pests and pollution; a shift to water intensive crops caused salinisation.
  • PMKSY: protective irrigation for all farms — "har khet ko pani", "per drop more crop", on-farm water-use efficiency, sustainable conservation.
  • Krishna-Godavari dispute: Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh object to Maharashtra's diversion at Koyna.
  • Floods box: basic safety precautions — warnings, emergency kit, valuables safe; if a flood comes, move to safe areas; turn off power and gas before leaving; during floods — stay out of flood water, boiled and filtered water, covered food, disinfect.
