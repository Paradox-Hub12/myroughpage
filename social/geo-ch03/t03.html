Topic 3 of 7

Dams and multi-purpose river projects

About 40 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Describe hydraulic structures in ancient India, what a dam is and how dams are classified.
  • Explain how multi-purpose projects integrate many uses of water, with Bhakra–Nangal, Hirakud and Sardar Sarovar as examples.

A single lake behind a wall of concrete can light a city, water thousands of fields, hold back a flood and keep a fishing boat afloat — all from the same water. How did people learn to do this, and why did a Prime Minister call such dams "temples"? This topic tells the story from ancient channels to modern projects.

  1. An old tradition: hydraulic structures in ancient India

Archaeological and historical records show that from ancient times we built sophisticated hydraulic structures — dams built of stone rubble, reservoirs or lakes, embankments and canals for irrigation. Your textbook's box "Hydraulic Structures in Ancient India" gives examples:

When Where What
First century B.C. Sringaverapura near Allahabad A sophisticated water harvesting system channelling the flood water of the river Ganga
Time of Chandragupta Maurya — Dams, lakes and irrigation systems were extensively built
(No date given) Kalinga (Odisha), Nagarjunakonda (Andhra Pradesh), Bennur (Karnataka), Kolhapur (Maharashtra) Evidence of sophisticated irrigation works
11th century Bhopal Lake One of the largest artificial lakes of its time
14th century Hauz Khas, Delhi A tank constructed by Iltutmish for supplying water to the Siri Fort area

Note on the textbook's wording. Your textbook places the Hauz Khas tank in the 14th century and names Iltutmish as its builder. Beyond your textbook: Iltutmish ruled in the 13th century. In the exam, write what your textbook says.

Memory hook — the three your textbook dates, in order of time: "Sringaverapura, Bhopal, Hauz Khas" — 1st century B.C. → 11th century → 14th century. Your textbook gives no date for the works of Chandragupta Maurya's time or for Kalinga, Nagarjunakonda, Bennur and Kolhapur, so do not put them in a "by century" order.

We continued this tradition in modern India by building dams in most of our river basins.

  1. What is a dam? (NCERT box)

  • A dam is a barrier across flowing water that obstructs, directs or retards the flow, often creating a reservoir, lake or impoundment.
  • "Dam" refers to the reservoir rather than the structure.
  • Most dams have a spillway or weir, over which or through which water is meant to flow, either now and then or all the time.

How dams are classified:

Basis Kinds
Structure and the materials used Timber dams, embankment dams, masonry dams (with several subtypes)
Height Large dams and major dams, or alternatively low dams, medium height dams and high dams
Intended purpose Named as a basis in your textbook (no list of kinds given)

NCERT Fig. 3.2 is a photograph of the Hirakud Dam.

  1. From one use to many uses

  • Dams were traditionally built to impound rivers and rainwater that could be used later to irrigate agricultural fields.
  • Today, dams are built for irrigation, electricity generation, water supply for domestic and industrial uses, flood control, recreation, inland navigation and fish breeding.
  • So dams are now called multi-purpose projects: the many uses of the impounded water are integrated with one another.

Your textbook's two examples:

Project River basin (your textbook) Uses integrated
Bhakra–Nangal Sutluj-Beas river basin Hydel power production and irrigation
Hirakud Mahanadi basin Conservation of water with flood control

  1. "Temples of modern India"

  • Multi-purpose projects were launched after Independence with an integrated water resources management approach.
  • They were seen as the vehicle that would lead the nation to development and progress, overcoming the handicap of its colonial past.
  • Jawaharlal Nehru proudly called the dams "temples of modern India". Reason: they would integrate development of agriculture and the village economy with rapid industrialisation and growth of the urban economy.

  1. A big project: Sardar Sarovar (NCERT box)

  • Sardar Sarovar Dam has been built over the Narmada River in Gujarat.
  • It is one of the largest water resource projects of India, covering four states — Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Rajasthan.
  • It is meant to meet the water needs of drought-prone and desert areas.

Reading the box's numbers — three questions:

  • Shows: land the project will irrigate — 18.45 lakh hectare in 3112 villages of 15 districts of Gujarat; 2,46,000 hectare in the desert districts of Barmer and Jalore in Rajasthan; 37,500 hectare in the tribal hilly tract of Maharashtra (through lift — water lifted to higher land).
  • Compare: 18.45 lakh hectare = 18,45,000 hectare, so Gujarat's area is about 7.5 times Rajasthan's (18,45,000 ÷ 2,46,000 ≈ 7.5) and about 49 times Maharashtra's (18,45,000 ÷ 37,500 = 49.2).
  • Textbook says: about 75 per cent of the command area in Gujarat and the entire command area in Rajasthan are drought prone; assured water supply will soon make this area drought proof.

Note on the textbook's wording. The box uses Indian place values: 18.45 lakh = 18,45,000; 2,46,000 = 2.46 lakh.

Worked example 1

Put them in order

Question. Arrange these in order of time, earliest first, using your textbook: P. Bhopal Lake is built; Q. Sringaverapura channels the flood water of the Ganga; R. the tank in Hauz Khas is constructed; S. dams are built in most of India's river basins.

Asked: the order of four events.

Concept used: Hydraulic structures in ancient India (section 1).

  1. 1

    Q — first century B.C. (Sringaverapura).

  2. 2

    P — 11th century (Bhopal Lake).

  3. 3

    R — 14th century (Hauz Khas).

  4. 4

    S — modern India, which "continued this tradition" by building dams in most river basins.

  5. Check: B.C. comes before every century A.D.; 11 comes before 14; "modern India" comes last. Every step uses a date your textbook prints.

  6. Answer Q, P, R, S

    Common trap: putting Bhopal Lake (11th century) after Hauz Khas (14th century) — the 11th century came first.

Worked example 2

One lake, four jobs

Question. An imagined reservoir in Vindhyagarh stores monsoon water. It turns turbines for electricity, feeds canals to farms, sends drinking water to two towns and holds back floods. (a) What does your textbook call a project like this? (b) Give your textbook's two examples, with the uses each one integrates. (2 marks)

  1. (Vindhyagarh is imagined; the examples are from your textbook.)

    Asked: the name of such a project and two textbook examples.

    Concept used: From one use to many uses (section 3).

  2. 1

    (a) Many uses of the impounded water, integrated → a multi-purpose project.

  3. 2

    (b) Bhakra–Nangal (Sutluj-Beas basin): hydel power and irrigation; Hirakud (Mahanadi basin): conservation of water with flood control.

  4. Check: each example has its river basin and its own pair of uses — swap them and the uses no longer match the textbook.

  5. Answer (a) A multi-purpose project; (b) Bhakra–Nangal — hydel power and irrigation; Hirakud — water conservation and flood control.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. It is a multi-purpose project, in which many uses of the impounded water are integrated with one another. (1)
    2. Bhakra–Nangal (Sutluj-Beas basin) gives hydel power and irrigation; Hirakud (Mahanadi basin) joins water conservation with flood control. (1)
    Common trap: writing "Hirakud gives hydel power and irrigation". That pair belongs to Bhakra–Nangal in your textbook.

Worked example 3

Wall or water?

Question. (a) What does the word "Dam" refer to, according to your textbook? (b) Name the two bases on which your textbook lists kinds of dams, and give the kinds under each.

Asked: the meaning of "Dam" and two bases with their kinds.

Concept used: What is a dam? (section 2)

  1. 1

    (a) "Dam" refers to the reservoir rather than the structure.

  2. 2

    (b) Structure and materials → timber, embankment and masonry dams.

  3. 3

    (b) Height → large and major dams, or low, medium height and high dams.

  4. Check: "timber, embankment, masonry" are all materials; "low, medium, high" are all heights — each list fits its own basis.

  5. Answer (a) The reservoir; (b) by structure and materials — timber, embankment, masonry; by height — large and major, or low, medium height, high.

    Common trap: answering (a) with "a wall of concrete". Your textbook says the word refers to the reservoir.

Common mistakes!

  • "A dam is only the concrete wall."

    Your textbook: "Dam" refers to the reservoir rather than the structure; a dam is a barrier that obstructs, directs or retards flow.

    Learn the definition in the textbook's words.

  • Mixing the bases of classification ("masonry" under height, "high" under materials).

    Structure and materials: timber, embankment, masonry. Height: large and major, or low, medium height, high.

    Keep each list with its own basis.

  • Listing only "electricity" as the use of a dam; swapping the two examples.

    Uses: irrigation, electricity, domestic and industrial water supply, flood control, recreation, inland navigation, fish breeding. Bhakra–Nangal → hydel power + irrigation; Hirakud → water conservation + flood control.

    Give several uses and each example with its own pair.

  • "Nehru called dams temples because they were beautiful."

    Dams would integrate agriculture and the village economy with rapid industrialisation and urban growth.

    Give the textbook's reason.

  • Ancient structures mixed — Bhopal Lake in the 14th century, Sringaverapura in Delhi.

    Sringaverapura (near Allahabad, 1st century B.C.); Chandragupta Maurya (dams, lakes, irrigation); Kalinga, Nagarjunakonda, Bennur, Kolhapur; Bhopal Lake (11th century); Hauz Khas tank (14th century, Siri Fort).

    Use the hook "Sringaverapura, Bhopal, Hauz Khas" for the dated three; learn the others by place.

  • Sardar Sarovar on the wrong river, or "covering Karnataka and Kerala".

    Built over the Narmada in Gujarat; covers Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Rajasthan.

    Learn "Narmada, Gujarat, four states" together.

Key points

  • Ancient India built sophisticated hydraulic structures: Sringaverapura (1st century B.C., Ganga flood water), works in Chandragupta Maurya's time, Kalinga, Nagarjunakonda, Bennur, Kolhapur, Bhopal Lake (11th century), the Hauz Khas tank (14th century).
  • A dam obstructs, directs or retards flowing water; "Dam" refers to the reservoir. Classified by structure and materials (timber, embankment, masonry) or height (large and major; low, medium, high).
  • Multi-purpose projects integrate many uses: irrigation, electricity, water supply, flood control, recreation, inland navigation, fish breeding. Bhakra–Nangal — hydel power + irrigation; Hirakud — water conservation + flood control.
  • Nehru: dams are the "temples of modern India" — they would join agriculture and the village economy with industry and urban growth.
  • Sardar Sarovar: on the Narmada in Gujarat; covers four states; irrigation for drought-prone and desert areas.
