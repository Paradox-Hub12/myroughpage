The whole chapter on one page

Renewable, yet scarce — about three-fourth of the earth's surface is water, but only a small proportion is usable freshwater, from surface run off and ground water, renewed through the hydrological cycle. Availability varies over space and time, but scarcity in most cases comes from over-exploitation, excessive use and unequal access. Many cities with ample water face scarcity. Scarcity can be of quality: water polluted by domestic and industrial wastes, chemicals, pesticides and fertilisers is hazardous for human use.

Causes and conservation — a growing population needs water for homes and food; water is over-exploited to irrigate dry-season crops — irrigated agriculture is the largest consumer (answer: drought resistant crops, dry farming). Farm wells and tube-wells may lower groundwater levels, hurting food security. Industries are heavy users of water and need hydroelectric power; city colonies pump their own groundwater → depletion. Why conserve: health, food security, livelihoods, ecosystems. Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM): potable piped water for every rural household, 55 litres per capita per day. Atal Jal: 8220 water-stressed Gram Panchayats in seven states (about 37 per cent of water-stressed blocks); consumption → conservation.

Dams and multi-purpose projects — ancient works: Sringaverapura (near Allahabad, 1st century B.C., Ganga flood water), Chandragupta Maurya (dams, lakes, irrigation), Kalinga, Nagarjunakonda, Bennur, Kolhapur, Bhopal Lake (11th century), Hauz Khas tank (14th century, Iltutmish, Siri Fort). A dam obstructs, directs or retards flowing water; "Dam" refers to the reservoir; spillway or weir. Kinds: timber, embankment, masonry (structure and materials); large and major or low, medium, high (height). Uses: irrigation, electricity, water supply, flood control, recreation, inland navigation, fish breeding → multi-purpose projects. Bhakra–Nangal (Sutluj-Beas basin): hydel power + irrigation; Hirakud (Mahanadi basin): conservation + flood control. Nehru: "temples of modern India" — joining agriculture and villages with industry and towns. Sardar Sarovar: on the Narmada in Gujarat; covers Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Rajasthan; irrigation for drought-prone and desert areas (Barmer and Jalore; Maharashtra's tribal hilly tract through lift).

Problems — natural and sediment flow affected; rockier stream beds; poorer aquatic habitats; rivers fragmented (no migration for spawning); vegetation and soil submerged. Dams have triggered floods (sedimentation); floods cause soil erosion; flood plains lose silt. Earthquakes, water-borne diseases, pests, pollution. Shift to water intensive and commercial crops → salinisation. Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchaee Yojana (PMKSY): "har khet ko pani", "per drop more crop", on-farm efficiency, sustainable conservation. Krishna-Godavari: Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh object to Maharashtra's diversion at Koyna. Floods box: basic safety precautions — warnings, emergency kit, valuables safe; if a flood comes, move to safe areas; turn off power and gas before leaving; during floods — stay out of flood water, boiled and filtered water, covered food, bleaching powder and lime.

Rainwater harvesting — guls/kuls (Western Himalayas), rooftop (Rajasthan), inundation channels (Bengal), khadins (Jaisalmer), johads (other parts of Rajasthan), tankas (Bikaner, Phalodi, Barmer): fed from sloping roofs, first spell not collected, palar pani, cool underground rooms; declining in western Rajasthan (Indira Gandhi Canal). Modern: Gendathur (Mysuru, Karnataka; ~200 households; ~50,000 litres per house), Shillong (15–25 per cent; Cherapunjee and Mawsynram 55 km away), Tamil Nadu (first state, compulsory), Fig. 3.3 (PVC pipe → filter → sump → well → recharge). Bamboo drip (Meghalaya, 200 years): 18–20 litres in → 20–80 drops per minute.

Map — Salal (Chenab), Bhakra Nangal (Satluj), Tehri (upper Ganga), Rana Pratap Sagar (Chambal), Sardar Sarovar (Narmada, Gujarat), Hirakud (Mahanadi), Nagarjuna Sagar (Krishna), Tungabhadra (Tungabhadra).

Terms, numbers and places sheet

Key terms (as in your textbook — learn the words in bold)

  • Hydrological cycle: the movement of all water, which continually renews and recharges surface run off and ground water — so water is renewable.
  • Water scarcity (our definition; your textbook gives none): a shortage of enough (or safe enough) water; in most cases caused by over-exploitation, excessive use and unequal access.
  • Dam: a barrier across flowing water that obstructs, directs or retards the flow, often creating a reservoir, lake or impoundment; "Dam" refers to the reservoir.
  • Multi-purpose project: a dam project in which the many uses of the impounded water are integrated with one another.
  • Salinisation: salt building up in the soil, after a shift to water intensive and commercial crops.
  • Palar pani: rainwater, considered the purest form of natural water (Rajasthan).
  • Khadins / johads: fields turned into rain fed storage structures (Jaisalmer / other parts of Rajasthan).
  • Tanka: an underground tank for drinking water, fed from the roof.

Numbers to know

  • three-fourth — earth's surface covered by water · 2025 — the textbook's year for its "two billion in absolute scarcity" prediction
  • 55 — litres per capita per day (JJM) · 8220, seven, about 37 per cent — Atal Jal
  • 1st century B.C. (Sringaverapura) · 11th century (Bhopal Lake) · 14th century (Hauz Khas)
  • four states — Sardar Sarovar · 18.45 lakh ha (Gujarat) · 2,46,000 ha (Barmer, Jalore) · 37,500 ha (Maharashtra) · 75 per cent of Gujarat's command drought prone
  • 6.1 × 4.27 × 2.44 m — a Phalodi tanka · 1,000 mm, 80 per cent, about 10 fillings, about 50,000 litres per house, nearly 200 households — Gendathur
  • 15–25 per cent — Shillong households · 55 km — Shillong to Cherapunjee and Mawsynram · 200 years, 18–20 litres, 20–80 drops per minute — bamboo drip

Memory hooks
