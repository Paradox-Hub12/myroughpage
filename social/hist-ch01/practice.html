P13●● StandardVery short answer · 2 marksOriginal

Why can the Habsburg Empire be called a "patchwork" rather than a nation-state? Give two reasons.

Full solution

Asked: two reasons the empire was not a nation-state.

Concept used: The Habsburg Empire (Topic 3); the nation-state (Topic 1).

  1. 1

    Many peoples and languages: German-speakers in Bohemia, Italian-speakers in Lombardy and Venetia, Magyar speakers in Hungary, a Polish-speaking aristocracy in Galicia, and subject peasant peoples such as Slovaks, Slovenes, Croats and Roumans.

  2. 2

    No shared identity: these groups did not see themselves as one people; the only tie was a common allegiance to the emperor.

  3. Check: a nation-state needs the majority of citizens to share an identity; point 2 shows the empire lacked exactly that, so the two reasons together prove the point.

  4. Answer It held many peoples with different languages, bound only by allegiance to the emperor, not by a shared identity.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. It joined many peoples with different languages: German, Italian, Magyar and Polish speakers, Slovaks, Croats and others. (1)
    2. They shared no common identity; the only tie binding them was allegiance to the emperor. (1)
    Common trap: writing that the empire was a patchwork because it was small. It was a patchwork because of its many different peoples.

P27●●● AdvancedLong answer · 5 marksOriginal

"Long before 1871, Prussia was preparing to lead Germany." Examine the role of Prussia from the customs union of 1834 to the proclamation of 1871.

Full solution

Asked: Prussia's role, in order, from 1834 to 1871.

Concept used: The Zollverein (Topic 3) and German unification (Topic 7). Why it applies: the question joins the economic and the political roads.

  1. 1

    Economic lead (1834): the zollverein was formed at Prussia's initiative; it abolished tariff barriers and cut currencies from over thirty to two, building economic nationalism.

  2. 2

    Crushing the liberal road (1848): Prussia's monarchy and military, backed by the Junkers, repressed the liberal attempt to unite Germany under an elected parliament.

  3. 3

    Taking the lead: from then on Prussia led the movement, with Bismarck, its chief minister, as the architect, using the army and bureaucracy.

  4. 4

    Three wars: with Austria, Denmark and France, all ending in Prussian victory.

  5. 5

    1871 and after: William I of Prussia was proclaimed German Emperor at Versailles; Prussian measures became a model for the new state's currency, banking, legal and judicial systems.

  6. Check: five points in time order, each showing Prussia acting; a point about Mazzini or Cavour would be outside the question.

  7. Answer Prussia led the customs union, crushed the liberal road, and under Bismarck won three wars and made its king German Emperor, shaping the new state on its own model.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    1. In 1834 Prussia took the initiative in forming the zollverein, which abolished tariffs, reduced currencies to two and fostered economic nationalism. (1)
    2. In 1848 Prussia's monarchy and military, backed by the Junkers, repressed the liberal attempt at unification. (1)
    3. Prussia then led the movement; its chief minister Bismarck was the architect, using the army and bureaucracy. (1)
    4. Three wars, with Austria, Denmark and France, ended in Prussian victory. (1)
    5. In January 1871 Prussia's king William I became German Emperor at Versailles; Prussian practices became a model for Germany. (1)
    Common trap: starting in 1866 and missing the zollverein. The question begins in 1834.

"The French armies were welcomed in many places — and then resented." Examine the statement with reference to the years of revolution and Napoleon.

Full solution

Asked: why the French were first welcomed and why they were later resented.

Concept used: Carrying the nation abroad; Napoleon's reforms and the reactions (Topic 2).

  1. 1

    Prepared ground: Jacobin clubs set up by students and the educated middle classes in many European cities prepared the way for the French armies.

  2. 2

    Welcome: in Holland, Switzerland and cities like Brussels, Mainz, Milan and Warsaw, the French were greeted as harbingers of liberty.

  3. 3

    Real gains: the Civil Code, the end of feudalism, serfdom and guild restrictions, and better transport gave peasants, artisans and businessmen a new-found freedom.

  4. 4

    Disappointment: the new administration did not bring political freedom.

  5. 5

    Resentment: increased taxation, censorship and forced conscription outweighed the gains — shown in Fig. 4, where French soldiers appear as oppressors.

    (a) Poland had been partitioned at the end of the eighteenth century by Russia, Prussia and Austria; it no longer existed as an independent territory.

  2. 2

  3. 3
