Topic 8 of 10

The strange case of Britain — three roads compared

About 30 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Describe the long-drawn-out making of the British nation-state (1688, 1707, 1798/1801, symbols).
  • Compare how Germany, Italy and Britain became nation-states.

Germany was made in seven years of war. Italy was made in about eleven years of war, diplomacy and a march of volunteers. Britain had no single year of birth at all. It grew slowly, over more than a century, as one people — the English — spread their parliament, their language and their flag over the Scots, the Welsh and the Irish. Some scholars call it the model nation-state. For the Scottish Highlanders and the Irish, the model came at a heavy price.

  1. No sudden upheaval

Some scholars call Great Britain the model of the nation or the nation-state. In Britain, the nation-state was not the result of a sudden upheaval or revolution, but of a long-drawn-out process.

  • There was no British nation before the eighteenth century. The people of the British Isles had ethnic identities — English, Welsh, Scot or Irish — each with its own cultural and political traditions.
  • England grew in wealth, importance and power, and this let it extend its influence over the other nations of the British Isles.

New word. Ethnic — relating to a common racial, tribal or cultural origin or background that a community identifies with or claims.

  1. Step by step

Step When What happened
1. Parliament takes power 1688 The English parliament seized power from the monarchy at the end of a long conflict. It became the instrument through which a nation-state with England at its centre was forged
2. Scotland joined 1707 The Act of Union between England and Scotland formed the "United Kingdom of Great Britain" — in effect, England imposed its influence on Scotland; the British parliament was dominated by its English members
3. Scotland's culture suppressed after 1707 Scotland's distinctive culture and political institutions were systematically suppressed. The Catholic clans of the Scottish Highlands suffered terrible repression whenever they tried to assert independence; Highlanders were forbidden to speak Gaelic or wear their national dress, and large numbers were forcibly driven out of their homeland
4. Ireland 1798, 1801 A largely Catholic country, sharply divided between Catholics and Protestants. England backed the Protestants so that they ruled, and Catholic revolts were put down. Wolfe Tone and his United Irishmen rose in 1798 and failed; in 1801 Ireland was forcibly incorporated into the United Kingdom
5. A "British nation" 18th–19th century Made by spreading a dominant English culture. New British symbols were actively promoted: the Union Jack (British flag), the national anthem (God Save Our Noble King) and the English language. The older nations were kept only as subordinate partners

Worked examples

Worked example 1

What Scotland lost

Question. Explain how the making of the United Kingdom of Great Britain affected Scotland. (3 marks)

Asked: three effects on Scotland.

Concept used: Steps 2–3 (section 2).

  1. 1

    Political: the Act of Union of 1707 meant England could impose its influence on Scotland; the British parliament was dominated by English members.

  2. 2

    Culture and institutions: Scotland's distinctive culture and political institutions were systematically suppressed.

  3. 3

    The Highlanders: the Catholic clans faced terrible repression; they were forbidden to speak Gaelic or wear their national dress, and many were forcibly driven out of their homeland.

  4. Check: each point is an effect on Scotland; a point about Ireland (Wolfe Tone, 1801) would be true but would not answer this question.

  5. Answer Scotland lost political weight to the English, its culture and institutions were suppressed, and the Highlanders lost their language, dress and homes.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Under the Act of Union (1707) England imposed its influence on Scotland; English members dominated the British parliament. (1)
    2. Scotland's distinctive culture and political institutions were systematically suppressed. (1)
    3. Highland clans were repressed, forbidden Gaelic and national dress, and many were forcibly driven out of their homeland. (1)
    Common trap: writing that Scotland was incorporated in 1801. That was Ireland; Scotland joined by the Act of Union of 1707.

Worked example 2

Filling one row of the table

Question. Complete the row "Method" of the three-roads table in one line for each country, and name the country whose road had no single revolution or war of unification.

Asked: the method for each country; the odd one out.

Concept used: Section 3.

  1. 1

    Germany: three wars over seven years (Austria, Denmark, France), led by Bismarck and the Prussian army.

  2. 2

    Italy: war and diplomacy — Cavour's alliance with France against Austria — plus Garibaldi's volunteers.

  3. 3

    Britain: a long-drawn-out process — the 1688 parliament, the Act of Union (1707), suppression of Scottish and Irish cultures, Ireland incorporated (1801).

  4. 4

    Odd one out: Britain.

  5. Check: only Britain's row has no war of unification and no single year of birth; both other rows name wars.

  6. Answer Germany — three wars; Italy — war, diplomacy and volunteers; Britain — a slow process. Britain is the odd one out.

    Common trap: writing "the British revolution of 1688". The textbook says parliament seized power from the monarchy; it stresses that Britain's nation-state came without a sudden upheaval.

Common mistakes!

  • "Britain became a nation-state through a revolution, like France."

    It was a long-drawn-out process, not a sudden upheaval.

    Begin every Britain answer with "long-drawn-out process".

  • "Ireland joined by the Act of Union of 1707."

    1707 = England + Scotland; Ireland = 1801, forcibly.

    Learn 1707 Scotland, 1801 Ireland.

  • "The Scots and Irish happily joined an equal union."

    Their cultures were suppressed; older nations became subordinate partners.

    Write the costs: Gaelic banned, Catholic revolts suppressed.

  • Mixing the three roads (for example, "Bismarck allied with France").

    Cavour made the alliance with France; Bismarck fought France.

    Use the three-roads table.

Key points

  • Britain: not a sudden upheaval but a long-drawn-out process; before the 18th century people were English, Welsh, Scot or Irish.
  • 1688: English parliament seizes power — the instrument of a nation-state with England at its centre. 1707: Act of Union (England + Scotland) → United Kingdom of Great Britain; English members dominate.
  • Scotland's culture and institutions suppressed; Highlanders forbidden Gaelic and national dress, driven out.
  • Ireland: Catholic–Protestant divide; English backed the Protestants; Wolfe Tone, 1798 fails → forcibly incorporated, 1801.
  • Symbols: Union Jack, God Save Our Noble King, English language; older nations = subordinate partners.
  • Three roads: Germany — war; Italy — war, diplomacy, volunteers; Britain — slow process.
