The whole chapter on one page

The idea of a nation — Sorrieu (1848) dreamt of "democratic and social Republics" (Fig. 1): peoples march past the statue of Liberty; shattered symbols of absolutist institutions; USA and Switzerland already nation-states; Germany not yet united. A nation-state: the majority of citizens, not only rulers, share a common identity and history. Renan (1882): a nation is a shared past and a common will — "a daily plebiscite"; nations guarantee liberty.

France and Napoleon — 1789: sovereignty passes from the monarchy to the citizens — the first clear expression of nationalism. Measures: la patrie, le citoyen, tricolour, National Assembly, hymns and oaths, uniform laws, no internal customs, uniform weights and measures, Paris French. Mission to liberate Europe; Jacobin clubs; French armies in Holland, Belgium, Switzerland, Italy. Napoleon destroyed democracy but made administration rational and efficient: Civil Code 1804 (no birth privileges, equality before law, right to property); feudalism, serfdom and guilds ended. Welcome turned to hostility: taxes, censorship, conscription.

Liberals and conservatives — Liberalism (liber = free): freedom, equality before the law, government by consent, constitution, parliament, free markets; but votes only for property-owning men. Zollverein (1834), Prussia: tariffs abolished, currencies 30+ → 2; List (Source B). Conservatism after 1815: preserve monarchy, Church, hierarchy, property, family, using modernisation. Vienna 1815 (host Metternich): Bourbons restored; Netherlands with Belgium; Genoa to Piedmont; Prussia west + part of Saxony; Austria north Italy; Russia part of Poland; 39-state confederation untouched. Autocracy and censorship (Fig. 6). Mazzini: Carbonari; exiled 1831; Young Italy (Marseilles), Young Europe (Berne); a unified Italian republic.

1830–1848 — July 1830: Louis Philippe; Belgium breaks away. Greece: struggle from 1821; Byron (died 1824), Delacroix; Treaty of Constantinople 1832. Romanticism: emotion over reason; Herder (das volk); Grimm brothers (1812); Poland: Kurpinski, polonaise, mazurka; Polish in church after 1831. 1830s hardship: population, slums, English goods, feudal dues, food prices. 1845 Silesian weavers (eleven shot). 1848 Paris: Louis Philippe flees; votes for men over 21, right to work. Liberal revolution: Frankfurt parliament, 18 May 1848, 831 deputies, Church of St Paul; crown rejected by Friedrich Wilhelm IV; disbanded by troops; women only observers (Source C: "Liberty is indivisible"). After 1848: serfdom abolished; Hungarians' autonomy 1867.

Unification — Germany: 1848 attempt crushed (monarchy, army, Junkers) → Bismarck, architect; army and bureaucracy → three wars (Austria, Denmark, France) → 18 January 1871, Versailles: William I Kaiser. Italy: seven states → Mazzini's republican programme; revolutionary uprisings of 1831 and 1848 fail → Cavour + France defeat Austria 1859 → Garibaldi, Red Shirts, South Italy 1860 → Victor Emmanuel II king 1861 → Venetia 1866 → Rome 1870. Britain: a long-drawn-out process — 1688 parliament → Act of Union 1707 (Scotland) → Gaelic banned → Wolfe Tone 1798 → Ireland 1801 → Union Jack, anthem, English.

Pictures and empire — Allegory: an abstract idea as a person. Marianne (France): red cap, tricolour, cockade; stamps (1850). Germania: crown of oak leaves (heroism); Veit 1848 (Box 3); Hübner 1850; Clasen 1860. After 1871 nationalism became a narrow creed; Balkans: romantic nationalism + Ottoman disintegration → breakaways → jealous states → big-power rivalry → wars → 1914. Anti-imperial movements: own varieties; the nation-state becomes universal.

Key definitions

(as in your textbook — learn the words in bold)

  • Absolutist: a government or system of rule with no restraints on the power exercised; in history, a centralised, militarised and repressive monarchy.
  • Utopian: a vision of a society so ideal that it is unlikely to actually exist.
  • Plebiscite: a direct vote by which all the people of a region are asked to accept or reject a proposal.
  • Suffrage: the right to vote.
  • Conservatism: a political philosophy stressing tradition, established institutions and customs, preferring gradual development to quick change.
  • Feminist: awareness of women's rights and interests based on the belief in the social, economic and political equality of the genders.
  • Ideology: a system of ideas reflecting a particular social and political vision.
  • Ethnic: relating to a common racial, tribal or cultural origin or background that a community identifies with or claims.
  • Allegory: when an abstract idea is expressed through a person or a thing; it has two meanings, one literal and one symbolic.
  • Nation-state: a state in which the majority of its citizens, and not only its rulers, share a sense of common identity and shared history or descent.
