Week 1

Set 2 — Week 1

5 questions, about 8 minutes

Focus: the reasons behind the facts.

W1.1 · 1 of 5●● Standard

France was already a full-fledged territorial state before 1789. So what was new about the nation after the Revolution?

Answer: Sovereignty passed from the monarchy to a body of French citizens; the people would now constitute the nation and shape its destiny. (Topic 2)

Did you get it?

W1.2 · 2 of 5●● Standard

Why did the conservatives of 1815 not simply bring back everything as it was before the Revolution?

Answer: They had learnt from Napoleon that modernisation — a modern army, an efficient bureaucracy, a dynamic economy, the end of serfdom — could make monarchies stronger. (Topic 4)

Did you get it?

W1.3 · 3 of 5●● Standard

Why did the Frankfurt parliament lose the support of workers and artisans?

Answer: It was dominated by the middle classes, who resisted the demands of workers and artisans. (Topic 6)

Did you get it?

W1.4 · 4 of 5●● Standard

For the ruling elites of Sardinia-Piedmont, what did a unified Italy offer?

Answer: The possibility of economic development and political dominance. (Topic 7)

Did you get it?

W1.5 · 5 of 5●● Standard

In what sense were the anti-imperial movements in the colonies "nationalist"?

Answer: They struggled to form independent nation-states, inspired by a sense of collective national unity forged in confrontation with imperialism — each with its own variety of nationalism. (Topic 10)

Month 1

Set 3 — Month 1

5 questions, about 12 minutes

Focus: linking topics — the kind of thinking the 5-mark questions need.

M1.1 · 1 of 5●●● Advanced

Link three ideas: the Napoleonic Code's treatment of women, Welcker's speech, and Louise Otto-Peters' editorial. What do they show together?

Answer: (1) The Code reduced women to the status of a minor; (2) Welcker argued that equality of the sexes would endanger family harmony; (3) Otto-Peters replied that "Liberty is indivisible". Together they show that liberal equality was not extended to women, who had to demand it. (Topics 3 and 6)

Did you get it?

M1.2 · 2 of 5●●● Advanced

Germany and Italy were both unified "from above". Give one piece of evidence for each country, and one reason this matters.

Answer: Germany — Prussia's army and bureaucracy under Bismarck, after the elected Frankfurt parliament had been crushed. Italy — the kingdom of Sardinia-Piedmont under Cavour and Victor Emmanuel II, while southern peasants thought "La Talia" was the king's wife. Why it matters: the nations were made by states and elites before many ordinary people shared the national idea. (Topic 7)

Did you get it?

M1.3 · 3 of 5●●● Advanced

Which state set up at Vienna in 1815 on France's northern border was broken up in 1830, and how?

Answer: The kingdom of the Netherlands, including Belgium — an uprising in Brussels after the July Revolution led Belgium to break away. (Topics 4 and 5)

Did you get it?

M1.4 · 4 of 5●●● Advanced

Using Box 3, explain why Veit put both a sword and an olive branch in Germania's hands.

Answer: The sword means readiness to fight and the olive branch means willingness to make peace — the nation is shown as ready to fight, yet willing to make peace. (Topic 9)

Did you get it?

M1.5 · 5 of 5●●● Advanced

Both Greece (1820s) and the later Balkan nationalities broke away from the Ottoman Empire. Why was the first welcomed by Europe's educated elite, while the later ones became a source of war?

Answer: Greece was praised as the cradle of European civilisation, and poets and artists mobilised opinion for it. The later Balkan breakaways came when nationalism had become a narrow creed: the new states were jealous of each other, and Russia, Germany, England and Austro-Hungary competed for control — leading to wars and finally 1914. (Topics 5 and 10)

Did you get it?
