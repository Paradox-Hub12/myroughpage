The age of revolutions, 1830–1848, and the Romantic imagination

About 35 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Describe the July Revolution of 1830 and the Belgian uprising.
  • Explain the Greek war of independence and why it mobilised Europe.
  • Explain how Romanticism, folk culture, music and language built national feeling (Herder, Grimm, Poland).

How do you make people who cannot read feel that they belong to one nation? You sing to them. You tell them the stories their grandmothers told. You dance their village dances on a stage and call them national dances. And when a foreign ruler bans your language in school, you pray in it in church. In the 1820s–1840s nationalism spread not only through wars but through poems, paintings, fairy tales and music.

  1. Liberalism, nationalism and revolution (1830–1848)

Conservative regimes clung to power, so in many regions liberalism and nationalism came to be tied to revolution — in the Italian and German states, the provinces of the Ottoman Empire, Ireland and Poland. The leaders were liberal-nationalists from the educated middle-class elite: professors, schoolteachers, clerks and members of the commercial middle classes.

  1. July 1830: France sneezes

  • The first upheaval came in France in July 1830. The Bourbon kings, restored in 1815, were overthrown by liberal revolutionaries, who set up a constitutional monarchy with Louis Philippe at its head.
  • Metternich once remarked: "When France sneezes, the rest of Europe catches cold."
  • In Brussels, the July Revolution set off an uprising: Belgium broke away from the United Kingdom of the Netherlands (the kingdom Vienna had set up in 1815).

  1. The Greek war of independence

Reading NCERT Fig. 8 — The Massacre at Chios, Eugène Delacroix, 1824. A very large painting (4.19 m × 3.54 m). On the island of Chios, Turks were said to have killed 20,000 Greeks. Delacroix, a leading French Romantic painter, made the scene dramatic: he put the suffering of women and children at the centre and used vivid colours to appeal to the emotions of viewers and create sympathy for the Greeks.

  1. The Romantic imagination and national feeling

Wars and new territory were not the only builders of nationalism. Culture — art and poetry, stories and music — also gave shape and voice to national feeling.

Romanticism was a cultural movement that sought to develop a particular form of nationalist sentiment:

  • Romantic artists and poets did not glorify reason and science; they valued emotions, intuition and mystical feelings instead.
  • They tried to create a sense of a shared collective heritage, a common cultural past, as the basis of a nation.

Herder and das volk. Johann Gottfried Herder (1744–1803), a German philosopher, said that the common people — das volk — kept true German culture alive. The spirit of the nation (volksgeist) lived in folk songs, folk poetry and folk dances, and spread through them. So, for him, collecting and recording folk culture was a key part of building a nation.

Why folk culture and the local language? To recover an old national spirit — and also to take the modern nationalist message to large audiences, most of whom could not read.

Box 1 — The Grimm brothers.

  • Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm, born in Hanau in 1785 and 1786; both studied law but began collecting old folktales.
  • They spent six years travelling from village to village, talking to people and writing down fairy tales handed down through generations; their first collection appeared in 1812.
  • They became active in liberal politics, especially the movement for freedom of the press, and published a 33-volume dictionary of the German language.
  • They saw French domination as a threat to German culture; for them the folktales expressed a pure and authentic German spirit, and collecting tales and developing the German language were part of the effort to oppose French domination and create a German national identity.

  1. Poland: music and language as weapons

At the end of the eighteenth century the Great Powers — Russia, Prussia and Austria — had partitioned Poland. Poland was no longer an independent territory, but music and language kept national feeling alive:

Music Language
Karol Kurpinski used his operas and music to celebrate the Polish struggle; he made the folk dances polonaise and mazurka into nationalist symbols Under Russian occupation, Russian was imposed everywhere and the Polish language was forced out of schools
In 1831 an armed rebellion against Russian rule was crushed
Many clergy turned language into a weapon of national resistance: they used Polish for Church gatherings and all religious teaching
Many priests and bishops were jailed or sent to Siberia for refusing to preach in Russian
So the use of Polish became a symbol of the struggle against Russian dominance

Worked example 1

A poet and a painter for Greece

Question. Neither Lord Byron nor Eugène Delacroix was Greek. Explain how each of them helped the Greek struggle. (3 marks)

Asked: Byron's help and Delacroix's help, and the link to European opinion.

Concept used: The Greek war of independence (section 3) and Fig. 8.

  1. 1

    Byron: the English poet organised funds, then went to fight in the war, where he died of fever in 1824.

  2. 2

    Delacroix: his huge painting The Massacre at Chios (1824) showed the suffering of women and children in vivid colours to appeal to emotions and create sympathy for the Greeks.

  3. 3

    The effect: poets and artists like them mobilised public opinion across Europe, praising Greece as the cradle of European civilisation.

  4. Check: each point names what the person did; one is money and fighting, the other is a painting. If both points said "they wrote poems", Delacroix's point would be wrong.

  5. Answer Byron raised funds and fought (dying in 1824); Delacroix's painting roused sympathy; together they mobilised European opinion for Greece.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. The English poet Lord Byron organised funds for the Greeks and went to fight, dying of fever in 1824. (1)
    2. Delacroix's painting The Massacre at Chios showed suffering women and children in vivid colours to win sympathy for the Greeks. (1)
    3. Such poets and artists praised Greece as the cradle of European civilisation and mobilised public opinion for its struggle. (1)
    Common trap: saying Greece became independent in 1821. The struggle began in 1821; the Treaty of Constantinople recognised independence in 1832.

Worked example 2

Why the priests chose Polish

Question. After 1831, why did many Polish priests use Polish in church, even though it led to punishment? (2 marks)

Asked: the reason for using Polish and what it meant.

Concept used: Poland: language as a weapon (section 5).

  1. 1

    Background: under Russian occupation Polish was forced out of schools and Russian imposed; the 1831 rebellion was crushed, so open revolt had failed.

  2. 2

    The choice: the clergy used language as a weapon of national resistance — Polish in Church gatherings and religious teaching kept national feeling alive and became a symbol of struggle against Russian dominance.

  3. Check: the answer gives the reason (failed rebellion, Russian imposed) and the meaning (resistance). Saying "Polish was the only language they knew" is not in the textbook and misses the political point.

  4. Answer After the 1831 rebellion was crushed, using Polish was a way to resist Russian rule; it became a symbol of the national struggle.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. Russia had forced Polish out of schools and crushed the armed rebellion of 1831, so open revolt had failed. (1)
    2. Using Polish in Church gatherings and religious teaching became a symbol of resistance to Russian dominance. (1)
    Common trap: writing "1931". The rebellion against Russian rule was in 1831.

Common mistakes!

  • "Romantics praised reason and science."

    They criticised the glorification of reason and science.

    Romanticism = emotion, intuition, folk culture.

  • Greek independence dated 1821.

    1821 = struggle begins; 1832 = Treaty of Constantinople.

    Learn the pair: 1821 → 1832.

  • "Herder collected the fairy tales."

    The Grimm brothers collected the tales; Herder gave the idea of das volk.

    Herder = idea; Grimms = collection.

  • "Belgium broke away from France in 1830."

    It broke away from the United Kingdom of the Netherlands.

    Write: Brussels uprising → Belgium leaves the Netherlands.

Key points

  • 1830–1848: liberalism and nationalism tied to revolution, led by the educated middle-class elite (professors, schoolteachers, clerks, commercial classes).
  • July 1830: Bourbons overthrown; Louis Philippe's constitutional monarchy; "When France sneezes…"; Brussels uprising → Belgium leaves the United Kingdom of the Netherlands.
  • Greece: Ottoman since the 15th century; struggle from 1821; exiled Greeks and West Europeans helped; poets and artists (Byron, died 1824; Delacroix, Fig. 8) mobilised opinion; Treaty of Constantinople 1832.
  • Romanticism: emotion and intuition over reason; Herder — das volk, volksgeist; Grimm brothers (tales 1812, dictionary, against French domination).
  • Poland: partitioned by Russia, Prussia, Austria; Kurpinski, polonaise, mazurka; after the 1831 rebellion, Polish in church — a symbol of resistance.
