History · Chapter 1

The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

10 topicsAbout 11 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

In 1848 a French artist drew a dream: all the peoples of Europe and America marching together towards a statue of Liberty, each people carrying its own flag. At that time there was no Germany and no Italy on the map — only kingdoms, duchies and empires, ruled by kings and emperors over many different peoples. By 1914, nationalism had helped to drag Europe into the First World War. This chapter tells how the idea of the nation was born in the French Revolution, how it travelled across Europe, how Germany, Italy and Britain became nation-states in very different ways, and how a hopeful idea turned into a dangerous one.

You will follow the story from France (1789) to Napoleon, the Treaty of Vienna (1815), the revolutions of 1830 and 1848, the unification of Germany and Italy, the making of Britain, the way artists pictured the nation, and the Balkans before 1914. It is one of the four History chapters in the board exam (History carries 18 marks + 2 map marks). In the CBSE board papers of 2026 it was the 5-mark History long answer in 12 of the 15 sets, and almost every paper since 2024 has at least one 1-mark question on it (often a chronology or a picture question).

What you'll learn

  • Read Sorrieu's print, explain what a nation-state is, and summarise Renan's idea of a nation.
  • Explain how the French Revolution created a sense of nation, how the idea spread, and what Napoleon's Civil Code changed.
  • Explain what liberalism stood for and how the Zollverein helped economic nationalism.
  • Explain the Treaty of Vienna, the conservative regimes and the secret societies of Mazzini.
  • Describe the revolution of 1830; the Greek war of independence; and how art, folk culture, music and language built national feeling.
  • Explain the hardship of the 1830s, the two revolutions of 1848 and the Frankfurt parliament.
  • Trace the unification of Germany and of Italy, step by step.
  • Describe how Britain became a nation-state and compare the three roads.
  • Identify Marianne and Germania and read the symbols in their pictures.
  • Explain why the Balkans became explosive and how nationalism led to 1914.
  • Put the whole story in order and answer mixed board-style questions.

Most of the marks in this chapter come from five skills, and every topic practises them:

  1. Keep the order of events — every topic ends with a mini-timeline; the wrap-up joins them into one.
  2. Trace a process step by step — how Germany, Italy and Britain became nation-states.
  3. Link cause → event → effect — why something happened and what it led to.
  4. Read a picture or source — what it shows, what it tells you, what its limit or point of view is.
  5. Write to the marks — 2 marks ≈ two points, 3 marks ≈ three points, 5 marks ≈ five explained points, each on its own line.

The spaced-review sets come back to you after 1 day, 1 week and 1 month. Don't skip them — remembering something after a gap is what makes it stick.

Not in the board exam

The textbook's Project (nationalist symbols in countries outside Europe) is a research task; it gets a short guided answer in the NCERT solutions and is not part of the board exam. Everything else in the chapter — including Sources A–C, Boxes 1–3, the "Some important dates" box and the figure captions — can be asked in the board exam. This chapter has no map items in the board exam.
