Wrap-up

Put Europe in order

About 25 minutes · 6 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain why the French Revolution was the first clear expression of nationalism.
  • Explain what liberalism stood for.
  • Explain conservatism and the Treaty of Vienna.
  • Explain why the unifications were led from above.
  • Compare how Germany, Italy and Britain became nation-states.
  • Identify Marianne and Germania and their attributes.

Almost every question on this chapter asks you to do one of four things: put events in order, tell liberals from conservatives, trace how a country became a nation-state, or read a picture of the nation. This page puts the chapter on four cards and works through four mixed questions.

Four mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

Which came first?

Question. Arrange in order, earliest first: I. Lord Byron dies of fever in Greece. II. The Zollverein is formed. III. Napoleon loses the battle of Leipzig. IV. Mazzini founds Young Europe in Berne.

Asked: the chronological order.

Concept used: The timeline (Card 1) and the topic timelines.

  1. 1

    Date each: III — 1813; I — 1824; IV — 1833; II — 1834.

  2. 2

    Sort: 1813 → 1824 → 1833 → 1834.

  3. Check: IV and II are only one year apart — Young Europe (1833) comes just before the Zollverein (1834); Leipzig (1813) is still in Napoleon's time, so it must be first ✔.

  4. Answer III, I, IV, II

    Common trap: putting the Zollverein first because "economics comes before politics". Use the dates, not a rule of thumb.

Mixed example 2

Reading a picture

Question. A picture shows a woman wearing a red cap, with a tricolour and a cockade, printed on a postage stamp of 1850. (a) Whom does she represent? (b) Why was a popular Christian name chosen for her? (c) Why were such images put on coins and stamps?

Asked: the identity, the reason for the name, the purpose of the images.

Concept used: Topic 9, section 2.

  1. 1

    (a) Marianne, the allegory of France (NCERT Fig. 16 shows stamps of 1850 with Marianne representing the Republic of France).

  2. 2

    (b) A popular Christian name underlined the idea of a people's nation.

  3. 3

    (c) Statues, coins and stamps reminded people of the national symbol of unity and persuaded them to identify with it.

  4. Check: the red cap, tricolour and cockade are Marianne's signs (from Liberty and the Republic); Germania would have a crown of oak leaves, so (a) cannot be Germania ✔.

  5. Answer (a) Marianne (France). (b) To stress a people's nation. (c) To remind people of the symbol of unity and make them identify with it.

    Common trap: writing that Marianne was a real French woman. The figure stood for no particular woman; it gave the idea of the nation a concrete form.

Mixed example 3

Planning a 5-mark answer

Question. "Between 1848 and 1871, nationalism in Europe changed sides — from the liberals to the conservatives." Support the statement. (5 marks)

Asked: evidence that nationalism moved from liberal hands to conservative ones.

Concept used: Topics 6 and 7. Why it applies: 1848 is the liberal attempt; 1871 is the conservative success.

  1. 1

    1848 — liberal nationalism: the middle classes combined national unification with demands for a constitution, a free press and freedom of association.

  2. 2

    Frankfurt fails: the King of Prussia rejected the crown; monarchs, aristocracy and the military opposed the parliament; troops disbanded it.

  3. 3

    After 1848: nationalism moved away from democracy and revolution; conservatives mobilised it for state power.

  4. 4

    Germany: Bismarck, the Prussian army and bureaucracy, backed by the Junkers, unified Germany through three wars; William I became emperor at Versailles (1871).

  5. 5

    Italy: not Mazzini's republic but a kingdom: Cavour's diplomacy and Victor Emmanuel II's war made Italy (1861), while most people did not know the idea of Italy.

  6. Check: points 1–2 show the liberal side failing; points 3–5 show conservatives succeeding — the two halves of the statement ✔.

  7. Answer The liberal attempt of 1848 failed; after it, kings, chief ministers and armies used nationalism to build Germany and Italy from above.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    1. In 1848 the liberal middle classes linked national unity with a constitution, a free press and freedom of association. (1)
    2. The Frankfurt parliament failed: the King of Prussia rejected its crown, and monarchs, aristocracy and army opposed it until troops disbanded it. (1)
    3. After 1848 nationalism moved away from democracy and revolution; conservatives used it to promote state power. (1)
    4. In Germany, Bismarck with the Prussian army and bureaucracy won three wars; William I became emperor at Versailles in 1871. (1)
    5. Italy became a kingdom, not Mazzini's republic, through Cavour's diplomacy and Victor Emmanuel II's war, while many people were unaware of it. (1)
    Common trap: writing about 1848 only. The statement covers 1848 to 1871 — show both sides of the change.

Mixed example 4

An assertion–reason item

Question. Assertion (A): The Treaty of Vienna set up the kingdom of the Netherlands, including Belgium, in the north. Reason (R): The Congress wanted new states along France's borders to stop France from expanding again. Choose: (A) both true, R explains A; (B) both true, R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

Asked: the truth of A and R, and whether R explains A.

Concept used: The Treaty of Vienna (Topic 4).

  1. 1

    A is true: the kingdom of the Netherlands, which included Belgium, was set up in the north.

  2. 2

    R is true: new states were set up along France's borders to stop it expanding again.

  3. 3

    R explains A: the Netherlands was one of those border states — that was its purpose.

  4. Check: ask "Why was the Netherlands set up?" — the answer is R itself, so R explains A ✔. (Belgium later broke away, in 1830, but that does not make A false.)

  5. Answer (A)

    Common trap: choosing (C) because Belgium broke away in 1830. A is about 1815, when it was true.
