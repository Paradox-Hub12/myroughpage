The whole chapter on one page

What a resource is — anything in our environment that can satisfy our needs, provided it is technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally acceptable (T-E-C). NCERT Fig. 1.1: an interactive relationship between nature, technology and institutions, with human beings at the centre. Resources are not free gifts of nature; they are a function of human activities, and human beings are essential components of resources.

Classification (O-E-O-S) — origin: biotic, abiotic · exhaustibility: renewable, non-renewable · ownership: individual, community, national, international · status of development: potential, developed stock and reserves. Fig. 1.2: renewable = flow (wind, water) + biological (forests, wildlife); non-renewable = recyclable (metals) + non-recyclable (fossil fuels); human = structures and institutions, quantity and quality.

Development of resources — indiscriminate use led to depletion (greed of a few), accumulation in few hands (haves and have nots) and global ecological crises (global warming, ozone layer depletion, environmental pollution, land degradation). Equitable distribution → sustained quality of life and global peace; planning → sustainable existence of all forms of life. Sustainable development: no damage to the environment, no compromise with the needs of future generations. Rio, June 1992: 100+ heads of states; Declaration on Global Climatic Change and Biological Diversity; Forest Principles; Agenda 21 (UNCED) — every local government draws its own local Agenda 21.

Planning and conservation — India has enormous diversity: Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh (minerals, coal); Arunachal Pradesh (water, no infrastructure); Rajasthan (solar, wind, no water); Ladakh (heritage; short of water, infrastructure, some minerals). Stages: identify and inventory → planning structure → match with national plans (since the First Five Year Plan). Availability is necessary but not enough: technology and institutions are needed; colonisers exploited through higher technology. Gandhiji: "enough for everybody's need and not for any body's greed"; against mass production, for production by the masses. 1968 Club of Rome → 1974 Schumacher, Small is Beautiful → 1987 Brundtland, Our Common Future → 1992 Rio.

Land — finite; relief plains 43 · mountains 30 · plateau 27 per cent. Five land-use categories; fallow ≤ 1 / 1–5 / > 5 years; gross cropped area = NSA + area sown more than once. Land use depends on physical and human factors. Fig. 1.4: forest 18.11 → 23.3, barren 12.01 → 5.5, non-agricultural 4.95 → 8.7, pasture 4.71 → 3.3, NSA 45.26 → 45.5 (2014-15 shares add to 99.8 as printed). 3.28 million sq km, data for 93%; NSA >80% Punjab, Haryana; <10% Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Manipur, Andaman and Nicobar; forest target 33% (National Forest Policy 1952).

Land degradation — 95% of basic needs from land. Mining (Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha); overgrazing (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra); over irrigation → water logging → salinity, alkalinity (Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh); dust from grinding limestone, calcite, soapstone retards infiltration; industrial effluents. Measures: afforestation, grazing management; arid — shelter belts, control on over grazing, thorny bushes on dunes; industrial — waste-land management, control of mining, treat effluents.

Soils — the most important renewable natural resource, a living system; factors: relief, parent rock, climate, vegetation and other life, time; profile: top soil → subsoil → substratum → unweathered parent bed rock. Alluvial (northern plains, eastern deltas; bangar old with more kankar, khadar new and fertile; sugarcane, paddy, wheat) · Black/regur (Deccan trap; clayey, moisture, rich in calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash, lime, poor in phosphoric contents; cracks, sticky; cotton) · Red and yellow (low rainfall, eastern and southern Deccan; iron → red, hydrated → yellow) · Laterite ('later' = brick; leaching; acidic pH < 6.0; tea, coffee, cashew) · Arid (red to brown, sandy, saline, kankar; western Rajasthan) · Forest (hills and mountains; loamy/silty valleys, coarse upper slopes).

Soil erosion — gully (→ bad land; ravines in the Chambal basin), sheet, wind; ploughing up and down the slope. Cures: contour ploughing, terraces (western and central Himalayas), strip cropping, shelter belts (desert in western India).

Terms, numbers and soils sheet

Key terms (as in your textbook — learn the words in bold)

  • Resource: everything available in our environment which can be used to satisfy our needs, provided it is technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally acceptable.
  • Sustainable economic development: development without damaging the environment, in which development in the present does not compromise with the needs of the future generations.
  • Agenda 21: the declaration signed by world leaders in 1992 at UNCED, Rio de Janeiro, aiming at global sustainable development; every local government should draw its own local Agenda 21.
  • Net sown area: the physical extent of land on which crops are sown and harvested. Gross cropped area: area sown more than once in an agricultural year plus net sown area.
  • Current fallow: left without cultivation for one or less than one agricultural year. Other than current fallow: 1 to 5 years. Culturable waste land: more than 5 years.
  • Soil erosion: the denudation of the soil cover and subsequent washing down.
  • Bad land: land made unfit for cultivation by gullies; called ravines in the Chambal basin.

Numbers to know

  • 43 · 30 · 27 — plains, mountains, plateau (per cent)
  • 3.28 million sq km — India's area; land-use data for 93 per cent
  • 33 per cent — forest target, National Forest Policy 1952; forest 23.3 per cent in 2014-15
  • 45.5 per cent — net sown area 2014-15 (about 54 per cent if other-than-current fallow is included)
  • 95 per cent — basic needs from land
  • pH < 6.0 — laterite
  • 1968 · 1974 · 1987 · 1992 — Club of Rome, Schumacher, Brundtland, Rio

Memory hooks

  • T-E-C (resource) · O-E-O-S (four bases) · Find → Build → Fit (planning stages) · Club, Small, Brundtland, Rio (milestones) · 1 · 5 · more than 5 (fallow) · Bangar is bygone, khadar is the kid · Slopes need steps and lines; winds need strips and trees.
