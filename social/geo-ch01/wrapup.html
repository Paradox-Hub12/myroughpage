Resources on one page

About 25 minutes · 7 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Classify resources on the four bases.
  • Explain the need for resource planning and its three stages.
  • Read the land-use data of NCERT Fig. 1.4.
  • Suggest measures to check land degradation.
  • Identify a soil from its features.
  • Identify the major soil types on a map.
  • Match conservation methods to the erosion they stop.

Almost every question on this chapter asks you to do one of four things: classify (resources, land, soils), read data (the land-use charts), identify a soil (from features or from a map point) or suggest measures (for land degradation or soil erosion). This page puts the chapter on four cards and works through four mixed questions.

Four mixed worked examples

Mixed example 1

A soil from three clues

Question. Identify the soil: (1) it is red to brown in colour; (2) common salt is obtained from it in some areas by evaporating water; (3) after proper irrigation it has become cultivable in western Rajasthan.

Asked: the soil that fits all three clues.

Concept used: Six soils (Card 3).

  1. 1

    Clue 1 fits arid soils ("range from red to brown").

  2. 2

    Clue 2 — salt so high that common salt is obtained — is in the arid paragraph.

  3. 3

    Clue 3 — western Rajasthan — confirms arid.

  4. Check: red soil is red but not salty; laterite is not linked with western Rajasthan. Only one row fits all three clues.

  5. Answer Arid soil

    Common trap: stopping at clue 1 and writing "red soil". Read every clue.

Mixed example 2

Reading the land-use numbers

Question. Using NCERT Fig. 1.4, show with figures that (a) land under non-agricultural uses has grown, and (b) India's forest area is still short of the National Forest Policy target.

Asked: two figure-based statements.

Concept used: Fig. 1.4 (Card 2; Topic 4).

  1. 1

    (a) Area under non-agricultural uses rose from 4.95 per cent (1960-61) to 8.7 per cent (2014-15) — land for settlements, roads, railways and industry has grown.

  2. 2

    (b) Forest was 23.3 per cent in 2014-15, far lower than the desired 33 per cent of the National Forest Policy (1952).

  3. Check: each statement quotes the textbook's numbers; 8.7 > 4.95 ✔ and 23.3 < 33 ✔.

  4. Answer (a) 4.95 → 8.7 per cent; (b) 23.3 per cent against a target of 33 per cent.

    Common trap: writing "forest is 33 per cent". 33 is the target, not the share.

Mixed example 3

Planning a 5-mark answer

Question. "India cannot use one resource plan for all its regions." Support the statement with examples, and explain how resource planning is carried out in India. (5 marks)

Asked: why one plan cannot fit all regions (with examples) and the process of planning.

Concept used: Resource planning (Topic 3).

  1. 1

    Diversity: India has enormous diversity in the availability of resources; some regions are rich in one resource and short of another.

  2. 2

    Examples: Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh — minerals and coal; Arunachal Pradesh — water but little infrastructure; Rajasthan — solar and wind but little water; Ladakh — cultural heritage but short of water, infrastructure and some minerals.

  3. 3

    Stage 1: identification and inventory — surveying, mapping and measuring resources.

  4. 4

    Stage 2: a planning structure with appropriate technology, skill and institutional set up.

  5. 5

    Stage 3: matching resource plans with national development plans, at national, state, regional and local levels.

  6. Check: two points answer "why not one plan", three answer "how" — five different points for five marks, none repeating another.

  7. Answer Regions differ in what they have and lack, so balanced planning at every level is needed, through identification and inventory, a planning structure and matching with national plans.

    Write it in the exam (5 marks):

    1. India has enormous diversity in the availability of resources: some regions are rich in certain resources but deficient in others. (1)
    2. Jharkhand is rich in minerals and coal; Arunachal Pradesh has water but lacks infrastructure; Rajasthan has solar and wind energy but lacks water. (1)
    3. So planning must first identify and inventory resources by surveying, mapping and measuring them. (1)
    4. Next, a planning structure with appropriate technology, skill and institutional set up is evolved. (1)
    5. Finally, resource plans are matched with national development plans, balanced at national, state, regional and local levels. (1)
    Common trap: writing only the three stages. The statement also needs the examples of regional diversity.

Mixed example 4

An assertion and its reason

Question. Assertion (A): Over irrigation is responsible for land degradation in Punjab and Haryana. Reason (R): Water logging caused by over irrigation increases salinity and alkalinity in the soil. Choose: (A) both true, R explains A; (B) both true, R does not explain A; (C) A true, R false; (D) A false, R true.

Asked: the truth of A and R, and whether R explains A.

Concept used: Causes of land degradation (Topic 5).

  1. 1

    A is true: your textbook names Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh.

  2. 2

    R is true: over irrigation → water logging → increase in salinity and alkalinity.

  3. 3

    R explains A: R gives how over irrigation degrades the land.

  4. Check: ask "Why does over irrigation degrade land in Punjab?" — "because water logging raises salinity and alkalinity" answers it ✔.

  5. Answer (A) — both are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

    Common trap: choosing (B) because R does not name Punjab. R does not need to name the place; it must explain the process.
