Topic 2 of 8

Development of resources and sustainable development

About 35 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain the problems caused by indiscriminate use of resources and why an equitable distribution of resources is essential.
  • Explain sustainable development, the Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit (1992) and Agenda 21.

Imagine a village well that everyone shares. One family installs a huge pump and draws water day and night to sell. Soon the well runs dry for everyone else, the pump owner grows rich, and the land around the well cracks. That small story has all three problems your textbook warns about — and it shows why the world now talks about sustainable development.

  1. Why resources were misused

Resources are vital for human survival and for maintaining the quality of life. But people believed that resources are free gifts of nature, so they used them indiscriminately. This led to three major problems:

Problem (your textbook) What it means
1. Depletion of resources for satisfying the greed of a few individuals resources run out because a few people take too much
2. Accumulation of resources in few hands, dividing society into haves and have nots (rich and poor) a few become rich, many stay poor
3. Global ecological crises — global warming, ozone layer depletion, environmental pollution and land degradation the whole planet suffers

  1. Equitable distribution and planning

  • An equitable distribution of resources has become essential for a sustained quality of life and global peace.
  • If the present trend of resource depletion by a few individuals and countries continues, the future of our planet is in danger.
  • Therefore, resource planning is essential for sustainable existence of all forms of life.
  • Sustainable existence is a component of sustainable development.

  1. Sustainable development (textbook box)

Sustainable economic development means 'development should take place without damaging the environment, and development in the present should not compromise with the needs of the future generations.'

Both halves matter: (1) no damage to the environment and (2) the needs of future generations protected.

  1. Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit, 1992 (textbook box)

Point Fact
When and where June 1992, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
Who More than 100 heads of states — the first International Earth Summit
Why to address urgent problems of environmental protection and socio-economic development at the global level
Outcome 1 the leaders signed the Declaration on Global Climatic Change and Biological Diversity
Outcome 2 the Rio Convention endorsed the global Forest Principles
Outcome 3 it adopted Agenda 21 for achieving Sustainable Development in the 21st century

  1. Agenda 21 (textbook box)

  • It is the declaration signed by world leaders in 1992 at the United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED), Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.
  • It aims at achieving global sustainable development.
  • It is an agenda to combat environmental damage, poverty and disease through global co-operation on common interests, mutual needs and shared responsibilities.
  • One major objective: every local government should draw its own local Agenda 21.

Remember: Agenda 21 is a global declaration, not an Indian plan — but it asks every local government (a town council, a panchayat) to make its own local plan.

Worked example 1

The sand-mining town

Question. In a river town, two contractors dig out the river's sand day and night and sell it in big cities. The contractors grow very rich; the boatmen and farmers along the river grow poorer; the riverbanks collapse and nearby fields are spoilt. Which three problems of indiscriminate use of resources from your textbook does this show? (3 marks)

Asked: the three problems, each matched to the story.

Concept used: Problems of indiscriminate use (this topic, section 1).

  1. 1

    Depletion for the greed of a few: the river's sand is being used up so that two contractors can profit.

  2. 2

    Accumulation in few hands: the contractors grow rich while boatmen and farmers grow poorer — society splits into haves and have nots.

  3. 3

    Ecological crisis: collapsing banks and spoilt fields are land degradation, one of the ecological crises named in the textbook.

  4. Check: the three points are three different problems — the greed of a few (who takes), the rich–poor split (who gains and loses) and the damage to land (what happens to nature). If two of your points both say "the sand runs out", one of them is wasted.

  5. Answer Depletion for the greed of a few; accumulation of resources in few hands (rich and poor); an ecological crisis — land degradation.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Depletion: the sand is being used up to satisfy the greed of two contractors. (1)
    2. Accumulation in few hands: the contractors grow rich while boatmen and farmers grow poorer — haves and have nots. (1)
    3. Ecological crisis: collapsing banks and spoilt fields show land degradation. (1)
    Common trap: writing "global warming" as the third point here. The story shows land degradation; pick the crisis the evidence supports.

Worked example 2

Agenda 21 and a city council

Question. A city council says, "Agenda 21 is for world leaders, not for us." Using your textbook, explain why the council is wrong. (2 marks)

Asked: what Agenda 21 expects from local governments.

Concept used: Agenda 21 (this topic, section 5).

  1. 1

    What it is: a declaration signed by world leaders at UNCED, Rio de Janeiro, in 1992, aiming at global sustainable development.

  2. 2

    Its local part: one major objective is that every local government should draw its own local Agenda 21 — so a city council has its own task.

  3. Check: point 2 answers the council directly; point 1 alone would agree with the council, so it cannot be the whole answer.

  4. Answer Agenda 21 (Rio, 1992) aims at global sustainable development, and one major objective is that every local government — including a city council — draws its own local Agenda 21.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. Agenda 21, signed by world leaders at UNCED, Rio de Janeiro, in 1992, aims at global sustainable development. (1)
    2. One major objective is that every local government should draw its own local Agenda 21. (1)
    Common trap: calling Agenda 21 an Indian government plan. It is a global declaration of 1992.

Common mistakes!

  • "Sustainable development means using fewer resources."

    The textbook's meaning has two parts: no damage to the environment and no compromise with the needs of future generations.

    Write both parts.

  • "Agenda 21 is India's plan for the 21st century."

    It is a global declaration signed at UNCED, Rio, 1992.

    Global declaration; every local government draws its own local Agenda 21.

  • Mixing up Rio (1992) and Brundtland (1987).

    Brundtland introduced the concept of sustainable development (1987); Rio adopted Agenda 21 (1992).

    1987 = idea introduced; 1992 = summit and Agenda 21.

Key points

  • Thinking resources were free gifts of nature led to indiscriminate use and three problems: depletion (greed of a few), accumulation in few hands (haves and have nots), global ecological crises (global warming, ozone layer depletion, environmental pollution, land degradation).
  • An equitable distribution of resources is essential for a sustained quality of life and global peace; resource planning is essential for sustainable existence of all forms of life, a component of sustainable development.
  • Sustainable development: development without damaging the environment, not compromising the needs of future generations.
  • Rio, June 1992: first International Earth Summit; 100+ heads of states; Declaration on Global Climatic Change and Biological Diversity; Forest Principles endorsed; Agenda 21 adopted.
  • Agenda 21: signed at UNCED, 1992; fights environmental damage, poverty and disease; every local government draws its own local Agenda 21.
