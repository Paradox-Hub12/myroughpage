Topic 5 of 8

Land degradation and conservation measures

About 35 minutes · 2 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain the human causes of land degradation, with the states your textbook names.
  • Suggest measures to check land degradation in arid areas and in industrial and suburban areas.

A mining company digs a hill for coal and leaves. Years later the hill is a pit with no trees. A herd grazes the same field every day until nothing grows. A farmer floods his field year after year until a white crust of salt appears. Three different places, one result: land that can no longer give what it once gave.

  1. Why land degradation matters

  • We have shared our land with past generations and will have to share it with future generations too.
  • Ninety-five per cent of our basic needs for food, shelter and clothing are obtained from land.
  • Human activities have not only degraded land but also aggravated the pace of natural forces that damage it.

  1. Causes of land degradation — and where

Some human activities such as deforestation, over grazing, mining and quarrying have contributed significantly. Your textbook links each cause to states:

Cause How it degrades land States named in your textbook
Mining (and the deforestation it causes) mining sites are abandoned after excavation, leaving deep scars and traces of over-burdening; deforestation due to mining causes severe degradation Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha
Overgrazing one of the main reasons for land degradation Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra
Over irrigation water logging leads to an increase in salinity and alkalinity in the soil Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh
Mineral processing — grinding limestone for the cement industry, calcite and soapstone for the ceramic industry generates huge quantities of dust; when it settles on land it retards the infiltration of water into the soil —
Industrial effluents as waste a major source of land and water pollution in many parts of the country —

Memory hook — "Mines in the east and centre, herds in the west and centre, too much water in the north-west": mining → Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha; overgrazing → Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra; over irrigation → Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh. (Madhya Pradesh appears in both the mining and the overgrazing lists.)

  1. Measures to check land degradation

There are many ways to solve the problem. Learn them by the kind of area:

Where Measures (your textbook)
Everywhere (helps to some extent) afforestation; proper management of grazing
Arid areas planting shelter belts of plants; control on over grazing; stabilisation of sand dunes by growing thorny bushes
Industrial and suburban areas proper management of waste lands; control of mining activities; proper discharge and disposal of industrial effluents and wastes after treatment

Match the cure to the cause:

  • mining → control of mining activities, afforestation;
  • overgrazing → control on over grazing / proper management of grazing;
  • industrial effluents and dust → treatment before discharge and disposal;
  • drifting sand in dry areas → shelter belts, thorny bushes on sand dunes.

Over irrigation. Your textbook names over irrigation as a cause but lists no separate measure for it. In an answer, describe the cause correctly; for measures, use the textbook's own list above.

Worked example 1

Three degraded sites

Question. A survey team reports three sites: Site 1 — an abandoned coal pit with bare slopes in a mining state; Site 2 — sand blowing from dunes onto fields at the edge of a dry region; Site 3 — a riverbank near a town where a factory pours untreated waste. Suggest one measure from your textbook for each site. (3 marks)

Asked: one textbook measure for each of the three sites.

Concept used: Measures by kind of area (section 3).

  1. 1

    Site 1 (mining): control of mining activities, and afforestation to cover the bare slopes.

  2. 2

    Site 2 (arid area): stabilise the sand dunes by growing thorny bushes, and plant shelter belts.

  3. 3

    Site 3 (industrial area): proper discharge and disposal of industrial effluents and wastes after treatment.

  4. Check: each of the three sites has its own measure, and each measure fits its cause: drifting sand needs bushes, not effluent treatment; a factory needs treatment, not shelter belts. If any site has no measure, the answer is incomplete.

  5. Answer Site 1: control mining and afforest; Site 2: thorny bushes on dunes and shelter belts; Site 3: treat effluents before discharge.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Site 1: control mining activities and carry out afforestation on the bare slopes. (1)
    2. Site 2: stabilise the sand dunes by growing thorny bushes and plant shelter belts. (1)
    3. Site 3: treat industrial effluents and wastes before their proper discharge and disposal. (1)
    Common trap: writing "mining causes deforestation" for Site 1. That is the cause; the question asks for a measure.

Worked example 2

The village beside the cement works

Question. Farmers in a village next to a cement works find a grey layer on their fields; after rain, water stands on the surface instead of soaking in. Explain the cause using your textbook. (2 marks)

Asked: why the water does not soak in.

Concept used: Mineral processing (section 2).

  1. 1

    Source: grinding limestone for the cement industry generates huge quantities of dust in the atmosphere.

  2. 2

    Effect: when the dust settles on the land, it retards the infiltration of water into the soil, so water stands on the surface.

  3. Check: the answer links dust to infiltration — the two facts the textbook joins. "Water logging from over irrigation" would be a different cause, which needs extra watering, not a factory.

  4. Answer Dust from grinding limestone settles on the fields and slows the infiltration of water into the soil.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. Grinding limestone for the cement industry generates huge quantities of dust in the atmosphere. (1)
    2. The dust settles on the land and retards the infiltration of water into the soil. (1)
    Common trap: blaming "salinity". Salinity and alkalinity come from water logging after over irrigation, not from cement dust.

Common mistakes!

  • "Deforestation is the main cause of land degradation in Punjab."

    In Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh the cause is over irrigation → water logging → salinity and alkalinity.

    Match cause ↔ states from the table.

  • Overgrazing states given as Jharkhand and Odisha.

    Overgrazing: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra; mining: Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha.

    Learn the three state lists separately.

  • Writing causes ("overgrazing", "mining") when measures are asked.

    A measure is a cure: control of grazing, control of mining, afforestation…

    Read the verb: "suggest measures" → write cures.

  • Measures with no place or purpose.

    Measures differ for arid and industrial/suburban areas.

    Say where each measure is used.

  • Writing one measure for a 3-mark "suggest measures" question, or a 5-mark "features of alluvial soil" answer with only fertility.

    Each mark needs its own point: 3 marks → three different measures; a 5-mark soil answer needs where, texture, age (bangar/khadar), nutrients and crops.

    Count the marks, write that many numbered points, and give an example when "with examples" is asked.

Key points

  • 95 per cent of our basic needs for food, shelter and clothing come from land; human activities degrade it and speed up natural forces.
  • Mining → Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha (deep scars, over-burdening, deforestation). Overgrazing → Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra. Over irrigation → Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh (water logging → salinity and alkalinity).
  • Dust from grinding limestone (cement) and calcite and soapstone (ceramic) retards infiltration; industrial effluents pollute land and water.
  • Measures: afforestation and proper management of grazing (everywhere); arid areas — shelter belts, control on over grazing, thorny bushes on sand dunes; industrial and suburban areas — manage waste lands, control mining, treat effluents before discharge and disposal.
