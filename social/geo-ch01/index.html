Resources and Development

8 topicsAbout 10 hoursBoard exam
Why this chapter matters

Everything you use — the water you drink, the food you eat, the electricity in your home — comes from a resource. But resources are not simply "there": people make them useful with technology, and people can also waste them, fight over them or destroy the land and soil that hold them. This chapter explains what a resource is, why India must plan and conserve its resources, how India uses its land, and how its six main soils differ from one another — and how to protect them.

You will learn the three conditions that make something a resource, the four ways to classify resources, sustainable development and the Rio Earth Summit (1992), the three stages of resource planning, India's land-use pattern (with the textbook's land-use charts), the causes of land degradation and the measures against it, the six soil types, the soil map, and the methods of soil conservation. It is the first chapter of Contemporary India – II and one of the Geography chapters in the board exam (Geography carries 17 marks + 3 map marks). In the board papers of 2024–2026 it was asked 39 times — mostly as 1-mark "identify the soil" questions and 2- or 3-mark "suggest measures" answers.

What you'll learn

  • Define a resource (T-E-C), read NCERT Figs 1.1 and 1.2, and classify resources on four bases.
  • Explain the problems of indiscriminate use, sustainable development, the Rio Earth Summit and Agenda 21.
  • Explain why India needs resource planning, its three stages, and the conservation milestones from 1968 to 1992.
  • Describe India's relief shares and land-use categories, and read the land-use charts of 1960-61 and 2014-15.
  • Explain the causes of land degradation by state and suggest measures for arid and industrial areas.
  • Explain how soil forms and describe the six soil types.
  • Identify the major soil types on an outline map of India.
  • Explain the types of soil erosion and match each conservation method to its problem.
  • Put the chapter on four cards and answer mixed board-style questions.

Most of the marks in this chapter come from six skills, and every topic practises them:

  1. Classify — resources (four bases), land use (five groups; fallow by years), soils (six types).
  2. Identify a soil — from its colour, texture, nutrients, crops or region.
  3. Read data — the relief shares and the land-use charts of 1960-61 and 2014-15.
  4. Cause → problem → measure — and write the measure when measures are asked.
  5. Map — identify the soil at a point on an outline map of India.
  6. Write to the marks — 2 marks ≈ two points (up to 40 words), 3 marks ≈ three points (up to 60), 5 marks ≈ five points (up to 120), each on its own line.

The spaced-review sets come back to you after 1 day, 1 week and 1 month. Don't skip them — remembering something after a gap is what makes it stick.

Not in the board exam

The textbook's Project/Activity 1–3 and the survey activity on p. 2 are classroom projects; they get short guided answers in the NCERT solutions and are not part of the board exam. Everything else in the chapter — including the boxes on sustainable development, the Rio Earth Summit, Agenda 21 and the Club of Rome, Figs 1.1–1.11 and the soil map — can be asked in the board exam. The syllabus map item for this chapter is "Identify Major Soil Types".
