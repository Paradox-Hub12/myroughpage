How this chapter is asked

The board papers of 2024–2026 asked this chapter 39 times: most often as a 1-mark "identify the soil" question and as 2- or 3-mark "suggest measures" answers on land degradation, soil erosion and conservation; then resource planning, soil features and land use. Two items were 5-mark answers (alluvial soil; resource planning) and two were 4-mark case studies (conservation of resources). No paper and neither sample paper has yet asked the soil map item. Each sample paper gives the chapter two 1-mark items.

About the marks

The sample-paper answers follow the official marking schemes. No marking scheme is published for the board papers, so their mark splits are our marking points, written to the CBSE word limits (2 marks — up to 40 words, 3 marks — up to 60, 5 marks — up to 120, case study — up to 100). Where an official question is printed with a slip, it is quoted exactly and a note explains the textbook answer. Where an official question can be argued two ways and no official key exists, the item is not a click-to-answer question: open "Show the answer" to see our key and the note.

Sample papers 2026-27 and 2025-26

CBSE Sample Question Paper 2026-27, Section B, Q101 mark

Question: A mineral deposit, that is known but cannot be used currently due to lack of technology, fits into which category of resources?

Asked: the class, by status of development, of a known deposit that cannot be used for lack of technology.

Concept used: Classification on the basis of status of development (Topic 1, section 4 and the word hint for "potential").

  1. 1

    Which basis? "Known but cannot be used now" is about status of development, so A (renewable — exhaustibility) is out.

  2. 2

    Developed? A developed resource is one already in use; this deposit is not used, so B is out.

  3. 3

    The sample paper's key: the official marking scheme gives C. Potential resource — a resource that is known but cannot be used now because the technology is lacking. Learn the term in this sense.

  4. Check: the stem says "cannot be used currently due to lack of technology"; the only option the marking scheme accepts is C.

    Common trap: choosing A because minerals sound "natural". Renewable/non-renewable is a different basis.
Option (C): Potential resource
1 mark

Note on the official text: your textbook only names the classes "potential, developed stock and reserves" and does not explain them; the answer above follows the official marking scheme's key for this sample paper.

Option (A): 1-Y, 2-X, 3-Z

Board 2026, 32/2/2 Q153 marks

Question: Explain any three factors responsible for soil formation.

Asked: three factors of soil formation, each explained.

Concept used: How soil forms (Topic 6, section 2).

  1. 1

    Parent rock: the rock from which soil forms decides its kind — black soil from lava (Deccan trap), red soil on crystalline igneous rocks.

  2. 2

    Climate: heavy rain causes intense leaching (laterite); a dry, hot climate leaves arid soils without humus and moisture.

  3. 3

    Time: it takes millions of years to form soil up to a few cm in depth.

  4. Check: each point names a factor from the textbook's list (relief, parent rock, climate, vegetation and other forms of life, time) and shows its effect with a textbook example. Relief or vegetation would also be correct.

    Common trap: listing the soil types. The question asks for the factors that form soil.
  1. Parent rock: black soil forms from lava rock; red soil develops on crystalline igneous rocks. (1)
  1. Climate: heavy rain leaches laterite soil; a dry, hot climate leaves arid soils without humus. (1)
  1. Time: soil forms very slowly; it takes millions of years to form a few centimetres. (1)

Board 2025, 32/2/1 Q26 · 32/2/2 Q28 · 32/2/3 Q263 marks

Question: Read the story of Rinjha and answer the question that follows :

Story of Rinjha. Rinjha lived with her family in a small village at the outskirts of Diphu in Assam. She enjoys watching her family members clearing, slashing and burning a patch of land for cultivation. She often helps them in irrigating the fields with water running through a bamboo canal from the nearby spring. She loves the surroundings and wants to stay here as long as she can, but this little girl has no idea about the declining fertility of the soil and her family's search for a fresh patch of land in the next season.

Suggest any three measures to Rinjha so that the fertility of the soil of her fields remains intact for a long time.

Asked: three measures to keep the soil fertile, for a family that clears and burns hill land.

Concept used: Soil erosion and conservation (Topic 8); land-degradation measures (Topic 5). The kind of farming in the story is taught in the Agriculture chapter.

  1. 1

    Keep the tree cover: clearing forest exposes the soil; afforestation protects it, because deforestation disturbs the balance between soil formation and erosion.

  2. 2

    Hold soil on slopes: contour ploughing slows water down the slope.

  3. 3

    Make terraces: terrace cultivation restricts erosion on hill slopes.

  4. Check: each measure protects the top soil, where the fertility is; none repeats another. Strip cropping or controlling grazing would also be acceptable.

    Common trap: writing "move to a new patch every year". That is what the family already does — and why the soil keeps losing fertility.
  1. Stop clearing new patches and plant trees; afforestation keeps the soil covered and protected. (1)
  1. Plough along the contour lines so that water flows slowly down the slope. (1)
  1. Cut terraces on the slopes; terrace cultivation restricts soil erosion. (1)
