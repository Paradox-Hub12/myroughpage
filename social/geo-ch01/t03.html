Topic 3 of 8

Resource planning and conservation

About 40 minutes · 3 things to learn
By the end you can…
  • Explain why resource planning is needed in India, with the textbook's regional examples, and its three stages.
  • Explain why the availability of resources alone does not bring development (technology, institutions, colonisation).
  • Explain resource conservation, Gandhiji's view and the international milestones from 1968 to 1992.

A family has plenty of rice but no cooking gas; the neighbours have gas but no rice. Each household alone is stuck — together, with a plan, both eat. India is like a big joint family of regions: some are rich in minerals, some in water, some in sunshine, and none has everything. That is why India needs resource planning.

  1. Why India needs resource planning

Planning is the widely accepted strategy for judicious use of resources. It is important in India because India has enormous diversity in the availability of resources:

  • some regions are rich in certain resources but deficient in others;
  • some regions are self-sufficient, and some have an acute shortage of vital resources.

Your textbook's examples:

Region Rich in Short of
Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh minerals and coal deposits —
Arunachal Pradesh water resources infrastructural development
Rajasthan solar and wind energy water resources
Ladakh (cold desert) a very rich cultural heritage water, infrastructure and some vital minerals; relatively isolated

This calls for balanced resource planning at the national, state, regional and local levels.

  1. Resource planning in India — three stages

Resource planning is a complex process which involves:

  1. Identification and inventory of resources across the regions of the country — surveying, mapping and qualitative and quantitative estimation and measurement of resources.
  2. Evolving a planning structure endowed with appropriate technology, skill and institutional set up for implementing resource development plans.
  3. Matching the resource development plans with overall national development plans.

India has made concerted efforts for achieving these goals right from the First Five Year Plan, launched after Independence.

Memory hook: "Find → Build → Fit." Find the resources (survey, map, measure) → Build the structure (technology, skill, institutions) → Fit the plans into the national plan.

  1. Is having resources enough?

  • The availability of resources is a necessary condition for development, but mere availability, without matching changes in technology and institutions, may hinder development.
  • Many regions are rich in resources but economically backward; some have a poor resource base but are economically developed.
  • Colonisation: rich resources in colonies attracted foreign invaders; the higher level of technological development of the colonising countries helped them exploit the resources of other regions and establish supremacy over the colonies.
  • So resources contribute to development only when accompanied by appropriate technological development and institutional changes. In India, development depends not only on the availability of resources, but also on technology, the quality of human resources and the historical experiences of the people.

  1. Conservation of resources

Resources are vital for development, but irrational consumption and over-utilisation of resources may lead to socio-economic and environmental problems. To overcome them, resource conservation at various levels is important.

Gandhiji's view:

  • "There is enough for everybody's need and not for any body's greed."
  • He placed greedy and selfish individuals and the exploitative nature of modern technology as the root cause of resource depletion at the global level.
  • He was against mass production and wanted to replace it with production by the masses.

  1. International milestones (textbook box)

Year Milestone
1968 Club of Rome — advocated resource conservation for the first time in a more systematic way at the international level
1974 Schumacher, Small is Beautiful — Gandhian philosophy presented once again
1987 Brundtland Commission Report — the seminal contribution; introduced the concept of 'Sustainable Development' as a means for resource conservation; published as the book Our Common Future
1992 Earth Summit, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil — another significant contribution (Topic 2)

Memory hook: "Club, Small, Brundtland, Rio" — 1968, 1974, 1987, 1992.

Worked example 1

A planning officer's three steps

Question. A new district is formed. Its planning officer must prepare a resource plan. Explain, in the order of your textbook, the three stages she should follow. (3 marks)

Asked: the three stages of resource planning, applied to a district.

Concept used: Resource planning in India (this topic, section 2).

  1. 1

    Identify and inventory: survey and map the district's land, water, minerals and forests, and estimate and measure how much there is.

  2. 2

    Build the planning structure: set up the technology, trained people (skill) and offices (institutional set up) needed to carry out the plans.

  3. 3

    Match with the national plan: make sure the district's resource plans fit the country's overall development plans.

  4. Check: each stage has a different action — finding out, building the means, fitting the plan. If two of your points both say "survey the resources", the second one is not stage 2.

  5. Answer Identification and inventory → evolving a planning structure with technology, skill and institutions → matching with national development plans.

    Write it in the exam (3 marks):

    1. Identify and inventory the district's resources by surveying, mapping and measuring them. (1)
    2. Evolve a planning structure with appropriate technology, skill and institutional set up. (1)
    3. Match the district's resource plans with the overall national development plans. (1)
    Common trap: writing "use the resources carefully" as a stage. That is the aim of planning, not a stage.

Worked example 2

Plenty of water, slow development

Question. Arunachal Pradesh has an abundance of water resources. Using your textbook, explain why this alone has not been enough for its development. (2 marks)

Asked: why abundant water has not been enough.

Concept used: Regional examples (section 1) and "Is having resources enough?" (section 3).

  1. 1

    The textbook's fact: Arunachal Pradesh has abundant water but lacks infrastructural development.

  2. 2

    The rule: availability is only a necessary condition; without matching technology and institutions, resources may not lead to development.

  3. Check: point 1 is the fact about the state; point 2 is the reason that explains it. An answer that only repeats "it has a lot of water" does not explain anything.

  4. Answer It lacks infrastructure; resources lead to development only with matching technology and institutions.

    Write it in the exam (2 marks):

    1. Arunachal Pradesh has abundant water resources but lacks infrastructural development. (1)
    2. Mere availability of resources, without matching technology and institutions, may hinder development. (1)
    Common trap: saying Arunachal Pradesh is "short of water". It is rich in water; it is short of infrastructure.

Worked example 3

Put the milestones in order

Question. Arrange in order, earliest first: I. The concept of sustainable development is introduced in a commission's report. II. Leaders meet in Brazil for the first International Earth Summit. III. A book presents Gandhian philosophy again. IV. The Club of Rome advocates systematic resource conservation.

Asked: the chronological order.

Concept used: International milestones (section 5).

  1. 1

    Date each: IV — 1968; III — 1974 (Small is Beautiful); I — 1987 (Brundtland); II — 1992 (Rio).

  2. 2

    Sort: 1968 → 1974 → 1987 → 1992.

  3. Check: "Club, Small, Brundtland, Rio" gives IV, III, I, II ✔. II must come last because Rio comes after Brundtland introduced the idea it worked on.

  4. Answer IV, III, I, II

    Common trap: putting the Earth Summit before the Brundtland Report because sustainable development "began at Rio". The concept was introduced in 1987.

Common mistakes!

  • "A region rich in resources is always developed."

    Availability is a necessary condition, but without technology and institutions it may even hinder development.

    Write: resources + technology + institutions → development.

  • Stages of planning in the wrong order.

    The order is identify and inventory → planning structure → match with national plans.

    "Find → Build → Fit".

  • "Gandhiji supported mass production."

    He was against mass production and wanted production by the masses.

    Quote his "need, not greed" line and his two causes.

  • Milestones out of order (e.g. Rio before Brundtland).

    1968 → 1974 → 1987 → 1992.

    "Club, Small, Brundtland, Rio".

Key points

  • Planning is the widely accepted strategy for judicious use of resources; India needs it because of enormous diversity in the availability of resources (Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh — minerals and coal; Arunachal Pradesh — water, not infrastructure; Rajasthan — solar and wind, not water; Ladakh — cultural heritage, short of water, infrastructure and some minerals).
  • Three stages: identification and inventory → planning structure (technology, skill, institutions) → matching with national plans; efforts since the First Five Year Plan.
  • Availability alone is not enough: resources lead to development only with appropriate technology and institutional changes; colonisers exploited colonies through higher technology.
  • Conservation: irrational consumption and over-utilisation cause socio-economic and environmental problems. Gandhiji: "enough for everybody's need and not for any body's greed"; blamed greedy, selfish individuals and exploitative modern technology; wanted production by the masses.
  • Milestones: Club of Rome 1968 → Schumacher 1974 → Brundtland 1987 (Our Common Future) → Rio 1992.
