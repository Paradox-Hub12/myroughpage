Topic 7 of 8

Map work: the major soil types

About 30 minutes · 1 thing to learn
By the end you can…
  • Identify the major soil types on an outline map of India from where they occur.

Your syllabus lists one map item for this chapter: "Identify Major Soil Types". A dot appears somewhere on the outline map of India, and you must say which soil is found there. If you know where each soil occurs — plains, lava plateau, desert, mountains, coast — the dot tells you the answer.

Quick reminder (states on the map)

On an outline map of India, north is at the top. Rajasthan is the large state in the north-west, with the Thar desert; Uttar Pradesh lies on the northern plains; Himachal Pradesh is in the Himalayas in the north; Maharashtra is in the west-centre and touches the Arabian Sea; Goa is the small state just south of Maharashtra's coast; Karnataka is south of Maharashtra; Andhra Pradesh has a long coast on the Bay of Bengal; Odisha lies on the east coast, north of Andhra Pradesh.

  1. How the soil map is asked

  • "Identify" — a place is marked on the outline map; you write the name of the soil found there.
  • For candidates with visual impairment, CBSE asks map items as short-answer questions ("Name the soil found in…").
  • Your textbook's map "India: Major Soil Types" (p. 8) shows six soils in colour: Forest & Mountainous, Alluvial, Red and Yellow, Black, Laterite, Arid. Study the coloured areas there; the map on this page shows only example points.
  • No board paper of 2024, 2025 or 2026 and neither sample paper has asked this map item yet, but it is in the syllabus — learn it.

  1. Where each soil occurs

Soil Where it occurs (your textbook and its map) On the map, look for…
Alluvial the entire northern plains (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra); a narrow corridor into Rajasthan and Gujarat; the eastern coastal plains, especially the deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri the wide plains below the Himalayas; the river mouths on the east coast
Black the Deccan trap (basalt) of the north-west Deccan plateau — plateaus of Maharashtra, Saurashtra, Malwa, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh; along the Godavari and Krishna valleys the big area in the west-centre of the peninsula
Red and yellow eastern and southern parts of the Deccan plateau; parts of Odisha, Chhattisgarh, the southern middle Ganga plain, the piedmont zone of the Western Ghats most of the east and south of the peninsula
Laterite southern states, the Western Ghats region of Maharashtra, Odisha, some parts of West Bengal, the north-east a narrow strip along the coast from southern Maharashtra (near Goa) to northern Kerala, and small patches elsewhere (the textbook map shows the coast near Mumbai as red and yellow)
Arid western Rajasthan the dry north-west
Forest (and mountainous) hilly and mountainous areas — the Himalayas the far north and the high north-east

Memory hook — read India from top to bottom: mountains → forest soil; plains → alluvial; desert (north-west) → arid; lava plateau (west-centre) → black; rest of the plateau (east and south) → red and yellow; the coastal strip from south Maharashtra to northern Kerala → laterite (the coast near Mumbai is red and yellow).

  1. Eight example points

These are the points used on the map and in the trainer below. Each point is only an example location of its soil, not the whole area of that soil.

Point Where Soil
1 central Uttar Pradesh, on the northern plains built by the Ganga Alluvial
2 Andhra Pradesh, in the Godavari delta on the eastern coastal plain Alluvial
3 the middle of Maharashtra, on the Deccan trap (lava) plateau Black
4 Odisha, on the eastern part of the Deccan plateau Red and yellow
5 Karnataka, on the southern part of the Deccan plateau Red and yellow
6 the south of Maharashtra, near Goa, on the coastal side Laterite
7 western Rajasthan, in the dry desert region Arid
8 Himachal Pradesh, in the Himalayan mountains Forest

Why point 6 is "near Goa". Your textbook names laterite in the "Western Ghats region of Maharashtra", but it also places red and yellow soils along the "piedmont zone of the Western Ghats". On the textbook map, the laterite strip starts in the south of Maharashtra and runs down the coast to northern Kerala; near Goa the dot sits inside it. Further north, near Mumbai, the coastal strip is red and yellow soil. A dot simply "in the Western Ghats of Maharashtra" could be on red, laterite or black soil.

  1. Reading a marked point — three steps

  1. Which state or region? (plains, desert, mountains, plateau, coast)
  2. Which landform or climate clue? (lava plateau, delta, heavy rain on the coast, dry desert)
  3. Which soil matches both? Use the table in section 2.

Worked examples

Worked example 1

Two marked points

Question. On an outline map of India, point A is marked in western Rajasthan and point B on the plateau in the middle of Maharashtra. Identify the soil at each point.

Asked: the soil at A and at B.

Concept used: Where each soil occurs (section 2).

  1. 1

    A — western Rajasthan: dry desert region → arid soil (your textbook: arid soils become cultivable after irrigation "as has been in the case of western Rajasthan").

  2. 2

    B — middle of Maharashtra, plateau: the Deccan trap lava plateau → black soil.

  3. Check: Rajasthan's west is not a lava plateau, and Maharashtra's plateau is not a desert, so the two answers cannot be swapped. Alluvial soil reaches Rajasthan only through a narrow corridor, not the dry west.

  4. Answer A arid soil; B black soil.

    Common trap: writing "alluvial" for A because alluvial soil "extends into Rajasthan". That is a narrow corridor; the western desert is arid.

Worked example 2

Why the point near Goa is laterite

Question. A point is marked in the south of Maharashtra, near Goa, on the coastal side. A student writes "red soil" because "red and yellow soils are found along the Western Ghats". Name the correct soil and explain the student's mistake.

Asked: the soil, and why the student's reason fails.

Concept used: Laterite and red and yellow soils (section 2); why point 6 is "near Goa" (section 3).

  1. 1

    Correct soil: laterite — your textbook names the Western Ghats region of Maharashtra for laterite, and on the textbook map the coastal strip near Goa is laterite.

  2. 2

    The student's mistake: red and yellow soils occur along the piedmont zone of the Western Ghats — a different belt, inland of the laterite strip. "Western Ghats" alone does not decide the soil; the coastal side near Goa does.

  3. Check: read the textbook map at this latitude: going inland from the coast near Goa, the soils run laterite → red and yellow → black, so a point on the coastal side is laterite. Do not turn this into a rule for the whole west coast — near Mumbai the coastal strip is red and yellow.

  4. Answer Laterite. The student used a clue ("Western Ghats") that fits two soils; the coastal position near Goa points to laterite.

    Common trap: choosing a soil from one word in the clue. Use all the clues: state, landform and climate.

Quick check · 1 of 3MCQ1 mark

A point is marked on the northern plains in central Uttar Pradesh. Which soil is found there?

Black soil is on the Deccan trap plateau.
On the textbook map laterite is a coastal strip from south Maharashtra to northern Kerala, with patches in Odisha, West Bengal and the north-east — not the northern plains; even the coast near Mumbai is red and yellow.
Arid soil is in western Rajasthan.
The entire northern plains are made of alluvial soil.
Quick check · 2 of 3MCQ1 mark

A point is marked in the delta of the Godavari on the coast of Andhra Pradesh. Which soil is found there?

Alluvial soil is found in the deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.
Forest soils are in hilly and mountainous areas.
Arid soils are in the dry north-west.
Black soil extends along the Godavari and Krishna valleys on the plateau, not the coastal delta.
Quick check · 3 of 3MCQ1 mark

A point is marked high in the mountains of Himachal Pradesh. Which soil is found there?

Red and yellow soils are on the eastern and southern Deccan plateau.
Alluvial soil is on the plains below the mountains.
Forest soils are found in the hilly and mountainous areas of the Himalayas.
Laterite is not the soil of the western Himalayas.

Common mistakes!

  • Black soil marked on the northern plains.

    Black soil is on the Deccan trap of the north-west Deccan plateau; the northern plains are alluvial.

    Plains → alluvial; lava plateau → black.

  • Laterite marked anywhere "in the Western Ghats".

    Red and yellow soils lie along the piedmont zone of the Western Ghats too.

    Laterite: the coastal strip from south Maharashtra (near Goa) to northern Kerala; the coast near Mumbai is red and yellow.

  • Arid soil marked in Gujarat's plains or eastern Rajasthan.

    Arid soil is in western Rajasthan; alluvial soil reaches Rajasthan and Gujarat through a narrow corridor.

    Arid = the dry west of Rajasthan.

  • Red soil marked on the Maharashtra lava plateau.

    That is black soil; red and yellow soils are on the eastern and southern Deccan.

    West-centre black; east and south red.

Key points

  • Syllabus map item: identify the major soil types on an outline map of India.
  • Alluvial — northern plains, eastern deltas; Black — Deccan trap (Maharashtra, Saurashtra, Malwa, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh); Red and yellow — eastern and southern Deccan, Odisha, Chhattisgarh; Laterite — southern states, Western Ghats region of Maharashtra (the coastal strip from south Maharashtra, near Goa, to northern Kerala), Odisha, parts of West Bengal, north-east; Arid — western Rajasthan; Forest — Himalayas.
  • Read a point in three steps: state or region → landform or climate → soil.
  • Each map point on this page is an example location, not the full area of a soil; study the coloured areas on your textbook's map (p. 8).
